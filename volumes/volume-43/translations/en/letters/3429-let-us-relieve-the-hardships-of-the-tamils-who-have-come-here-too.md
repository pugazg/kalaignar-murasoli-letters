# 3429. Let Us Relieve the Hardships of the Tamils Who Have Come Here Too!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This pilot translation follows the audited canonical Tamil in source order. It preserves the letter’s humanitarian framing, administrative chronology, figures, lists, source-supplied English, and the distinction between the source’s references to Sri Lankan Tamil refugees and its later political-familial description of them simply as Tamils. No outside facts are added or used to reconcile the source.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3429](../../../chapters/3429-ingu-vanthulla-tamizharkkum-idar-kalaivom.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 33](../../../pages/page-033.md)–[PDF 39](../../../pages/page-039.md)  
**Date:** 4 November 2009

**Udanpirappē,**

On the evening of 1-11-2009, I was travelling by car. That week’s magazines had been placed beside me in the car. Among them was the Tamil edition of *India Today* dated 11-11-2009. I turned its pages. On page 40, under the heading “Darkened Camps,” there was an article listing the sufferings of Sri Lankan Tamil refugees living in camps in Tamil Nadu without basic facilities and asking whether they too would receive greater freedom like refugees in other countries. I could not bear it after reading the article.

I immediately called my secretaries and told them to arrange a discussion on the matter at the Secretariat on the morning of 2-11-2009, and that all ministers and officials responsible for it should take part. I also called the Finance Secretary by telephone and had all the notes relating to the matter prepared. Even after that my mind was not at ease. I immediately called an assistant, personally dictated a long statement, and had it sent to the newspapers.

Nor did I stop there. I telephoned Maravanpulavu K. Sachithananthan — a Sri Lankan Tamil, a distinguished writer, and one of those interviewed in *India Today* — and asked him to give me, by eight the next morning, a note on the urgent and essential needs of the Sri Lankan Tamil refugees. Only after all this did I go to take part in the discussion on constituting committees for the World Classical Tamil Conference.

I could not sleep peacefully through the night. Again and again the lines of the *India Today* article moved through my mind. This was because only a few days earlier, on 24-10-2009, *The Hindu* had carried an item under the heading:

> Facilities at Refugee Camps good : U.S. Consul-General

and had continued with the words:

> U.S. Consul-General Andres T. Simkin, on Friday expressed satisfaction with the facilities provided to refugees living in camps in Tamil Nadu

The *India Today* article was contrary to those reports.

Yesterday, 2-11-2009, dawn itself came with sad news. I heard that Jayaraman, the Vandavasi MLA, had passed away. I immediately went to the hospital, paid my respects and returned. Maravanpulavu Sachithananthan arrived at eight in the morning as he had said he would. He too had bought and read *India Today* the previous night. When he saw me, he was deeply distressed and said that the article contained many things that he had not said in his interview. Even so, I took his notes and hurried to the Secretariat meeting.

By now you would have read the interview given to reporters by the minister in charge of the department, K.K.S.S.R. Ramachandran, about the decisions taken at that meeting.

You may be thinking: why, then, this letter? I am writing it so that you and others may know what our DMK government has done for the Sri Lankan Tamils staying in the refugee camps here. Here is that long list.

In Tamil Nadu, Sri Lankan Tamils have been settled in 115 camps across 26 districts, including two special camps. To date, 73,241 people belonging to 19,340 families are staying in the camps. About 31,802 people belonging to 11,288 families are living outside the camps.

In 2006, the roofs of 9,562 houses were repaired at a cost of ₹2 crore 80 lakh. Basic infrastructure was improved at a cost of ₹3 crore. In 2006, basic facilities such as toilets and drinking water were provided in 86 camps at a cost of ₹9 crore 12 lakh. In 2007–08, new toilets and drinking-water facilities were completed in 50 camps at a cost of ₹5 crore 24 lakh. In 2008, ₹3 crore 12 lakh was spent to renovate houses and provide toilets and drinking-water facilities in 72 camps. In the 2009–10 Budget, ₹5 crore was allocated for such maintenance work.

In Tirunelveli district, 96 houses were completed at Gangaikondan at a cost of ₹1.20 crore; 90 houses at Nattarasankottai, Chennalkudi at a cost of ₹30.80 lakh; 62 houses in the Gudiyatham camp in Vellore district at ₹21.08 lakh; 63 houses at Kangeyam in Erode district at ₹22.68 lakh; and 26 houses at Pavoorchatram in Tirunelveli district at ₹8.84 lakh. Sri Lankan Tamils have been settled in these houses.

Government orders have been issued to build 450 houses at Achimangalam in Karur district at a cost of ₹1.16 crore. Permission has also been granted and work begun to construct 150 new houses at Vedar Colony camp in Coimbatore district at a cost of ₹86 lakh; 115 houses at Thirumoorthinagar camp at ₹98 lakh; 87 houses at Chinnapallikuppam camp in Vellore district at ₹68.80 lakh; nine houses at Naranammalpuram in Tirunelveli district at ₹7.74 lakh; 46 houses at Aladiyur camp at ₹39.56 lakh; and 60 houses at Athiyadipillaiyarkovil refugee camp at ₹51.60 lakh.

From 1.8.2006, the cash dole provided by the Union and State governments was doubled: for the head of a family, from ₹200 to ₹400; for each adult in the family, from ₹144 to ₹288; for the first child in the family, from ₹90 to ₹180; and for each of the other children, from ₹45 to ₹90.

Rice is being supplied at the subsidised rate of 57 paise per kilogram.

The Dr. Muthulakshmi Reddy maternity-assistance scheme providing ₹6,000 to pregnant women was extended from 2007 to Sri Lankan Tamils living in the camps. About 1,000 people received it in 2007–08 and 2,242 people in 2008–09.

Students from Sri Lankan Tamil families living in the camps are provided free education up to Class 12, free textbooks, notebooks and uniforms, the noon meal, and free bicycles for Class 11 students. In 2008–09, 20,106 students benefited, and in 2009–10, 19,737 students benefited.

For 2008–09 and 2009–10, appropriate permission and orders were issued allowing Sri Lankan Tamil students to be admitted to vacancies remaining in arts colleges after Tamil Nadu students had been admitted. In 2008–09, 917 students studied under this arrangement, and in 2009–10, 1,084 students are studying.

Students living in the camps have been permitted to stay in other districts for higher education, and orders have been issued for the cash dole due to them to be paid to their parents.

In 2009–10, government orders were issued allowing Sri Lankan Tamil students to be admitted to vacancies arising in the general category in self-financing engineering colleges.

Public-health facilities have been provided to Sri Lankan Tamils in all camps. Free treatment is given at primary health centres near the camps. In addition, medical teams visit the camps twice a month and carry out necessary examinations. Where specialised treatment is required, arrangements have been made for higher treatment through government hospitals in the respective districts and cities.

₹15,000 is provided from the Hon’ble Chief Minister’s Special Relief Fund for Sri Lankan Tamils for accident treatment and life-saving medical treatment.

Before 2006, electricity had been supplied only from 6 p.m. to 6 a.m. After 2006, orders were issued for electricity to be supplied continuously, 24 hours a day, and that supply is being provided.

To enable Sri Lankan Tamils living in camps to improve their livelihoods, they are allowed to go elsewhere for work from 6 a.m. to 6 p.m. Those who wish to go to other places, stay there and work are given special permission, with orders allowing them to return only on the day the cash dole is distributed and collect it.

Births of children born to Sri Lankan Tamils in the camps are registered by the concerned Village Administrative Officers, and all relief assistance due to them is immediately provided. The relevant Sri Lankan birth certificates are also issued from time to time by the Sri Lankan diplomatic mission.

The Tamil Nadu government continues to provide all the above assistance to Sri Lankan Tamils living in the camps.

I do not consider our duty to have ended with this. That is why, as I wrote at the beginning, I convened this meeting in such urgency. I want a lasting dawn of relief to arrive for the Sri Lankan Tamils in those camps.

At the centenary-completion celebration of Perarignar Anna held at Kanchi on 26-9-2009, Deputy Chief Minister M.K. Stalin, while reading the final resolution, said that over and above all these measures, the Union Government should be consulted so that, as an effort to resettle them under the Indian Citizenship Act and other related laws, a way could be created for them to reside permanently in Tamil Nadu, and arrangements could be made for their accommodation and livelihood.

Not only was that request made. The very next day, 27-9-2009, I wrote to the Prime Minister:

> I request your kind intervention on humanitarian grounds and use of your esteemed offices to resolve this larger issue occupying the centre stage of attention of the Tamil people enabling the permanent settlement of Sri Lankan Tamil Refugees in Tamil Nadu. Once their stay is legally regularised, the Government of Tamil Nadu will be able to take up necessary steps for the betterment of their livelihood, economically and socially

Therefore, as far as this government is concerned, they are not Sri Lankan Tamil refugees; they too are Tamils. They are people joined to us by an umbilical-cord relationship. We act with the view that every effort to enable them to live is an effort to enable ourselves to live.

Because a weekly magazine pointed them out, this government has allocated ₹12 crore to identify the deficiencies that remain here and there and undertake relief work. We are also confident that assistance from the Union Government will continue to be available.

I request that not only our ministers but Udanpirappus like you too should extend every possible cooperation to them and to this government in relieving the hardships of the Sri Lankan Tamils living here.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**4-11-2009**

## Letter-specific notes

1. The source’s printed English from *The Hindu* and the Prime Minister letter is retained verbatim.
2. `பணக்கொடை` is rendered **cash dole**, preserving the administrative welfare context.
3. `மத்திய அரசு` is rendered **Union Government** and `கழக அரசு` as **DMK government** where the source context clearly identifies the party government.
4. The source alternates between administrative “Sri Lankan Tamil refugees” language and the closing political-familial assertion that “they too are Tamils”; that shift is preserved rather than flattened.

esteemed offices to resolve this larger issue occupying the centre stage
of attention of the Tamil people enabling the permanent settlement of
Sri Lankan Tamil Refugees in Tamil Nadu. Once their stay is legally
regularised, the Government of Tamil Nadu will be able to take up
necessary steps for the betterment of their livelihood, economically and
socially” என்று எழுதியிருந்தேன்.
எனவே இந்த அரசைப் பொறுத்தவரையில் - அவர்கள்
இலங்கைத் தமிழ் அகதிகள் அல்ல; அவர்களும் தமிழர்கள்தான்
- நம்மோடு தொப்புள் கொடி உறவு கொண்டவர்கள்தான் -
அவர்களை வாழ்விக்கச் செய்யும் அனைத்து முயற்சிகளும்
நம்மை நாமே வாழ்வித்துக் கொள்ளச் செய்யும் முயற்சிகள் தான்
என்ற நோக்கோடு செயல்படுகின்றது. “வார ஏடு” ஒன்று சுட்டிக்
காட்டியதால் அங்கொன்றும் இங்கொன்றுமாக இருக்கின்ற
குறைகளையும் கண்டறிந்து நிவாரணப் பணிகளை மேற்
கொள்ளத்தான் இந்த அரசின் சார்பில் 12 கோடி ரூபாய்
நிதிஒதுக்கீடு செய்யப்பட்டுள்ளது. மேலும் மத்திய அரசின்
உதவிகளும் தொடர்ந்து கிடைக்கும் என்ற நம்பிக்கையிருக்கிறது.
நமது அமைச்சர்கள் மட்டுமல்லாது; உன் போன்ற
உடன்பிறப்புகளும் இங்குள்ள இலங்கைத் தமிழர்களின்
இடர் களைந்திட ஒல்லும் வகையெல்லாம் அவர்களுக்கும்,
இந்த அரசுக்கும் ஒத்துழைப்பு தர வேண்டுமெனக் கேட்டுக்
கொள்கிறேன்.
அன்புள்ள,
மு.க.
4-11-2009
