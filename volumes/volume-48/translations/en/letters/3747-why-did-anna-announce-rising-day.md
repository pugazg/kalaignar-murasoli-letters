# 3747. Why Did Anna Declare a Day of Uprising?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

For how many years has the Sethu Samudram project been the dream of the people of Tamil Nadu?

The Sethu Samudram project is a dream cherished by the Tamil people for 150 years. Perarignar Anna, even while in office, observed an **Ezhuchi Naal — Day of Uprising** for the Sethu Samudram project. It was not only the DMK: people have not forgotten that the AIADMK too demanded the Sethu Samudram project in its 2001 and 2004 election manifestos. They said they would press the government formed at the Centre and secure the Sethu project. Yet those very people have now, on 29 April 2013, filed a petition in the Supreme Court saying that the Sethu Samudram project is not wanted.

Why? Even when my dear friend M.G.R. was Chief Minister, on 10 May 1986 a resolution was adopted in the Legislative Assembly urging the Union Government to implement the Sethu Samudram project.

The scene at the inauguration of the Sethu Samudram project works on 2 July 2005 now comes before my eyes: Madurai city itself seemed to shake as people from the southern districts gathered in enormous numbers. Thambi T.R. Baalu, then Union Minister for Shipping, presided over the Madurai function. Prime Minister Manmohan Singh inaugurated the project works. Mrs Sonia Gandhi and I were present in the lead. Surjit Singh Barnala, then Governor of Tamil Nadu, participated as the sole representative of the Tamil Nadu government and as a special guest. Union Ministers P. Chidambaram, Dayanidhi Maran, A. Raja, Anbumani Ramadoss, Mani Shankar Aiyar and K.H. Muniyappa, and leaders of various Tamil Nadu parties including then Tamil Nadu Congress president G.K. Vasan, MP, Dr Ramadoss, Vaiko, N. Varadarajan, Tha. Pandian and K.M. Kader Mohideen, MP, also participated and spoke.

It is my duty now to recall what they said at that function.

Prime Minister Manmohan Singh said:

> “I am very happy today to launch the implementation of the Sethusamudram Ship Canal Project in Madurai. This is an auspicious day on which our government fulfils the sacred promise made in the National Common Minimum Programme to the people of India, and particularly to the people of Tamil Nadu. The Sethu Samudram project marks a historic day for increasing maritime economic revenue and creating employment.
>
> “A dream born 150 years ago has become reality today. I congratulate our respected leaders, Mr Karunanidhi and Mrs Sonia Gandhi, who made this dream come true.
>
> “The Sethu Samudram project is a great project of a kind not seen before in India’s port sector. It reduces travel time. At the same time, it can be of help to fishermen. I have no doubt that this project will bring overall growth and development to Tamil Nadu and to the Coromandel coastal region.
>
> “The Sethu Samudram project will play an important role in this new enterprise and industrial activity. I believe this project will revive our country’s distinguished maritime-trading tradition. A short while ago, when Kalaignar Karunanidhi spoke here, he requested that a world-class automobile testing centre be established in Chennai. We have decided to establish a world-class automobile testing centre at Oragadam near Chennai. I am very happy to announce that here.”

Mrs Sonia Gandhi said:

> “Today a 150-year-old dream is becoming reality. Though the Sethu Samudram project has been spoken of for so many years, nothing was done to implement it. Today the United Progressive Alliance government has taken a determined step to realise this technologically challenging and economically important project. By creating a continuous sea route within Indian waters, this major project is of great importance nationally, regionally and for the security of the country. Tamil Nadu, and the southern districts in particular, will benefit greatly from it. The economy and maritime trade of Tamil Nadu’s coastal region will grow substantially.”

Union Finance Minister P. Chidambaram said:

> “The United Progressive Alliance government assumed office at the Centre on 22 May 2004. In exactly six weeks the Prime Minister gave his approval, the Cabinet gave its approval, and on 8 July 2004 we announced in our very first Budget that the Union Government would implement the Sethusamudram Ship Canal Project. And here, on 2 July 2005, we are laying the foundation stone for this great project. The project is to be completed at a cost of ₹2,427 crore. The Union Government has given its guarantee for the loans to be raised for the project. Therefore the project will certainly be completed within the stipulated time and at the stipulated cost.”

Vaiko, General Secretary of the MDMK, said at the inauguration:

> “It is most fitting that this celebration is taking place in the same month of July in which Perarignar Anna, after forming the government in 1967, observed an Ezhuchi Naal with the call, ‘Let the Sethu Canal be built; let classical Tamil Nadu prosper.’ The Sethu Samudram project was one for which C.P. Adithanar, leader of the Naam Tamilar movement, and Congress leader K.T. Kosalram raised their voices. My beloved elder brother Kalaignar sounded the drum for it in the Legislative Assembly as early as 1958. It was the project for which Perarignar Anna sounded a clarion call in that same Assembly, asking why the Union Government was still delaying implementation of the Dr Ramasamy Mudaliar Committee report. It was the project announced by Pandit Nehru on 12 September 1963 at the insistence of Perunthalaivar Kamaraj. At the Renaissance Rally held on 15 September 1998 for the birthdays of Perarignar Anna and Thanthai Periyar, then Prime Minister Vajpayee announced that the Union Government would implement the Sethu Canal project. For twenty-four years I raised my voice for it in Parliament to the best of my ability. In 2004, through the efforts of my beloved elder brother Kalaignar, when Dr Manmohan Singh and Pranab Mukherjee undertook the responsibility of drafting the Common Minimum Programme of the Union Government, I went to them and asked that the Sethu Canal project be included; they accepted that request and included it. The day on which it begins is a day of joy. Like the Panama Canal and the Suez Canal, this Tamil canal will become famous. Dr Manmohan Singh, who has provided ₹2,427 crore and 40 lakh for it, your name and the name of your government will be engraved in the inscription of time.”

Anbumani Ramadoss, then a Union Minister, said:

> “Every Tamil heart has been longing for this day. This is a very important day. In history this is a very important day for all Tamils. Above all, our Tamil Nadu leader Kalaignar has struggled for many years for this project to come to Tamil Nadu, and today that dream is becoming reality. During the past year the United Progressive Alliance government has delivered many achievements for Tamil Nadu. Among them, this Sethu Samudram project is a very important achievement.”

Thambi G.K. Vasan, then president of the Tamil Nadu Congress and now a Union Minister, said:

> “The Congress-led United Progressive Alliance has today fulfilled the long-standing dream of the people of Tamil Nadu, the Sethu Samudram project. This is a matter of pride for Tamil Nadu. The Sethu Samudram project is a foundational project capable of bringing Tamil Nadu to the position of the foremost State in the coming years. I thank Ayya Kalaignar, who raised his voice so effectively behind such a distinguished project, the Prime Minister and the leaders of the parties.”

N. Varadarajan, then State Secretary of the Communist Party of India (Marxist), said:

> “I extend my greetings to the inauguration of the Sethu Samudram project. This is a time when the people of India are fighting poverty. The work of the Sethu Samudram project should serve that struggle. It must be developed as a project that provides employment. Everyone must cooperate to implement a good project.”

Speaking on behalf of the Communist Party of India, Tha. Pandian said:

> “Today is a good festive day for the people of Tamil Nadu and, in another sense, for the whole of India. It is a good day on which a project that had lain dormant is being started.
>
> “A child that had been in the womb for 145 years has been born today. Though what is being cut is a canal, what it will set in motion is Tamil Nadu’s economic transformation and growth. It will also help the Indian economy grow. It will reduce the distance travelled by ships that currently go around. So this is good news for the country. Some people think the world will become dark if they close their eyes. But here the function is taking place splendidly.”

At that function I said:

> “Today is a sweet day. It is the day when the Ezhuchi Naal uprising has achieved victory. When Arignar Anna became Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu in 1967, he said that we would never surrender our rights at any time, at any moment or for any reason; we would argue and struggle before those who had the power to grant those rights. In that spirit he observed Ezhuchi Naal and demanded, along with Thoothukudi harbour, the second Neyveli mine and the Salem steel-plant project, that the Sethu Samudram project too must be implemented. When then Chief Minister Perarignar Anna spoke at those Ezhuchi Naal meetings, whether the project would actually be realised remained a genuine question mark.
>
> “By bringing many parties together, this alliance has shown that it can unite not only parties but seas. As the form and symbol of that union, today we are beginning the Sethu Samudram project. Our Finance Minister P. Chidambaram has briefly explained here the benefits this project can bring. The Sethu Samudram project is the creation of a sea route within Indian waters through Adam’s Bridge, linking the Gulf of Mannar and the Bay of Bengal. Viewed as a whole, this canal is a project capable of serving as a fundamental source for increasing industrial prosperity not only in Ramanathapuram, Sivaganga, Thoothukudi, Pudukkottai, Nagai and Virudhunagar districts, but for the country itself.”

At the time, some people filed a petition in the Madras High Court seeking to stop even that inauguration. In that case, the Bench of the Chief Justice of the Madras High Court held:

> “The petitioner has rushed to court with the object of stopping the Sethu Samudram project, which is being brought forward in the national interest. Everyone knows that the Sethu Samudram project will bring good benefits to the country. At present ships have to sail around Sri Lanka to reach the Bay of Bengal. If a shipping canal is cut across the Palk Strait, large amounts of money and time can be saved. This canal project will produce beneficial results. Therefore there is no bar on the function.”

After all this, after ₹829 crore of the people’s tax money has been spent on the project, should Tamil Nadu not understand the attempt now being made to somehow hold a closing ceremony for it by invoking religious reasons? Even after knowing that southern Tamil Nadu would benefit greatly from the project and that, in particular, lakhs of young people would obtain employment, is it right to think that the project must not come?

Those who govern a State would normally try to secure such projects, implement them and enable the people of the State to benefit. Would they instead go to court on behalf of the government and say that a project secured after enormous effort is not needed?

The DMK has a duty to make all this understood by the people of Tamil Nadu. It is to fulfil that duty that the party headquarters has decided to hold **Ezhuchi Naal** meetings throughout Tamil Nadu on Wednesday, 15 May 2013, demanding that the Sethu Samudram project be continued. As one of those who worked hard for the Sethu Samudram project, I ask all of you to conduct these meetings splendidly, as always.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**3 May 2013**
