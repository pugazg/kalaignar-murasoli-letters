# 3622. A Party in Anna's Name; What a Farce!

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3622](../../../chapters/3622-anna-peyaraal-katchi-kelikkooththu.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 239](../../../pages/page-239.md)–[PDF 245](../../../pages/page-245.md)  
**Date:** 8 May 2012

**Udanpirappē,**

For the past month the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly has been in continuous session. After the general discussion on the Budget, debates have been taking place every day on the demands for grants of various departments. Traditionally, during an Assembly session opposition parties submit adjournment motions and calling-attention motions on current issues; the Speaker permits one such matter to be raised, the member explains it and the concerned minister replies. But now the opposition is prevented even from coming to the House to submit such motions, while the Chief Minister reads statements under Rule 110 every day and allied-party members rise to praise them.

For many days sugar-mill workers have been agitating and have been on strike since 4 April 2012. Neyveli contract workers, pressing ten demands, have been on strike since 21 April; the strike has reached its seventeenth day. A rail blockade by the Jeeva Contract Workers Union and a hunger strike by a federation including the LPF, CITU, PMK and UTUC have been announced. Powerloom weavers in Tiruppur, Coimbatore, Erode and Salem districts too are on strike because power cuts make it impossible to continue their occupation. More than 13,000 Makkal Nala Paniyalargal, who had worked regularly for the Government for years, continue to struggle for their jobs despite obtaining judicial relief from the High Court and Supreme Court because of the AIADMK Government's obstinacy.

Does the AIADMK Government care about these struggles? Have adjournment or calling-attention motions been allowed so these issues can be debated in the Assembly? No.

Thirteen thousand Neyveli Lignite Corporation contract workers have been on strike since 21 April. A Tamil Nadu minister replied in the Assembly that the State had nothing to do with the issue because it came under the Union Government. The Tamil Nadu police are even said to have told workers that a hunger strike cannot be held inside Neyveli town and may, if necessary, be held at Mandarakuppam. One of the workers' demands is that the entire electricity generated at Neyveli be supplied for Tamil Nadu's use. Even if the State Government has no direct jurisdiction, it could have pressed the Union Government and tried to resolve the problem affecting Tamil workers there.

When Neyveli workers announced an agitation over the same issue in 2008 under DMK rule, the Government called all trade-union leaders at the plant, spoke with them and facilitated an amicable settlement. When the Union Government attempted to sell shares in Neyveli Lignite Corporation, the DMK Government extended support to all unions opposing it; I myself wrote to the Union Government and the issue was resolved. Even if the present State Government remains indifferent, I request the Union Government and the minister concerned to contact Neyveli management immediately, call the agitating workers, discuss their demands and seek an amicable settlement; I request the Prime Minister to take the necessary steps.

The cooperative sugar-mill workers' issue is similar. Wage revision for sugar workers is ordinarily settled once in four years through negotiations between central trade unions and South Indian sugar-mill owners. The AIADMK Government of 2001–2006, without consulting unions, unilaterally issued a Government Order on 17 January 2006 extending the agreement period. After the DMK Government assumed office in May 2006, accepting the workers' demand—particularly the LPF's request—it issued an order on 8 October 2008 granting workers in cooperative and public-sector mills a wage increase from 1 April 2009 on the same basis as private mills. Later, on 14 September 2010, it ordered that cooperative and public-sector workers receive 90 per cent of the benefits of the private-sector wage agreement, through which all workers received 80 per cent agreement benefit.

Thereafter unions demanded pay equivalent to that received by certain government employees. As the general election was approaching, the DMK Government on 1 March 2011 ordered the constitution of a committee to examine the demand. Government changed two months later. The committee submitted its recommendations on 1 August 2011, but the AIADMK Government, which should have considered and implemented them, has delayed a solution in the name of negotiations. With no decision, sugar-mill workers are now agitating and thousands of workers' families are affected.

Lakhs of sugarcane farmers' families too suffer because they cannot send harvested cane to mills. With cooperative and government sugar mills not functioning, farmers are not permitted to cut cane at the proper time. The AIADMK election manifesto promised a procurement price of ₹2,500 a tonne for sugarcane; instead the Government has announced ₹2,100 and has not come forward to resolve the workers' issue either.

Powerloom owners in Coimbatore, Tiruppur, Erode and Salem have begun an indefinite strike against the electricity-tariff increase and disconnection of units that did not pay. Textile production worth ₹49 crore has been affected. In Palladam, Avinashi, Thekkalur, Perumanallur, Mangalam and Tiruppur and in the Somanur and Sulur areas of Coimbatore district alone there are more than two lakh powerlooms. Though the first 500 units are free, electricity beyond that is charged at ₹4 a unit. They say the powerloom tariff has risen 110 per cent compared with other industries. More than one lakh workers have lost employment because of the strike, yet the AIADMK Government appears unconcerned.

Even after courts ordered reinstatement of the Makkal Nala Paniyalargal, the Government is interested only in filing appeals, not in restoring their livelihood on humanitarian grounds. Nineteen workers have so far sacrificed their lives because of the rulers' obstinacy, yet their hearts do not seem moved.

They planned how to keep DMK members out of the House lest we speak about all these issues. A ruling party can govern properly only by hearing what opposition members say. Ayyan Valluvar said: “The unguarded king who has none to rebuke him will perish even without anyone to destroy him.”

Someone may ask whether we acted that way during DMK rule. Yes, we did. On 13 November 2008, when electricity was being debated and I was not in the House, I learnt that members of the principal opposition, the AIADMK, had been expelled. Thinking that they had been eager to participate and should not lose that opportunity, I tried to telephone the Deputy Leader of the Opposition. When I could not reach him, I contacted ministers M.K. Stalin, Duraimurugan, Veerapandi Arumugam and K.N. Nehru and asked them to go to the Opposition Leader's room, pacify the AIADMK members and invite them to participate. I also telephoned the Speaker and requested that the expulsion order be withdrawn and that they be invited in the House. He did so.

For whatever reason, they neither returned nor took part in the electricity debate. Yet I had the satisfaction that we had conducted ourselves with the political culture Anna taught us. But those who still run a party in Anna's name?

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**8 May 2012**
