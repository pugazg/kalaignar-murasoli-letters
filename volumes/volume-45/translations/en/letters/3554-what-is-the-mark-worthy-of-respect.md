# 3554. What Is the Mark Worthy of Respect?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3554](../../../chapters/3554-madhippirkkuriya-madhippen-enna.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 150](../../../pages/page-150.md)–[PDF 154](../../../pages/page-154.md)  
**Date:** 13 April 2011

**Udanpirappē,**

Today is election day—**13-4-2011**. You will not even have time to read this letter! You will be running about and working! Yet at this moment, a letter to you—yes, a final-hour letter of appeal! Because today is the day on which the people of Tamil Nadu give us marks for the work we have done during the past five years.

In school, teachers give marks to students; students do not mark their teachers. But in politics, today is the day on which the public—having watched whether those who governed them wrote their examination properly, orderly and steadily over the past five years—give the marks. It is the day when a farmer who has spent the entire year going around the field, applying fertiliser, planting seedlings, removing weeds and tending the crop harvests it and takes stock of the yield.

Udanpirappē, when we think about how much we have worked for the people of Tamil Nadu during these five years, we can only feel proud. No one can look at us and ask, “What did you actually do while you were in office?” When we went to ask for votes and met the people, none of them turned their faces away. This time, the welcome from mothers was of such a scale that it surpassed even the men; remembering those scenes again and again fills me with pride.

After meeting the vast ocean of people who had gathered in Cuddalore, I came to Chidambaram that night and, in the name of sleep, merely lay turning about for a few hours. The next morning our `van` set out towards Tiruvarur, where I am contesting.

Between Kollidam and Sirkazhi, people in a village stopped us on the road and welcomed us. A little girl—perhaps ten years old! Dark-skinned! A shawl in her hand! I do not know how she made her way through the crowd and came near the van. Putting her face close through the van window, she said in a ringing voice: **“Grandfather! You will win for the sixth time and come this way again! Then too I will put a shawl on you at this very place.”** Everyone in that vehicle felt a thrill through the body when she said it. That child still has not left my eyes!

After travelling another **ten `kal`**, still talking about that girl and continuing to receive the people's welcome, we reached a village near Mayiladuthurai. When the van stopped again by the roadside, an elderly man was standing in the crowd with his grandson. Everyone else was competing to hand over shawls. Then that elder told me: **“Ayya, it was only because of the ₹75,000 government assistance you gave last year that my grandson here underwent an operation and is alive even today. He is seeing you too. You must remain well.”** When he blessed me like that, could there be anything that gave greater happiness?

There were many such scenes on this tour. Yesterday was the last day of election campaigning, was it not? Thambi T.R. Baalu, Tiruvarur district DMK secretary Thambi Poondi Kalaivanan, Thambi Shanmugam—the secretary of the Labour Progressive Federation who worked for my election in Tiruvarur—and DMK Rajya Sabha member Thambi Selvaganapathy sat together and compressed what should have been a three-day journey for me into a single day and took me along. Contrary even to what they expected, I had to swim through a sea of people all along the route, and because of that I could not reach a few places.

In particular, I could not go to villages in the Tiruvarur constituency such as **Odacheri, Puthur, Amur and Vadakudi**, or to many streets in Tiruvarur town. I have therefore asked the district DMK secretary to go there and explain the situation to the people there.

In that situation, if we were to return to the Tiruvarur house for lunch, eat, rest and then go to the public-meeting stage, there was no time. There is a small office of the district secretary on the way at Koradacheri. Poondi Kalaivanan hesitantly asked whether we could eat some lunch there and continue the journey. The reason for his hesitation was my physical condition.

You know that only last year I underwent a major operation on my back. You also know that I am **87 years old**. Only those who are with me know what kind of pain I sometimes suffer at the place where the operation was performed. At times I cry out aloud. With this body, I travel in that `thallu-vandi`. You would understand only if you sat in that vehicle yourself! I cannot move my body this way or that.

While travelling in it, I have to hide the severity of my pain and, before people who are shouting with joy, smile and wave my hand. People will be standing in a small village. Immediately Murasoli Selvam, who travelled with me, and my daughter Selvi will call out, “Wave your hand!” My hand itself seems to look at me pitifully and ask, “Must I be lifted?” Yet without showing it, when I lift my hand and wave, the love poured out by those people reduces the pain.

Meanwhile my wife Dayalu, seated behind me, says, **“Look this side—see how many people are waving!”** When I wave my hand alternately on both sides, the pain that I did not notice before suddenly makes itself known!

In that condition, we entered the district secretary's small office at Koradacheri, ate, and then set out again. After asking for votes in **Kankodutha Vanitham, Erukkattur, Kamalapuram, Kulikkarai, Ammaiyappan, Thandalai and Vilamal**, it was already **4.15 p.m.** by the time I reached the stage directly.

For someone like me who bears hardship to this extent, I do not have the option of flying in by helicopter or aeroplane, looking for a few minutes at a speech written and handed to me at each place, and then returning to Chennai to rest—what can I do? Whenever I have worked for the people, I have never considered my own suffering important. Because I was not born to rest! Nor am I someone who meets the people only for the purpose of asking for votes!

Those who meet people merely as a matter of obligation, driven by a frenzy that they must somehow come to office, may if they wish arrive with every kind of luxury by helicopter or aeroplane and meet people only at public meetings. But I, who regard meeting the people by the roadside itself as a duty, have never learned how to rest.

My wives, my sons and my daughters will ask me to reduce one day's travel, take a little rest, and then begin the journey. But I have always asked those who prepared my schedule, **“Why did you waste a few hours? You could have made use of me during that time too,”** rather than thinking that I ought to rest.

That is why, when everyone says I should become Chief Minister for the sixth time, I said at the Coimbatore meeting that I do not take it as people giving me a position; I take it only as their judgement that **I am the worker suited to serve the people**.

I began with one subject, but the letter has somehow kept growing in all directions. Today, think from experience about how both sides conducted themselves in the past. Which government gave the people benefits, achievements and concessions? Which government, at the same time, gave them cruelties, torments and suffering? Weigh the two carefully! Think for one minute before voting!

Which government acted to put a full stop to all the achievements made for the people? Which government restored those achievements to the people after they had been stopped? Which government failed to fulfil any of the promises it made in the 2006 election manifesto? Which government fulfilled all the achievements promised in its manifesto and, beyond that, added new achievement after new achievement for the people?

In the election manifesto we announced that we would provide one kilogram of rice for ₹2. Jayalalithaa then declared that it was impossible to implement. But is it not the DMK government that went on to say it would provide one kilogram of rice for **₹1**, and has continued to provide it to this day?

When I stayed for ten minutes at Koradacheri, the town Congress Committee president, Mr S.M.V. Natarasan, came to meet me and thanked me. When I asked what the thanks were for, he opened his shirt and showed me. He had undergone surgery under the Kalaignar Insurance Scheme and had come with bandages on his chest. **“Ayya, if you had not announced this scheme, I would not be alive today,”** he said.

In this way, in every town, when one person after another spoke of schemes you had implemented, I gave myself marks. But today is the day on which **you** must give the marks, is it not? Where have you set out for? To give the marks? Thank you, thank you.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**13-4-2011**

## Letter-specific notes

1. The title preserves the source's wordplay between `மதிப்பு` (value/respect) and `மதிப்பெண்` (mark/score); the English therefore retains the repeated idea of “mark”.
2. PDF 151 uses the source distance form `பத்துகல்`; the English keeps `ten kal` rather than silently converting it to a modern distance unit.
3. PDF 152 refers to the campaign vehicle as `தள்ளு வண்டி`; the English retains this conservatively as `thallu-vandi` because the audited text alone does not establish a more precise vehicle type.
