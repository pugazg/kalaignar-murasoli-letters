# 3728. The Duty the DMK Government Performed for Arunthathiyars!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 4 April 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

On the front page of the 1 April 2013 issue of *Theekkathir*, a report appeared under the headline **“Dawn in Arunthathiyar Lives; the Credit Belongs to the Marxist Party.”** The headline referred to a speech by Jakkaiyan, State President of the Arunthamizhar Liberation Movement, at a felicitation function in Dindigul for Selvaraj, author of the Sahitya Akademi Award-winning novel *Thol*. I had been very happy at the time that the Sahitya Akademi Award went to that novel; it was an appropriate award for a deserving person.

In his speech, the speaker referred to a conference for internal reservation for Arunthathiyars conducted in Dindigul by Comrade N. Varadarajan and to the participation of Sister Balabharathi, now the MLA for that constituency. He then said: **“If the Arunthathiyar community today has obtained three per cent internal reservation, the leaders of the Communist Party of India (Marxist) are responsible for it.”** We have no objection whatever to that. But the speaker failed to say under which government that important demand of the Marxist Communist Party was accepted and the Arunthathiyar community began receiving the benefit of internal reservation. I do not understand why. Since he omitted it, I think it appropriate through this letter to recall what was done for the Arunthathiyar community under the DMK government.

Among the Adi Dravidar communities, the Arunthathiyars were at the very bottom in social and economic conditions and severely backward. Considering that special measures were necessary for their advancement, it was under the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam government, on 27 November 2008, that the decision was taken and a government order issued providing Arunthathiyars with three per cent internal reservation from within the eighteen per cent reservation available to Adi Dravidars.

As a continuation, in 2009 the **Tamil Nadu Arunthathiyars Special Reservation Act**—providing a separate allocation within Scheduled Caste reservation for seats in educational institutions, including private educational institutions, and for appointments or posts under the State—was enacted. Rules relating to it were framed on 29 April 2009, and the system has been implemented successfully, the letter says.

Because of the internal reservation provided during the DMK government, in 2009–10, 56 Arunthathiyar students entered medical colleges and 1,165 entered engineering colleges, for a total of 1,221 beneficiaries. In 2010–11 the numbers increased further: 70 in medical colleges and 1,813 in engineering colleges, for a total of 1,883.

In 2009–10, the first year after the three per cent reservation was provided, I wished to encourage the 1,221 Arunthathiyar students who had gained admission to medical and engineering colleges. I donated the ₹50 lakh I received as remuneration for writing the story and dialogue for the film *Pen Singam*, added ₹11 lakh and ₹5,000 from my own money, and thus contributed a total of ₹61 lakh and ₹5,000. On 5 December 2009, each of the 1,221 students received ₹5,000 as an educational-development grant.

There is one history, the letter recalls, of Sundaralingam, an Adi Dravidar who served as one of the commanders of Veerapandiya Kattabomman in the struggle against the British and ultimately sacrificed himself. There is another: even before Kattabomman, the warrior Puli Thevan of Nelkattumseval in the Tirunelveli region fought the British. His principal military commander in several battles against the British was **Ondiveeran**, who belonged to the Arunthathiyar community. The DMK government, which had established memorials to Kattabomman, Puli Thevan and Sundaralingam, announced in the Budget on 19 March 2010 that an appropriate memorial would be built in the Tirunelveli region to honour the freedom fighter Ondiveeran of the Arunthathiyar community.

The Tamil Nadu Sanitary Workers Welfare Board was established on 8 June 2007 under the chairmanship of the Adi Dravidar Welfare Minister to provide financial assistance to Arunthathiyars at different stages of life. Under the DMK government, 22,811 people were enrolled as members, and ₹17,86,440 in financial assistance was given to 1,133 member families.

The DMK government also implemented a rehabilitation scheme valued at ₹22 crore to help people formerly engaged in the manual removal of human waste move into alternative occupations. To prevent workers from having to enter sewer lines, corporations were to purchase machines for clearing blockages. In Chennai, 47 desilting machines were purchased at a cost of ₹3.97 crore and put into sanitation work; necessary blockage-clearing machines were also procured for the other nine corporations. The work of using machines to clean sewer blockages was begun with the aim of ending the degrading practice of sending human beings into drains for that purpose.

When I received the **Lifetime Achievement Award for 2008** from the Santhome Arts Festival Forum in Chennai, I said in my speech:

> “Even though, on behalf of the government, we may have done many good things for minorities, the poor and simple, beggars, persons with disabilities and everyone else without leaving anyone out, one section remains. A community still exists in Tamil Nadu whose people live as though blind despite having eyes, unable to speak despite having mouths, unable to live, and without respect. That community is the Arunthathiyar community.
>
> “The Arunthathiyar community asked for separate reservation. A committee under Justice Janarthanam was constituted and gave its report to the government. I am happy to announce that at our Cabinet meeting in another day or two, we will discuss that report and this government will provide the concession needed by that community.
>
> “For they are the human beings who remove excreta; the human beings who carry human waste on their heads. Even though in this age of civilisation many methods exist to remove waste and clean toilets, in rural areas the terribly cruel condition in which human beings carry that waste has still not disappeared.
>
> “A human being must be made fully human. For one human being to carry another human being's excreta is no pride for any of us. It is shameful. There is no greater humiliation for us. They are not symbols of shame. We, who conduct a government while keeping them in that condition, are the symbols of shame. To me, what matters is the action taken to remove that shame.”

I further said that when government secretaries asked whether this had to be done immediately, I replied:

> “People kept low, people degraded, people writhing like worms, people reduced to such a condition that one human being does not respect another—the task of this Karunanidhi is to lift those people by the hand. That is what I have been doing. In that record, in that history, what has so far remained absent is our showing love to the Arunthathiyar people and lifting them up. Even if it is on the last page of my history, the final news to be written and printed there must be: ‘I gave social justice to the Arunthathiyars.’”

There is more. On the day the Bill for Arunthathiyar reservation was to be passed in the Assembly, I was receiving treatment at Ramachandra Hospital. On 26 February 2009 I wrote a letter in my own hand to the Speaker. It said:

> “Hon'ble Speaker, dear Perasiriyar as Leader of the House, beloved Udanpirappus of all parties in the Legislative Assembly: though I was to come and meet you today, I could not come because of the advice—and warning—of the Delhi doctor and the team of doctor-friends in Chennai. I ask your pardon. Unable to straighten, unable to turn, nerves tightened as though an ‘anchor’ had been dropped—pain in the spine! Pain!
>
> “One is bodily pain; another is the pain of mind you have given me! Even amid differences of opinion, the kindness you show—the love you show beyond political antagonisms—joins us again today. Mother Tamil's hand embraces us together. Let us cultivate gratitude and humane goodwill beyond the differences of rationalism and spirituality, atheism and theism.
>
> “As an early socialist, and as one among the devoted workers of Ayya, Anna, Kamarajar and Annan Jeeva, I ask you to give your support and pass the Bill I place before the House today so that the ‘human lives’ lying in misery below even the lowest social level—the ‘Arunthathi’ people—may see a new world, a revolutionary age.
>
> “Thus,
> wishing always to live drenched in the river of your affection,
> an Udanpirappu—one among you,
> affection-unforgetting (M. Karunanidhi)
> 26.2.2009”

If another example is needed of the depth of my involvement with the Arunthathiyar community, Thambi Nithya—my close aide who devotes himself fully to looking after me every day—belongs to the Arunthathiyar community. Why, one of the DMK's three Deputy General Secretaries belongs to that community as well.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**4 April 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. **Arunthathiyar** and **Adi Dravidar** are retained as the community names used in the source. The letter's historical terminology and its description of Scheduled Caste reservation are translated without replacing them with broader contemporary labels.
2. The formal 2009 Act title is rendered for meaning from the Tamil title in the audited source; no outside statutory text is substituted.
3. The discussion of manual removal of human waste and sewer work is necessarily direct because the source's argument is explicitly about dignity, labour and social justice.
4. All reservation percentages, student counts, donations, welfare-board figures and machinery costs are preserved exactly as stated in the source.
