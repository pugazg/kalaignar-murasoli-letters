# 3611. The Aryan Illusion Striving to Destroy Anna's Dream!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation follows the locked Volume 46 conventions. The Sethu Samudram chronology, election-manifesto quotations, court passage, references to Adam's Bridge/Ramar Bridge, and Kalaignar's closing `ஆரியமாயை` wordplay are preserved from the canonical source without outside correction. The complete audited Tamil remains authoritative. Bilingual source-alignment review is complete.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3611](../../../chapters/3611-annavin-kanavai-azhikka-thudikkum-aariyamaayai.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 156](../../../pages/page-156.md)–[PDF 162](../../../pages/page-162.md)  
**Date:** 3 April 2012

**Udanpirappē,**

With the desire that the Sethu Samudram Project—the long-standing dream of the Tamils—should be fulfilled, the DMK worked continuously for many years. On 2 July 2005, in the presence of United Progressive Alliance Chairperson Mrs. Sonia Gandhi and myself, Prime Minister Dr. Manmohan Singh came to Madurai and inaugurated the project works. When those works were moving rapidly, a group, driven by the malicious thought that completion of the project would bring the DMK and the UPA Government great credit for implementing an important scheme, began efforts to wreck it. They went all the way to court and achieved some success in obstructing the project. Their stated reason was that the alignment ran through “Ramar Bridge,” and that completion of the Sethu Samudram Project would damage it. In further support of that effort, Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Jayalalithaa wrote to the Prime Minister two days ago asking that the Central Government file a reply before the Supreme Court declaring “Ramar Bridge” a national monument.

As early as 23 July 1967, Perarignar Anna, then Chief Minister, decided to hold “Awakening Day” meetings throughout Tamil Nadu with alliance parties to press the Central Government to implement the Sethu Samudram Project. He sought the support of all parties in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly. Anna said that the Sethu Canal Project and the Tuticorin Harbour Project, taken together, could be regarded as Tamil Nadu’s “Suez Canal”; that foreign ships now forced to travel around Ceylon and Colombo Harbour, adding nearly 600 miles, could avoid that detour; that a deep-sea harbour would allow large international vessels to berth at Tuticorin; and that such development would bring many industries and substantial economic growth.

Some people attempted through the courts to prevent even the inauguration of the Sethu Samudram Project. In that case, the Bench headed by the Chief Justice of the Madras High Court observed that the petitioner had rushed to court seeking to obstruct a project brought forward in the national interest; that the project would bring good results to the country because ships currently had to circle Sri Lanka to enter the Bay of Bengal; that a shipping canal across the Palk Strait would save substantial time and money; and that the canal would have beneficial consequences. The Court therefore refused to stay the inauguration.

Do you know what the AIADMK, which now works with an inner intention that the Sethu Samudram Project should not come into being, said about this very project in its 2001 election manifesto? It said the scheme would benefit not only India but South-East Asian and other coastal countries; increase trade and industry, foreign investment and foreign exchange; reduce sailing distance, fuel use and travel time; increase exports and imports; improve the standard of living and employment opportunities in backward southern districts such as Ramanathapuram; expand Tuticorin into an international harbour; and promote tourism. It further promised to press the Central Government continuously to secure funding, including from international institutions such as the World Bank, and complete the project within a time-frame instead of citing financial difficulties as an excuse.

Again, in the AIADMK parliamentary election manifesto issued on 10 May 2004, it said the country knew well that the DMK, MDMK and PMK, despite holding ministerial responsibility at the Centre for five years, had failed to take proper steps to implement the Sethu Samudram Project, which would play an important role in Tamil Nadu’s economic growth and the country’s overall industrial development. The AIADMK said it would press the incoming Central Government to allot sufficient funds immediately and complete the project within a fixed period.

Only after that did the works begin and proceed rapidly. When some then tried to obstruct them, the DMK’s 2009 parliamentary election manifesto stated that the Sethu Canal Project, a long-standing Tamil demand, had been inaugurated on 2 July 2005 because Chief Minister Kalaignar had repeatedly pressed for it; that the project was stopped on religious grounds just as the works were nearing completion; and that the Central Government should quickly conclude the pending Supreme Court case, complete the remaining works and thereby promote the economic development of Tamil Nadu’s southern districts as well as India’s security.

But what did the AIADMK finally say about Sethu Samudram in its manifesto issued on 16 April 2009? It said that in the modern age of very fast ships capable of carrying very heavy cargo, the project was outdated; that this truth had been deliberately concealed; that implementation of the canal as then planned would bring no significant economic gain to the country; and that it would hurt the religious feelings of crores of people. It therefore called for the project itself to be cancelled.

When we raise our voice demanding the Sethu Project, some oppose it in the name of religion, some in the name of mythology, some in the name of God. They campaign that demolishing that bridge would endanger the country and India itself; they go to court; some even say that we do not like Rama, that we are enemies of Rama, and that this is why we seek to demolish Ramar Bridge. I wish to remind them of something. When I was asked what symbol should be placed at Rajaji’s memorial, I said that Rama was his beloved deity and that Rama’s crown itself could be made the tower. That is exactly what stands there. Did we reject the bridge because nothing bearing Rama’s name should exist? We ask only this: why campaign that we are demolishing a bridge that does not exist?

There is more. During Prime Minister Vajpayee’s period itself, and following our insistence, the Tuticorin Port Trust on behalf of the Central Government entrusted the National Environmental Engineering Research Institute at Nagpur in March 1996 with the environmental study. In October 2002, NEERI recommended that the best alignment was the route cutting across what is called “Adam’s Bridge”—the very feature now called Ramar Bridge.

The AIADMK’s own 2001 manifesto, on pages 83–84, described the problem by saying that India lacked a continuous shipping route around the peninsula and that ships sailing from west to east had to circle Sri Lanka. It said the solution was the Sethu Samudram Project, whose principal purpose was to remove and deepen sandbanks and rocks obstructing navigation in the Adam’s Bridge region between Rameswaram and Talaimannar and create a canal. Having itself called the place “Adam’s Bridge” and described it as sandbanks and rocks, why has the Chief Minister now suddenly written to the Prime Minister demanding that “Ramar Bridge” be declared a national monument? Should the people of Tamil Nadu not understand this double role and contradiction?

Just as they remained silent when the Kudankulam Nuclear Power Station began, allowed more than ₹10,000 crore to be spent, and then opposed it as it neared completion, so too they remained silent when the Sethu Samudram works began, allowed many crores to be spent, and now seek to obstruct it under the name “Ramar Bridge.” One must ask whether this is right, whether it suits Tamil Nadu’s development, and whether it is scientifically acceptable.

That is why our DMK Members of Parliament met Mrs. Sonia Gandhi and Prime Minister Dr. Manmohan Singh personally and requested help to complete the Sethu Samudram Project quickly. On behalf of the Kazhagam, I too once again urge the Central Government to accept that request.

At the same time, I ask the people of Tamil Nadu to understand who is trying to injure this project by putting forward one imaginary reason or another.

Are they people confused about policy? Are they sophists digging a pit beneath Tamil Nadu’s economic development? What can one do? The “Aryan Illusion”—`Aariya Maayai`—is striving to destroy the very dream of Anna, who wrote *Aariya Maayai*!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**3 April 2012**
