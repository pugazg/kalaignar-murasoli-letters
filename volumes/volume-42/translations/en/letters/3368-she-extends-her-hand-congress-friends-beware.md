# 3368. She Extends Her Hand; Congress Friends, Beware!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This letter moves from the author’s medical condition to Sri Lankan Tamil politics, the Madras High Court violence, a verbatim English letter to the Acting Chief Justice, and finally a warning addressed to Congress supporters. Political allegations, sarcasm and accusations are preserved as source-framed statements. The complete audited Tamil is reproduced below and remains authoritative.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3368](../../../chapters/3368-kaiyai-neettugirar-kangirasare-echarikkai.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 41](../../../pages/page-041.md)–[PDF 49](../../../pages/page-049.md)  
**Date:** 21 February 2009

**Udanpirappē,**

My severe spinal illness had been described as dangerous, and many were distressed, wondering where it might lead. At a time when several doctors could not confidently say whether it could be cured, Dr. Marthandam and Dr. Thanikachalam of Ramachandra Hospital, along with their team, stepped forward boldly as though accepting a challenge. They called in the well-known medical expert and orthopaedic surgeon Dr. Arvind Jaiswal from Delhi and undertook treatment. Some improvement has been seen. The wishes continuously offered by you, the Udanpirappugal of Tamil Nadu and the brothers in our allied parties have become medicine for me. Though I am still at Ramachandra Hospital in Porur, Chennai, my mind keeps circling around you and around the great Tamil community made up of Udanpirappugal like you.

The day before yesterday, Dr. Arvind Jaiswal, who had come from Delhi, examined my condition and insisted that I must remain at bed rest for at least another four weeks, that I must not get down from the bed, and that everything must be done from the hospital bed itself.

Accepting his insistence and resting may be good for my body. But only if you were in my place could you know how impossible it is to relieve the pain felt by my eyes that cannot see you and by my heart that cannot know your feelings face to face.

**Udanpirappē,**

Do not ask me whether you still do not know that you and I are, in the source’s phrase, “two — one heart”!

The doctors tell me to rest peacefully. Whatever time the medical friends in this hospital allow me to meet government officials and issue orders on government matters, I spend even that time on the administration of the State and on preserving the peaceful atmosphere that must be protected in Tamil Nadu. Through brother Shanmuganathan, who stays beside me, supports me and has almost become one of the doctors caring for me, I dictate what I think and what I feel, and convey it to you and to the country.

There are some who do not want the peace, rest and encouragement I find in understanding matters and explaining them clearly to people like you to continue; they seem to think that the one thing that ought to continue is his physical illness. Speaking one thing outwardly while holding another within, hiding truths, disturbing the peace of the town, they even plan whether this government for the disadvantaged, functioning in a good manner, can be brought down through violence. What else can I do but bless them, with a mother’s heart, saying, “May all of them live long lives”?

Though I myself appealed, and though Professor Anbazhagan appealed on my behalf, asking, “Do not place a burden of suffering on his shoulders at this time,” that appeal was ignored. On the true, emotional and firm principle of protecting Sri Lankan Tamils, we convened, on behalf of the government itself, an all-party meeting to unite the people of Tamil Nadu and raise a common voice. We resolved that there must be a ceasefire in Sri Lanka, and that otherwise all of us — the Members of Parliament from Tamil Nadu — should resign. The meeting unanimously accepted that resolution.

But in support of that resolution, only all the DMK Members of Parliament — including the Rajya Sabha members — agreed and gave their resignation letters to me. I waited for the other letters to arrive. All the all-India parties from Tamil Nadu that had attended the meeting hesitated to give resignation letters without instructions from their respective high commands.

After that came the massive human chain; the journey to Delhi to meet the Prime Minister in person; the agreement to send Pranab Mukherjee to Sri Lanka; the delay that followed; my own moving, under the name “Final Resolution,” a resolution in the Legislative Assembly; the walkout by some parties such as the AIADMK and MDMK because they could not wholeheartedly agree; and, without becoming discouraged by that, my continuing to press the Union Government on the resolution.

Our Members of Parliament met Union Ministers, the Prime Minister and Sonia Gandhi and conveyed my anguish. After that, Pranab Mukherjee went to Sri Lanka and returned. A forty-eight-hour ceasefire was announced. We continued pressing for a complete ceasefire and wrote several letters to Delhi.

I formed the Sri Lankan Tamils’ Welfare Rights Council and made several people members of it on a non-party basis. Along with it, a subcommittee of judges was formed under the chairmanship of Law Minister brother Duraimurugan. I thought that, if a ceasefire agreement were to take place in Sri Lanka and an agreement guaranteeing the lives of Tamils in that country were then concluded, this committee of judges could be of support to it. For that reason I sent the committee to Delhi and had it meet Ms. Sonia Gandhi. They also met the President of India and explained the situation. I asked that the matter be taken to the United Nations.

Meanwhile, many countries sent messages calling for the war in Sri Lanka to stop. My own efforts, and the events in which Tamil society here surged with feeling, were among the underlying causes. It is difficult for me to describe the consolation I draw from thinking that those events brought about a major change in Delhi and that perhaps, because of Delhi’s advice, some foreign countries expressed views on a Sri Lankan ceasefire. It is enough if those who can understand it do so.

For more than fifty years in Tamil Nadu I have raised my voice for Sri Lankan Tamils —

argued and struggled —

Professor and I threw away our positions as Members of the Legislative Assembly —

twice our government itself was dismissed over the Sri Lankan Tamil issue —

and even now, like a mother, regarding Sri Lankan Tamils as siblings born of the same womb, I remain ready to dedicate my work to them.

The sorrows embedded in this fifty-year history of Sri Lankan Tamils, the agonies they endured, have not dispersed like clouds circling in the sky. They remain deeply imprinted in the depths of my mind, like pictures from Sita’s forest exile. I have no personal enmity with the Tigers fighting there. From then until today, all I have asked of them is this: give up fratricidal wars, come together and protect the Tamils there. The demand for a ceasefire is the firm and unshakeable position of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam. Under my leadership, this movement will continue raising its voice until that position succeeds.

It is true that some disturbing, tearful and shocking developments in the intervening period — atrocities such as the assassination of Rajiv Gandhi — shattered me. But I know very well that, today, the Congress friends who lost that young leader Rajiv, including my own family, do not accept the genocide of Tamils now taking place in Sri Lanka.

The All India Congress Party is the party that has the opportunity, after setting right several conditions, to provide the impulse and encouragement needed for a complete ceasefire in Sri Lanka. Its leader is Ms. Sonia Gandhi. That is precisely why some anti-social elements, acting in the name of political parties, insult her, burn her portraits and speak abusively, while calling such conduct dignified and claiming it to be the true voice raised for Tamils. Even if Anna and Gandhiji were in power, they too could tolerate such conduct only to a certain extent.

Beyond even that, some things are being done in Tamil Nadu with a partisan outlook. One part of this concerns friend Subramanian Swamy — a politician known throughout India and leader of the Janata Party, and, even if he is not my friend, one who could be described as a thoughtful person. On the basis of the religious belief he holds, he came to the High Court itself to argue against the court judgment permitting the government to take over the Chidambaram Nataraja Temple. Lawyers who had gathered there — whom I regard like my younger brothers — humiliated friend Subramanian Swamy by throwing eggs at him.

Under the previous government, Subramanian Swamy had been humiliated even more badly inside the same court complex. But that was not by lawyers — it was through an extremely disgraceful action by the AIADMK Women’s Wing! Those who saw that scene did not appear to have been hurt by it. But now, when some lawyers threw eggs at Subramanian Swamy inside the court, the matter was magnified; complaints were made asking whether such a thing could be allowed inside a court, and lawful action was sought against those lawyers.

All this took place inside the court. Police came to the court to carry out the High Court’s order to arrest the offenders who had thrown eggs at Subramanian Swamy. What happened there? From the complaint made against Subramanian Swamy to the sight of the police station inside the court being burned to ashes, everything could be seen on television.

At that stage, while I was still in hospital and had not fully recovered, and while the doctors had warned that what remained at the surgical site was not to be removed until after the following week, I sent the Tamil Nadu Chief Secretary, the Home Secretary and police officials to the Acting Chief Justice of the High Court. The unrest had not completely subsided. When I heard that the Chief Justice was discussing the matter with the judges, I sent the following letter from the hospital:

> Unprecedented violence has taken place. I have sent the Chief Secretary, Secretary Home and D.G.P. to appraise you of the situation. To find out who is at fault, whether the Advocates or the Police and to ascertain the basic causes for this situation, your co-operation is very essential for me and for this Government. I am expecting your valuable suggestions to contain this violence and to ensure justice is done and peace is restored. Though I am in the hospital, if you wish to see me, I will come and meet you even in an Ambulance.
>
> Thanking you,
>
> Yours sincerely,  
> Sd.  
> (M. Karunanidhi).
>
> To  
> Hon’ble Justice S.J. Mukhopadhaya,  
> Acting Chief Justice.  
> High Court of Madras, Chennai.

Because Acting Chief Justice Hon’ble Justice Mukhopadhaya was concerned with maintaining law, order, peace and justice, I had written in that very letter that, if it were necessary for me personally to speak to him, I would get into an ambulance from the hospital and come there. After seeing the letter, he consulted our Chief Secretary and police officials and conveyed the view, in the manner to be informed to the court, that the government should order a CBI inquiry. Accepting that, the government stated that it would order a CBI inquiry and sent the required request to the Union Government.

Through that CBI inquiry, the facts will soon become known: what was true and what was untrue, what was planned and what happened without planning, whether the lawyers caused the disturbance, whether the police did, or whether the rotten eggs that triggered this disastrous episode were the cause.

To create peace —
to quell the commotion —

while these measures have been taken, both I and the people of Tamil Nadu who live in peace await the result of the CBI inquiry with eagerness.

I ask every party and every party leader in Tamil Nadu not to allow this to be turned into an endless serial, but to treat it now as a tragic incident that has ended and help the good government now functioning to continue.

Not for me —
not for any individual —
for the people of Tamil Nadu —
for the greatness of the democracy we have built and protected!

There are two or three leaders in Tamil Nadu who, at the slightest “āsu, ūsu,” cry out in agitation, “Dismiss Karunanidhi’s government!”

I am not one who wanders about arrogantly because I came to power;

nor is this Karunanidhi one who sits with his hand on his cheek, worrying about being toppled!

Then why have I borrowed Anna’s heart?

Until yesterday, someone kept saying there was no connection, no relationship, with Congress; after deceiving the Communist parties too, at yesterday’s wedding function she performed a political dance deceiving the country by saying, “I too am going to join in alliance with Congress.” I do not know that kind of political dance.

I know — and the country knows — who asked, “A foreign woman — politics for India?”

I know — and the country knows — who called that pure gold named Sonia Gandhi a person “without devotion to her husband.”

I also know what the person who spoke that way about devotion to a husband wrote, in her own hand, to Rajiv Gandhi about M. G. R. It appeared in the newspapers themselves.

And Congress is now apparently supposed to take such a person into alliance!

Veerappa Moily — a dutiful friend born in the Karnataka State where she too was born — has today given her a rebuttal. Congress friends of Tamil Nadu, it is your responsibility to think about it!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**21-2-2009**
