# 3562. “Some People at Certain Times!”

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

Here is a letter!

Before you begin reading it, I am now going to explain to you who wrote it and when it was written.

In my life, so many dear friends have associated with me—because of political involvement, or because of connections through art and literature. Some of them, though I had committed no wrong against them, if I may put it in the language of the puranic storyteller, broke off the feeling of comradeship either by the force of fate or by the difference brought by time. They did not merely cut the chain of friendship; they crossed over and stood on the opposite side, taking pleasure in abusing me, speaking against me, and hurling arrows of opposition.

But as far as I am concerned, when we were friends, I have remembered again and again—thousands of times, crores of times—the affection and love I had for them, and have been moved by it. I am the same even now; I have always been the same!

Just because a friendship broke, I have never showered them with harsh, grating abuse. Yet how did they speak? How did they write? Even now, they continue to follow that same method. Thinking how they once were, how much affection they once showed me, and how now, without reason, they throw mud at us, I was looking through stacks of books that I had finished reading when I came across a letter. With wonder rising in me, I began to read it.

That is the letter I now place before you and ask you to read.

Here it is. When my beloved younger brother **Murasoli Maran** was in poor health, this letter was sent to me from a prison. Which prison? Who wrote it? Read it yourself and find out!

> **To beloved leader Kalaignar,**
>
> Greetings. Since you know better than I do what the loneliness and heat of prison life are like, I was moved by the affection with which you sent **Tolkappiya Poonga** itself into the prison and enabled me to breathe the fragrance of Tamil and rejoice.
>
> When I was in hospital, Thambi **Ila. Pugazhendhi** met me, inquired after my health on your behalf and gave me **Tolkappiya Poonga**. I felt as though I had found a great treasure. The more I read the book, the more the illness disappeared and the loneliness flew away.
>
> On one side, concern over the health of respected friend **Thiru Maran** tormented me; on the other, the shoreless love you have for Tamil urged me on. I have no words with which to praise the manner in which you created and gave us **Tolkappiya Poonga**.
>
> In my postgraduate class, my professor **Thiru T.P. Meenakshi Sundaranar** taught **Tolkappiyam—Ezhuthu—with Naccinarkkiniyam**; **Mahavidwan Thiru S. Dandapani Desikar**, who was also your teacher and my teacher, taught **Tolkappiyam—Soll—with Senavaraiyam**; and Professor **Thiru M. Annamalai** taught **Tolkappiyam—Porul—with Ilampuranam**. Even so, when entering Tolkappiyam in my student days, an inexplicable fear and hesitation used to trouble me. Great professors taught us and somehow removed that fear.
>
> Had you written **Tolkappiya Poonga** in those days itself, people like me would not have suffered that unnecessary fear and hesitation.
>
> You have changed the condition in which **Tolkappiyam** was regarded as a work that only learned scholars could understand, and created a situation in which all Tamils can read it willingly and savour it.
>
> In simple language, in the beautiful Tamil that is uniquely yours, you have shaped the greatness of Tolkappiyam into illustrative stories so that everyone may understand it. There is no doubt that this service of yours will be remembered forever with gratitude by Tamils.
>
> As I read the book again and again and savoured it, the only longing I felt was: why did you stop with just **one hundred flowers**?
>
> Though I am in prison, you remembered me and sent the book wrapped in your great affection. For that, I shall remain forever indebted.
>
> I am not of an age to bless you.
>
> I pray that you may create many more flowers for Mother Tamil, and I say to you: **Pallandu! Pallandu!**—may you live for many years!
>
> I wish that Thiru Maran may recover fully, return home and continue his work.
>
> I request you to convey my affection to respected **Perasiriyar** and to Thambi **Stalin**.
>
> Once again, my thanks!
>
> **With affection,**  
> **Pazha. Nedumaran**  
> **Cuddalore Prison**  
> **14-2-03**

Udanpirappē,

By now you will have understood who wrote the letter and when it was written! I have so many other friends like this. Yet, at a time when I would not even dream of wishing them harm, I do not know why they developed such hatred toward me! They unnecessarily write and speak abusing me! Even so, as I wrote at the beginning, I have not cultivated hatred toward them.

How they once were—and how they have changed like this! I remain unable to understand the reason. Was it thinking of such people that my friend **Jayakanthan** wrote **“Some People at Certain Times”**?

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**13-5-2011**

## Letter-specific notes

1. The central prison letter is translated in full and remains a quoted document inside Kalaignar's letter; its sender, place and date are preserved in source position.
2. Names of Tolkappiyam divisions and commentaries are kept in transliteration rather than replaced with outside scholarly editions. `Pallandu` is retained in transliteration because the source uses it as a blessing-formula whose repetition is rhetorically important.

எத்தனையோ நண்பர்கள் எனக்கு உண்டு. ஆனால் அவர்களுக்கு
நான் கனவில்கூட தீங்கு எண்ணாத நேரத்தில், அவர்கள் ஏன் தான்
என் மீது ஒரு வெறுப்பை வளர்த்துக் கொண்டார்களோ, எனக்குத்
தெரியாது! தேவையில்லாமல் என்னைத் திட்டி எழுதுகிறார்கள்,
பேசுகிறார்கள்! ஆனாலும் நான் முதலில் எழுதியபடி அவர்கள்
மீது துவேஷத்தை வளர்த்துக் கொள்ளவில்லை. எப்படி
இருந்தவர்கள், இப்படி மாறி விட்டார்களே, அதற்கு என்ன காரணம்
என்று புரியாமல் இருக்கிறேன். இப்படிப்பட்டவர்களை நினைத்துத்தான்
“சில நேரங்களில் சில மனிதர்கள்” என்று என் நண்பர்
ஜெயகாந்தன் எழுதினாரோ?
