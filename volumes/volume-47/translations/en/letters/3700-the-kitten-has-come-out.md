# 3700. The Kitten Has Come Out!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This thought-preserving translation retains the source's account of the *Vishwaroopam* ban, court proceedings, political reactions, newspaper quotations, allegations and the reproduced Jayalalithaa letter. Tamil remains authoritative. `Udanpirappē` is retained as Kalaignar's distinctive address.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3700](../../../chapters/3700-veliye-vandhu-vittadhu-poonaikkutti.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 359](../../../pages/page-359.md)–[PDF 365](../../../pages/page-365.md)  
**Date:** 31 January 2013

## English translation

Udanpirappē,

The ban imposed by the Tamil Nadu Government on *Vishwaroopam*, produced, directed and acted in by Kalaignani brother Kamal Haasan, has become an issue that has drawn everyone's attention in Tamil Nadu. On 29 January 2013 alone, the case was heard for six hours before Justice K. Venkataraman in the Madras High Court.

*Vishwaroopam* was scheduled for release in Tamil Nadu on 25 January. A complaint arose that it contained scenes hostile to Muslims. On the basis of a complaint from an Islamic organisation, the Tamil Nadu Government prohibited the film's release in the State. Rajkamal, the production company, challenged the ban in the Madras High Court, leading to the six-hour hearing.

As soon as the issue arose, in my statement of 26 January 2013, I clearly explained the affection, regard, respect and esteem that I, brother Kamal and our friend Superstar Rajinikanth have for the Muslim community. I also stated that neither I nor the DMK under my leadership had ever supported criticism or agitation directed against Muslims anywhere in the world. I urged that the controversy over *Vishwaroopam* not be prolonged, that it be resolved through democratic discussion and consultation, and that the Tamil Nadu Government, which bears responsibility for law, order and peace, cooperate in finding such a solution.

Superstar Rajinikanth too appealed to Muslims and asked them to help enable the film's release. Tamil Nadu Congress president Gnanadesikan, Dravidar Kazhagam leader K. Veeramani, CPM State secretary G. Ramakrishnan, PMK founder Dr Ramadoss, BJP leader Pon. Radhakrishnan, VCK leader Thol. Thirumavalavan, directors Bharathiraja, Parthiban and Ameer, among others, issued statements asking the Government to lift the ban. They also wrote of Kamal's character and that he was not one to behave in a way that deliberately hurt anyone. Nadigar Sangam president Sarathkumar said the Chief Minister should call Kamal Haasan for talks to achieve an amicable solution.

Above all, Kamal himself stated that he was not anti-Muslim, that the film was not against Muslims, and that Muslims were his brothers. Yet the Tamil Nadu Government did not come forward to withdraw its ban.

I do not know why the Government imposed such a severe ban. One version being spoken about is that a television company very close to the AIADMK tried to buy the film for a throwaway price, but the producers, having spent more than ₹100 crore, refused and sold it to another television company for a higher amount. Another claim is that, at a book-release function concerning Union Finance Minister P. Chidambaram, Kamal said that “a Tamil wearing a veshti should become Prime Minister,” referring to Chidambaram, and that this too caused anger. We do not know how true these claims are.

The High Court judge hearing the case personally watched the film at a special screening. During the hearing on the 28th, the judge himself suggested that the two sides discuss the matter and arrive at a good solution. The Tamil Nadu Government did not make an effort even toward that.

On 29 January, *The Hindu* published a lengthy editorial titled “Quash the Ban, Screen the Film.” It said that the delay in overturning the unjustifiable ban on Kamal Haasan's *Vishwaroopam* was itself becoming unjustifiable. It recalled that only two years earlier the Supreme Court had set aside the two-month ban on the Hindi film *Aarakshan* on the ground that States cannot prohibit a film cleared by the Central Board of Film Certification merely because they apprehend a law-and-order problem. It cited the landmark *S. Rangarajan v. P. Jagajivan Ram* case, where the Supreme Court held that freedom of expression cannot be suppressed because of threats of demonstrations, processions or violence. The editorial called for the ban to be quashed and for police to protect theatres and moviegoers.

Even after *The Hindu* wrote this, did the Tamil Nadu Government voluntarily come forward and withdraw the ban it had imposed? Had it done so, perhaps it might have become a Government with humanitarian feeling!

Because the Government did not, the High Court heard the case for six hours yesterday. Do you know who argued for Kamal? Senior advocate P.S. Raman, who served efficiently as Advocate General during the DMK Government. He argued that *Vishwaroopam* insulted no Indian Muslim; questioned how all thirty-one district collectors could simultaneously presume a law-and-order breakdown and issue bans; pointed out that there had been no disagreement among members of the Censor Board when the certificate was granted; argued that a State Government cannot ban a film after certification; and stated that Kamal Haasan had invested his entire life's earnings in the film.

After hearing both sides, Justice Venkataraman delivered his ruling only at 10.15 p.m., staying the Government's Section 144 prohibition on the film. Did the AIADMK Government soften even after that? No. That very night, at 11.30 p.m., Government representatives went to the residence of Acting Chief Justice Elipe Dharma Rao and submitted an appeal against the ruling. The Acting Chief Justice accepted it and permitted an appeal at 10.30 the following morning, 30 January. Is any further example needed to show how much democracy and humanity this Government possesses?

Why is all this being done? Today's *Times of India* carried a report under a headline asking whether the AIADMK was trying to attract the Muslim vote bank. The report said political observers believed the AIADMK Government's support for Muslim objections to *Vishwaroopam* was aimed at attracting Muslim votes. It also noted that when objections were raised against the film *Thuppakki*, a Muslim leader said Chief Minister Jayalalithaa had instructed the Home Secretary to bring both sides together and resolve the issue.

Even if, as the *Times of India* suggests, Jayalalithaa is now trying somehow to win Muslim votes, the degree of her genuine attachment to Muslims cannot erase certain realities: her position during the kar seva; her statement that minorities should adjust to the majority; her question asking where else a Ram temple could be built if not at Ayodhya; and the fact that the AIADMK sent people for kar seva.

But what is the real reason for Jayalalithaa showing such hostility to Kamal's film? This hostility did not arise now. It dates back to the time when our beloved friend Makkal Thilagam M.G.R. was alive. When M.G.R. attended a special fundraising screening of Kamal's *Vikram*, Jayalalithaa wrote him a handwritten letter. In it she complained that Kamal Haasan had invited M.G.R. but failed to publicise his participation, thereby insulting him. She wrote that on the very next day, when Kamal's film was released, he knew how to place full-page advertisements for himself in every newspaper, but had not advertised the participation of a Chief Minister with such immense public influence. She asked whether Kamal thought M.G.R. was insignificant.

When one recalls that handwritten letter, does the real reason behind the ban on *Vishwaroopam* not become clearer? Thanthai Periyar used to say, “The kitten has come out of the bag.” Do you now understand why this ban was imposed?

With affection,
M.K.
31-1-2013
