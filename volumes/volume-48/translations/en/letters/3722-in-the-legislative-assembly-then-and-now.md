# 3722. In the Legislative Assembly—Then and Now!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

When the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly met on 8 February 2013, an MLA who had been elected on the DMDK ticket and was later said to have left the party's discipline—Tamilazhagan—spoke in the House. He openly invited other DMDK members to follow his example, saying that because he had moved away from the party the rulers were doing good things for his constituency, and that the other DMDK MLAs too could benefit if they took a similar decision.

DMDK MLAs objected to that speech in the House, and a quarrel arose between the two sides. This was a dispute among members who had all been elected on the DMDK ticket. It was then alleged that a DMDK-elected member acting outside the party's discipline had been assaulted. Proceedings were initiated against six members who continued to remain firmly with the DMDK, and the matter was referred to the Privileges Committee.

Within a month the Privileges Committee examined the issue and submitted its report. On that basis, action was taken suspending the six MLAs from attending the Assembly for one year. They would receive no salary during that year. Condemning this action, the DMK, Congress, Communist Party of India (Marxist) and Puthiya Tamilagam walked out. This is the situation that has befallen a party that had been an ally of the AIADMK in the last Assembly election and worked for its victory.

In this context, I think it would be appropriate to recall a few incidents from the past in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly.

★

On 24 November 1986, during M.G.R.'s government, as one stage of the language agitation, copies of the Constitution of India had been burned. For this, the Speaker read out a ruling permanently expelling from the Assembly DMK members including Perasiriyar, S. Balan, A. Selvarasan, Parithi Ilamvazhuthi, P. Ponnurangam, Coimbatore M. Ramanathan, Dharmapuri R. Chinnasamy, Alandur M. Abraham, Madurantakam C. Arumugam and Arakkonam V.K. Rasu. However much the opposition parties pleaded, the ruling AIADMK did not come forward until the end to withdraw that decision.

★

What happened in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly on 28 December 1988? Have the people of Tamil Nadu forgotten how members of the “Ja” faction and “Je” faction pulled microphones out with their hands and attacked one another, creating a huge commotion without precedent in Assembly history? An internal party dispute entered the Assembly, the town laughed at it, and the whole country laughed.

★

Another example. On 4 July 1991, during the AIADMK government, the Governor addressed the Legislative Assembly. When the Governor began speaking, Panruti Ramachandran, then a PMK member, began reading a statement in English. Thamaraikani, then an AIADMK member, came near him and struck him.

On 5 July 1991, as soon as the House assembled—even before the condolence proceedings—the Speaker hurriedly read out a ruling. It said, in substance:

> **Speaker:** “On 4 July 1991, while the Governor was delivering his Address, Panruti S. Ramachandran rose and interrupted the Governor. Member Mr. Thamaraikani then left his seat, stood behind Panruti Ramachandran's seat and, in an undesirable manner, struck him forcefully on the shoulder. At the Business Advisory Committee meeting held on 4 July 1991, Chief Minister Jayalalithaa asked me to take suitable action because the conduct of both members was contrary to the rules. I too consider the incidents to have seriously disturbed the order of the House. I therefore rule that Panruti Ramachandran and R. Thamaraikani be suspended from the proceedings of the Assembly for three days from today.”

★

An incident on 22 March 1999. During the debate on the Budget in the Legislative Assembly, Thamaraikani, then an AIADMK member, punched Minister Veerapandi Arumugam in the face. The ring he wore struck Veerapandi Arumugam's nose and blood poured out. The same Thamaraikani later left the AIADMK and joined the DMK.

★

Here is another example. On 7 January 2011, during the DMK government, AIADMK members created a commotion while the Governor was speaking and severely assaulted Assembly marshals who came to remove them. One member kicked the marshals, snatched their caps and struck them in the face. Photographs appeared in the newspapers at the time.

Speaker R. Avudaiyappan ordered the suspension of nine AIADMK members who had behaved in that manner during the Governor's Address—not for months or years, but only for the remainder of that session. AIADMK members walked out in protest. But do you know what happened the next day?

As Chief Minister I said in the Assembly:

> “Ten DMK MLAs including Perasiriyar were expelled during the AIADMK government and were not allowed back by that government. But on behalf of today's DMK government, which follows Anna's path, I ask the expelled AIADMK members to return to this House and cooperate.”

★

Another incident. On the morning of 18 October 2007, Local Administration Minister M.K. Stalin was speaking on a question of breach of privilege. All the AIADMK members in the House rose together, shouted loudly, created disorder and prevented him from speaking, obstructing the proceedings. They left their seats in a group, went to the front of the Speaker's Chair and sat on the floor in an undignified manner.

The Speaker repeatedly asked them to return to their seats, but they did not. He therefore called the Assembly marshals and ordered that they be removed. At that point A.K. Bose, MLA for Tirupparankundram, snatched the cap of marshal P. Mathivanan, who had come to remove him, and threw it toward the Speaker. Every member of the House saw it.

For that act directed at the Speaker himself, the initial decision during the DMK government was to suspend him for six months. I immediately intervened and said that it would be enough to suspend him for the remainder of that session and the first ten days of the next session. That is what was done. This is another example from DMK rule.

★

Has there ever been another instance of action being taken against even the Leader of the Opposition, Vijayakanth, preventing him from participating in Assembly proceedings for ten days? That too happened under the AIADMK government.

★

On 13 November 2008, during the DMK government, I was not in the House when I learned that the members of the principal opposition, the AIADMK, had been expelled from the Assembly. I thought: they had been eager to take part in the debate on the Electricity Board; because they had been expelled, would they not lose the opportunity to participate?

I personally tried to speak by telephone with the Deputy Leader of the Opposition. When that did not succeed, I telephoned ministers from the ruling party—Thambis M.K. Stalin, Duraimurugan, Veerapandi Arumugam and K.N. Nehru—and asked them to go to the opposition leader's room, pacify the AIADMK members and invite them to return for the debate on electricity. I also spoke to the Speaker by telephone and asked him to withdraw the expulsion order and invite them in the House itself to return. He did so. Yet, for whatever reason, they did not return and did not participate in the electricity debate. Still, I had the consolation that we had acted with the political culture Anna taught us. But what of those who still run a party in Anna's name?

★

25 March 1989! After an interval of thirteen years I had become Chief Minister for the third time. As Finance Minister too, I began reading the Budget. What happened? Jayalalithaa, then Leader of the Opposition, stood up and said, **“The Chief Minister is a criminal offender; he must not read the Budget.”** She tried to snatch the Budget from my hand. AIADMK members surrounded her and shouted, **“Don't read! Don't read!”**

The MLA from Andhiyur grabbed the Budget book from my hand, pulled it away and tore it. When I tried to stop him, I was punched in the face and my spectacles fell to the floor and shattered. Minister Veerapandi Arumugam was struck on the head with a microphone stand, bled and had to be taken to hospital.

Do you know what punishment was imposed even for an incident of that magnitude? A resolution was passed temporarily suspending the AIADMK members who had created the disturbance for one week. But on 27 March 1989, when Kumari Ananthan—then Deputy Leader of the Congress Legislature Party and described here as *Ilakkiya Selvar*—asked that even that temporary suspension be reconsidered, I said:

> “Neither I nor this government has the narrow-minded view that the proceedings of this House should be conducted without the opposition. I approach this issue with the thought that what has happened may remain in the past and what happens hereafter should be good. I request that the resolution passed in the emotion and agitation of that day be withdrawn and that the Leader of the Opposition and the others return to work in the House.”

That is the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam. Even Bharat Ratna C. Subramaniam praised this through the newspapers at the time.

★

Udanpirappē, I have written all these incidents to show how and when democracy has been practised in the Legislative Assembly. Even as I write this, information has reached me that the action against the DMDK members has now been reduced from one year to six months. Even that is a maximum punishment.

After reading these explanations, I hope the AIADMK government will reconsider the action now taken against the DMDK members and either cancel it or reduce it to a matter of days or weeks. I ask them to come forward and do so.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**27 March 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. The letter is a comparative political argument about legislative discipline. Descriptions of assaults, factional conduct and motives are translated as Kalaignar recounts them from the Assembly record and his recollection.
2. The labels **“Ja”** and **“Je”** reproduce the source's shorthand for the two AIADMK factions referred to in the 1988 incident; no external faction history is added.
3. `அவைக் காவலர்கள்` is rendered **Assembly marshals** for the personnel responsible for maintaining order in the House.
4. The source distinguishes permanent expulsion, session-long suspension, ten-day suspension and the contemporary one-year/six-month sanction. Those differences are preserved rather than normalised into a single English term.
