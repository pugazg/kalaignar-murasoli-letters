# 3687. The Water Karnataka Blocks and the Tears Tamil Nadu Sheds!

## English translation

Udanpirappē,

Seeking action to publish the Cauvery Water Disputes Tribunal's final award in the Gazette, the Tamil Nadu Chief Minister wrote to the Prime Minister on 20 December and again on the 22nd. Even before those letters, the DMK executive committee met on 13 December 2012. Its resolution strongly condemned Karnataka for refusing to release Tamil Nadu's due share despite repeated directions from the Supreme Court, the Cauvery River Authority, the Cauvery Monitoring Committee and the Union Government, with Karnataka's coming Assembly election in mind. We also regretted that while Karnataka had convened all-party meetings three times, the Tamil Nadu Government, which had to secure the water, ignored repeated demands for an all-party meeting and acted unilaterally, avoiding democratic consultation.

Neither Karnataka nor, indeed, our own Tamil Nadu Government paid heed to that resolution. When those here do not listen, why would those there? Yet the DMK did not remain silent. In my statement of 17 December I asked the State Government to declare the delta districts disaster-affected, pay ₹40,000 per acre for the withered samba crop, and provide a government job to one member of each farmer's family where a suicide had occurred. I also asked our MPs to meet the Prime Minister that very day and press for publication of the Tribunal's final award in the Gazette. They did so, and the Prime Minister assured them that the matter would receive proper consideration.

Only after these DMK initiatives did the Chief Minister write her letters of the 20th and 22nd.

The Tribunal's final award was delivered on 5 February 2007, during the DMK Government. Leaders of all parties in Tamil Nadu welcomed it. I said then that, as far as Tamil Nadu was concerned, it offered some consolation, but that I would be happy only if farmers in both Tamil Nadu and Karnataka accepted it. The award neither drastically reduced Karnataka's water nor greatly increased Tamil Nadu's; if both States reflected on that, peace could be found. Jayalalithaa, however, called it an unacceptable award. She imagined that my words “offers consolation” meant “gives happiness,” called that regrettable, accused me of failing to obtain Tamil Nadu's just due and demanded the DMK Government's resignation. Now she writes letter after letter asking the Prime Minister to Gazette that very award.

The Mettur dam, normally due to open on 12 June, was opened only on 17 September under the AIADMK Government. Kuruvai cultivation therefore failed completely. Farmers said that because of the delayed opening they would need water until the end of February. Delta irrigation normally continues until 28 January; yet this year the shutters were closed at 8 p.m. on 21 December, before January had even begun. What are the farmers to do? How will they find a livelihood, carry on with life or redeem their pledged jewellery? Who should resign for this? Jayalalithaa had hastily demanded the resignation of the DMK Government that had helped constitute the Tribunal and obtain its interim award. What does she say now?

In my explanatory statement of 10 February 2007, I said the Government would consult irrigation experts, lawyers and farmers' representatives, discuss the matter with all-party leaders, seek review of deficiencies in the final award and pursue a sound legal remedy.

Even after the final award, the DMK Government did not choose to act unilaterally. On 19 February 2007 it convened an all-party meeting. Representatives of every party participated, including O. Panneerselvam and N. Jothi for the AIADMK. After detailed discussion, it was unanimously decided that Tamil Nadu should petition the Tribunal seeking clarification to protect the State's interests. A second all-party meeting was held under my chairmanship on 15 April 2007 to discuss the points to be pressed in the review petition; Panneerselvam and Jothi again represented the AIADMK. That was how action was taken after consultation. Today, despite opposition parties asking for the same democratic process, the Chief Minister behaves as though whatever she thinks is the decision and whatever she does is the action.

Whenever Jayalalithaa comes to power, drought somehow seems to visit Tamil Nadu. Ten farmers have so far taken their lives after the samba crop withered. Yet the Chief Minister says officials report that only two or three were suicides. Yesterday a farmer named Gopalakrishnan hanged himself in Tiruthuraipoondi; it is said the police there even refuse to register the case. What can one do when they must understand the wishes of those above and act accordingly?

There was a similar drought under the AIADMK Government in 2002–03. There was no rain; photographs appeared of people in the delta eating rats. There was no water even for kuruvai, and samba withered. Unable to bear the poverty, Shanmugam of Palayakottai near Mannargudi wrote a letter blaming the Government and took his life. Police then struggled to seize that letter, and at midnight raided and damaged the house of CPI MLA Sivapunniyam. More than fifteen farmers died after samba crops withered in 2003. Junior Vikatan of 26 January 2003 even published photographs and interviews describing farmers reduced to eating snails when rats were unavailable.

The situation is again similar, yet this Government tries to conceal and divert attention from it. Nature may fail under any government. The great wrong is for a government to try to hide the consequences.

Tamil Nadu's revenues are substantial. The Government therefore bears responsibility for preventing the State's farmers from being left to die and for protecting their livelihoods. It must act with compassion and generosity: promptly provide liberal compensation to farmers who suffered losses; give relief to agricultural labourers who have lost work; stop trying to conceal the number of farmers who died by suicide and provide proper relief to their families without delay; and give a government job to one member of each such family. That is the duty of a good democratic government. Showing compassion to people in distress is civilisation.

With affection,
M.K.
27-12-2012
