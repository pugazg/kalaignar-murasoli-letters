# 3749. The Sethu Project That Does Not Hurt Hindus in the Least! — Kalaignar Serial Letter 3

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

The DMK has decided and announced that **Ezhuchi Naal** meetings will be held throughout Tamil Nadu on 15 May 2013 in support of the Sethu Samudram project. So that everyone may know the full details of that project, I wrote several notes on 1 May and 3 May 2013. Continuing from them, I shall write some further explanations whenever time permits.

The Indian peninsula has a coastline extending 3,554 nautical miles. Yet no facility exists in this region for ships to travel continuously along the coast. Ships travelling from India’s western coast to Thoothukudi on the eastern coast have to sail around Sri Lanka. This is because between Rameswaram in India’s south-eastern sea area and Talaimannar in Sri Lanka there are sandstone reef formations, known as Adam’s Bridge. The depth of the sea in this area is only about eleven feet. Because of this shallow depth, ships have to go around Sri Lanka.

From 1860 onward, various proposals were considered for cutting a “Sethu Samudram Canal” within Indian waters so as to reduce the distance travelled by ships:

- 1860 — Commander A.D. Taylor scheme
- 1861 — Townsend scheme
- 1862 — British Parliamentary Committee scheme
- 1863 — Madras Presidency Governor Sir William Denison scheme
- 1871 — Stoddart scheme
- 1872 — Harbour Engineer Robertson scheme
- 1884 — Sir John Coode scheme
- 1903 — South Indian Railway Engineer scheme
- 1922 — Government of India Harbour Engineer Sir Robert Bristow scheme

Many such schemes were considered, but none was implemented.

After India attained independence, the Union Government in 1955 constituted a Sethu Samudram Project Committee chaired by Sir A. Ramasamy Mudaliar. The other members were S.K. Mukherjee, then Chief Commercial Superintendent of the Southern Railway; P.N. Chatterjee, retired Chief Engineer of the Calcutta Port; and maritime adviser Captain J.R. Davis. Sir R.A. Gopalaswami Ayyangar, I.C.S.—whom the source notes later became chairman of the first Police Commission constituted by the DMK government in 1969—served as member-secretary of the committee.

The committee examined:

1. the feasibility of cutting a canal near Adam’s Bridge to connect the Gulf of Mannar and the Palk Strait so that deep-sea ships could reach India’s east coast from the west coast; and
2. whether such a canal would help turn Thoothukudi into a deep-sea port.

As a result, the committee concluded that the Sethu Canal project was essential if Thoothukudi port was to succeed.

After detailed research and careful assessment, the Sir A. Ramasamy Mudaliar Committee recommended in 1956 that the project was feasible and beneficial and that it should be completed during the Second Five-Year Plan itself.

The committee stated that completion of the project would reduce the Chennai–Thoothukudi sea voyage by 360 nautical miles and save one and a half days of travel. Yet the Sethu Canal project was not implemented.

The Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam continually pressed the Union Government in both the Legislative Assembly and Parliament to implement the Sethu Samudram project immediately. Resolutions on the project were repeatedly adopted at DMK conferences. It was also repeatedly included in DMK election manifestos for Assembly and parliamentary elections.

After the DMK government led by Perarignar Anna came to office in 1967, an Ezhuchi Naal was observed demanding the Sethu Samudram project. On 23 July 1967, Anna, together with the alliance parties, decided to hold Ezhuchi Naal meetings across Tamil Nadu urging the Union Government to implement the project. In that connection, he sought the support of everyone in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly. Anna said:

> “If the Sethu Canal project and the Thoothukudi harbour project are integrated, it may be regarded as the ‘Suez Canal of Tamil Nadu’.
>
> “Foreign ships which now go around Ceylon, call at Colombo and travel almost 600 extra miles will be able to avoid that situation when this project is completed.
>
> “Large foreign ships cannot now berth at Thoothukudi harbour. If it becomes a deep-sea harbour, great ships can come; ships from many countries can come; it can become a great international harbour.
>
> “When that happens, many kinds of industries will arise. If they arise, there will be abundant economic growth.”

Thus Anna pressed the Sethu Samudram project in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly.

On 23 July 1967, Ezhuchi Naal was observed throughout Tamil Nadu demanding implementation of the Salem steel-plant project, the Sethu Samudram Canal project and the project to deepen Thoothukudi harbour.

At public meetings held throughout Tamil Nadu that day, an Ezhuchi Naal resolution was adopted. It stated:

> “This meeting, comprising all parties, once again reminds the Union Government that Tamil Nadu’s leaders have for years explained why the Thoothukudi and Salem projects are necessary and what they are needed for. This meeting repeatedly and emphatically informs the Union Government that the Salem steel plant and the Thoothukudi deep-sea harbour are indispensable needs of the Tamil people, and strongly urges the Union Government to take immediate action and engage itself in completing these projects as quickly as possible.”

Even so, the Sethu Samudram project was not implemented.

Only after the United Progressive Alliance government, of which the DMK was a part, came to office at the Centre in 2004 did the following happen:

- approval was granted to implement the Sethu Samudram project at an estimated cost of ₹2,427 crore;
- 2 July 2005 became the day on which a demand of nearly 150 years could move towards fulfilment;
- on 2 July 2005 Prime Minister Dr Manmohan Singh inaugurated the project and laid the foundation stone, while UPA Chairperson Mrs Sonia Gandhi and I were present in the lead; and
- the work of cutting the Sethu Samudram Canal began and proceeded well.

But after the project had begun and many crores of rupees of the people’s tax money had been spent, efforts of various kinds began to block it for the single reason that completion of the project would bring credit to the DMK. The issue was coloured as “Rama’s Bridge” and attempts were made to create a religious controversy.

On behalf of the Union Government, then Union Minister for Tourism and Culture Ambika Soni stated categorically in a written reply in the Rajya Sabha that there was no evidence of any Rama Sethu bridge having existed in that area, and that no archaeological research had confirmed the existence of such a bridge.

Dr S. Kannaiyan, chairman of the Environmental Monitoring Committee for the Sethu Samudram project, gave an interview to journalists on 25 April 2007. He said:

> “At seventy locations on Adam’s Bridge, which is called Rama’s Bridge, we tested rock samples to a depth of twenty metres by geochemical methods. What we found were marine deposits. Scientifically there is no sign that human beings created Rama’s Bridge. If the canal were dug somewhere else instead of at the place where the bridge is said to be, there would be a high possibility that the twenty-six islands in the Gulf of Mannar would be destroyed.”

Parties such as the AIADMK and BJP claimed that the American space-research agency NASA had released satellite imagery as evidence for the existence of Rama’s Bridge.

Then Union Minister T.R. Baalu rejected that claim at the time, saying that there was no truth in the assertion that NASA had released pictures showing the existence of Rama’s Bridge. He explained that a letter had been sent to NASA on behalf of the Union Government, but NASA had sent no reply on the matter.

In an interview in Ramanathapuram, T.R. Baalu further said:

> “The issue that Rama’s Bridge exists in the sea off Dhanushkodi is being raised with the aim of obtaining the votes of the entire Hindu population. There is not even a trace of evidence that a bridge created by human construction existed in that sea area. From 2004 to 2007, deep drilling tests were carried out at eighty-one locations in the sea. They revealed neither basic evidence that a bridge existed nor environmental damage. We are firm that the sentiments of Hindu people must not be hurt. We have the scientific research evidence for this. We invited Hindu religious sanyasis in Delhi and showed them that evidence. They too accepted it.”

I shall continue writing again.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**7 May 2013**
