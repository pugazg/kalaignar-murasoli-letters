**Udanpirappē,**

In a statement issued on 20-12-2010, Jayalalithaa said that through Raja an enormous corruption had been committed in the allocation of second-generation spectrum and that assets had been accumulated. A CBI inquiry and an Enforcement Directorate inquiry into spectrum allocation are to proceed in accordance with the Supreme Court’s decision.

Jayalalithaa points to the audit report’s statement that the Union Government suffered a revenue loss of ₹1 lakh 76 thousand crore and speaks as though corruption of that entire amount had taken place. Yet former minister of that very department Arun Shourie himself, the letter says, has stated that an irregularity of ₹1 lakh 76 thousand crore did not occur and that the revenue loss was only around ₹30,000 crore.

One sentence in that audit report itself reads:

> “Any loss ascertained while attempting to value the spectrum can only be ‘presumptive’..........”

That is, any loss stated after attempting to calculate the value of spectrum is only an estimate based on presumption. Whether anyone was actually responsible for that loss can be known only at the conclusion of the investigation.

If merely because something appears in an audit officer’s report it is to be treated as final, what did audit officers say about wrongs that occurred during Jayalalithaa’s own government? I have written about this earlier, but I wish to remind you again.

An audit report placed during AIADMK rule appeared in all newspapers, including *The Hindu*. On page 5 of *The Hindu* dated 3-8-2004, under the heading **“Short levy / audit report for 2003 – 2004” / Rs. 1033/- crore potential Revenue loss**, the source prints:

> The State Government suffered a loss of Rs. 1033 /- crore potential Revenue in 2003 – 2004 as a result of short levy of Taxes. Besides, it incurred an “avoidable expenditure” of various companies and autonomous bodies under government control was to the tune of Rs. 3681/- crores in the last financial year.
>
> Giving these figures,T. Teethan, Accountant General (audit) for Tamil Nadu, told press persons here today that the aggregate of “Short levy” and “wasteful expenditure” was Rs. 2982 crores in 2002-2003; Rs. 3930 crores in 2001-2002; Rs. 2621 crores in 2000-2001 and Rs.1901 crores in 1999-2000. ........
>
> A Rs. 61 crore cash loss was incurred owing to an excess procurement of 1.75 lakh tonnes of paddy. Poor planning, lack of infrastructure and a defective pricing policy of the Tamil Nadu Housing Board led to blocking of Rs. 96 crores on 18,755 plots under 17 schemes completed.”

The source then gives a Tamil rendering of the same material: ₹1,033 crore potential revenue loss in 2003–2004 due to short levy of taxes; ₹3,681 crore in avoidable expenditure by various bodies under government control; aggregate short levy and wasteful expenditure of ₹2,982 crore in 2002–2003, ₹3,930 crore in 2001–2002, ₹2,621 crore in 2000–2001 and ₹1,901 crore in 1999–2000; a ₹61-crore loss from excess procurement of 1.75 lakh tonnes of paddy; and ₹96 crore blocked in 18,755 plots under 17 Housing Board schemes.

That same day’s *Hindu*, the letter says, also carried an audit report concerning the department held by Jaya as Chief Minister. It prints:

> “The Comptroller and Auditor General of India (CAG) has criticized the Tamil Nadu Home Department for not following open tender system in purchase of tents for the police. Though the rates quoted by ordinance factories were higher than open market prices, orders were still placed with them resulting in an extra expenditure, the CAG said in his report for the year ended March 2003 placed in the Legislature.”

The source’s Tamil rendering says that the CAG strongly criticised the Home Department for not following an open-tender system in purchasing tents for the police and stated that, despite ordnance-factory quotations being higher than open-market prices, orders were placed with those factories, resulting in extra expenditure.

All newspapers carried these reports. One Tamil newspaper, the letter recalls, used a headline saying that an Accountant General interview backed by evidence showed a ₹11,000-crore loss under Jaya’s government; that administration had been disrupted by repeatedly shifting ministers and officers; that ₹66 crore was lost because the Union Government’s cheaper rice was not purchased; and that ₹3,500 crore was returned because 46 schemes had not even been begun. It then reported in detail the errors described in the audit report and the audit officer’s interview.

What did Jayalalithaa, who was then Chief Minister, do when she saw all this? Today, when an audit report refers to revenue loss, she says a parliamentary committee must be formed; that the opposition will not rest until then; that Raja, who had been minister of that department, must be arrested; and she announces a demonstration in Puducherry. What did she do then?

Did she say: “The audit report has said all this, so I resign from office. Resignation alone is not enough; I will have myself arrested and a case should be filed against me”? No.

What did she do? She said that the Tamil Nadu audit officer’s statements to reporters were profoundly shocking, that nothing like this had happened in the history of independent India and that it was contrary to the Constitution. As Chief Minister, Jayalalithaa issued an unprecedented full-page response. That statement in her name, replying to the audit officer, was published in newspapers as a full-page government advertisement. The letter says ₹1 crore 22 lakh 81 thousand 100 of Tamil Nadu taxpayers’ money was spent on those advertisements published in Tamil Nadu and North India.

The response issued by Jayalalithaa said, in substance, that the CAG report on the Tamil Nadu government’s accounts for 2002–2003 had only recently, in July 2004, been placed before the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly; that it would in due course be discussed in the Public Accounts Committee; that the report pointed to no grave irregularity but merely contained observations by the CAG on certain matters; that the process would become complete only after the Public Accounts Committee had finished its work; and that in many instances the report itself indicated that the alleged loss was merely a notional loss. It therefore said it was improper to magnify those matters and create an impression that a huge actual revenue loss had occurred.

If an audit officer reports irregularities during Jayalalithaa’s government, the letter asks, is that merely a clay pot — the clay pot broken by the mother-in-law? But if an audit officer’s report criticises Raja, does it suddenly become a golden pot? Must Raja be arrested for it? What justice is this?

The heading of another audit report concerning Jayalalithaa’s government was:

> “TANSI - Loss of revenue due to failure to follow uniform procedure in disposal of assets”

The source then prints:

> “.........The sale of land in all the three defunct units had been effected almost at the same time, in the same locality, the sale price of land of Tansi Foundry to Jaya Publications Limited at Rs. 1350 per square metre was very low when compared to the sale price of Rs. 2080 and Rs. 2100 per square metre obtained in case of sale of land of Tansi Enamelled Wires and Tansi Forgings, respectively. Thus, the Company had not fully explored the possibility of getting a higher rate..........”

The accompanying Tamil rendering says that while TANSI Enamelled Wires and TANSI Forgings land in the same locality was sold at ₹2,080 and ₹2,100 per square metre, TANSI Foundry land alone was sold to Jaya Publications at the lower price of ₹1,350 per square metre, and that TANSI therefore lost the opportunity to obtain a higher amount.

This information still remains in the audit officer’s report. I have merely reminded you again of those sentences. There are many more audit observations about losses during Jayalalithaa’s rule. I have no desire to keep recalling all of them. But because she herself wants some statement or another to appear under her name every day, strings together stories that do not exist and uses uncivil language, it becomes necessary to provide factual explanations.

For example, in the statement Jayalalithaa issued yesterday, she wrote that the names of Karunanidhi’s son and Deputy Chief Minister Stalin and Karunanidhi’s wife Dayalu were heard in audio tapes recorded by the Income Tax Department, and that this made it clear a huge conspiracy had been planned to loot the country’s assets and had been successfully completed.

If the Income Tax Department records two unidentified people speaking and releases the tape, does everything those people said become true? A few days earlier, when Jaya’s new political adviser, *Thuglak* Cho, published the same information in his magazine, I immediately replied and asked whether he was prepared to publish that report under his own name; if he did, I said I would meet him legally. If Jayalalithaa then repeats the same material in her statement and we do not reply, will people not think what she said was true?

That is why I spend my time writing all these explanations.

**Udanpirappē,** I need not write these explanations to you again and again. But when false reports are spread against us, should we allow even a few members of the public to hear them and think, “Perhaps it may be so”? Is it enough for you alone to read all these details? Should we not explain these truths to the public?

The Kazhagam’s front-line functionaries and speakers must make these matters clear through the meetings at which they speak. They must explain to the people what is true and what is false. Village after village, meetings should explain what happened at Siruthavur, what happened at Kodanadu, what led to three students being burned alive at Dharmapuri, why the case is being heard in the Bengaluru Special Court and what the Supreme Court said about that case.

These details must be taken to the people through **veranda campaigns** and **street-corner campaigns**.

Let our campaign brigade set out!

Every district — district Kazhagam secretaries, union and city Kazhagam secretaries, the Youth Wing, Students’ Wing, Women’s Wing, Lawyers’ Wing and all other wings — should prepare to receive this campaign brigade. The Kazhagam’s campaign secretaries will publish the list of commanders and warriors who will come to raise the slogan. I ask you to contact them and make the necessary arrangements.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
22 December 2010

வெளியிட்டால் நான் சட்டரீதியாகச் சந்திக்கிறேன் என்று பதிலும் அளித்திருக்கிறேன். அதன் பின்னர் அதே செய்தியை ஜெயலலிதா தனது அறிக்கையிலே எழுதுகிறார் என்றால், அதற்கு நாம் பதில் அளிக்காவிட்டால் அவர் கூறியது உண்மை என்று ஆகிவிடாதா? எனவே இவ்வளவு விளக்கங்களையும் என்னுடைய நேரத்தைச் செலவிட்டு எழுதுகிறேன். உடன்பிறப்பே, இந்த விளக்கங்களை உனக்கு நான் மீண்டும், மீண்டும் எழுதத் தேவையில்லை தான்! இருந்தாலும் நமக்கு எதிராக தவறான செய்திகள் பரப்பப்படும்போது, பொது மக்களில் ஒருசிலர் அதைக்கேட்டு, அப்படியும் இருக்குமோ என்று நினைத்து விடக்கூடாதல்லவா? இந்த விவரங்களையெல்லாம் நீ மட்டும் படித்தால் போதுமா? பொதுமக்களிடம் இந்த உண்மை களையெல்லாம் விளக்கிட வேண்டாமா? கழகத்தின் முன்னணியினர், பேச்சாளர்கள் இந்த விவரங்களை தாங்கள் பேசுகின்ற கூட்டங்களின் மூலமாக மக்களுக்குத் தெளிவாக்கிட வேண்டும். உண்மை எது, பொய் எது என்பதை மக்களிடம் விளக்கி, சிறுதாவூரில் நடை பெற்றது என்ன, கோட நாட்டில் நடைபெற்றது என்ன, தர்மபுரியிலே மூன்று மாணவிகள் உயிரோடு எரித்துக் கொல்லப்பட காரணமாக அமைந்தது எது, பெங்களூர் சிறப்பு நீதி மன்றத்திலே நடைபெறும் வழக்கு எதற்காக, அந்த வழக்கு பற்றி உச்சநீதிமன்றம் தெரிவித்த தீர்ப்பு என்ன என்பது பற்றியெல்லாம் ஊருக்கு ஊர் கூட்டம் போட்டு விளக்கிட வேண்டும், திண்ணைப் பிரச்சாரமாக, தெருமுனைப் பிரச்சாரமாக இந்த விவரங்களை மக்களுக்குப் புரிய வைத்திட வேண்டும். புறப்படட்டும் நமது பிரச்சாரப்படை ! இப்பிரச்சாரப் படையை வரவேற்க ஒவ்வொரு மாவட்டமும் - மாவட்டக் கழகச் செயலாளர்களும் - ஒன்றிய, நகரக் கழகச் செயலாளர்களும் - இளைஞர் அணியினரும், மாணவர் அணியினரும், மகளிர் அணியினரும், வழக்கறிஞர் அணியினரும், ஏனைய பிற அணியினரும் தயாராகட்டும்! முழக்கம் செய்ய வருகின்ற தளபதிகளும், வீரர்களும் யார், எவர் என்ற பட்டியலை நம்முடைய பிரச்சாரக் கழகச் செயலாளர்கள் வெளியிடுவார்கள். அவர்களோடு தொடர்பு கொண்டு ஆவன செய்திட வேண்டுகிறேன். அன்புள்ள, மு.க. 22-12-2010
