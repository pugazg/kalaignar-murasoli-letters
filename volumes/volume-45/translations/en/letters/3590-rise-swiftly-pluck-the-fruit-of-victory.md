# 3590. Rise Swiftly; Pluck the Fruit of Victory!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 24 September 2011

**Udanpirappē,**

The dates for the local-body elections have been announced! Elections are to be held on 17 and 19 October. The first list of our party's candidates has also been announced.

Normally, the Government issues the election-day notification, and only a few days later does the period for filing nominations begin. But this time, nomination filing began the very next day after the election notification was issued. Not only that: as I have already pointed out, the basic information about which constituencies are general constituencies, which are reserved for women, and which are reserved constituencies was not communicated to the opposition parties at all.

“As though hiding the comb could stop the wedding!” That seems to have been the ruling party's expectation. But, by mistake—and because of the haste she always displays while imagining it to be cleverness—Jayalalithaa herself released her party's list of candidates. In an election that is supposed to be conducted for everyone, is it democratic to give advance information to only one party? A case could be filed against this Government for that alone!

Because the rulers kept the constituency classifications hidden in this way, political parties were placed under severe pressure when constituencies that had until then been general seats, where many people had expected to contest, were suddenly declared reserved constituencies. Likewise, in places suddenly converted into constituencies reserved for women, parties were forced to select women candidates within a day or two. But because the ruling party had already been informed which constituencies were reserved for whom, it was as though, before a race had even begun, the rules were violated and a few runners alone were brought forward and placed ahead while everyone else was pushed back.

After I raised this question, the Government had no answer to give. When reporters met the State Election Commissioner and asked about it, he merely said that it was not his job to select and announce which constituencies were meant for whom. But he did not open his mouth on why it had still not been announced which constituencies belonged to which categories. His own conscience must have told him that what had been done was wrong. So he confined himself to denying that the task was his. Somehow, through our own efforts, we managed to identify the constituencies to some extent and announce our candidates.

The State Election Commissioner told reporters that a notice indicating the category of the constituency had been pasted at the place where nomination forms were issued. In other words, every candidate was expected to go to the place where he or she had to obtain a nomination form, look at the notice pasted there, find out the constituency category, and only then collect the form! Although nomination filing was to begin on 22-9-2011, in many places people who went that day to ask for forms were told that the forms had not yet arrived from the Election Commission.

Even *Dina Thanthi*, a newspaper that publishes news supportive of the ruling party, carried this on its front page:

> “Because nomination filing began the very next day after the election date was announced, no one had made the necessary advance arrangements. Moreover, the places where nominations were to be filed were announced only yesterday evening, and many people struggled without knowing where they had to go to file their nominations.”

Nothing like this happened in any previous local-body election.

Because of these actions by the Government, and because many constituencies have been changed, we have had to select and announce new candidates. Some may be disappointed that they have not been able to stand as candidates. Others may suddenly find themselves required to contest. Do not keep dwelling on all that. There may be little time left for campaigning, asking for votes and going house to house to appeal to the people. Therefore, party Udanpirappugal must begin their work immediately. Since these are local-body elections, do not waste time organising large campaign meetings; go from house to house and ask for votes.

Explain to the voters how extensively the DMK has worked on local issues in the past. Someone who held a position last time may not have received an opportunity this time, and another person may have been given the chance. The former should not brood over not having been given an opportunity this time. Elsewhere, someone who was given an opportunity last time may have been given one again. There is no need to become angry and ask, “Why does this person get the chance every time?”

Udanpirappē, in these local-body elections we are contesting against those in power. And by “those in power,” I mean a party that won the recent Assembly general election with brute strength and now stands at the height of arrogance. Because of the competition that arose among ourselves, and because of the way some of our own people behaved, we suffered a very great defeat in the Assembly election.

Taking advantage of that political situation, the Chief Minister and other ministers on the ruling benches have spoken in the Assembly in all manner of ways, belittling and abusing us. I have written down a few examples. Take them out and read them once again! Think about how, in the Assembly, members of our party were not even allowed to sit inside and carry out honest democratic work; the ruling party's aim seemed only to say whatever it wished, abuse us and send us out.

If we are to rise from all these circumstances and return, as usual, to serving the people, we must properly use the rare opportunity that these local-body elections have placed before us.

Precisely because the ruling party does not want us to win the local-body elections, it is hunting down our party's frontline workers, foisting false cases on them without any evidence and sending them to prison. If a court grants bail in one case, they are not even allowed to come out of prison. Somewhere, someone is found, a complaint petition is obtained, and without the police first investigating whether that complaint is true or false, there is immediate arrest and imprisonment. We must expose these lawless actions of the ruling party before the people of the State.

If the party's frontline workers who would normally lead and labour in the election campaign are in prison, then every other party worker at the next level must regard himself or herself as the candidate and throw himself or herself into election work.

Those now in office after winning a huge Assembly victory may engage in every kind of malpractice in this election in order to retain their hold. They may pour out enormous sums of money and try somehow to win. We must tell the people how, within just three or four months of assuming office, they have already begun to disregard, humiliate and push around even their own alliance parties.

Drunk with the magnitude of their Assembly victory, they now speak in an authoritarian style as though they can pick up anyone and throw anyone down. At least to correct such people, we must try to teach them a lesson in these local-body elections. If, by some accident, they win again in this election too, they will make the people of the State suffer terribly thereafter. Those who already refuse to respect anyone may later believe there is no one left to question them; one cannot even imagine what cruelties they might then unleash. They will try to foist false cases on the remaining frontline workers of the party and continue deceiving the public.

We have received one more piece of information. It is said that, if candidates contesting on behalf of our party also contested last time, they must have submitted their election-expense accounts, and that the ruling party, with the help of the State Election Commission, may try to reject the nominations of those who did not submit such accounts. We must therefore be vigilant on this matter too.

Newspapers and media outlets have shut their eyes, thrown journalistic ethics to the winds and are supporting the ruling party. Newspapers that are ready to publish the ruling party's entire list of municipal-corporation councillor candidates—and to publish it on the front page—print the names of candidates announced by us almost as an afterthought on the seventh or eighth page, while staying wide awake in the hope that perhaps they will at least be allotted time for an interview with the Chief Minister.

We must carry out our work despite all this. And we must begin that work immediately, rushing from village to village. We must explain the state of the country to voters at every doorstep and along every street. The days remaining are few; the tasks to be done are many! Delay will be of no help now. Set out at once! Rise swiftly; pluck the fruit of victory!

**With affection, M.K.**  
24-9-2011

வாக்குகளைக் கேட்க வேண்டும்.

வாக்காளர்களுக்கு எடுத்துச் சொல்ல வேண்டும். கடந்தமுறை
அந்தப் பொறுப்பிலே இருந்தவர்களுக்கு இந்த முறை வாய்ப்பு

அவர்கள் தங்களுக்கு இந்த முறை வாய்ப்பு கிடைக்கவில்லையே
என்று எண்ணிவிடக் கூடாது. வேறு சில இடங்களில் கடந்த
முறை வாய்ப்பு பெற்றவர்களுக்கே இந்த முறையும் வாய்ப்பு

வாய்ப்பா என்றும் கோபப்படத் தேவையில்லை.

மிருக பலத்தோடு வெற்றி பெற்று, கர்வத்தின் உச்சியில் உள்ள
ஒரு கட்சியை எதிர்த்துப் போட்டியிடுகிறோம். நமக்குள்ளே
எழுந்த போட்டியின் காரணமாகவும் - நம்மவர்கள் சிலர் நடந்து
கொண்ட செயல்பாடுகளின் காரணமாகவும் மிகப்பெரிய
தோல்வியை சட்டமன்றத் தேர்தலில் நாம் அடைந்தோம். இந்த
அரசியல் நிலையைப் பயன்படுத்திக்கொண்டு, சட்டமன்றத்திலே
ஆளுங்கட்சி வரிசையிலே உள்ள முதலமைச்சரும் மற்ற
அமைச்சர்களும் நம்மைப் பற்றி இழித்தும் பழித்தும் என்னென்ன
பேசுகிறார்கள் என்பதற்கான ஒருசில உதாரணங்களை
நான் எழுதியிருக்கிறேன். அவற்றையெல்லாம் எடுத்து ஒரு
முறை படித்துப் பார்! சட்டமன்றத்திலே நமது கட்சியின்
உறுப்பினர்கள் உள்ளே அமர்ந்து நேர்மையான ஜனநாயகப்
பணியாற்றுவதற்குக் கூட அனுமதிக்காமல் ஆளுங்கட்சியினர்
என்னென்னவோ பேசி, நம்மை ஏசி, வெளியே அனுப்புவதிலேயே
குறியாக இருந்ததையெல்லாம் யோசித்துப்பார்! இந்தச்
சூழ்நிலைகளிலிருந்தெல்லாம் நாம் எழுந்து நின்று வழக்கம்
போல் மக்கள் பணியாற்றிட வேண்டுமென்றால், நமக்குள்ள அரிய
வாய்ப்பாக வந்துள்ள இந்த உள்ளாட்சி மன்றத் தேர்தல்களை
முறையாக நாம் பயன்படுத்திக் கொள்ள வேண்டும்.

உள்ளாட்சி மன்றத் தேர்தல்களில் நாம் வெற்றி பெற்றுவிடக்
கூடாது என்பதற்காகவே ஆளுங்கட்சி எந்தவிதமான ஆதாரமும்
இல்லாமல், நமது கழக முன்னணியினரையெல்லாம் தேடிப் பிடித்து
பொய் வழக்குகளைச் சுமத்தி சிறையிலே தள்ளி வேடிக்கை பார்க்கின்றது. ஒரு வழக்கில் நீதிமன்றம் சென்று ஜாமீன் பெற்றால்,
அதன்படி சிறையிலிருந்து வெளியே வருவதற்கே விடாமல்,
எங்கேயாவது தேடி, யாரையாவது பிடித்து அவர்களிடம் ஒரு புகார்
மனுவினைப் பெற்று, அது உண்மையா, பொய்யா என்பதைப்
பற்றி காவல் துறையினர் மூலமாக விசாரணை நடத்தாமல், புகார்
வந்தவுடனேயே கைது, சிறைவாசம் என்று ஆளுங்கட்சியினர்
அராஜக நடவடிக்கைகளில் ஈடுபட்டிருப்பதையெல்லாம் நாட்டு
மக்களுக்கு அம்பலப்படுத்த வேண்டும். தேர்தலில் முன் நின்று

சட்டமன்றத் தேர்தல்களில் பெரு வெற்றி பெற்று,
ஆட்சிப் பொறுப்பிலே இருப்பவர்கள், அதைத் தக்கவைத்துக்

முறைகேட்டிலும் ஈடுபடக் கூடும். ஏராளமாக பணத்தை
வாரி இறைத்து, எப்படியாவது வெற்றி பெற முயற்சிப்பார்கள்.
பொறுப்பேற்றிருக்கின்ற காரணத்தினால், அவர்கள் மூன்று, நான்கு
மாதங்களுக்குள்ளாகவே தங்களுடைய தோழமைக் கட்சிகளையே

சட்டமன்றத் தேர்தலில் பெற்ற பெரு வெற்றியின் காரணமாக

அவர்களுக்கு ஒரு பாடத்தைக் கற்பிக்க நாம் முயற்சிக்க வேண்டும்.
தப்பித்தவறி அவர்கள் மீண்டும் இந்தத் தேர்தலிலும் வெற்றி

விடுவார்கள் என்று கற்பனையே செய்ய முடியாது. கழகத்தின்
சார்பில் எஞ்சியிருக்கின்ற முன்னணியினர் மீதும் ஏதாவது பொய்
வழக்குகளைப் போடுவதற்கும், பொது மக்களைத் தொடர்ந்து
