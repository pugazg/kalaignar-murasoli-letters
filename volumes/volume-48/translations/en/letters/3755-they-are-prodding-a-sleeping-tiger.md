# 3755. They Are Prodding a Sleeping Tiger!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 14 May 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

The School Education Minister of the AIADMK government announced during the School Education demand for grants in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly on 10 May 2013:

> “In the last academic year, two English-medium sections each were started in Classes I and VI in 320 government schools. A total of 640 sections were started, and 22,400 students are studying in them. Following this, from the coming academic year, English-medium sections will be started in all government primary schools, middle schools, high schools and higher secondary schools where there is demand. As a result, 1.5 lakh boys and girls will benefit each year.”

The important announcements that ought to have been made on behalf of this department had all been read by the Chief Minister herself as statements under Rule 110, while this contradictory and controversial announcement alone was left for the minister to make in the House. The minister too—whether knowing or not knowing what he was announcing, and without learning the long Dravidian-movement history concerning Tamil as the medium of instruction—made this announcement.

The belief that knowledge can develop only if education is pursued through English as the medium of instruction is merely a state of mind; there is not even the slightest truth in it. Education in one's mother tongue gives self-directed thought the opportunity to flourish. Learning through another language can become a major obstacle to independent thinking. Leaders with concern for society, including Mahatma Gandhi and Perarignar Anna, educationists and thinkers have all stressed the necessity and importance of studying in the mother tongue.

In many countries of the world, English is not even a language of ordinary public use. In countries such as Russia, Germany, Japan and China, their own national languages function as both the language of administration and the medium of instruction.

It is not an acceptable course to declare English the medium of instruction merely so that government schools can attract students on a par with private schools. After the introduction of Samacheer Kalvi, private schools and government schools are on an equal footing as far as the syllabus is concerned. Therefore the proper way to turn government schools into improved centres of education is to create and develop basic infrastructure in them, as in good private schools, and provide quality education.

While speaking about Tamil as the medium of instruction, I think it appropriate to recall briefly the work we carried out in the past on this issue.

Continuing the efforts made by Arignar Anna to formulate the language policy to be followed in educational institutions, when I was Chief Minister a meeting was held in my presence on 16 November 1998 to decide on Tamil as the language of study. The then Education Minister Perasiriyar, Tamil Development Minister Tamilkudimagan and officials participated. The following decisions were taken and announced:

1. Recognition would be given only to schools that taught Tamil **(as a subject)**.
2. From the 1999–2000 academic year, subjects such as geography, history, mathematics and social science were to be taught through Tamil **(through the Medium of Tamil)**.

The Tamil Nadu Primary School Teachers Federation welcomed the government's announcement that recognition and concessions would henceforth be given only to nursery and primary schools that taught Tamil as a subject. The Tamil Teachers Association of Tamil Nadu similarly welcomed it and issued a statement.

Although these decisions were welcomed by many, some people raised doubts. To examine them, a consultative meeting was held under my chairmanship on 12 January 1999. Its important decisions were as follows:

As the Tamil Nadu government had announced on 16 November 1998, nursery and primary schools should teach Tamil as a subject; in addition, instead of conducting all other subjects in English, from the next academic year at least two out of three subjects—social studies, including geography and history; mathematics; and science—should be taught through Tamil **(through the Medium of Tamil)**. The government insisted on this.

Even after such announcements, one section continued campaigning against Tamil-medium education, and newspapers continued to magnify that campaign. At the same time, Tamil scholars continued demanding that Tamil be implemented as the medium of instruction from nursery school through higher education. Following those demands, under DMK rule a committee headed by Justice S. Mohan was constituted on 3 May 1999 under Government Order No. 117. The committee's recommendations were later properly considered, and the following orders were issued in Government Order No. 324, School Education Department, dated 19 November 1999:

1. In all schools—matriculation schools, State Government and aided schools, and recognised unaided schools—Tamil or the mother tongue must be taught as the first language.
2. From Class I to Class V, in all matriculation schools and schools following the State Government **(State Board)** syllabus, only the mother tongue or Tamil must be the medium of instruction.
3. In the Matriculation Board rules, Tamil and the mother tongue would be included as media of instruction along with English. Separate amendments to the rules governing matriculation schools would be issued for this purpose.

Seeking to have this Government Order declared invalid and **(Quash)** it, the Tamil Nadu associations of nursery, primary, matriculation and higher secondary school managements, together with some parents, filed various writ petitions in the Madras High Court. The High Court quashed Government Order Standing No. 324, School Education, dated 19 November 1999, in its judgment of 20 April 2000. Against that judgment, the government filed Special Leave Petitions **(SLP)** in the Supreme Court on 10 July 2000—**SLP Civil No.13299-13301/2000**—and the case remained pending, according to the source.

Claiming that its government would ensure that no boy or girl in Tamil Nadu left school without learning Tamil, the AIADMK government introduced a subject called **“Scientific Tamil”** under Chief Minister Jayalalithaa. From the 2003–2004 academic year, it ordered that “Scientific Tamil” be taught alongside other subjects from LKG to +2 in all kinds of schools, and textbooks were provided free for it. Even so, the scheme was not fully implemented.

Taking all that had happened until then into account, after the DMK government assumed office for the fifth time in 2006, on 9 June 2006 we enacted a law enabling Tamil to be learned as one of the subjects taught in all schools in Tamil Nadu. That law was called the **Tamil Nadu Tamil Learning Act, 2006**. Its important provisions included the following: Tamil was to be taught as a subject in Classes I to X in all schools, beginning with Class I in the 2006–2007 academic year, Classes I and II in 2007–2008, and extending progressively in that manner up to Class X.

After the DMK government enacted this law, the matter proceeded well without any setback. Nor did the DMK government's efforts concerning Tamil as the medium of instruction in schools stop there. In June 2010, on the distinguished occasion of the World Classical Tamil Conference in Coimbatore, orders were issued enabling students in Anna Universities to study Civil Engineering and Mechanical Engineering through their mother tongue—through Tamil—for the first time in any Indian State. In the very first year, 2010–2011, 1,378 students joined Tamil-medium engineering classes out of the 1,400 places fixed for those branches.

Further, until the 2009–2010 academic year, university question papers for engineering degree courses had been provided only in English and answers had to be written only in English. From 2010, universities under DMK rule were instructed to introduce a system in which question papers could be given in English or Tamil and answers could be written in English or Tamil.

Instead of carrying forward these efforts made by the DMK government concerning Tamil as the medium of instruction, the AIADMK minister has now spoken in the Assembly in a way that would dig a pit and bury all those efforts. At a time when progressive demands are growing stronger that all Union Government employment examinations should also be conducted in Tamil and that Tamil should be accepted as a language of proceedings in the Madras High Court, suddenly introducing English-medium education in government schools is like needlessly prodding a sleeping tiger and inviting trouble.

When I see some politicians in Tamil Nadu—who behave as though they alone were born for the sake of Tamil—keeping their mouths shut on this important issue for fear that speaking might invite the Chief Minister's anger, I am reminded of the proverb: **“Those who are afraid of the tiger, lie down on me!”**

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**14 May 2013**
