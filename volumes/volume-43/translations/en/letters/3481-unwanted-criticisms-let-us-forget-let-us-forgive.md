# 3481. Unwanted Criticisms; Let Us Forget! Let Us Forgive!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This letter contrasts a 2010 *Wall Street Journal* article praising Chennai’s automobile sector with the author’s recollection of a paid four-page advertisement placed in *THE ASIAN WALL STREET JOURNAL* during the earlier AIADMK Government. Source-supplied English — including the 2010 headline, `Advertisement - Advantage Tamil Nadu-`, and the printed email-rate sentence — is preserved exactly as printed. All allegations about public expenditure and advertising commission remain source-attributed.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3481](../../../chapters/3481-vendaatha-vimarsanangal-marappom-mannippom.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 380](../../../pages/page-380.md)–[PDF 385](../../../pages/page-385.md)  
**Date:** 14 July 2010

**Udanpirappē,**

Retired Supreme Court Justice Thiru Rathinavel Pandian, after losing his wife some months ago, had told me that he was going to visit the homes of his sons who work in the United States. Two days ago he telephoned me and said that he had happened to see an English publication there called *The Wall Street Journal*. He told me with happiness that it carried a major article greatly praising the Tamil Nadu Government under the headline:

`A New Detroit Rises in India’s South - Car Makers Are Lured by Chennai’s Port, Educated Workers and Limited Hassles`

I immediately asked him to send me the article. He responsibly told his sons, and the article reached me.

The article, as rendered in Tamil by the source, says:

- Chennai is like Detroit. With many international automobile manufacturers and suppliers establishing factories there, this metropolis of five million people is prospering. Ford, Hyundai, Nissan, Renault, Daimler and BMW have all converged there.
- They are spending several hundred crore dollars to make Chennai a major centre for manufacturing the world’s small cars, both for export and for increasingly prosperous Indians.
- Chennai will soon produce **15 lakh cars a year**, more than any American State produced in the previous year.
- Automobile-component suppliers are also investing. France’s Michelin tyre company and Saint-Gobain, the automobile-glass manufacturer, are establishing some of their largest factories in Chennai. Germany’s Daimler is building a test track at a cost of several hundred crore dollars.
- Taken together, these investments have created employment for **two lakh people**.
- Tamil Nadu is better than many Indian States at providing the land, roads and electricity required by the automobile industry.
- Tamil Nadu has created a single office — a **single-window system** — through which the dozens of Government approvals needed to start or expand a factory may be obtained.
- In India, large companies have often faced problems when State Governments change hands between parties. Automobile-company executives, however, said they saw no change in the way they were treated when the DMK assumed State power in 2006.
- The student population of the American School in Chennai has risen fourfold as new students from the United States, Japan, Europe and Korea have begun arriving.

Thus, the source says, the article wrote about Tamil Nadu, the factories established here and the DMK Government’s concern for industrial development.

When I read this article in the American newspaper *The Wall Street Journal*, I remembered news about the same publication that had been widely discussed in Tamil Nadu some years earlier. At that time the AIADMK Government under Jayalalithaa was in office, and large advertisements about that Government were being issued every day.

To give one example: it did not stop with advertisements in newspapers here about the AIADMK Government in Tamil Nadu. In the American English publication *Wall Street Journal*, a very large advertisement covering **four pages** was published.

A very large photograph of Jayalalithaa, then Chief Minister, occupied about half a page. At a public meeting in M.G.R. Nagar I held up the publication and told the people:

“This in my hand was not published in Tamil Nadu, nor in newspapers of another State. Why, it was not even published in India. This is a newspaper published in America. Its name is THE ASIAN WALL STREET JOURNAL. What is printed here? Advertisement - Advantage Tamil Nadu- — that is the heading. Look at Jayalalithaa’s photograph and see its size! This publication carries not a quarter-page or half-page advertisement, not one page or two pages, but advertisements across four pages. Page after page carries Jayalalithaa’s photograph. Even the picture of the Madurai temple occupies only half a page, while Jayalalithaa’s interview is included. How many people in Tamil Nadu know of this newspaper published in America? An advertisement about the Jayalalithaa Government has been placed in an American newspaper. Has any State Government committed such an outrage? Was the advertisement free? Do you know that our tax money was paid as the advertising charge?

“The amount was stated by email. Further to our telecon please find rates for 4 pages color, centre section Pull out on Section A of “Wall Street Journal** - Global Edition - Rs. 3,88,62,225 (3 கோடியே 88 இலட்சத்து 62 ஆயிரத்து 225 ரூ.) Whose money is this? How many people benefit from it? Our tax money was wasted on an advertisement published in America!”

When I spoke this way at that public meeting, leading figures from parties that are now close allies of that lady were themselves on the stage and spoke to me in astonishment.

If, as early as 2004, about **₹4 crore** was spent merely to advertise the AIADMK Government in an American publication — indeed, to publish Jayalalithaa’s own interview as an advertisement — one can easily understand, the author argues, the scale of ostentatious administration at that time.

Moreover, when an advertisement of this value is placed, those who procure it may receive commission of around **15 to 25 per cent**. At that meeting I asked who received the commission for this advertisement and who shared it. The author says those then in power gave no answer and did not even take notice of the accusation.

One should compare, he argues, the extent to which taxpayers’ money was wasted then with the present situation: without such paid advertising, the same American publication has now written a major article about our achievements.

Those who, in the author’s allegation, committed corruption on such a scale while in power are now supposedly conducting public meetings condemning our Government, with some other parties lending them a shoulder....

And that is not all. Newspapers have also reported that an industrial survey conducted by the Associated Chambers of Commerce and Industry of India **(Assocham)** found Tamil Nadu to be among the **top three States** in India where investors wished to invest.

In keeping with the thought, “Let those who praise, praise; let those who fling dust and abuse, abuse,” whatever agitations, statements and condemnations may be conducted, such praise continues to come for the work of the Tamil Nadu Government. Let us gain still more encouragement from that praise and work on; let us forget unwanted criticisms..... let us forgive!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**14-7-2010**
