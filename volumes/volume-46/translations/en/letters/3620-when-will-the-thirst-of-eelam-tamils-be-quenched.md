# 3620. When Will the Thirst of Eelam Tamils Be Quenched?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This draft follows the locked Volume 46 conventions. Kalaignar’s continuing argument on the Sri Lankan Tamil question, his responses to contemporary political statements, cited historical episodes, demands concerning rehabilitation and political rights, and his insistence on a democratic solution are retained in source order. Source-attributed political and historical claims are translated as printed and are not externally reconciled. The complete audited Tamil remains authoritative. This draft awaits separate bilingual source-alignment review.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3620](../../../chapters/3620-endru-thaniyum-eezhaththamizhar-thaagam.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 221](../../../pages/page-221.md)–[PDF 228](../../../pages/page-228.md)  
**Date:** 28 April 2012

**Udanpirappē,**

In the letters I have been writing continuously about the Sri Lankan Tamil question, I have explained why the demand of the Eelam Tamils cannot be dismissed merely by saying that the armed struggle has ended. The sufferings endured by that people, the lives lost, the displacement, the camps, the missing persons and the denial of political rights remain before us.

Statements are being made that the situation in Sri Lanka has changed and that Tamils there are not asking for a separate Eelam. But can the feeling of a people be measured only from what can be spoken under conditions in which military power surrounds their everyday life? Before arriving at conclusions about what Eelam Tamils want, their circumstances, their history and their right to express their will freely must be considered.

I have already written that promises of devolution and political settlement have repeatedly been made and repeatedly left without implementation. Agreements were signed; committees were appointed; proposals were spoken of; assurances were given to India and to representatives who visited Sri Lanka. Yet the Tamil people have continued to ask when a just political settlement will become reality.

The question before us is not one that can be answered by forgetting what happened at the end of the war. The people who lost their homes and lands must be resettled with dignity. Those kept in camps must be allowed to return to normal life. Families searching for those who disappeared must be told the truth. Military domination in Tamil areas must end, and the Tamil people must be enabled to live without fear.

The Government of India cannot treat these merely as the internal affairs of a neighbouring country and turn away. India has a responsibility arising from history, geography and the concern felt by the people of Tamil Nadu. Whenever assurances are obtained from the Sri Lankan Government, India must also see whether those assurances are actually carried out.

What is wrong in saying that the wishes of the Tamil people themselves must finally be known democratically? I have not asked that India send an army and create Tamil Eelam by force. I have asked that, following democratic precedents recognised by the United Nations and the international community, the Tamil people be given an opportunity to express their will.

If it is claimed that the Tamils do not want Separate Tamil Eelam, then why should anyone fear a free expression of their opinion? Let their voice be heard without intimidation. Let Eelam Tamils living in their homeland and those driven across the world by war and persecution have the opportunity to state what political future they seek.

The sacrifices made over decades cannot be erased from history. Nor can a political question be extinguished merely by defeating an armed movement. A durable settlement can arise only from justice, equality, dignity and the consent of the people concerned.

The Tamil people of Sri Lanka have waited through agreement after agreement and promise after promise. Their longing is not for words placed on paper and forgotten. Their thirst is for a life in which they can protect their language, land, culture, political rights and dignity.

How long must they continue to wait? When will the tears of Eelam Tamils dry? When will their political rights cease to be a promise and become a lived reality? When will the thirst of Eelam Tamils be quenched?

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**28 April 2012**
