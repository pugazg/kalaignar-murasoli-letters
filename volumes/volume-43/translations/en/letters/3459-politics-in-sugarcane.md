# 3459. Politics in Sugarcane!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This letter is a rebuttal on sugarcane pricing. It distinguishes the price sought by growers from what sugar mills can pay, repeatedly invokes the sugarcane recovery rate, and sets out cooperative-mill losses, loan waivers and State support-price policy. All figures and criticisms remain in the source’s own argumentative frame.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3459](../../../chapters/3459-karumbil-arasiyal.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 257](../../../pages/page-257.md)–[PDF 259](../../../pages/page-259.md)  
**Date:** 19 February 2010

**Udanpirappē,**

Imagining that, on the issue of sugarcane prices, the ruling party and the opposition are quarrelling like little schoolchildren;

and imagining themselves to be classroom monitors who do not get involved in such matters;

they have issued a long statement asking, “Should the differences of opinion among political parties be imposed upon sugarcane growers?”

Some newspapers that keep opposing the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam without cause have enthusiastically published that statement in large type.

In the statement, while pointing out that it was wrong for the former government in Tamil Nadu not to provide a State support price, they have also said that prices from ₹2,100 to ₹2,300 are being paid in Uttar Pradesh and in States such as Maharashtra, Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh.

They have stated the prices, but none of them has explained the “recovery rate” of the sugarcane produced there.

First, one detail must be understood. From whom are the farmers who demand a higher price for sugarcane demanding it? Are they asking the government? Or are they asking the owners of the mills that buy the sugarcane, crush it and sell it as sugar? This must be understood.

Even if the government is prepared to announce the price demanded by sugarcane farmers, what answer is the government to give to the outcry of sugar mills that state categorically that paying that price is not viable for them? What answer would silence that outcry? It should also be understood that announcing a sugarcane price does not mean that the government itself is going to pay that price to sugarcane farmers. Even if the government were prepared to pay it in the case of cooperative mills, if private mill owners said a similar price was not viable for them, would it not again be the sugarcane farmers who would be affected?

Forgetting — or concealing — who is required to pay the fixed sugarcane price to whom, and creating a controversy among the public, is good neither for sugarcane farmers nor for the mills producing sugar. If ₹2,100 to ₹2,300 is being paid in States such as Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh, should they not also have said what recovery rate that sugarcane possesses?

They have added another imaginary story to this statement: “This government, which pours money out of public funds for free television sets, rice at one rupee a kilogram, salary increases for government employees and the 100-day employment scheme, has sown the seeds for farmers’ suicides by failing to order profit-making sugar mills to pay a higher price.” Everyone knows that all the schemes they have listed above are people’s welfare schemes.

Why, while mocking all these, did the authors of this statement conveniently forget that the government announced and implemented cancellation of ₹7,000 crore in cooperative loans for farmers? It is surprising that they also failed to remember that farmers are now being given cooperative crop loans completely free of interest.

In the sugar industry, production is higher in some years and lower in others. It changes from one to the other. Even at times when sugar production was high, sugar prices were low and cooperative mills were operating at a loss, this government announced a State support price higher than the minimum price announced by the Union Government, provided the necessary subsidy to cooperative mills itself and ensured that farmers received a fair sugarcane price. Why have those issuing statements now forgotten this?

At the end of 2006–2007, the total accumulated loss of cooperative sugar mills was ₹1,270 crore. By the end of 2007–2008 it had risen to ₹1,648 crore, and at the end of 2008–2009 it stood at ₹1,625 crore. Even so, it is noteworthy that, considering the welfare of sugarcane farmers, this government has continued to provide them with a fair support price.

In States such as Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh and Karnataka, the State government does not fix any recommended price above the price determined by the Union Government. Although private sugar-mill owners may appear to pay more for sugarcane when sugar prices are high, it is customary for them to pay a lower price for sugarcane when sugar prices fall. Without recognising this, instead of appreciating the policy of this government, which every year fixes a higher price for sugarcane purchased by cooperative mills, the fact that this federation of sugarcane farmers’ associations alone has issued a statement as though unaware of the truth shows that it carries a political motive.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**19-02-2010**

## Letter-specific notes

1. `பிழி திறன்` is rendered **recovery rate**, the technical factor the source repeatedly says must accompany comparisons of sugarcane prices.
2. The criticisms of welfare schemes and the response concerning ₹7,000 crore in cooperative-loan cancellation are preserved as arguments attributed to the statement and to the author respectively.
3. Cooperative-mill accumulated-loss figures — ₹1,270 crore, ₹1,648 crore and ₹1,625 crore — are retained exactly as printed.
4. No canonical Tamil change was required during this source-check.

சர்க்கரைத் தொழிலில் உற்பத்தி சில ஆண்டுகளிலே
அதிகமாக இருக்கும், சில ஆண்டுகளிலே குறைவாக இருக்கும்.

கொடுப்பது வழக்கம் என்பதை இவர்கள் உணராமல் தொடர்ந்து

கொள்கையைப் பாராட்டுவதற்குப் பதிலாக இந்தக் கரும்பு
விவசாயிகள் சங்க கூட்டமைப்பினர் மாத்திரம் இந்த உண்மையை
அறியாதது போல் அறிக்கை விடுத்திருப்பதுதான் அரசியல் உள்
நோக்கம் கொண்டதாகும்.
