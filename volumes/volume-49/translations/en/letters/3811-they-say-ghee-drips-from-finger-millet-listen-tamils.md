# 3811. They Say Ghee Drips from Finger Millet; Listen, Tamils!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 26 September 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

The Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu did not attend the National Integration Council meeting chaired by the Prime Minister in Delhi on 23 September 2013. As usual, however, a speech prepared for her attacking the Union Government was taken there and read by one of her ministers.

The speech claimed that law and order in Tamil Nadu was excellent and that the State was therefore spoken of nationally as a “park of peace.”

Only on 21 September 2013, I had written at length about the Tamil Nadu Police and the collapse and deterioration of law and order—not by presenting merely my own opinion, but by compiling reports from publications issued in Tamil Nadu, including *Junior Vikatan*, *Dinamalar*, the *Indian Express*, *Theekkathir* and *Dinakaran*. After reading the Chief Minister's Delhi speech, one can only wonder whether she reads any of these publications or understands the actual condition of the State.

Tamil newspapers reproduced the Chief Minister's long address without a letter out of place under headlines such as “Tamil Nadu is a park of peace because law and order is excellent—Jayalalithaa's address to the National Integration Council in Delhi.” I shall briefly list the law-and-order reports that appeared in those very newspapers during the previous evening and the following morning.

- Dr Subbaiah, a neurologist serving at the Government Medical College in Chennai, entered his car on the 14th to leave Billroth Hospital in Abhiramapuram. A gang of five men attacked him repeatedly with sickles. He was admitted with twenty-seven cut injuries and died despite treatment.
- Near Sholinganallur, a young man was found unconscious with bloody injuries in the back seat of an auto on Old Mahabalipuram Road at Semmenchery. He died after being admitted to the Chennai Government General Hospital. Investigation identified him as auto driver Sasibalan. In the same Semmenchery area, construction worker Velmurugan was also murdered and found dead in a thorny bush.
- Near Mangadu outside Chennai, businessman Senu, who was also a deputy secretary of an AIADMK branch, was stabbed to death the previous morning.
- In Salem, an elderly woman named Saraswathi was murdered and fifty sovereigns of jewellery were stolen.
- In Madurai on the morning of the 23rd, onion merchant Pandi—the brother-in-law of V.K. Gurusamy, former DMK East Zone Chairman in the Corporation—was hacked to death by a gang of four.
- One hundred and fifty visually impaired protesters were arrested at Parry's Corner. Their agitation had continued for an eighth day.
- Three defamation cases were filed against Dr Ramadoss on Jayalalithaa's behalf—in a single day. How many had come before? Twelve defamation cases had so far been filed against me; thirty-four against Opposition Leader Vijayakanth. Even his wife appeared to be pursued by defamation cases. There was no need even to ask about the newspapers. And Tamil Nadu was said to be a park of peace!
- In Tiruvallur, textile merchant Nataraj locked his shop after business and left. When he opened it the next morning, the cash box had been broken and garments lay scattered. ₹9 lakh in cash was missing, and bundles of expensive cloth had been carried away.
- Traders complained that five shops had been broken open in the Neelankarai–Injambakkam area and ₹1.5 lakh stolen.
- The Tamil Nadu Primary School Teachers' Federation announced a continuing blockade agitation from the 25th, demanding, among other things, that Tamil Nadu teachers receive pay equivalent to Central Government schoolteachers.
- In a case concerning sand theft, High Court Justice Manikumar ordered a CBI investigation. The Tamil Nadu Government appealed against that order.
- BJP members protested that banners erected at more than fifty places in Tiruchi for Narendra Modi's participation in a party conference had been torn down.
- A ₹50-lakh land fraud was reported near Gummidipoondi, with complaints against eight persons.
- At Tambaram, a young woman was deceived and her chain snatched; a gang was caught smuggling red-sanders logs; and three persons were arrested in connection with gold worth ₹54 lakh.
- A fraud of ₹33 lakh was reported in which employment was promised by invoking the name of a Union Minister.
- A rowdy known as Bun Sekar in Thoraipakkam habitually drank, harassed women passing through the area and provoked quarrels. When he attacked a man named Selvam, Selvam complained to the police. Officers brought Bun Sekar to the station for questioning. His relative, AIADMK South Chennai South district secretary Virugai Ravi, then arrived with supporters, argued at the police station and took Bun Sekar away. The Chief Minister claimed that the police had been given complete freedom—this was apparently what that freedom looked like.
- The High Court adjourned the hearing of a petition seeking murder charges against ministers over the death of prisoner Sukumar in Salem jail.
- A bomb was thrown at a house near Katpadi, and three persons went into hiding.
- Shops were closed in Cuddalore after a clash over torn banners.
- The DMDK MLA from Tirupparankundram was arrested, while his wife went into hiding.
- A woman named Aarthi attempted self-immolation at Ponneri police station.
- In Ernavoor, a woman named Nadiya was strangled to death.

The newspapers also carried the English headline:

> Students protest for promised stipend - Research scholars at Madras varsity are yet to get funds, 2 years after scheme was launched!

These were merely headlines gathered from a few newspapers on a single day. Every day presents the same picture. Perhaps believing that no one in the national capital would know any of this, the Chief Minister declared in Delhi that law and order was properly maintained and Tamil Nadu remained a park of peace.

Her speech further claimed that special attention was being given to minority welfare through the Tamil Nadu State Minorities Commission, the Tamil Nadu Minorities Economic Development Corporation and the Directorate of Minorities Welfare.

The Tamil Nadu State Minorities Commission she cited was first established on 13 February 1989 under a DMK Government. The Tamil Nadu Minorities Economic Development Corporation was likewise created on 1 July 1999 under DMK rule. When the AIADMK subsequently came to office, it merged that Corporation with the economic development corporation for backward classes and reduced its importance. When the DMK Government returned in 2006, it re-established the Minorities Economic Development Corporation as a separate body, through which loans were extended to minority entrepreneurs to start small and micro enterprises.

The Chief Minister also spoke proudly of the Directorate of Minorities Welfare. A separate directorate for backward-class welfare was first established in 1969 under the DMK Government. It was the DMK Government that appointed a separate minister for backward-class welfare and introduced a separate demand for grants in 1971. In the same way, recognising that the social, economic and educational advancement of minorities required the Government's special attention, the DMK Government announced in the 2007–2008 Budget that a separate directorate would be formed for their welfare. An order permitting the establishment of the Directorate of Minorities Welfare was issued on 6 April 2007. By concealing all this and claiming credit for special attention to minority welfare, the Chief Minister's speech revealed how far it departed from the truth.

Everyone in Tamil Nadu knows how contrary to reality is Jayalalithaa's claim that the State is a park of peace. Fear makes many hesitate to speak openly. Newspapers that should perform a democratic duty are themselves terrified. Imagine a “park” where plants and creepers have fallen and dried, every tree has withered, the fence has broken, murderers, hired gangs and thieves have settled in, and snakes, centipedes and scorpions crawl everywhere. If that is called a park of peace, have we lost consciousness so completely that we should believe it? They say ghee drips from finger millet—listen, Tamils!

**With affection,**
**M.K.**
26 September 2013
