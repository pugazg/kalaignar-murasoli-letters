# 3768. It Is Among People Like These That....

It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. **Udanpirappē,**

“Minister Vaigaichelvan blunders again and again! Major confusion at the function to honour high-achieving students.” This was the front-page headline in a newspaper. It was not a report published in dailies such as *Murasoli* or *Dinakaran*. It appeared on the front page of the 20 June 2013 edition of *Dinamalar*.

For many years, the Tamil Nadu Government has given cash prizes to the students who study Tamil as their first subject and secure the first three places at State level in the Plus Two and Class 10 examinations. It has also borne the cost of their higher education. The function is held every year in the Chief Minister's chamber. The eligible students are brought to the Secretariat. In the Chief Minister's chamber, they and their parents take photographs with the Chief Minister and receive the prizes and certificates. It was about this year's function that *Dinamalar* carried the report and photograph under the headline quoted above.

Students eligible for the awards were brought with their parents from across the State after being told that Chief Minister Jayalalithaa would personally present the prizes and certificates. Education Department officials, acting through the Chief Educational Officers, informed the parents that the Chief Minister would present prizes to 211 students from the two examinations together, and that everyone should report at the DPI campus in Chennai by eight in the morning on the 19th.

Accordingly, the students and their parents travelled to Chennai from distant places such as Tirunelveli, Coimbatore, Thoothukudi and Kanyakumari. They found places to stay somehow, prepared themselves, and arrived at the DPI campus by 7.30 in the morning. Officials told them that the Chief Minister's prize-giving function would take place at around ten. They later announced that the morning function had been cancelled and would instead be held in the evening. Were the prizes and certificates at least given to everyone that evening as announced? No.

At around 2.30 in the afternoon, Chief Minister Jayalalithaa presented prizes and certificates only to twenty-two students: thirteen who had secured the first three places in the Plus Two examination and nine who had secured first place in the Class 10 examination. Photographs were taken and prominently publicised in all the newspapers. But the fifty-two students who had secured second place and the 137 who had secured third place in the Class 10 examination were not given their prizes or certificates by the Chief Minister at the Secretariat, as initially announced.

Officials apparently told them that School Education Minister Vaigaichelvan would distribute their certificates at the DPI campus. Thus, the 189 students who had travelled from distant places to receive prizes and awards from the Chief Minister did not receive them from her at the Secretariat as promised. Was that all?

Those 189 students were seated in a hall at the DPI campus, while their parents were made to wait outside. Officials kept saying that the School Education Minister was about to arrive and would distribute the prizes and awards as soon as he came. The Minister had not arrived even by four in the evening. The parents who had waited until then became angry.

Many had come from distant places with their children and had no means of staying in Chennai. They had booked tickets to return at around six that evening and were left not knowing what to do. The officials, unable to answer the angry parents, apparently went into a room inside the hall, sat there and locked the door. More cruelly still, the students who had waited for many hours were not even given drinking water.

Some parents waited until 4.30 and, having no other choice, said that they wanted neither the prize nor the certificate and took their children away. Fearing that all the students would leave if there were any further delay, the authorities hurriedly distributed certificates through the Director of the Central Secondary Education Scheme and sent them away. The students had been unable to receive them from the Chief Minister; they had waited in the hope that they might at least receive them from the Minister. Denied even that, they returned in sadness, wondering why they had come to Chennai at all.

*Dinamalar* reported all these details fully and systematically. There is another absurdity. The Information and Public Relations Department of the Tamil Nadu Government prepared and distributed a press release about the function. That press release stated that the Chief Minister had presented prizes and certificates and commended the students who secured second and third places at State level in the Class 10 examination. Thus, it described an event that had never taken place as though it had occurred and distributed that claim to all newspapers.

*Dinamalar* itself reported this on page four under the headline, “Information Department Gave Incorrect Report on Chief Minister's Function.” When incorrect reports appear in privately run newspapers, no one accepts them. But if a press release issued on behalf of the AIADMK Government of Tamil Nadu itself supplies false information in this manner, what remedy can there be? Did the Government's Information Department truly prepare and issue an incorrect release? Or did *Dinamalar* publish an incorrect report? The Government must explain.

Perhaps because it published all these spectacles enacted under the AIADMK Government, *Dinamalar* alone has not carried the two full-page advertisements that appeared in every other newspaper today for two functions attended by the Chief Minister. Perhaps they were not given to that paper—who knows?

At the same time that these reports about the Education Department have appeared, another report has emerged about the Electricity Board. Under the headline “Electricity Board grievance centre not functioning properly—Minister Viswanathan asleep,” it gives extensive details and, with great concern for the Government, writes:

> “To prevent people from suffering because of the Electricity Board's negligent attitude, Electricity Minister Natham Viswanathan must take the necessary action. Otherwise, in the coming elections, this Minister alone will cause the AIADMK to lose a large number of votes and its influence will decline. The fact that the Minister has failed to take note of this must be brought to the notice of Chief Minister J.”

From this it is clear that newspapers such as *Dinamalar* are unable to conceal the Government's failings. At the same time, they are unable to bear the possibility that Chief Minister Jayalalithaa might acquire a bad name because of those failings. It is among people like these that we have to carry the movement forward.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**21 June 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. **DPI campus** refers to the Directorate of Public Instruction complex in Chennai, a long-established public name for the School Education administrative campus.
2. The four full stops in the Tamil title are intentionally reflected in the English title.

நடைபெற்றுள்ளது.

இந்தத் தகவலையெல்லாம் சாங்கோபாங்கமாக “தினமலர்”
நாளேடு விவரமாக எழுதியிருக்கின்றது. இதைவிட மற்றொரு
வேடிக்கை, இதுபற்றி தமிழக அரசின் செய்தித் துறை, ஏடுகளுக்கு
செய்திக் குறிப்பு தயாரித்து வழங்கியுள்ளது. அந்தச் செய்திக்
குறிப்பில், பத்தாம் வகுப்பில் மாநில அளவில் இரண்டாம் இடம்

நிகழ்ச்சியை நடைபெற்றதைப் போலச் செய்திக்குறிப்பு தயாரித்து
