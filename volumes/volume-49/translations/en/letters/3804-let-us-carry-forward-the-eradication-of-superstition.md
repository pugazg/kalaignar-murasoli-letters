# 3804. Let Us Carry Forward the Eradication of Superstition!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

The Maharashtra Government has recently promulgated an ordinance against superstition. It is directed against human sacrifice, black magic, sorcery and other cruel and inhuman practices. Under the law, those arrested for fraudulent acts cannot immediately obtain bail; upon conviction, they may receive seven years’ imprisonment. The Bill had been introduced three times in the Maharashtra Legislative Assembly and amended twenty-nine times, yet remained unpassed.

The law, blocked in the Maharashtra Legislature for thirteen years, was hurriedly brought into force on 22 August 2013. The reason was the atrocious murder of Narendra Dabholkar, the renowned rationalist and courageous fighter against superstition, on the streets of Pune on 20 August. The brutal event shocked rationalists throughout the country. While Dabholkar was on his morning walk, two unidentified men arrived on a motorcycle and shot him dead. News of his killing spread like fire across Maharashtra. Protest demonstrations and a State-wide shutdown followed.

Dabholkar, a medical graduate, was a repository of many abilities. A front-line fighter in the anti-superstition movement, he showed deep commitment to social reform. He believed that caste was the foundation of inequality in Indian society. Labouring to eradicate the caste system completely, he argued that equality was impossible without a cultural revolution.

“I need no weapon in the struggle to remove ignorance,” Dabholkar said. His friend Ajit Abhyankar observed: “Dabholkar was opposed neither to God nor to religion; he fought against exploitation carried out in the name of belief.” Reading this reminded me of the line I wrote in *Parasakthi*: “It is not because there should be no temple, but because the temple must not become a den of the wicked.”

Alongside his scientific campaigning, Dabholkar fought for oppressed people. To establish social justice and allow equality to flourish, he encouraged inter-caste marriages and personally helped conduct them. Throughout his life he fought hard against sorcerers and fraudulent godmen. His sustained struggles against superstition created enemies for him on many fronts in Maharashtra. For years he demanded a law against black magic, sorcery and superstition. His demand was fulfilled only after he was murdered.

Dravidar Kazhagam leader, my younger brother Veeramani, as well as professors, social activists and members of science movements, have demanded that Tamil Nadu enact and implement a law similar to Maharashtra’s. There have also been calls to create an atmosphere in schools and colleges where superstition can be discussed critically.

Article 51-A of the Constitution of India declares that developing scientific temper is a fundamental duty of every citizen:

> It shall be the duty of every citizen of India to develop the scientific temper, humanism and the spirit of inquiry and reform

Scientific temper means following logical methods, freeing oneself from likes and dislikes, and not forcing preconceived ideas into every matter. Serious consultation, extensive discussion and deep inquiry are among its essential features.

Black magic, sorcery, astrology and occult acts are based on superstition. They therefore stand against the framework of scientific temper and are contrary to the Constitution of India.

Many acts based on superstition continue in Tamil Nadu. It is cruel and painful that they persist despite so much campaigning and explanation—and that even educated people take part in them. Two recent events show how superstition manipulates society.

Tilakaraj of the Retteri area in Kolathur near Chennai operated a blood-testing centre. His wife, Myna alias Lakshmi, had trained in beauty work, and their daughter Sneha was also interested in cosmetology. Mother and daughter decided to open their own beauty parlour and rented the first floor of a building at Puthagaram near Puzhal.

At about 5.30 a.m. on 1 September 2013, an opening ceremony was held for the establishment, named “Lakshmi Beauty Parlour.” A priest named Ramamurthy from Nagappa Nagar, Chromepet, conducted a homam inside. He then applied sandal paste and kumkum to the digital signboard, garlanded it and performed a puja. When the rituals ended, Tilakaraj, Sneha, electrician Naveenchandran and priest Ramamurthy carried the board outside at the prescribed “auspicious time.”

Standing on the balcony, the four lifted the signboard to mount it. The priest instructed that it be positioned according to Vastu Shastra. Following his directions, they shifted it this way and that. When they finally turned it as he instructed, iron in the board rubbed against a high-voltage electric cable above. Electricity passed through it and struck all four, burning them to death at the spot. Every newspaper reported the tragedy.

Learning of this heart-rending event, DMK Treasurer Thalapathi M.K. Stalin visited the bereaved families on 2 September 2013, paid floral tribute before the victims’ portraits and offered consolation.

Another report appeared in a weekly. A priest called Divyananda, also known as Palanisamy, who had amassed large sums through black magic and incantations, built a Sivakaliamman temple beside the Kollapuri Amman temple at Irummathur, on the eleventh kilometre of the Dharmapuri–Tiruvannamalai road, in competition with it.

He decided to conduct the consecration on 8 September 2013 and printed and distributed invitations. Reports said that he extorted lakhs by claiming: “I can disable the hands and legs of enemies, kill them, perform black magic and occult acts.” It also came to light that he had encroached upon two acres of government land beside the Amman temple, made and installed a seventy-two-foot cement statue, and occupied public land for his black-magic business. Having deceived innocent people for personal gain through claims of black magic and occult power and encroached on government land, priest Palanisamy was arrested before the consecration, causing a sensation in the area.

Another report concerned human sacrifice.

In my *Murasoli* letter of 15 April 2013, titled “The Cruelty of Human Sacrifice in a Civilised Age?”, I described an incident in Vyasarpadi, Chennai. Believing a soothsayer’s claim that sorcery had been placed upon her, Maheswari of Vyasarpadi tied on an amulet he gave her. She then brought Geetha’s two-and-a-half-year-old child, Vishnu, to her house and tried to kill him by forcing him into a water tank. As the child struggled for breath, Maheswari carried him to a water barrel on the neighbouring terrace, placed him upside down in it and pretended that he had fallen accidentally. The child died after being taken to hospital. Witnesses said there were injuries on Vishnu’s face, hands and legs. People in the area suspected that Maheswari, believing the soothsayer, had killed the child as a human sacrifice to remove a supposed curse.

In that earlier letter I wrote:

> Reading this report of human sacrifice pierced my thoughts with the doubt: are we really living in the twenty-first century? This is an age in which science has advanced greatly. We have reached a stage at which nothing is beyond human knowledge. The evolution of living beings, complete study of the planets and the science called information technology have reduced the world to a small village; the world now rests in one’s palm. Even as we see and hear all this and marvel at the greatness of human intelligence, when reports speak of “sorcery—the soothsayer—the amulet—the curse—human sacrifice,” it seems that, even after science has risen to astonishing heights, a section of humanity has still not escaped the abyss called superstition.

The murder of Narendra Dabholkar teaches us that the campaign against superstition must be transformed into a people’s movement. In 1923, Thanthai Periyar advised: “To redeem ourselves—to live as people possessing self-respect—we must first abandon enslaving superstitions and blind customs.” Events in our country show that, even ninety years later, our society has not fully followed that counsel.

Campaigning for rationalism and scientific temper must be conducted more intensely and without interruption. As indicated by the Constitution, legal standards must be framed and properly implemented.

The Union and State Governments must enact and enforce a suitable law to eradicate superstition. Scientific temper must be included in school and college curricula. Because this has become an urgent need and a compulsion of our times, let every section of society unite and carry the demand forward with determination. That will be the heroic tribute we offer to the memory of Narendra Dabholkar.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**

10 September 2013

என்று அந்தக் கடிதத்தில் நான் குறிப்பிட்டிருந்தேன்.

நரேந்திர தபோல்கர் படுகொலை செய்யப்பட்டுள்ள சம்பவம்
மூடநம்பிக்கைகளுக்கு எதிரான பிரச்சாரத்தை மக்கள் இயக்கமாக
மாற்ற வேண்டும் என்று உணர்த்தியிருக்கிறது. “நாம் மீள வேண்டு
மானால் - சுயமரியாதை பெற்ற மக்களாக வாழ வேண்டுமென்று
நினைத்தால் - அடிமைப்படுத்தும் மூடநம்பிக்கைகளையும்,
குருட்டுத்தனமான பழக்க வழக்கங்களையும் முதலில் விட்டுவிட
வேண்டும்” என்று தந்தை பெரியார் அவர்கள் 1923ஆம் ஆண்டில்
எடுத்துச் சொன்ன அறிவுரை, 90 ஆண்டுகள் கழிந்ததற்குப் பிறகும்
இன்னமும் நமது சமூகத்தில் முழுமையாகப் பின்பற்றப்படவில்லை
என்பதை நாட்டில் நடைபெறும் நிகழ்வுகள் புலப்படுத்துகின்றன.

மேற்கொள்ளப்பட வேண்டும் என்பதும் ; இந்திய அரசியலமைப்புச்

மூடநம்பிக்கை ஒழிப்புக்கு உரிய சட்டம் ஒன்றை மத்திய
அரசும், மாநில அரசும் கொண்டுவந்து நிறைவேற்ற வேண்டும்;
அறிவியல் மனப்பான்மை பற்றிய பாடத்திட்டம் பள்ளி -
கல்லூரிப் படிப்பில் இணைக்கப்பட வேண்டும்; என்பது அவசரத்
