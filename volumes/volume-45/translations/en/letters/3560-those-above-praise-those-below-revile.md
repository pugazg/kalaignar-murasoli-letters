# 3560. Those Above Praise; Those Below Revile!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 5 May 2011

**Udanpirappē,**

From the day we assumed the responsibility of government on **13.5.2006**, throughout the past five years, we have treated **“My duty is to keep doing my work”** as our very breath. Every moment, thinking again and again about what work had to be done for the welfare of the people of Tamil Nadu and for the advancement of Tamil Nadu, we have laboured without regarding bodily strain, without looking to hunger and without letting the eyes sleep. We have fulfilled every promise given in the DMK's election manifesto during the 2006 general election. We have implemented not only the promises stated in the manifesto, but schemes that were not stated there as well. From the cancellation of **₹7,000 crore** in cooperative loans owed by farmers to the **Kalaignar Housing Scheme**, we have implemented innumerable schemes.

While we were concentrating our eyes and minds on great works for the people, certain dominant forces, with the evil purpose of tiring our minds and diverting our attention, have deliberately undervalued and distorted the excellence of our work and the quality of our achievements, publishing them in newspapers and other media in the name of criticism. One example is an article published two days ago in a Tamil daily—**Dinamalar, 1.5.2011**—under the title **“Tamil Nadu Is Going in Reverse Gear.”** The writer is described as the editor of an English newspaper.

Yet it is distressing that even two-thirds, or even one-third, of the qualities that should form the foundation of a newspaper editor—neutrality, social concern, and the principle **“Even if you cannot do good, at least refrain from doing harm”**—do not appear in the writer's thinking. Their aim is to write something in the name of criticism, under the “banner” of a neutral newspaper, confuse public opinion, and portray the greatness and reputation of the DMK government as damaged.

That article harshly criticises the DMK government's free schemes for the poor and ordinary people. Along with that, Tamil Nadu is pictured as a backward State.

It is under the DMK government—and only in Tamil Nadu—that **one kilogram of rice is supplied for one rupee**. Under the DMK government, protective schemes needed by the middle classes, the poor and ordinary people and weaker sections have been implemented.

For **food security**: one kilogram of rice for one rupee; groceries at subsidised prices; ten cooking essentials for ₹50; and every month palm oil, toor dal, urad dal, rava, maida and wheat flour at low prices.

For **medical security**: the Kalaignar Insurance Scheme for Life-Saving Advanced Treatment; the **108 ambulance scheme**, which comes when called and enables emergency treatment; the Varumun Kappom Scheme; and the heart-surgery scheme.

For **housing security**: the Union Government's Indira Gandhi Memorial Housing Scheme; under the Kalaignar Housing Scheme, intended to create villages without huts, **21 lakh concrete houses in all villages over six years**; **2.5 lakh concrete houses in Town Panchayat areas over five years**; houses and plots on **1,100 acres** belonging to the Housing Board at prices within the purchasing power of lower- and middle-income groups; and safe houses for all poor families in urban slum areas. All these are being implemented.

Including the Agricultural Labourers Welfare Board, **33 unorganised-workers welfare boards** were created, and **₹957 crore 48 lakh** in assistance was given to **20 lakh 3 thousand** unorganised-worker families. Free colour television sets were given to **1 crore 58 lakh 82 thousand families**. Free gas stoves with gas connections were given to **28 lakh 34 thousand families**. **73 lakh children and students** received eggs five days a week, or bananas, along with the nutritious meal. Numerous concessions were given in school and college education. Renovation works costing **₹387 crore** were carried out in **4,020 temples**, followed by consecration ceremonies.

Under the Moovalur Ramamirtham Ammaiyar Marriage Financial Assistance Scheme, **4 lakh 53 thousand poor women** received **₹936 crore 60 lakh**. Under the scheme giving **₹6,000 each** to poor pregnant women, **20 lakh 75 thousand poor women** received **₹1,052 crore 83 lakh**. Under the Kalaignar Insurance Scheme, **2 lakh 56 thousand poor people** received life-saving surgery at a cost of **₹667 crore**. **22 lakh 27 thousand** destitute elderly people, widows and persons with disabilities received assistance of **₹500 a month**. Women's self-help groups received **₹281 crore 88 lakh** in revolving funds. **5 lakh 5 thousand young people** were provided new employment in government offices.

Further, with the support of service-minded organisations, Rajya Sabha member poet Kanimozhi, working with the ministers of the respective districts, District Collectors and industrial establishments, organised employment camps through which **1,33,998 people** obtained jobs. In industry, **28 memoranda of understanding**, involving investment of **₹60,055 crore** and providing employment to **2 lakh 33 thousand people**, were signed. The Anaithu Grama Anna Marumalarchi Thittam was implemented in **12,618 village panchayats** at a cost of **₹2,549 crore**.

A **Metro Rail Project** in Chennai was taken up at an estimate of **₹14,600 crore**. A **new Legislative Assembly–Secretariat complex** was built at the Omandurar Government Estate in Chennai at a cost of **₹910 crore**. The **Anna Centenary Library** at Kotturpuram in Chennai was built at a cost of **₹181 crore**. The Hogenakkal combined water-supply scheme was estimated at **₹1,929 crore**; the Ramanathapuram combined water-supply scheme cost **₹630 crore**. Government employees and teachers were paid **₹11,093 crore** in arrears. Thus, during these five years we have implemented countless schemes that are sweet even to enumerate and have created achievements.

It is because of the free-electricity scheme for farmers that foodgrain production, which stood at **61.17 lakh tonnes in 2005-2006**, rose to **96.08 lakh tonnes in 2010-2011**.

As a result of the public-distribution scheme, the Kalaignar Insurance Scheme for Life-Saving Advanced Treatment, the Kalaignar Housing Scheme and the many other social-security schemes implemented by the DMK government for the poor and ordinary people—and because the effects of economic growth reached widely across many sections of society—per-capita income rose from **₹39,692 in 2005-2006** to **₹69,377 in 2009-2010**.

Do all these details show Tamil Nadu moving backward in “reverse gear”? Do they not show that it is advancing rapidly in social and economic terms? Those with a conscience will certainly understand this. So that everyone may understand how far Tamil Nadu has advanced on every front under the DMK government, I think it is appropriate to cite what qualified people have explained and what world-renowned institutions and publications have written.

Speaking at Coimbatore on **9.4.2011**, Prime Minister Dr Manmohan Singh praised Tamil Nadu in these words:

> “The various schemes being implemented by the Tamil Nadu government under Chief Minister Kalaignar with a long-term perspective have made Tamil Nadu a model State in many ways—particularly in rural development and agricultural reform. I recently saw a report prepared by some economists. In that report, they have declared Tamil Nadu the leading State in India.”

What Union Finance Minister **Pranab Mukherjee** explained about Tamil Nadu's finances is not only satisfying to all of us but also worth remembering. On **9.4.2011** in Chennai he explained the State's financial position as follows:

> “The Thirteenth Finance Commission, in its recently submitted report, has recommended that no State should borrow more than **3 to 3.5 per cent** of its Gross State Domestic Product in 2011-12. The Tamil Nadu government's level of borrowing is much lower than the recommended level.
>
> “Tamil Nadu is among the few States in India that are satisfactorily maintaining fiscal discipline and stability. As of **24 March 2011**, the Tamil Nadu government's cash balance was **₹13,537 crore**. The Tamil Nadu government has not taken an overdraft from the Reserve Bank of India even for a single day because there was no money in its account. Many States, however, have taken overdrafts.”

The leading news organisation **CNN-IBN**, since 2008, has evaluated all States through appropriate judges on the basis of growth, law and order, infrastructure and various other factors, selected the best States, and presented them with **“Diamond State Awards.”** In **2010**, Tamil Nadu received the special award for **best large State in India**, as well as Diamond State Awards in three categories—citizen security, drinking water and sanitation, and women's development.

The well-known magazine **India Today** has for several years studied the development of large and small States at the national level and ranked them on performance. For its 2010 awards, States were studied and evaluated under eight headings—current development and financial position, law and order, health, education, consumer market, agriculture, infrastructure and investment. On that basis Tamil Nadu placed **third among 20 large States** in overall development, and the **India Today Award** was presented to Tamil Nadu by the Vice-President at a ceremony in New Delhi on **19.11.2010**.

Writing about the **Vazhnthu Kattuvom Project**, implemented by the Tamil Nadu government to eliminate poverty among the poor and reduce social disparities, former World Bank implementation head **Meena Munshi** praised it in a letter, saying:

> “The ‘Vazhnthu Kattuvom Project’ began functioning rapidly after the middle of 2006 and achieved very great success. Because the Tamil Nadu government continuously monitored the scheme and periodically gave appropriate guidance to project personnel, it gained a very strong reception in the field and achieved major success at both the national and international levels.”

In a judgment following an inquiry into allegations concerning implementation of the Union Government's Mahatma Gandhi rural employment-guarantee scheme in various States, Chief Justice of India **K.G. Balakrishnan** and Justices **Deepak Verma** and **P.S. Savyaan** praised Tamil Nadu and Meghalaya as two States implementing the scheme with greater concern, and stated that other States should follow the guidelines of these two States and implement it efficiently.

The **Wall Street Journal**, in its issue dated **8 July 2010**, strongly praised the functioning of the DMK government. It said:

> “Tamil Nadu performs better than other Indian States in providing the land, roads and other basic infrastructure facilities required by automobile-manufacturing factories. For starting and expanding industries, it provides more than twelve government-department approvals at a single place through a single-window system.”

A multinational private consultancy called **“Oxford Analytica (ஆக்ஸ்போர்ட் அனலிட்டிகா)”** conducted a study called **“India Deconstructed.”** According to that study, **“Tamil Nadu stands first in India in attracting foreign investment,”** and the reason was said to be Tamil Nadu's quality infrastructure and governance. Analysing India's **28 States and 2 Union Territories**, it declared **“Tamil Nadu is the best.”**

Even as recently as **3.5.2011**, the World Bank praised the effective implementation of the maternity-welfare scheme in Tamil Nadu; **The Hindu** reported it under the English headline **“Maternity Health Scheme commended.”** The World Bank praised the Tamil Nadu government's effective implementation of the maternity-welfare scheme and the substantial reduction in infant mortality.

After seeing all these assessments, I hope those who continue casually to criticise and belittle the DMK government's performance, welfare works and achievements will correct their approach and reduce their anger and indifference. Whoever praises us or lauds us; whoever throws dust and abuses us or simply ignores us; our journey toward our ideals, our work for Tamil Nadu, the Tamil language and the Tamil people, and our unceasing labour will continue forever just as they do today!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**5-5-2011**

ஏதுவாக, மாநிலங்களைத் தரப்படுத்த ஒவ்வொரு மாநிலத்திலும்,
தற்போது உள்ள வளர்ச்சி மற்றும் நிதிநிலை, சட்டம்-ஒழுங்கு,
சுகாதாரம், கல்வி, நுகர்வோர் சந்தை, விவசாயம், உள்கட்டமைப்பு,
முதலீடு ஆகிய எட்டு தலைப்புகளின்கீழ் பல்வேறு பொருள்கள்
குறித்து ஆய்வுகளும், மதிப்பீடுகளும் செய்யப்பட்டு, அவற்றின்
அடிப்படையில், 20 பெரிய மாநிலங்களின் ஒட்டு மொத்த வளர்ச்சி
அடிப்படையிலான வரிசைப் பட்டியலில், தமிழ்நாடு மூன்றாம்
இடம் பெற்றுள்ளதை ஒட்டி, 19.11.2010 அன்று புதுதில்லியில்
நடந்த விழாவில் குடியரசுத் துணைத் தலைவர் அவர்களால்
