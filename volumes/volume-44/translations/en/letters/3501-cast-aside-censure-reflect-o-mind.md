# 3501. Cast Aside Censure; Reflect, O Mind!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation follows the audited canonical Tamil and the locked Volume 44 conventions. It preserves the source’s hut-housing figures, quotations, political contrasts, Siruthavur/TANSI references, ration-shop report and `நிந்தனை / சிந்தனை` closing wordplay without outside reconciliation. **Date:** 20 September 2010

**Udanpirappē,**

In *Janasakthi*, the daily newspaper of the Communist Party of India, that party’s Tamil State Secretary, Comrade Tha. Pandian, wrote a long article on 18-9-2010 under the title “Let a Tamil Nadu Without Huts Blossom...” In the first part of that article he said, “When it was announced that we would create a Tamil Nadu without huts, we welcome the announcement in principle.” But it seems that the memory of today’s alliance immediately returned! He then asked question after question: How many huts are there in Tamil Nadu in all? When did you count them? Is it enough to say that, according to some estimate, there are 21 lakh huts? Was a study or census conducted for this? Is it only a projected estimate?

Would a government make such a major announcement without taking account? Comrade Pandian has one shortcoming. I have told him this once myself: he does not read the newspapers properly. As early as 6-1-2010, when the Governor addressed the Assembly, it was stated:

> “Because poor and simple people throughout Tamil Nadu continue to live in roofed houses with mud walls, the grievous condition of having to face natural hardships still continues. On the basis of the previous population census and various studies, it has been learned that in the rural areas of our State alone the number of such houses is about 21 lakh.
>
> “If these huts in our State were to be converted into permanent houses only under the Indira Housing Scheme now being implemented throughout the country with financial assistance from the Union Government, it would take another thirty years. Keeping this situation in mind, as a first stage, this government has devised a great scheme to bring light into the lives of the poor and simple people living in 21 lakh houses in rural areas by converting all those houses into permanent homes with concrete roofs entirely with Tamil Nadu government funds and giving them free of cost. Beginning in 2010–2011, under this scheme, permanent houses will be constructed by this government over six years in place of the 21 lakh roofed houses with mud walls.”

This was published in all newspapers.

The announcement did not stop there. Follow-up action was taken, and when the launch function was held at Tiruchirappalli on 3-3-2010 with leaders of all parties invited, Communist Party of India Legislature Party leader friend Sivapunniyam and Communist Party of India (Marxist) Legislature Party leader Ms Balabharathi participated. They welcomed and praised the scheme, while also suggesting certain changes — particularly insisting that the ₹60,000 allocated per house was not enough and should be raised somewhat.

I did not disregard even those views. I announced that the amount per house would be raised from ₹60,000 to ₹75,000. Everyone knows this. After all that, is it right for Tha. Pandian yesterday to ask how many huts there are in Tamil Nadu and when they were counted? Is that an act of genuinely welcoming this scheme? At least a few friends of the Communist party who raise their voice for those living in hut houses should think about it.

Now, they say ₹3 lakh should be allocated per house! The size of the house should be increased! They shower cheap recommendations. At the ₹75,000 per house now announced by the government, the annual expenditure itself will be ₹2,250 crore. If it is raised to ₹3 lakh, ₹9,000 crore will be needed every year. In truth, the aim is that this scheme should not be completed and that the DMK government should not gain a good name from it! The people of Tamil Nadu themselves will understand that this is why Communist Party State Secretary Pandian makes such recommendations deliberately.

Going one step beyond Tha. Pandian, Comrade N. Varadarajan — who had been Tamil State Secretary of the Communist Party of India (Marxist), had maintained an alliance with the DMK and had often met and spoken with me — has said at a public meeting that “the DMK government in Tamil Nadu has become the fountainhead of corruption.” That has been published in their newspaper.

It was this same Varadarajan who met me at the Secretariat and submitted a written complaint that land reserved for Dalit people at Siruthavur had been occupied by Jayalalithaa and her friends. I immediately arranged an inquiry through Justice Sivasubramaniam. After he reported that land reserved for Dalit people had indeed been grabbed, is the DMK government that arranged the inquiry now the fountainhead of corruption, while Jayalalithaa, who is said to be responsible for that grabbing, is the abode of truth?

In the petition signed and submitted to me by Comrade N. Varadarajan on 26-7-2006, it was written: “Documents show that the place where the Siruthavur bungalow in which former Chief Minister Ms Jayalalithaa stayed is situated stands registered and under patta in the names of Ilavarasi and Sudhakaran, close relatives of her friend Sasikala.” Those were the words given in writing. Is that Jayalalithaa now appearing as a holy woman to the Communists while the DMK government has become the fountainhead of corruption?

Communist party friends once issued a statement saying that former Chief Minister Jayalalithaa was trying to cover up the betrayal committed by the AIADMK government against agricultural labourers. Is it proper for them now to describe the DMK government as the fountainhead of corruption?

They conduct demonstrations demanding pattas. Yet on 14-11-2008, when CPI(M) Comrade Nanmāran spoke in the Assembly, he said: “Although it was stated that the issue of pattas had ended with September 30 last year, we are happy that, on the basis of a request from our leader Comrade N. Varadarajan, it has again been extended for one year.” That is still in the Assembly proceedings!

Whether it was the debate on the Governor’s Address or on the Budget, did not both Communist parties welcome and praise the Tamil Nadu government’s schemes and actions? How did the DMK government suddenly become a fountainhead of corruption? What corruption have we committed? Did we buy land reserved for Dalits by paying a price for it? Or did anyone buy the government-owned TANSI land at a low price? Did we enact any ESMA or TESMA laws against government employees? Government employees praise this government; every section welcomes its work. What, then, is the reason for the Communists to oppose this government?

For the sake of the poor and simple people, the scheme providing one kilogram of rice for one rupee through ration shops is functioning very well. Groceries are being provided at low, concessional prices. But what is the situation in West Bengal, a State where the Communist Party of India (Marxist) is the ruling party?

Yesterday’s *Dinamalar* carried a report under the heading “Ration Shops in West Bengal in Private Hands — Shocking Information Released by the Supreme Court.” It said:

> “A recent study conducted there by a Supreme Court committee found that the majority of ration shops and Anganwadi centres in West Bengal are in private hands. In most States of the country, ration shops and Anganwadi centres functioning under the Public Distribution System operate under the control of State governments. But the Marxist party, which opposes privatisation, has handed the majority of ration shops in the West Bengal State it governs to private hands. The Supreme Court Food Commission conducted a detailed study there and released a report. It states:
>
> “Ninety-seven per cent of ration shops functioning under the Public Distribution System in West Bengal are in private hands. Likewise, 97.5 per cent of Anganwadis are with contractors. Three-fourths of these Anganwadis do not have their own buildings. Two-thirds of the Anganwadi centres do not have basic facilities such as drinking water and toilets for children.
>
> “Most poor families living below the poverty line have still not been given ration cards. But because ration shops are in private hands, numerous fake ration cards are being prepared and goods supplied at subsidised prices are being sold in the black market for huge profits. In schools, 43 per cent of middle-school students do not benefit under the midday-meal scheme. Apart from this, West Bengal is very backward in implementing social-development schemes.”

I do not say this in order to find fault with that government. I have only written what was reported in yesterday’s newspaper. The reason I was compelled even to write this is that G. Ramakrishnan, State Secretary of the Communist Party of India (Marxist), who spoke at the Madurai meeting, said: “The Tamil Nadu government cannot suppress any movement through repression. It is the West Bengal government that leads the struggle of oppressed people,” and that speech was carried in their paper. That is why I had to mention the condition of the West Bengal government!

May both Communist parties at least henceforth cast aside `நிந்தனை` — censure — and engage in `சிந்தனை` — reflection!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**20-09-2010**
