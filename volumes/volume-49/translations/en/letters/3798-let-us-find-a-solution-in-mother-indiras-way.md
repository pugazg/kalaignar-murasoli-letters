# 3798. Let Us Find a Solution in Mother Indira’s Way!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

Speaking in Parliament on 22 August 2013, Union Minister for External Affairs Salman Khurshid said:

> “One hundred and six Indian fishermen are being held in Sri Lankan prisons. The Sri Lankan Government must release them on humanitarian grounds. The imprisonment of our fishermen by Sri Lanka has become an emotional, social and political issue for the Indian people. Sri Lanka must therefore act to resolve it. Unless a solution is found, bitterness will inevitably arise in the efforts both countries are making to maintain peace. The Union Government attaches the highest importance to the welfare and safety of Indian fishermen. Attacks on Indian fishermen by the Sri Lankan Navy cannot be justified in any way. The Union Government is taking vigorous action to secure the release of Indians held in Sri Lankan prisons. Preparations are under way for elections in Sri Lanka’s Northern Province. Because demands are being made there for action against foreign fishermen, efforts to release the Indian fishermen have been delayed. The Union Government is consulting Sri Lanka about convening a meeting between the fishermen of the two countries. India has not detained a single Sri Lankan fisherman. That is not a sign of weakness; it is a sign of justice. India expects Sri Lanka to act with the same fairness. Sri Lanka is watching this issue being debated in our Parliament, and we expect its Government to understand the seriousness of the matter and act. When the Sri Lankan Foreign Minister recently visited Delhi, he too was urged to secure the fishermen’s early release.”

Those remarks offer us considerable reassurance.

The Minister further said that the Union Government was taking many measures to resolve the attacks on fishermen, but that the only real solution was for Indian and Sri Lankan fishermen to meet and speak directly. If the two sides held talks, he said, they could create a climate of understanding and reach a settlement. Sri Lanka was prepared to implement the proposal, and the Tamil Nadu Government should cooperate with the fishermen’s dialogue.

Reporting this, the English daily *Express* wrote:

> “Khurshid felt that for a long-term solution to the problem, there should be a third round of meeting between the fishermen associations of India and Sri Lanka. He appealed to the Tamil Nadu Government to give ‘final approval’ for the fishermen to travel to Sri Lanka for a ‘face-to-face’ meeting.”

Only a few days earlier, Union Minister Narayanasamy had also told journalists, in much the same manner, that the Tamil Nadu Government must cooperate.

Speaking to a *Dinamani* journalist yesterday, Minister Khurshid said:

> “We expect the Tamil Nadu Government to work together with the Union Government in this matter. A delegation of fishermen from Tamil Nadu wishes to visit Sri Lanka and return with the State Government’s support. I have been informed, however, that the Tamil Nadu Government has not yet granted the required permission. Chief Minister Jayalalithaa should give the matter her personal attention and cooperate so that Tamil Nadu fishermen may travel to Sri Lanka and return. The moment the Tamil Nadu Government gives its approval, the Indian High Commission in Sri Lanka will arrange talks between Tamil Nadu and Sri Lankan fishermen.”

The Tamil Nadu Government must therefore clarify whether it is truly delaying conciliatory talks on this important issue. Should the report be true, it must stop passing the blame to the Union Government merely to let time go by. At least now, it must avoid further delay and come forward to take immediate action to resolve the fishermen’s problem.

On the very day our Union Minister gave this reply in Parliament, Sri Lankan Fisheries Minister Rajitha Senaratne said in an interview in Colombo:

> “The Sri Lankan fishing community and the Indian fishing community have requested that each side be permitted to fish in the other’s waters for seventy days. I have said that this cannot be allowed. All countries accept the principle of international maritime zones, but it applies only to countries that have signed the relevant agreement. Neither the Tamil Nadu Government nor its fishermen can violate this rule. Permitting such an arrangement—that is, allowing Tamil Nadu fishermen to fish in Sri Lankan waters and Sri Lankan fishermen in Indian waters—would be an injustice to the fishermen of northern Sri Lanka.”

His statement only worsens the problem and diverts it in another direction.

Even this Minister may be considered mild compared with Sri Lankan Foreign Minister G.L. Peiris, who visited Delhi the previous week. This week’s *Ananda Vikatan* carried an entire editorial about his interview under the title “Who Gave Him This Audacity?”

The editorial said:

> “It is our Government’s duty to take action to prevent Indian fishermen from plundering our country’s resources. In the past, when Tamil Nadu fishermen entered Sri Lankan waters and were arrested, we released them immediately. That effort produced no result. Therefore, we have no intention of immediately releasing the more than one hundred Tamil Nadu fishermen now in Sri Lankan prisons. We shall produce them before the courts and obtain punishment for them.”

It pointed out that this was the interview given in the Indian capital by G.L. Peiris, Sri Lanka’s visiting Foreign Minister, arrogance resounding in every word.

At a time when arguments are growing that India should not attend the Commonwealth conference in Sri Lanka—on grounds ranging from genocide to the killing of “Indian” fishermen—the Sri Lankan Minister had come to deliver the invitation for that very conference. Yet he declared that other countries’ views about human-rights allegations against Sri Lanka had no connection with whether those countries participated in the Commonwealth conference, and included India in that pronouncement. *Ananda Vikatan* wrote in anguish: if a visiting minister from another country can sit near the Indian Prime Minister’s residence and tell India which conferences it should and should not attend, who gave him such courage? If the answer is the Congress Government at the Centre, it is a disgrace.

If *Ananda Vikatan* has been moved to write such a pained editorial, it is because the fishermen’s crisis has continued without end.

During the two years before the war between the Liberation Tigers and the Sinhala military ended in 2009, four hundred Indian fishermen were arrested by the Sri Lankan Navy. In the four years after the war, 1,200 fishermen were arrested and imprisoned in Sri Lanka. Since the ethnic conflict began in Sri Lanka in 1983, 362 fishermen have been shot dead by the Sri Lankan Navy and another ninety have disappeared. Since May 2009, nine fishermen have been shot dead and eleven have disappeared. The Sri Lankan Navy arrested one hundred fishermen in 2010, five hundred in 2011, three hundred in 2012 and 253 so far in 2013.

These figures alone reveal both the background to the Sri Lankan Minister’s arrogant interview and the change in Sri Lanka’s approach before and after the end of the war.

I wish to recall one example that allows us to compare the way the Union and Tamil Nadu Governments act for Tamil Nadu fishermen on India’s eastern coast with the way the Kerala and Union Governments act for Kerala fishermen on the western coast.

In February last year, two fishermen from Kerala were fishing in the open sea off Kerala when security guards aboard an Italian vessel shot and killed them without cause. The police registered a case and arrested two naval officers who had served as guards on the ship. Both the Union Government and the Kerala Government declared categorically that they would be proceeded against under Indian law. The Italian Government made repeated diplomatic efforts to free them from the case, but those efforts did not succeed. The issue created a dispute between Italy and the Union Government. Prime Minister Manmohan Singh issued Italy a stern warning, and Congress President Sonia Gandhi accused the Italian Government of betrayal.

Were the Union Government and the Tamil Nadu Government to show even one-hundredth of the concern displayed by the Union and Kerala Governments when the Italian marines killed two Kerala fishermen, many of the problems faced by Tamil Nadu fishermen would be avoided.

There is another event worth remembering. When Mrs Indira Gandhi was Prime Minister, she learned that a Sinhala captain repeatedly crossed into Tamil Nadu’s territorial waters and attacked Tamil Nadu fishermen. Angered, Prime Minister Indira Gandhi ordered our Navy to arrest him. Indian naval personnel captured the Sinhala captain and imprisoned him at Mandapam. The Sri Lankan Government took him and the other Sri Lankan personnel back only after making them apologise.

The Union and Tamil Nadu Governments should remember that episode. Only if firm action of the kind taken during Indira Gandhi’s period is adopted now will the Sri Lankan Government fall into line, Sri Lankan ministers stop speaking as they please and Tamil Nadu fishermen be protected.

It was with the objective of permanently resolving the problems that Tamil Nadu fishermen have faced for many years that the Conference for the Protection of the Right to Life of Eelam Tamils, organised by TESO in Chennai on 12 August 2012, adopted the following resolution:

> “While ensuring protection and relief for the Sri Lankan Tamils, it is our indispensable duty to protect Indian fishermen from the cruelties inflicted by the Sri Lankan Navy. Innocent and unarmed Tamil Nadu fishermen are mercilessly attacked, arrested and shot dead by the Sri Lankan Navy; their fishing boats are sunk and the fish they catch are seized. Tamil fishermen are treated inhumanely by Sri Lankan naval personnel.
>
> “Because Katchatheevu, which had been within India’s administrative boundaries, was handed over to the Sri Lankan Government, Sri Lankan naval personnel attack and harass fishermen whenever they enter that region.
>
> “To put an end to this cruelty, India must bring Katchatheevu back under its administration and establish an Indian naval base at Dhanushkodi or Mandapam Camp.”

Remembering that resolution, I once again insist that the Union and Tamil Nadu Governments immediately establish an Indian naval base at Dhanushkodi or Mandapam Camp to protect Indian fishermen from the cruelty of the Sinhala Navy, and that they follow Mother Indira Gandhi’s approach. This alone can provide a permanent solution to the fishermen’s problem.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**

26 August 2013

நடவடிக்கையைப் போலத் தீவிரமான நடவடிக்கை தற்போதும்
எடுக்கப்பட்டால் மட்டுமே, இலங்கை அரசு வழிக்கு வரும்,
இலங்கை அமைச்சர்களும் தங்கள் மனம் போனபடி பேசுவதை
நிறுத்திக்கொள்ள முன்வருவார்கள், தமிழக மீனவர்களும்
காப்பாற்றப்படுவார்கள்!
