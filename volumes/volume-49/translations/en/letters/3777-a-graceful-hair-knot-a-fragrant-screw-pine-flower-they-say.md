# 3777. A Graceful Hair-Knot, a Fragrant Screw-Pine Flower, They Say!!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

News has arrived that the issue of selling shares in Neyveli Lignite Corporation has ended satisfactorily and that all the workers have returned to work. When Thambi Shanmugam, President of our Labour Progressive Federation, told me the news by telephone that evening, I expressed my great happiness. Yet a few troubling questions continued to prick my mind.

Neyveli Lignite Corporation had been operating at an annual profit of about ₹1,500 crore. When the Union Cabinet moved on 21 June 2013 to sell five per cent of its shares, worth ₹466 crore, to the public, I issued a statement the very next day, 22 June.

Chief Minister Jayalalithaa, however, has said in her statement that as soon as reports appeared in some newspapers in May that the NLC shares might be sold to private parties, she wrote to the Prime Minister on 23 May 2013.

During the same period, writing in *Murasoli* on 26 May 2013 on behalf of the DMK, I generously pointed out that Jayalalithaa had written to the Prime Minister saying that she understood the Union Government was planning to sell a further five per cent of its holdings in Neyveli Lignite Corporation; that such an effort was undesirable; and that alternatives should be explored instead of selling the NLC shares. I have never possessed the narrow mentality of hiding what was written on behalf of the AIADMK Government.

A similar issue arose after the DMK returned to office in 2006. On 22 June 2006 the Union Government announced its decision to sell ten per cent of the Neyveli shares. The very next morning, 23 June, the DMK trade union—the Labour Progressive Federation—convened a consultation with CITU, AITUC, INTUC and the officers’ association. The meeting decided to begin an indefinite strike on 4 July 2006.

On 25 June 2006 I wrote to the Prime Minister:

> “The Union Government must not disregard the opposition that has arisen in Tamil Nadu to divesting ten per cent of the Neyveli shares. Whatever reason may be offered for departing from the already accepted Common Minimum Programme, it will be viewed as weakening the commitment to adhere to the policy initially agreed upon. I therefore request the Union Government to reconsider this decision.”

When no immediate reply came, the strike at Neyveli began on 4 July. Asked by journalists on 6 July 2006, I said:

> “If the Union Government is in a position where it cannot fulfil the workers’ demand in the Neyveli issue, we are considering whether the DMK should remain in that Government and accept responsibility for the decision it has taken. I have asked the DMK Ministers in Delhi to convey this to the Prime Minister.”

I immediately had Thambi Dayanidhi Maran, then a Union Minister, meet the Prime Minister and convey my position. That very night, the Union Government announced that its decision to sell ten per cent of the Neyveli shares had been put on hold. The workers then withdrew their strike.

In the lengthy statement she has now issued as Chief Minister, Jayalalithaa completely conceals all this and says: “The AIADMK held a massive protest condemning the Union Government’s decision. Thereafter, the Union Government abandoned it.” She congratulates herself as though the AIADMK alone caused the withdrawal in 2006. And now advertisements proclaim that in 2013 Jayalalithaa has achieved a “Himalayan victory” without precedent in history. One can only laugh on seeing them.

As soon as news came that the Union Cabinet had decided on 21 June 2013 to sell five per cent of NLC’s shares to private parties, the workers were deeply shocked and began preparations for protests expressing their strong condemnation and opposition. Pointing this out, I issued a statement on 22 June saying:

> “It appears that the Union Government will receive only ₹466 crore by selling its five per cent stake. This is not a large sum for the Union Government. It may say that the decision has been taken because the Securities and Exchange Board of India requires at least ten per cent of the shares of public-sector companies to be held by the public. But, as Chief Minister Jayalalithaa has already pointed out in her letter to the Union Government, the securities-contract rules can be amended to exempt Neyveli Lignite Corporation from this restriction, or NLC itself can be removed from the list of companies whose shares must be sold.
>
> “The Union Government must take that point into account. It must also remember that it previously attempted to sell NLC shares but postponed its decision after accepting my request. On behalf of the DMK, I strongly urge the Union Government to cancel the present Cabinet decision.”

It was on that same day, 22 June, that the Chief Minister too wrote to the Prime Minister. In that letter she asked only that the NLC shares not be sold to private parties. She did not ask that they be sold to Tamil Nadu Government public-sector institutions. Her earlier letter to the Prime Minister likewise contained only the request that the shares not be sold to private parties.

Has that request now been fulfilled? Has the Union Government refrained from selling the shares and withdrawn its decision, as it did under the DMK Government in 2006? Has it accepted Jayalalithaa’s demand and declared that the shares of this public-sector company will not be sold to anyone? No. Yet ruling-party newspapers and papers supporting them proclaim a “Himalayan victory” for Jayalalithaa. The ruling party’s own newspaper alone carried fifty-five pages of advertisements, while other papers too carried page after page.

At first Jayalalithaa had urged the Union Government not to sell the Neyveli shares. Then a journalist wrote to the Chief Minister suggesting that, instead of selling the five per cent stake to private parties, it could be sold to the State Government. Contrary to her letter of 22 June, she accepted that journalist’s idea as the Tamil Nadu Government’s position and wrote to the Prime Minister on 25 June saying that the State Government was ready to purchase the shares. Whether she took this decision after convening the Cabinet is a separate matter for debate.

After the NLC workers began their strike on 3 July, I wrote an urgent letter on 4 July to Sonia Gandhi, the Prime Minister and the Finance Minister. I reminded them that in 2006 the Union Government had announced the sale of NLC shares and then withdrawn the decision at the DMK Government’s request, and asked that it do the same now.

Our Chief Minister immediately wrote another letter to the Prime Minister on 7 July, asking that the shares be given to Tamil Nadu Government institutions. But on 8 July all the Neyveli trade unions met. Every union except the AIADMK union declared that the Union Government should not sell the shares even to the Tamil Nadu Government. Only the AIADMK union said it would abide by whatever decision the Chief Minister took.

On 12 July I again wrote to Prime Minister Manmohan Singh. I sent the letter through Thambi T. R. Baalu and had him explain the matter fully. Recalling the workers’ struggle, I asked first that the decision to sell five per cent of NLC’s shares to private parties be abandoned immediately and that a special exemption be considered. I added that only if there were absolutely no alternative, as a final measure, the Tamil Nadu Government institutions could purchase the shares and they could be counted as “public shareholding”, thereby bringing the issue to an end.

Only after this did SEBI agree to the sale of the Neyveli shares to Tamil Nadu Government public-sector institutions, and the Tamil Nadu Government accepted it. This is what is now being celebrated as “victory, victory”.

Had the Tamil Nadu Government remained firm in its original demand that the NLC shares should not be sold to anyone, the pressure of the workers’ indefinite struggle might have compelled the Union Government to grant an exemption and withdraw its decision, just as it did in 2006. Instead, the State Government has merely agreed with and assisted SEBI’s original decision that the shares must be sold. Is this truly a “Himalayan” victory? Does it justify so many advertisements in the AIADMK newspaper?

Meanwhile, it is said that leaders of all parties have praised Ammaiyar’s decision. Which parties? Not even all the parties allied with the AIADMK have praised it. Only one or two have done so. And for that, such clamour! Advertisements of praise costing many lakhs of rupees!

As the proverb says: **“What a graceful hair-knot, they say; what a fragrant screw-pine flower, they say—while lice and nits are said to live inside!”**

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**19 July 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. The title and final sentence invoke a Tamil proverb mocking impressive outward display that conceals an unpleasant reality. The screw-pine flower (`தாழம்பூ`) is traditionally worn in a coiffure for its fragrance.
2. **Labour Progressive Federation** renders `தொ.மு.ச.` (Thozhilalar Munnetra Sangam), the DMK-affiliated trade-union federation.

துணையாக இருக்கிறதே தவிர, வேறொன்றும் இல்லை. இது
உண்மையிலேயே “இமாலய” வெற்றியா? இதற்கு அ.தி.மு.க.வின்
நாளேட்டுக்கு இவ்வளவு விளம்பரங்களா?
இதற்கிடையே அம்மையாரின் முடிவுக்கு அனைத்துக் கட்சித்
தலைவர்களும் பாராட்டாம்! எந்தெந்த அரசியல் கட்சிகள் என்று
பார்த்தால்; அ.தி.மு.க.விடம் தோழமைக் கட்சிகளாக இருக்கின்ற
எல்லாக் கட்சிகளும் இதற்குப் பாராட்டுத் தெரிவிக்கவில்லை.
ஓரிரு கட்சிகள்தான் பாராட்டுத் தெரிவித்திருக்கின்றன.
அதற்குத்தான் இவ்வளவு கூப்பாடுகள்! பல லட்ச ரூபாய்க்குப்
பாராட்டு விளம்பரங்கள்! ஒய்யாரக் கொண்டையாம் தாழம்பூவாம்;
உள்ளே இருக்குமாம் ஈரும் பேனும்!
அன்புள்ள,
மு.க.
