# 3713. Are You Ready for the Siege Protest?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 4 March 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

Tomorrow is March 5—the siege protest we are to conduct on behalf of TESO in front of the Sri Lankan mission! Yes, news has come today that it is on this very day that a resolution against Sri Lanka is to be tabled at the U.N. Human Rights Council, and that the United States is working intensively to have that resolution adopted without a vote.

I know very well that you have read the detailed Udanpirappu letter I wrote on 27 February 2013 under the title **“March 5—the Day We Turn Our Tears into a Shield”** and that you have been engaged in preparations for it. Even before that, in my statement of 20 February 2013, I referred to the tragic killing of Balachandran, Prabhakaran's younger son, a child of only twelve, by Sinhala thugs. Yet in an exclusive interview to *The Hindu*, Sinhala President Rajapaksa has said that the Sinhala army did not kill Balachandran. Balachandran was killed in Sri Lanka. Without even considering who else could have killed him if the Sinhala army did not, Rajapaksa commits a brutal killing and now denies it, trying to appear virtuous before the international community! Why, Rajapaksa may even deny that Prabhakaran and thousands upon thousands of Eelam Tamils were killed, or that Tamil women were sexually violated.

It is precisely because Rajapaksa is capable of telling lies on this scale that video documentation of what happened is being released in ways that can prove the events to countries around the world. The Channel 4 organisation in London has no inherent need to make a documentary against Sri Lanka and show the world what it says is the truth. A documentary showing events in Sri Lanka has been screened for representatives at the U.N. human-rights forum. It has also been shown in Delhi to journalists and television organisations. Last year the United States, without hostility and taking what it presented as a neutral position, brought a resolution against Sri Lanka. But the wording of that resolution was then diluted. That is why this year too the United States is bringing a resolution at the U.N. human-rights forum.

The wish of every Tamil in Tamil Nadu is that India itself should move ahead of the United States and take the lead in a resolution condemning Sri Lanka. But it is being said that the U.S. resolution will be put to a vote on the 21st of this month. India's failure so far to announce its position surprises us here in Tamil Nadu. Rajapaksa is making every effort he can to defeat the U.S. resolution. But representatives of various countries who have watched the documentary on alleged war crimes in Sri Lanka are firm that Rajapaksa should receive appropriate punishment. The heartache of Tamils across the world is that, to this day, it has not been announced that our India is on that list of countries.

In an interview to reporters yesterday evening, our Union Minister Narayanasamy said:

> “The Union Government is watching the Sri Lankan issue with great concern. Full details of the resolution being brought by the United States are not known. Therefore the Union Government has not yet taken a decision. Once the complete details are known, the Union Government will take the right decision at the right time. The Government of India will follow this time too the position it adopted last time—support for the resolution.”

To learn the Government of India's position on the Sri Lankan issue, the DMK moved a special calling-attention motion in the Rajya Sabha. On 27 February 2013, members of all parties in the Council of States urged India to support the resolution being brought by the United States at the United Nations Human Rights meeting seeking an international investigation into the war crimes said to have occurred in Sri Lanka. Speaking particularly on behalf of the DMK, Thambi Tiruchi Siva said:

> “I speak in this House with tears swelling in my eyes and blood flowing through my heart. Countries around the world, hearts broken by genocide and human catastrophe, are joining together in search of a remedy. But our motherland alone stands apart. Tell us: do you want the friendship of Sri Lanka, or do you want the relationship of the people who live in the southern part of this very country?”

On this issue in the Rajya Sabha, Dr. V. Maitreyan spoke for the AIADMK, D. Raja for the Communist Party of India, Venkaiah Naidu for the BJP, and T.K. Rangarajan for the Communist Party of India (Marxist). Representatives of the Shiv Sena, Trinamool Congress, Telugu Desam, Samajwadi Party, Lok Janshakti Party, Janata Dal (United) and Rashtriya Janata Dal also spoke condemning the atrocities in Sri Lanka.

In the Lok Sabha, DMK Member of Parliament from Dharmapuri, advocate R. Thamaraiselvan, said that India itself should bring a separate resolution.

Replying to the debate on behalf of the government, Union External Affairs Minister Salman Khurshid said that **“a solution acceptable to everyone will be found regarding the demand that India vote for the human-rights resolution against Sri Lanka at the U.N. meeting.”** Yet his further remarks—that the Union Government cannot intervene in Sri Lanka's internal affairs and that India is under no obligation to follow a resolution merely because the United States is bringing one against Sri Lanka—cause deep anguish and shock. Saying that the minister's reply was unsatisfactory, members of the DMK, AIADMK and Left parties walked out of the House.

Dr. Nimalka Fernando, a human-rights activist and leader of the International Movement Against All Forms of Discrimination and Racism, has said that **“the resolution to be brought at the Geneva conference against human-rights violations and war crimes in Sri Lanka must be very strong.”**

For several years the Government of India has been providing financial assistance for the resettlement of Tamils affected in Sri Lanka. This year India provided Sri Lanka ₹500 crore; the source also states that ₹290 crore was given to Sri Lanka as a grant. In the preceding year ₹180 crore was provided. This assistance is given in the belief that it will be spent for the welfare of Tamils in Sri Lanka. But it is said that Sri Lanka does not use this money for Tamils and instead diverts it so that Sinhalese people benefit. It is also said that, rather than settling affected Tamils in houses built with this assistance, occupying Sinhalese settlers are being housed in them.

It is to give voice to all this anguish, and to make the world recognise Sinhala-racist President Rajapaksa as an international war criminal, that the TESO consultative meeting held at Anna Arivalayam on 25 February 2013 decided and announced a peaceful siege protest at the Sri Lankan mission in Chennai tomorrow, March 5, and a democratic protest in front of Parliament in Delhi on the same day.

Taking part in this siege protest means making the world know that **we are here for our Tamil people who are still dying in Sri Lanka without anyone to turn to.** Are you ready for it? Rajapaksa still stands upright and gives interviews saying he is not a war criminal. This siege protest is being held to prove to him that **“the Tamil people have not been completely destroyed.”**

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**4 March 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. **TESO** is retained as the acronym for the Tamil Eelam Supporters Organisation.
2. Statements about war crimes, the documentary evidence, resettlement funds and their use remain attributed to Kalaignar, named speakers or the reports he cites; the archival translation does not independently establish those claims.
3. The Tamil source's sentence containing the **₹500 crore** and **₹290 crore** figures is syntactically irregular. Both printed figures are preserved without silently reconciling their relationship.
4. `முற்றுகைப் போர்` is rendered **siege protest** rather than literally “siege war”, reflecting the surrounding description of a political protest.
