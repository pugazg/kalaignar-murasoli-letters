# 3491. That Shout That Echoes Back!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation follows the audited canonical Tamil and the locked Volume 44 conventions. It preserves the source’s employment chronology, dismissals, reported deaths, court quotation, TASMAC wage/benefit figures and closing political invective without outside reconciliation. The complete audited Tamil is reproduced below and remains authoritative.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3491](../../../chapters/3491-ethirolickira-antha-koochal.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 80](../../../pages/page-080.md)–[PDF 87](../../../pages/page-087.md)  
**Date:** 11 August 2010

**Udanpirappē,**

It seems Leader of the Opposition Jaya has found no other subject on which to issue a statement today! She has issued one about the problems of **TASMAC** workers. Does Jayalalithaa have any qualification to issue statements about government employees? Does she dream that the people have forgotten the suffering Tamil Nadu government employees endured during her rule? One could write an entire large book titled **“Government Employees and Madam Jayalalithaa”!** Even so, I shall remind you of just a few things.

When the DMK government was in office in 1989, in order to provide employment to educated unemployed youth, it appointed **13,000 People’s Welfare Workers** on an honorarium of ₹750 a month. But the moment Jayalalithaa became Chief Minister in 1991, she dismissed all those People’s Welfare Workers and sent them home. When the DMK government returned in 1996, I once again provided employment to all the People’s Welfare Workers who had lost their posts and brought light back into their lives.

Likewise, in order to provide still more employment to young people, I gave **10,000 youth** jobs as road workers. When Jayalalithaa again became Chief Minister in 2001, with her “compassionate heart” she dismissed and sent home both the 13,000 People’s Welfare Workers and the 10,000 road workers who had been appointed under DMK rule.

What would people who had worked for years, earned wages and supported their families do if they suddenly had no job? Many died by suicide. Many had marriages already arranged; the marriages were stopped and the women who were to marry them were left stranded.

For example, Veerappan, a People’s Welfare Worker from Kameswaram village, Vairavankattalai, near Keezhaiyur in Nagapattinam district, consumed poison and died by suicide on 11-6-2001. Because C. Kandasamy, a People’s Welfare Worker in the Oddanchatram union of Dindigul district, lost his job, his wife Prabha consumed poison and died by suicide on 11-6-2001. On 12-6-2001, Thilagar, who had been working as a People’s Welfare Worker in Natham union, unable to bear the anguish, took his own life. The DMK provided relief assistance of ₹25,000 each to these families. Ramamurthy of Aruppukkottai, after losing his job, died after remaining without food in grief. Ilangovan of Tharangambadi in Nagapattinam district consumed poison and died by suicide. Kabilan of Mandapam in Ramanathapuram district, after losing his job, died after going without food.

A government order was issued stating that a government employee who went on strike could be punished with **three years’ imprisonment and a ₹5,000 fine**. What name should be given to Jayalalithaa’s “decisive action”? Is it not intimidation? The same order also announced that no kind of leave could be taken on protest days, that the list of those absent from work must immediately be sent to the government, and that salary would not be paid for those days. Not only that: in 2002, under the Essential Services Maintenance Act (**ESMA**), leaders of striking government employees were arrested, and a new emergency law called the Tamil Nadu Essential Services Maintenance Act (**TESMA**) was enforced and employees were dismissed.

That very Madam is now shedding crocodile tears for TASMAC workers.

After seeing this government’s dismissal list, the list of those who lost their lives continued: Headmistress Renuka Periyarasan near Peravurani in Thanjavur district; Kumarasamy of Anthiyur; Assistant Headmistress Sarojiini of Kodumudi; Assistant Elementary Education Officer Alagammal; Sengott Raj of Denkanikottai; Directorate of Collegiate Education employee Solaiyappan; Pachaiyappan of Villivakkam; and Anjugam of Pullambadi.

As a further cruelty, after dismissing officials, without even waiting to see what judgment would come in the case, the government announced that it would newly recruit **15,500 people**, creating great confusion. If people are to be selected for government service, recruitment should be conducted through the Public Service Commission. Instead of following any proper procedure, they selected people on the recommendations of party members.

Petitions were filed in the Madras High Court seeking cancellation of the dismissal of government employees and release of those who had been arrested. In this case, on 6-7-2003, the judge delivered the following judgment:

> “I gave the government two days to arrive at a good decision in this matter. The government announced no decision. It also delayed the matter. The court only asked that everyone be released. But the government failed even to do that. The government employees themselves have come forward and said that they are withdrawing the agitation. I therefore accept that and issue this order. It is unfortunate that the government has taken no action. The government’s delay in this matter is deeply regrettable and painful. Dismissing government employees without even a fair inquiry is wrong and illegal. I release all government employees. Everyone arrested under Sections 4 and 5 of the ESMA must be released immediately. The Police D.G.P. must implement this immediately. I stay all orders suspending and dismissing government employees. Everyone must immediately return to work. No new persons should be appointed.”

After this judgment, when the government lawyer asked that the order be stayed for two days, the judge said: **“I cannot accept this. Just as people who had done nothing were awakened from sleep and arrested in the middle of the night, release them this very midnight.”**

Even after a High Court judge gave a judgment this strongly in support of government employees, the Jayalalithaa government refused to accept it. Overnight it went to the residence of the Chief Justice of the Madras High Court, appealed, and obtained an interim stay of the judgment that the High Court judge had delivered that evening. Jayalalithaa, who displayed such hostility toward government employees, now acts as though she loves them the moment an election approaches.

When government employees went on strike in July 2003, the government hurriedly selected **16,000 people** and put them to work in order to intimidate the strikers. Because they were newly appointed and had no experience, they were unable to perform the work. The striking employees pursued their case all the way to the Supreme Court and were later reinstated. The government did not know what to do with the 16,000 newly appointed employees, and they remained in government offices as unwanted guests, drawing salaries without work.

In June 2004, the AIADMK government suddenly issued an order removing all those employees. I issued a statement saying it was not right to ruin the lives of those who had extended a helping hand. Leaders of various opposition parties too issued statements condemning their removal from service. After that, the Jayalalithaa government yielded and withdrew the order dismissing them. That same Jayalalithaa now says in a statement that they are being made to suffer because they were appointed during her rule. Jayalalithaa herself tried to send home, once her purpose had been served, the very people recruited to assist her government. It is this Jayalalithaa who has now issued a statement for TASMAC workers.

Although TASMAC workers had been appointed in this way during AIADMK rule, after the DMK government assumed office in 2006 it did not send them home as Jayalalithaa had done; it continued their employment. At the time of appointment they had been taken only as part-time, contract, temporary employees on consolidated pay. Under AIADMK rule, supervisors were fixed at ₹2,000 a month and salespersons at ₹1,000 a month as consolidated pay.

When the DMK government assumed office in 2006, supervisors were receiving ₹3,000, salespersons ₹2,000 and bar assistants ₹1,500 as monthly pay.

After this government came to office, these employees were given **two pay increases**. In July 2007, supervisors’ pay was raised from ₹3,000 to ₹3,500, salespersons’ pay from ₹2,000 to ₹2,400, and bar assistants’ pay from ₹1,500 to ₹1,800.

From August 2009 the pay was again raised: supervisors have been receiving ₹4,000, salespersons ₹2,800 and bar assistants ₹2,100 a month.

During the previous AIADMK rule, liquor-shop working hours were **16 hours**; after this government took office, they were reduced to **12 hours**. As for employees, they now work only **eight hours a day in shifts**. Weekly leave is provided on a rotation basis.

Besides the five public holidays a year that had already existed under AIADMK rule, **May Day** was declared an additional holiday this year, and an order was issued granting double pay to those who work on that day.

Under AIADMK rule, no interest was paid on employees’ security deposits. It was under DMK rule that an order was issued providing **3.5 per cent annual interest from 1-7-2007**. Through this, supervisors receive ₹1,750 a year, salespersons ₹525 a year and bar assistants ₹350 a year. So far, **₹7 crore 57 lakh** has been paid to all employees for this purpose.

Under the earlier incentive scheme in force during AIADMK rule, only specified employees benefited. In 2005–06 only **₹26 crore** was provided as incentive. After this government assumed office, the incentive scheme for retail-sales employees was revised so that all employees could benefit, and it has remained in operation. Under the new scheme, **₹33.33 crore** was paid as incentive in 2009–10.

During the previous government, in 2005–06, TASMAC employees received only **10 per cent bonus and ex-gratia, amounting to ₹8 crore**. Under this government, for the past three years the bonus and ex-gratia have been raised to **20 per cent**. Through this, **₹18 crore 64 lakh** was paid to employees in 2009–10.

Under the previous government, when an employee died, **₹1 lakh** was provided from the Family Welfare Fund. Under this government, from July 2007 the amount has been raised to **₹1.5 lakh**. Further, it was under DMK rule that, for the first time from 1-11-2009, supervisors have been provided a monthly travel allowance ranging from ₹150 to ₹250.

Udanpirappē, without looking at which government originally appointed TASMAC workers, the DMK government has provided as many concessions as it could accept from their demands. In this situation, only some among those workers speak of agitation, speak of strike, and try to intimidate the government. News keeps coming that within their own ranks the majority have begun weighing the past against the present and thinking about it.

What can one do? There will always be a few whose occupation is to seize upon any propaganda — especially any voice raised against the DMK government — join that voice and attack our government. Those few stand before the broad, long wall called **“Support for Amma”** and raise a loud chant. But the country continues to hear and laugh as that shout strikes the wall and echoes back:

**“Witch! Schemer! Chandāli!”**

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**11-8-2010**

வழங்கவும் ஆணை பிறப்பிக்கப்பட்டுள்ளது.

ஊழியர்களின் காப்புத் தொகைக்கு அ.தி.மு.க. ஆட்சியில்
வட்டி ஏதும் வழங்கப்பட வில்லை. கழக ஆட்சியில்தான்
1-7-2007 முதல், ஆண்டு ஒன்றுக்கு 3.5 சதவிகிதம் வட்டி
வழங்க ஆணை பிறப்பிக்கப்பட்டுள்ளது. இதன் மூலம்
மேற்பார்வையாளர்களுக்கு ஆண்டு ஒன்றுக்கு 1,750 ரூபாயும்,
விற்பனையாளர்களுக்கு ஆண்டு ஒன்றுக்கு 525 ரூபாயும், மற்றும்
மதுக்கூட உதவியாளர்களுக்கு ஆண்டு ஒன்றுக்கு 350 ரூபாயும்
வழங்கப்படுகிறது. இதுவரை மொத்தப் பணியாளர்களுக்கும்
இதற்காக 7 கோடியே 57 லட்ச ரூபாய் வழங்கப்பட்டுள்ளது.

இருந்த ஊக்கத் தொகை திட்டம் இந்த அரசு பதவியேற்றபின்
அனைத்துப் பணியாளர்களும் பயன்பெறும் வகையில் திருத்தி
அமைக்கப்பட்டு, நடைமுறையில் இருந்து வருகிறது. இந்த
ஆட்சியில் 2009-10ஆம் ஆண்டில் புதிய திட்டத்தின்படி ஊக்கத்
தொகையாக ரூபாய் 33.33 கோடி வழங்கப்பட்டுள்ளது.

டாஸ்மாக் ஊழியர்களுக்கு கடந்த ஆட்சிக் காலத்தில் 2005-

ரூபாய் 8 கோடி மட்டுமே வழங்கப்பட்டது. இந்த ஆட்சியில் கடந்த
மூன்று ஆண்டுகளாக போனஸ் மற்றும் கருணைத் தொகை 20
சதவிகிதமாக உயர்த்தப்பட்டு வழங்கப்பட்டது. இதன் மூலம்
கடந்த 2009-10ஆம் ஆண்டில் 18 கோடியே 64 இலட்ச ரூபாய்
பணியாளர்களுக்கு வழங்கப்பட்டது.

கடந்த ஆட்சிக் காலத்தில் பணியாளர் இறக்க நேரிட்டால்
குடும்ப நல நிதியிலிருந்து ரூபாய் ஒரு இலட்சம் வழங்கப்பட்டு
வந்தது. இந்த ஆட்சிக் காலத்தில் ஜூலை 2007 முதல்
இந்தத் தொகை ரூபாய் ஒன்றரை இலட்சமாக உயர்த்தப்பட்டு
வழங்கப்பட்டு வருகிறது. மேலும், தி.மு.க. ஆட்சியில்தான்
முதல் முறையாக 1-11-2009 முதல் மேற்பார்வையாளர்களுக்கு
மாதாந்திர பயணப்படி ரூபாய் 150 முதல் ரூபாய் 250 வரை
அளிக்கப்படுகிறது.

எந்த ஆட்சியிலே பணியிலே நியமிக்கப்பட்டார்கள் என்றெல்லாம்
பார்க்காமல், எந்த அளவிற்கு அவர்களின் கோரிக்கைகளை

பணியாளர்களில் ஒருசிலர் மட்டும் போராட்டம் என்கிறார்கள்,
