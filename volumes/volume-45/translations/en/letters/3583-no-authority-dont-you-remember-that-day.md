# 3583. No Authority? Don't You Remember That Day?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

When Tamils everywhere argued that the death sentences of Perarivalan, Santhan and Murugan should be cancelled and asked that the Legislative Assembly too pass a resolution, Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Jayalalithaa stubbornly refused. Finally, after seeing the uprising of Tamils, she passed such a resolution in the Assembly. While doing so, Jayalalithaa said in her speech that under Article 161 of the Constitution the State Chief Minister had no authority in this matter and that the State Governor too had no authority.

But *The Indian Express*, reporting what senior advocate Colin Gonsalves—who argued in the High Court alongside Ram Jethmalani for the death-row prisoners—had said, carried the following:

> “The Chief Minister has the power to reduce Capital Punishment to life in Jail, and it is the right time to convene a Meeting and take a Cabinet Decision; ....... Jayalalithaa can exercise her power to stay the death sentence under Article 161 of the Constitution .... said one of the three advocates, Colin Gonsalves, who appeared before the High Court for the convicts.”

(The Tamil rendering printed in the source says: “The Chief Minister has the authority to reduce a death sentence to life imprisonment; this is the right moment to convene the Cabinet and take a decision on it...... Jayalalithaa can exercise the authority under Article 161 of the Constitution to stay the death sentence.....,” said Colin Gonsalves, one of the three advocates who argued before the Madras High Court.)

On 29-8-2011, while reading a statement in the Legislative Assembly under Rule 110, Chief Minister Jayalalithaa referred to the Cabinet meeting held on 19-4-2000 when the DMK was in office. At that meeting it was decided that among those convicted in the Rajiv Gandhi assassination case, mercy could be shown to Nalini alone and her death sentence commuted to life imprisonment, while the mercy petitions of the others could be rejected. Without quite realising it, Jayalalithaa thereby acknowledged that it was the DMK Government that caused Nalini's death sentence to be reduced to life imprisonment.

The Supreme Court confirmed the death sentences of these four, including Nalini, on 11-5-1999. Their review petition before the same Supreme Court was dismissed on 8-10-1999. Mercy petitions submitted to the Governor on 17-10-1999 were rejected by him on 27-10-1999. In the case filed before the Madras High Court against that decision, the Court on 25-11-1999 set aside the Governor's order and directed him to obtain the Cabinet's advice and issue a fresh order. It was in that situation that the DMK Cabinet met on 19-4-2000 and took its decision.

In the same statement Jayalalithaa read, she also said: “The Tamil Nadu Government, by its letter dated 28-4-2000, forwarded the mercy petitions of these three persons to the Union Government.” It was because the DMK Government forwarded those mercy petitions to the Union Government on 28-4-2000 that the death sentences of the three were not carried out and they were able to remain alive in prison for these eleven years. Jayalalithaa herself has revealed that through her statement.

It is because the President's office did not decide on the petition sent after the Cabinet's decision for eleven years that the advocates have now cited that delay in the High Court, and the Court has stayed the punishment.

Jayalalithaa asks why, when Nalini's punishment was reduced, the DMK Government did not recommend reduction for the others. But do you know what Jayalalithaa herself said in a statement published in the 23-10-2008 issue of *Namadhu M.G.R.*?

> “For peace to prevail in Tamil Nadu, after I assumed office as Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu in 1991 I pressed the Union Government to ban the terrorist Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam organisation. It was only because of my great efforts that the LTTE was banned by the Union Government on 14-5-1992. In the Rajiv Gandhi assassination case, V. Prabhakaran has been declared a proclaimed offender by the designated court. Activities supporting such a banned organisation are now taking place again in Tamil Nadu. At the protest held in Rameswaram condemning the massacre of Sri Lankan Tamils, film directors Seeman and Ameer took part and expressed many objectionable, anti-national views opposed to Indian sovereignty. Karunanidhi has so far taken no action against those expressing such views against Indian sovereignty........ If a Government led by me were now in office in Tamil Nadu, I would certainly have arrested those who spoke in support of banned organisations. During my rule I took action under the POTA law against people who spoke in this manner and imprisoned them. Now, in the foolish courage that POTA has been repealed, some have begun openly speaking anti-national views. Even without POTA, action can be taken under the existing Unlawful Activities Prevention Act against those who speak against Indian sovereignty. But the Chief Minister of the minority DMK Government has no mind to act. Whenever Karunanidhi comes to power, speeches supporting the LTTE are openly made in Tamil Nadu. Supporting such a banned terrorist organisation is an anti-national act. There is no room for any differing view on this.”

It is the very Jayalalithaa who issued such a statement in 2008 who now says that when the DMK was in office, its Cabinet in 2000 failed to recommend reduction of the death sentences of Perarivalan, Santhan and Murugan—and says that this was wrong.

Though Jayalalithaa has passed a resolution in the Assembly, one is led to think that by saying she has no authority she intends merely to brush the issue aside. For it is not only what advocate Colin said; the statements of two people who were once sentenced to death and later saved make the position clear.

Comrade Thiagu, in particular, was one of the comrades of a Left movement who received a death sentence and was later saved. I will give what he said as it is in substance:

“It was a time when we were deeply involved in the work of a Left movement. The party to which we belonged had adopted a policy of eliminating enemies. In accordance with a party decision, Muthu Thangappa of the Orathanadu area was murdered in 1970. Comrades Rengasamy Lenin, Gurumoorthy and I were arrested and lodged in Tiruchi Central Prison. The Sessions Court tried our case and in 1972 sentenced all three of us to death. We appealed to the Madras High Court, but the High Court too confirmed our death sentences.

“Kalaignar was then Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu. We petitioned the Governor to reduce the death sentences confirmed by the High Court. The Governor sought the Tamil Nadu Government's view. Kalaignar convened his Cabinet, discussed my mercy petition, decided that the punishment could be reduced to life imprisonment and recommended that to the Governor. On that recommendation, the Governor cancelled our death sentences and reduced them to life imprisonment. Thus we, who had been about to kiss the noose, were saved.

“Mercy petitions may be sent any number of times to the President of the country and the Governor of the State. Even if mercy petitions are rejected by the President, the State Government can recommend to the President or the Governor that the punishment be reduced. Disputes over whether authority exists or does not exist under our Constitution have always occurred. That is what is happening now. We say the authority exists. Jayalalithaa says she has no authority. Since the implication is, ‘If I had the authority I would save them,’ why cannot Jayalalithaa at least appeal to the President, who does have authority, to save all three? The one who says she has no legal authority can at least do this.”

*Nakkheeran* published this statement of Comrade Thiagu.

Like Thiagu, the death sentence of Pennadam Pulavar Kaliyaperumal was also cancelled. He is no longer alive; he died in 2007. His younger son Chozhanambiyar told *Nakkheeran*:

“My father Kaliyaperumal was the State secretary of the Tamil Nadu Naxalbari Marxist-Leninist movement. He was an intense Tamil patriot. In a case concerning the murder of a police informer, my father Kaliyaperumal, my brother Valluven, I and seven members of my family were arrested. The trial was held in the Cuddalore Sessions Court. In 1971 Judge Singaravelu sentenced my father Kaliyaperumal and my elder brother Valluven to death. The other five of us received life imprisonment.

“My brother Valluven alone appealed to the Madras High Court, and the High Court itself reduced his punishment to life imprisonment. At that time Comrade Kalyanasundaram was a Member of Parliament. When he met President V.V. Giri and spoke about Kaliyaperumal, Giri expressed the view that signatures could be collected from the people and sent to him. Accordingly, we held a conference at Pennadam, collected one lakh signatures and sent them to the Tamil Nadu Governor in 1974.

“The Governor sent the petition to Kalaignar, who was then Chief Minister. Kalaignar discussed it in his Cabinet, said the State Government could use its authority to reduce Pulavar Kaliyaperumal's punishment, decided accordingly and recommended it to the Governor. Accepting the State Government's recommendation, the Governor cancelled my father's death sentence and reduced it to life imprisonment. Therefore, if a State Chief Minister sets his or her mind to it, anyone can be saved from the death sentence. The question now is whether the State Chief Minister has such a mind.”

It was with the statements of these two in mind that I wrote in my previous letter that, as one among those who had helped people to be released from death's grip, I was pressing the Union and State Governments.

Instead of clinging to what someone has told her—that she has no authority under Article 161 of the Constitution to save these three—and allowing time to pass, Jayalalithaa should at least now recognise the authority available to her and come forward to save the lives of Perarivalan, Santhan and Murugan, who are hanging between the gallows and a continuing life.

**With affection, M.K.**  
1-9-2011

## Source-specific note

Across the physical PDF 348→349 boundary, the printed Tamil reads `வாக்க` / `எதிரிகளை`, i.e. `வாக்க எதிரிகளை`. A direct scan re-check during bilingual alignment confirmed that printed form. The aligned English therefore renders the phrase conservatively as “eliminating enemies” and does not silently normalize the Tamil to `வர்க்க எதிரிகளை`. Canonical Tamil is unchanged.
