# 3475. Come, Striped Tiger, to Show That Truth Will Triumph!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This letter argues for Tamil to have a place both among India’s official languages and as a language of advocacy in the High Court. It sets out the DMK Government’s 2006–07 Assembly, Governor, High Court and Union Government correspondence, responds to criticism surrounding a Madurai lawyers’ hunger strike, recalls the anti-Hindi language struggle, and ends as a mobilisation for the World Classical Tamil Conference. The source-supplied English letter to the Chief Justice is preserved verbatim rather than retranslated or corrected.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3475](../../../chapters/3475-vaaimai-vellum-enbadhai-unartha-varippuliye-varuga.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 348](../../../pages/page-348.md)–[PDF 353](../../../pages/page-353.md)  
**Date:** 16 June 2010

**Udanpirappē,**

It is the firm policy of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam that Tamil too should be one of the official languages of India. We have passed resolutions on this many times in the party General Council and Executive Committee. Likewise, we have continuously argued that Tamil should also have a place as the language in which cases are argued in courts. Yesterday, Law Minister Thambi Duraimurugan issued a statement setting out the steps the DMK Government has taken for that purpose.

There is no need to remind Tamil Nadu of our attachment to Tamil — our commitment to its growth, its influence, its dignity and to securing the place that rightfully belongs to it.

But as news appears every day about the World Classical Tamil Conference beginning in Coimbatore on 23 June, and as review meetings and elaborate arrangements continue, some people who cannot bear to see it are struggling to seize upon any pretext and turn it into a news issue. For example, some lawyers in Madurai are said to be on hunger strike demanding the right to argue in Tamil in the High Court. AIADMK and MDMK representatives are said to have met those lawyers and expressed support. Wherever anyone raises a voice against the Government, these two parties set out at once to support them.

I want to make clear whether the DMK Government of Tamil Nadu has in fact made no effort on the demand raised by those lawyers.

On behalf of their Tamil forum, Madras High Court lawyers filed a writ petition in the High Court in September 2001. Dismissing that writ petition, the High Court observed that under Article 348(2) of the Constitution of India the Court could not intervene in that matter. But Jayalalithaa, who was then in power, did nothing further on the issue. Now, by contrast, the moment she heard that lawyers in Madurai were protesting over this question, she issued a statement encouraging and taking political advantage of that agitation, says the source.

Keeping the aspiration of Tamil Nadu’s lawyers and people in mind, and in order to fulfil it, I moved a resolution in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly under the DMK Government on 6-12-2006 to introduce Tamil, the State’s official language, in all proceedings of the Madras High Court, including judgments and orders. The resolution was passed unanimously.

After obtaining the recommendation of the Governor of Tamil Nadu and the in-principle approval of the Madras High Court, we sent the resolution to the Union Government. The Governor considered introducing Tamil in the Madras High Court to be legally proper. The Honourable Madras High Court too held the same view in principle. We asked the Union Government to obtain the approval of the President of India.

In the letter sent by the Union Government in response, it was stated that the Tamil Nadu Government’s proposals had been discussed with the Supreme Court and that the Chief Justice of India was of the view that introducing a regional language in all proceedings of a High Court, including judgments and orders, was not presently feasible.

Jayalalithaa, who invited people to come and submit petitions but, according to the source, received petitions from party members for only about an hour before hurriedly leaving for Kodanadu, has also lamented about the World Tamil Conference. In her statement she has described it as a self-serving conference for self-praise.

So this is supposedly a self-serving conference! But what name should be given to the “song of praise to Jayalalithaa” that was sung in the name of an invocation to Mother Tamil at the World Tamil Conference she conducted in Thanjavur in 1995? To those who held a conference with such a song, the Coimbatore conference will naturally look like a self-serving conference!

Unable to bear the news that Tamil enthusiasts and scholars from many countries are coming in a manner that may make this conference surpass earlier conferences, Jayalalithaa finds fault with it, the source argues. Even from Kodanadu, her daily statements continue to be filled with writing about me. Seeing leading figures from there joining the DMK, and seeing many thousands come in numbers too great even for Arivalayam to hold, she consoles herself by saying that people are going to come from the DMK to the AIADMK, while trying to prevent the remaining AIADMK members from coming here.

Jayalalithaa has said in her statement that the Madras High Court sent the Tamil Nadu Government a proposal asking it to sanction ₹32 crore for infrastructure needed to implement Tamil as a language of argument in the High Court, but that no response was received from the DMK Government.

When Honourable Chief Justice A.P. Shah of the Madras High Court wrote to me on this issue in a letter dated 29-11-2006, I replied within just five days, on 4-12-2006. It is appropriate to recall that reply now:

Dear Chief Justice,

I am indeed grateful to you and other Hon’ble Judges of the
Madras High Court for having accepted in principle, the Policy of the
Government to introduce Tamil in the Proceedings of the High Court.
I do understand that it is necessary for us to put in place the necessary
infrastructure in the form of additional translators, stenographers,

increasing the availability of Tamil Books in Law and enhancing
their availability in the Library and formation of a separate section in the
Library for the Tamil Law Books. The Government is fully committed in
ensuring that the above facilities are put in place as speedily as possible.

Again, in a letter dated 21.2.2007, the Registrar General of the High Court sent a proposal to create infrastructure at a cost of ₹22 crore — not ₹32 crore as stated in Jayalalithaa’s statement — and the Tamil Nadu Government wrote back asking the High Court to send estimates for constructing the building. Therefore, the source says, Jayalalithaa’s statement that no response at all was sent is, as usual, contrary to the facts presented in the letter.

From the letter I wrote, one can understand how seriously the Tamil Nadu Government was concerned with this issue.

Jayalalithaa need not certify the relationship between the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam and Tamil. The DMK is the movement that contains the warriors who sacrificed their lives for Tamil in 1965: Singa Thamizhan Chinnasamy of Keezhapazhuvur in Tiruchi district; Sivalingam of Virugambakkam, Chennai; Aranganathan; Veerappan of Ayyampalayam, Tiruchi; Muthu of Sathyamangalam; student Sarangapani of Mayiladuthurai; Shanmugam of Viralimalai; Muthu of Keeranur; Rajendran of Sivaganga; and Dandapani of Peelamedu, Coimbatore.

Even before 1965, in 1963, Arignar Anna conducted an agitation for Tamil, attempted to burn the Constitution and went to prison. During the 1965 agitation, I was arrested, taken in a police lorry from Chennai all the way to Tirunelveli and confined in solitary imprisonment at Palayamkottai. On the language question, the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam has never yielded to anyone. Among those who sacrificed for language, the source declares, no other movement has people who surpass the DMK.

The moment I heard that the Madurai lawyers were on hunger strike, I contacted Union Minister Thambi M.K. Alagiri, who was in Madurai, explained the matter and asked him to attend to it immediately. Lawyers’ association office-bearers A.K. Ramasamy and Vellaichamy met Thambi Alagiri and explained the situation. Following this, Union Minister Thambi Alagiri immediately telephoned Honourable Union Law Minister Veerappa Moily and spoke to him about the issue. The Union Minister assured him that he would attend to it quickly and take appropriate action. When Thambi Alagiri explained all these details to the representatives of the Madurai lawyers’ association, they too accepted the explanation and left.

From this one can understand the interest, responsibility and concern the Tamil Nadu Government has shown in securing a place for Tamil in the High Court. Yet Jayalalithaa and those who wave white yak-tail fans for her conceal the facts and, the source charges, seek to provoke unrest.

The dream of those who hide all this, incite a few people, magnify it and seek to cause harm to the World Classical Tamil Conference will certainly not be fulfilled.

Udanpirappē, do not worry about those who, on seeing the arrangements for the Coimbatore conference, pick up a grinding stone and strike themselves with it. Come to the conference! Give Tamil and Tamil rule strength that will never diminish! Come, striped tiger, to make the deceivers understand that truth alone will triumph!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**16-6-2010**

## Letter-specific notes

1. The source-supplied English beginning `Dear Chief Justice,` is reproduced verbatim, including capitalization, wording, punctuation and paragraph break; it is not silently edited.
2. The source distinguishes a ₹32-crore figure attributed to Jayalalithaa’s statement from the ₹22-crore proposal it says was actually sent; both figures are preserved.
3. `உலகத் தமிழ்ச் செம்மொழி மாநாடு` follows the locked **World Classical Tamil Conference** treatment. Article 348(2), the 6-12-2006 Assembly resolution and the 2006–07 correspondence remain in source order.
4. The closing `வரிப் புலியே வருக` is rendered **“Come, striped tiger”** to retain the mobilisation image rather than reducing it to a generic call to attend.
5. No canonical Tamil change was required.
