# 3575. From That Day to This Day....!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

There are many examples to show that no one can surpass Jayalalithaa in filing false cases. Whenever she comes to power, she immediately spares no one she dislikes. Whether there is a reason or not, she does not fail to set the police upon them, fabricate a false case and take action.

Take, for example, the Kanchi Sankaracharyas. They do not belong to any political party. Only a few days ago, they themselves told the court that what had been filed against them by the AIADMK government in 2004 was a “false case.” Even that case was transferred by the Supreme Court from the Chengalpattu court to a Puducherry court for trial. Do you know what Supreme Court Justices R.C. Lahoti and G.P. Mathur said on 26-10-2005 while ordering that transfer?

“We have discussed above many facets of the case which do show that the State machinery in Tamil Nadu is not only taking an undue interest but is going to any extent in securing the conviction of the accused by any means and to stifle even publication of any article or expression of dissent in media or press, interview by journalists or persons who have held high positions in public life and are wholly unconnected with the criminal case. xxx

Taking into consideration the entire facts and circumstances of the case and the material on record, we have no hesitation in holding that the petitioner and other co-accused of the case have a reasonable apprehension that they will not get justice in the State of Tamil Nadu xxxx”

The State Government in Tamil Nadu was not merely showing undue interest; it was prepared to go to any extent to secure punishment for the accused. The efforts made by the Tamil Nadu Government to intimidate newspapers and media organisations that published reports or interviews concerning the case were condemnable. The government machinery had placed various obstacles in the way of the accused and the lawyers appearing for them, creating a situation in which those lawyers could not discharge their duties with dignity. *(Dinamani, 27.10.2005)*

Jayalalithaa is the owner of such a certificate from the Supreme Court. I do not know whether people such as Mr Cho and Mr Gurumurthy, who support Jayalalithaa today, will deny what the Sankaracharyas have said—that false cases were filed against them during the AIADMK government—or deny the observations made by the Supreme Court when it transferred the case to the Puducherry court.

Some newspapers at the time reported the reason for Jayalalithaa taking such severe action against the Sankaracharyas: that there was hostility between her and the Sankaracharyas over the proposed takeover of a large hospital on the outskirts of Chennai, and that this was the reason for action of that magnitude.

If such retaliatory action could be taken even against the Sankaracharyas, who would politicians be before that lady? Not politicians alone. I think I should give a few more examples of the false cases filed during her earlier rule because of her tendency to take revenge.

The people of Tamil Nadu will not have forgotten the false cases claiming that Muthu, the caretaker of the MGR memorial on Arcot Road in Chennai, and the son-in-law of a person who had been a judge of the Madras High Court possessed ganja; that Jayalalithaa's foster son V.N. Sudhakaran possessed heroin; and that a woman named Serina from Madurai possessed ganja. All the newspapers, during Jayalalithaa's own rule, also reported why Serina was arrested and why she was kept in Tiruchirappalli prison.

Within a month of coming to power in 2001, who sent police into my bedroom at midnight on 29-6-2001, assaulted me, announced that I was under arrest and dragged me away without even telling me where I was being taken? Was it not Jayalalithaa herself? Why that vindictiveness? What crime had I committed? They said there had been wrongdoing in the construction of flyovers in Chennai. What happened to that case? Did they prove it? Could they prove it? Was it not a false case? Could they even file a charge-sheet against me? They dug into every flyover in Chennai looking for evidence. On what evidence was I arrested? What case should be filed against those who filed such a false case? What should be done to them for the crime of arresting me and subjecting me to such cruelty?

Did not Jayalalithaa's government similarly foist a false case on younger brother M.K. Stalin, who as Mayor of Chennai had carried out many noteworthy works, citing those same flyovers, and arrest him? On the very day I was arrested, younger brothers Murasoli Maran and T.R. Baalu, then Union Ministers, were assaulted and arrested. P.T.R. Palanivel Rajan, my son M.K. Alagiri and others who took part in a blockade in Madurai were arrested there and lodged in Madurai prison. Were all these lawful actions? Were they not reflections of a policy of revenge?

In its editorial of 1-7-2001, *The Hindu* condemned the manner in which I had been arrested as a disgraceful act of vengeance.

When the students of Queen Mary's College, Chennai, went on hunger strike against Jayalalithaa's announcement that their college would be demolished to build a new Secretariat, DMK members—our younger brothers M.K. Stalin, K. Ponmudy, E.V. Velu, J. Anbazhagan and Pugazhenthi—went to console them. The police themselves permitted them to enter the college. They went in, expressed their support to the students and returned home. Yet at midnight the police were sent to their houses and arrested them; a case was filed saying they had broken the college gate and entered, and they were imprisoned. Is any further example needed of Jayalalithaa's habit of filing false cases and taking revenge?

Because then Governor Channa Reddy granted permission in 1995 to prosecute Jayalalithaa in the TANSI corruption case, Jayalalithaa sought revenge against him: she filed cases against the Governor in the High Court and Supreme Court, and had a resolution passed in the Assembly asking the Union Government to recall Governor Channa Reddy immediately. This very Jayalalithaa even accused Governor Channa Reddy in the Assembly of behaving improperly with her. Did not everyone understand that this was an outright falsehood?

The very next day after Jayalalithaa assumed office in 2001, a case of attempted murder was registered on 11-5-2001 against younger brother Parithi Ilamvazhuthi, then a DMK MLA, alleging that he had attacked John Pandian. In truth, Parithi was the one attacked. *The Hindu* itself wrote on 11-5-2001: “Ilamvazhuthi escapes armed gang attack on election office.”

On 26-5-2001, the Jayalalithaa government arrested the brother of Arcot Veerasamy, who was then the DMK Treasurer. The next day, 27-5-2001, a false accusation was made against former MP Tiruporur Parasuraman; he too was arrested and imprisoned. It was said he had been arrested on a complaint by a contractor named Deivasigamani. A case was registered against Purasai Ranganathan, then an MLA. He surrendered in the Egmore Magistrate Court and was released on bail; immediately, on 30-5-2001, another case was registered alleging that he had threatened a theatre owner with a gun, and he was arrested and imprisoned. At the time Purasai Ranganathan belonged to the Tamil Maanila Congress Democratic Forum. When friend P. Chidambaram went to the prison to see him both as leader of that party and as a senior lawyer, he was sent back without even being permitted to meet him. Were these true cases framed according to law? Were they not all false cases?

Thamaraikani, the Srivilliputhur MLA who had long been in the AIADMK, announced that he intended to join the DMK because he disliked Jayalalithaa's conduct. A complaint was obtained against him from his own son and Thamaraikani was arrested. Even after a court granted him bail, the Jayalalithaa government refused to release him, saying he was being arrested in another case.

V. Ettiyappan, the DMK member who was president of the Neelankarai panchayat, vice-president Ravi and union committee members Venkataraman and S.K. Ravi were all arrested after false cases were registered on the basis of complaints given by AIADMK members.

Even the *Indian Express*, seeing Jayalalithaa's retaliatory actions, wrote an editorial on 30-5-2001 under the title “Vendetta Drama, Phase Two,” with the sub-heading, “When will Jaya realise that Tamil Nadu is bigger than Poes Garden?”

During the DMK government, on a complaint by an AIADMK member named Preethi Baskar alleging that members of the Al-Umma movement had been imprisoned, cases were registered by the AIADMK government against me, former DGP Sharma, former Home Secretary Shantha Sheela Nair and police officer K.V.S. Murthy. The very Mrs Shantha Sheela Nair whom the AIADMK government had named as an accused in that case has now been appointed Vice-Chairperson of the State Planning Commission by this government. Regarding that case, *Thuglak* dated 27-6-2001 wrote:

> “There is room for suspicion that this issue is being handled in this manner because of an intention somehow to take revenge on Karunanidhi. In a matter of this kind, which can inflame religious antagonisms, this government has not shown the responsible approach that ought to be shown. This is not good.”

I firmly believe that very soon friend Cho will once again begin writing criticisms of the Jayalalithaa government in the same way.

As I noted above, V.N. Sudhakaran—Jayalalithaa's foster son, whose wedding Jayalalithaa arranged at a cost of ₹100 crore—was suddenly arrested under Jayalalithaa's government on 13-6-2001, booked under twelve sections including attempted murder, and lodged in Palayamkottai prison. His father Vivekanandan was also arrested. As Sudhakaran was being taken away after his arrest, he told reporters that there was a planned conspiracy to throw him inside; that God would call everyone to account; that all twelve cases against him were deliberately fabricated false cases; and that without any search having been conducted at his house, they were claiming heroin had been found there. Immediately after arrest he was lodged in Chennai Central Prison. Within hours he was taken to Vellore prison. He was then moved from there to Palayamkottai prison. All of it happened within twenty-four hours. What became of the case that was filed with such commotion? No one has forgotten the old story of Jayalalithaa filing false cases even against members of Sasikala's family.

On 18-6-2001, Mudakkathan Pandian, State Treasurer of the Viduthalai Chiruthaigal organisation, was murdered in Madurai. When it was announced that a demonstration would be held demanding a CBI inquiry, the organisation's leader Thirumavalavan was arrested on 21-6-2001.

As another example of Jayalalithaa's retaliatory actions, in September 2002 she dismissed overnight ten thousand road workers who had been appointed during the DMK government.

Because journalist Nakkheeran Gopal wrote critically of the Jayalalithaa government, she was not satisfied with arresting him under POTA; all his family members were subjected to harassment in the name of inquiry. Gopal's father-in-law, subjected continuously to such interrogation, died in anguish on 6-7-2003. In the name of POTA, MDMK General Secretary Vaiko was arrested and imprisoned for months.

Thus, filing false cases against opposition members and tormenting them is a habit deeply rooted in Jayalalithaa. One could go on writing about that particular trait. It is only as a continuation of it that, even after coming to power for a third time now, she has begun the harmful practice of retaliating against opposition members by invoking the imagined reason of “land grabbing”; and even within that, by saying action will be taken only on land-grabbing cases said to have occurred during the DMK period from 2006 to 2011, while taking no action over wrongdoing by members of her own party during those five years.

As an illustration—like testing a whole pot of rice by one grain—I reproduce here in Tamil translation a report that appeared not in *Murasoli*, but in the English daily *Times of India* on 15-7-2011.

Cases were registered against some DMK members and a senior police officer, and the newspapers carried reports such as “Land grab—Kodaikanal municipal chairman arrested.” But the very father of the complainant wrote to the police and the court stating that the complaint was not true and that the land transaction had taken place with his consent. Yet without knowing this truth, merely on receiving the complaint and without the police first investigating and establishing the facts, Kodaikanal municipal chairman Mohammed Ibrahim of the DMK, along with Sekar Sebastian and Dalip Singh, were ordered to be arrested and remanded.

That is what the *Times of India* wrote. It is an example both of how the police act on wrong information without knowing the truth, and of the extent of the vindictive spirit with which they proceeded so far as to arrest the Kodaikanal municipal chairman.

This report also appeared in the English daily *Deccan Chronicle* on 20 July. But none of the Tamil newspapers published it.

It is in this manner that, under this government, false cases are being filed against DMK members—especially former Ministers Veerapandi Arumugam, K.N. Nehru and I. Periasamy, Madurai district DMK secretary Thalapathi, and other frontline members—and they are being dragged about in the name of inquiry. Veerapandi Arumugam's health has been affected; he was admitted to hospital for many days and has only now begun to recover somewhat. Yet they keep even him for three days in the name of inquiry and try to derive pleasure from it.

Jayalalithaa has taken up the wrong weapon to take revenge on the DMK. It is a weapon sharpened on both edges. Jayalalithaa's present actions only show that she is “an expert at fabricating false cases for revenge,” “accustomed to false cases,” and “one who derives a kind of pleasure from them”!

**With affection, M.K.**  
30-7-2011
