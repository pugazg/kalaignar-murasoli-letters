# 3399. Identify Those Who Oppose the ‘Sethu’ Project!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This letter rebuts claims that the Sethusamudram project would harm fishermen and the marine environment. It reproduces technical, environmental, livelihood and welfare claims from the author’s source material and concludes with an electoral appeal. All figures and assessments are preserved as source claims; they are not independently verified here. The complete audited Tamil is reproduced below and remains authoritative.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3399](../../../chapters/3399-sethu-thittam-ethirpporai-adaiyaalam-kaanka.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 242](../../../pages/page-242.md)–[PDF 251](../../../pages/page-251.md)  
**Date:** 25 April 2009

**Udanpirappē,**

AIADMK leader Jayalalithaa, who had strongly stated in the 2001 and 2004 election manifestos that the Sethusamudram project must be implemented, is now saying at election meetings that fishermen’s livelihoods will be affected, thousands of fishermen will be rendered helpless and the coastal environment will be damaged.

The AIADMK manifesto too says that lakhs of fishermen will lose their livelihoods and that, if her alliance wins this election, the AIADMK will cancel the project.

Although explanations have been given many times, Jayalalithaa keeps repeating the same points at every meeting like a trained parrot, so I wish to explain them once again.

The source says the project was prepared with the advice of scientists and experts from government and well-known private institutions, including L&T, Ramboll, Allianz Hydraulic Consultancy of the Netherlands, Indomer Coastal Hydraulics Pvt. Ltd., the National Environmental Engineering Research Institute (NEERI), IIT Madras, the National Institute of Ocean Technology, the Central Marine Fisheries Research Institute, officials of the Central Environmental Monitoring Committee, the Department of Ocean Development in Goa, the National Ship Design and Research Centre in Visakhapatnam and the National Institute of Hydrology in Dehradun.

It says the views of fourteen leading institutions in India and abroad were considered, along with the advice of the Army and Navy.

Based on inputs from scientists, technical experts and institutions, NEERI conducted detailed techno-economic feasibility and environmental-impact studies for two years and submitted a detailed project report.

The source gives the following environmental points:

- Along the selected route, compared with the overall coastal area, only a very small quantity of marine microorganisms and fewer deep-sea organisms are present.
- Of the Gulf of Mannar Biosphere Reserve’s 10,500 square kilometres, only about six square kilometres might be affected, described as a very small fraction.
- The Gulf of Mannar Biosphere Reserve is a National Marine Park area containing coral reefs and marine resources. Apart from seagrass areas and marshes adjoining Vedaranyam, some coral reefs serve as breeding areas for certain fish species. The canal route, the source says, lies about twenty kilometres from those breeding grounds.
- According to the source, dredging and ship movement would not affect the coral reefs or mangrove forests.
- The project was designed, it says, with the position that the rich marine resources — mangroves, seagrass and coral reefs — should remain unaffected, and therefore fish resources would be protected.
- Because the Gulf of Mannar coral reefs are fish-breeding centres, the area is maintained under a United Nations Development Programme biosphere initiative. As marine resources are central to coastal fishermen’s livelihoods, the organisation trains local fishing communities to protect the biosphere.
- Model studies on sedimentation and sea currents, the source says, showed that the biosphere area would not be affected by dredging or disposal of dredged material along Route No. 6.
- It also states that there are no mangrove forests at Adam’s Bridge in the area proposed for deepening.

The author concludes from these points that the project would not harm the marine environment in the area.

## Livelihood-related details

The source says that when the Union Ministry of Environment and Forests approved the Sethusamudram project, it required two per cent of the total project cost to be allocated for livelihood opportunities and training for coastal fishermen in the project area.

The detailed project report allocated ₹60 crore for community-development works, including drinking-water facilities in project-area villages.

The source lists the following proposed facilities:

- a multi-purpose harbour facility at Rameswaram at an estimated cost of ₹40 crore;
- ₹10 crore in assistance for fishing harbours and fish-landing centres at Mookaiyur, Thondi, Sethubavachatram and Muthupettai;
- ₹5 crore for community infrastructure in villages with high fishing populations in Ramanathapuram, Nagapattinam, Thanjavur and Pudukkottai districts, at ₹5 lakh per village;
- ₹3 crore for coastal community-development programmes, vocational training and livelihood training in the districts;
- ₹2 crore for other expenditure.

The source says vocational and essential training useful to coastal fishermen was identified under the multi-purpose coastal community-development programme.

In a second phase, various non-governmental organisations were involved.

The source lists training provided to **9,725 beneficiaries** in Thoothukudi, Tiruvarur, Nagapattinam, Pudukkottai and Thanjavur districts, including:

- diesel-engine repair;
- palmyra-leaf handicrafts;
- seashell jewellery;
- crab fattening;
- entrepreneurship development;
- rope handicrafts;
- marine-technology training;
- radio, television and mobile-phone repair;
- refrigeration-equipment repair;
- jute-bag production;
- nylon-net weaving;
- tailoring;
- nursing;
- laboratory-technician training.

Under a micro-finance scheme, the source says ₹32.15 lakh was provided for works in twelve villages in Thoothukudi and Ramanathapuram districts, including wooden classroom furnishings, a health centre, solar-powered lights, anganwadi and community-hall construction, and drinking-water facilities.

It also says ₹4,45,385 was provided as educational assistance to students in coastal districts and that one hundred computers were distributed in Thoothukudi and Ramanathapuram districts.

The author says these programmes were intended to improve coastal fishermen’s livelihoods.

In a developing world, the source argues, sea transport plays a major role in economic growth.

It says the Sethusamudram Canal Project was formulated after obtaining expert advice and public opinion, with special importance given to fishery resources and fishermen’s livelihoods.

The letter then lists measures attributed to the DMK government over the previous three years for fishermen generally:

1. ₹500 per family during the fishing-ban period.
2. A world-class fishermen’s training institute at Muttukadu to increase income opportunities.
3. Fishing equipment, nets, life jackets, torches, bicycles and ice boxes for 42,000 tsunami-affected fishermen, costing ₹110.36 crore.
4. ₹50 lakh in assistance for one hundred fishing-community youth studying marine education.
5. Waiver of ₹19.56 crore in loans obtained by 5,635 fishermen through the fisheries cooperative federation.
6. Outboard motors at subsidised rates to 1,550 fishermen at a cost of ₹3.10 crore.
7. Harbour and landing infrastructure:
   - a fishing harbour at Colachel in Kanyakumari district at a cost of ₹27 crore;
   - approval for a fishing harbour at Thengapattinam at a cost of ₹40 crore;
   - fishing harbours at Muttom and Rajakkamangalam in Kanyakumari district;
   - accelerated action toward a fishing harbour at Poompuhar;
   - ten fish-landing centres at ₹5 crore each, total ₹50 crore, for coastal fishermen;
   - improvement of Thoothukudi fishing harbour at a cost of ₹5.04 crore;
   - improvement of Chennai fishing harbour at a cost of ₹6.38 crore;
   - cold-chain facilities in four coastal districts at a cost of ₹1 crore.
8. Educational awards:
   - ₹5,000 for fishing-community students securing first place at State level in Class 12;
   - ₹3,000 for district-level first place in Class 12;
   - ₹3,000 for State-level first place in Class 10;
   - ₹1,000, ₹500 and ₹300 respectively for district-level first, second and third places in Class 10.

The source says compensation from the Chief Minister’s Public Relief Fund for families of fishermen killed in firing by the Sri Lankan Navy was increased from ₹1 lakh to ₹3 lakh, and to ₹5 lakh for families left without support.

For fishermen injured in such firing, it gives ₹25,000 for minor injuries and ₹50,000 for serious injuries.

9. For families of fishermen missing at sea, relief of ₹50 per day, amounting to ₹1,500 per month.
10. One hundred per cent tax-exempt diesel allocation for mechanised boats increased from 1,000 litres to 1,500 litres per month; 250 litres per month for motorised country boats.
11. Under the National Marine Fishermen Savings-cum-Relief Scheme, ₹51,31,57,000 was said to have been provided over three years to 4,30,479 members.

Under the corresponding women’s scheme, ₹37,57,41,000 was said to have been provided over three years to 3,13,471 members.

12. Over three years, 8,298 fishermen received a diesel subsidy of ₹1.50 per litre.

The source also says a Fishermen Welfare Board, chaired by the Fisheries Minister, was established with ₹5 crore to benefit people engaged in fishing and related occupations.

Benefits for Welfare Board members are listed for accidental death, death while fishing, natural death, funeral expenses, education, marriage, maternity, miscarriage and pension.

The monetary benefits printed in the source include:

- accidental death — ₹1 lakh;
- loss of both hands — ₹1 lakh;
- loss of both legs — ₹1 lakh;
- loss of one hand and one leg — ₹1 lakh;
- complete loss of sight in both eyes — ₹1 lakh;
- loss of one hand or one leg — ₹50,000;
- severe injury resulting in loss of a body part — ₹20,000;
- death while fishing, or immediate non-accidental death after fishing — ₹1 lakh;
- fisherman missing while fishing — ₹1 lakh to the family;
- natural death — ₹10,000;
- funeral assistance — ₹2,500;
- maternity assistance — ₹6,000, at ₹1,000 per month for six months;
- miscarriage — ₹3,000;
- abortion — ₹3,000;
- old-age pension — ₹400 per month.

The author says the government is working day and night through such programmes for the advancement of the fishing community.

Yet Jayalalithaa, he says, goes from town to town campaigning contrary to the truth that fishermen’s welfare will be harmed.

He therefore asks friends from the fishing community themselves to remind fishermen of these measures.

And he closes with a partisan appeal:

Tamil people, identify those who, through deceptive talk, seek to mislead Tamil Nadu and its fishermen about the “Sethu” project that the source says would bring prosperity.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**25-4-2009**
