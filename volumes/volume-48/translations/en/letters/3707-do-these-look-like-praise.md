# 3707. Do All These Look Like Praise?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 22 February 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

At the mass wedding of 65 couples organised in Chennai to mark Chief Minister Jayalalithaa's 65th birthday, after telling her little stories, Jayalalithaa turned to politics. With pride, she praised her own decision, saying that her government had given farmers ₹15,000 per acre for crop loss and that even the opposition parties had praised it.

But do you know how the opposition parties actually “praised” it?

In a statement, Vijayakanth, Leader of the Opposition in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly, said:

> “At a time when several lakhs of farmers have been affected after losing their samba crop, the Chief Minister's announcement that compensation relief will apply to only 1.75 lakh farmers is like offering a handful of puffed grain to satisfy an elephant's hunger. The DMDK has been insisting that farmers who have lost their samba crop should be given ₹25,000 per acre and agricultural labourers ₹10,000. But Jayalalithaa has announced compensation of ₹15,000 per acre for farmers. This compensation is not sufficient.”

Does Amma regard this as praise from the DMDK?

In a statement issued on 9 February 2013, PMK founder Dr. Ramadoss said:

> “When it has cost ₹25,000 to cultivate samba on one acre, the government's payment of only ₹15,000 per acre as relief is not sufficient. Nor is it fair to say that compensation will be given only for 3.61 lakh acres of crops belonging to 1.75 lakh farmers. The families of farmers who committed suicide should be given compensation of at least ₹5 lakh each.”

Does Amma regard this as praise from the PMK?

In the statement issued on behalf of the Communist Party of India (Marxist), while initially welcoming the drought-relief announcement, State Secretary Comrade Ramakrishnan also said:

> “Agricultural labourers have not been given appropriate relief. With the kuruvai crop having failed and the samba crop also having withered, agricultural labourers who are without work and suffering in poverty must also be given appropriate relief.”

Is this the Marxist Communist Party's praise for Amma?

The Communist Party of India, as a party supportive of the ruling party, welcomed the Chief Minister's announcements. Even so, in an editorial in *Janasakthi* under the headline **“Cancel Crop Loans”**, it wrote:

> “With input costs having risen, farmers had sought ₹25,000 per acre to recover from the losses they have suffered, and ₹10,000 for each family of agricultural labourers suffering without work. If possible, the Tamil Nadu government may reconsider these demands.”

Is this the certificate of praise that the Communist Party of India has given Amma?

Speaking to reporters, M.R. Sivasamy, State President of the Tamil Nadu Farmers' Association, said:

> “Of the 12 lakh acres in the Delta districts on which samba cultivation was undertaken, compensation has been announced for only 3.61 lakh acres. This is deeply disappointing. It costs ₹30,000 per acre to produce paddy in the Cauvery Delta. Half of that—₹15,000—has been announced as compensation. Even this compensation is said to be only for 3.61 lakh acres. Demanding that drought relief of ₹20,000 be announced for all farmers, a hunger strike will be held at Orathanadu at the end of February.”

Is even an announcement of a hunger strike against the AIADMK government praise for Amma?

In a statement, P. Shanmugam, General Secretary of the Tamil Nadu Farmers' Association, said:

> “At a time when production costs have increased several-fold, ₹15,000 per acre including crop insurance is not sufficient. Agricultural labourers have spent this entire year without work, living on loans and in poverty; the failure to announce any relief for them is deeply disappointing.”

Is the farmers' disappointment also a form of praise that gladdens Amma's heart?

In a statement, Durai Manickam, State General Secretary of the Tamil Nadu Farmers' Association, said:

> “The Chief Minister has announced that 1.75 lakh farmers whose crops have been affected by more than 50 per cent will be given ₹15,000 per acre. This is not sufficient. The announcement of ₹3 lakh for the families of farmers who committed suicide is also not sufficient. The absence of any announcement of relief assistance for agricultural-labourer families is disappointing.”

Is “not sufficient” itself a speech of praise that gives Amma satisfaction?

In the statement I issued regarding the relief announced by Jayalalithaa in the Assembly, I wrote at length:

> “Farmers who have lost both the kuruvai crop and the samba crop initially demanded relief assistance of ₹40,000 per acre, and continuously pressed the demand that at least ₹25,000 must be given, while also conducting various forms of protest. All the opposition parties in Tamil Nadu supported and stressed this demand.
>
> “But what has been announced as relief? Chief Minister Jayalalithaa has read out that relief will be given only for 3.61 lakh acres of crops belonging to 1.75 lakh farmers whose yield has been affected by more than 50 per cent, at the rate of ₹15,000 per acre. If so, what is to become of the farmers whose yield has been affected by less than 50 per cent? Even for those affected by more than 50 per cent, is the entire ₹15,000 relief amount to be paid by the Tamil Nadu government? Does it not include disaster relief and agricultural crop-insurance payments as well? If so, exactly how much is the Tamil Nadu government itself going to give as relief per acre? Farmers everywhere are anguished that the amount now announced is nowhere near sufficient. The Chief Minister's statement also says nothing about relief for the several lakhs of farmers who, because there was no water, could not cultivate at all and completely lost their livelihood. There is no announcement on the demand that farmers' cooperative loans be cancelled.
>
> “A demand had been made that the families of farmers who died by suicide should receive compensation of ₹10 lakh each. It was also demanded that one person from each deceased farmer's family be given a government job. The ₹3 lakh announced for each family of a deceased farmer is a very small amount. I once again insist that this amount be increased, that compensation be given to the families of all farmers who died by suicide, and that one person in each family be given a government job.”

Does she regard even my statement as praise offered to Amma on behalf of the DMK?

The major opposition parties in Tamil Nadu and farmers' organisations spoke in one voice: the relief announced by Amma was insufficient; disappointing; unsatisfactory; and had created disparities among farmers. Concealing all those loudly expressed views, the Chief Minister said at the wedding function that the opposition parties had welcomed all the announcements she made in her reply to the debate on the Governor's Address. I have compiled and presented these comments here so that the people of Tamil Nadu may understand how contrary to the truth that claim is. The Chief Minister is trying to hide a fire smouldering beneath ash by covering it with silk cloth. Think for yourselves what kind of Chief Minister Tamil Nadu has received!

After praising herself in the debate on the Governor's Address and declaring that even the opposition parties and farmers had accepted and praised the relief she had given, today's (21 February 2013) *Dinamalar* carried a report under the headline **“Agricultural Labourers Leaving the Delta—Disappointed After Waiting, They Head to Kerala”**. It said:

> “Agricultural labourers from the Delta districts, disappointed after waiting in the expectation of drought relief from the government, are heading to Kerala in search of work. ₹25,000 per acre had been sought for farmers and ₹10,000 for agricultural labourers. Agricultural labourers had been waiting in the hope that they could use this money for travel and food expenses and go to other States for work. But Chief Minister J. has announced only ₹15,000 per acre as relief for farmers. No announcement regarding relief from the government for agricultural labourers has been made. As a result, lakhs of agricultural labourers from the Delta districts have been disappointed.”

But Jayalalithaa does not seem to know the actual situation in Tamil Nadu. Conducting everything only through “video-conferencing”, she fails to understand the suffering of more than five lakh farmers and more than nine lakh agricultural labourers, while claiming that everyone is praising her. I once again insist that she come down a little, see the conditions, understand the realities and provide appropriate relief to farmers and agricultural labourers.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**22 February 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. `kuruvai` and `samba` are retained as the source's agricultural-season terms; they refer to distinct paddy-cultivation seasons in the Cauvery Delta.
2. The repeated questions asking whether criticism counts as “praise” are central to the letter's irony and are preserved rather than condensed.
