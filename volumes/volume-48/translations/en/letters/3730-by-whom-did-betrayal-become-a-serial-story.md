# 3730. By Whom Did Betrayal Become a Continuing Story?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 10 April 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

Yesterday in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly, DMK MLA Thambi E.V. Velu gave a calling-attention motion about the Karnataka government diverting water from the South Pennar. Members spoke and the Public Works Minister replied. At that point, both that minister and the Leader of the House—who bears responsibility for conducting the House calmly—unnecessarily began speaking about the Cauvery issue, Mullaperiyar and Sri Lanka, and alleged that the DMK and I had betrayed Tamil Nadu on all of them. They did not allow Thambi Duraimurugan, a former Public Works Minister who was ready to answer, to speak.

I do not criticise every AIADMK minister. But a few ministers appear to raise issues in the Assembly either because they think their leadership will celebrate them only if they speak in this way, or perhaps to preserve their own positions. When opposition parties try to answer, democratic opportunity is denied; without patience to hear an alternative view, members are even expelled.

This is not confined to the Tamil Nadu Assembly. On 7 April 2013 a joint conference of State Chief Ministers and High Court Chief Justices was held in Delhi under the Prime Minister's chairmanship. The Tamil Nadu Chief Minister did not attend. A Tamil Nadu minister read the Chief Minister's speech in her place. Tamil newspapers reported it as saying:

> “Because of the uninterrupted struggle I [Jayalalithaa] have conducted for more than twenty years for Tamil Nadu's rights in the Cauvery issue, ultimately, following the Supreme Court's order, the Cauvery Tribunal's final award was published in the Central Gazette.”

So, we are told, it was because of the Chief Minister's uninterrupted struggle for more than twenty years that the Tribunal's final award was published following the Supreme Court order! In villages there is a saying, **“Ghee is dripping from finger millet.”** That is what comes to mind.

If the Chief Minister's speech is like that, in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly too, whatever the subject, ministers say without basis and without fully understanding the history: the DMK betrayed Tamil Nadu on Cauvery; the DMK betrayed Tamil Nadu on Sri Lanka; the DMK betrayed Tamil Nadu on Mullaperiyar; the DMK betrayed Tamil Nadu on Katchatheevu.

Who truly owns these betrayals? Who repeatedly betrayed the Tamil people and Tamil interests? This letter is written to explain precisely that.

- When, on 23 April 2009, I appealed for cooperation with a one-day strike to press the Union Government to secure a permanent ceasefire in Sri Lanka, Jayalalithaa said, **“The war Rajapaksa is now conducting in Sri Lanka is not against Tamils; it is only against the Liberation Tigers.”** Was that betrayal, or was it not?
- When Eelam Tamils were being killed in Sri Lanka, Jayalalithaa said on 17 January 2009 that **“when there is a war, it is only natural that innocent people are killed.”** Can there be a greater betrayal than that?
- On 16 April 2002, Jayalalithaa herself moved and passed a resolution in the Assembly saying **“Prabhakaran must be arrested and brought to India.”** Can there be a greater betrayal than that?
- When all parties met at the Chennai Secretariat over the Sri Lankan issue, Jayalalithaa announced that she would boycott the meeting. Was that outright betrayal, or was it not?
- When a massive **human chain** was held in Chennai in 2009 for the Eelam Tamils, Jayalalithaa described it as a **“deceptive drama.”** Was that betrayal, or was it not?
- When all political parties in Tamil Nadu went to Delhi under my leadership and asked the Prime Minister to work for a permanent ceasefire in Sri Lanka, Jayalalithaa boycotted the effort. Was that betrayal, or was it not?
- Jayalalithaa calls the Liberation Tigers enemies even today. Who is the traitor—Jayalalithaa, or I?
- The DMK lost government twice over the Eelam Tamils. Perasiriyar and I resigned even our Assembly seats. Why, we have now withdrawn even from the United Progressive Alliance. Are these acts of betrayal, or are people such as Jayalalithaa who criticise them the traitors?
- Jayalalithaa passed a resolution in the Assembly saying that no person belonging to the banned Liberation Tigers organisation should be allowed to enter India. Was that betrayal, or was it not?
- When an all-party meeting in Tamil Nadu passed a resolution condemning what it described as genocide in Sri Lanka and demanding an immediate ceasefire, Jayalalithaa issued a statement saying: **“Looking at that resolution, the suspicion has arisen in the minds of the people of Tamil Nadu that Karunanidhi may be acting in support of the Liberation Tigers. It is astonishing that Karunanidhi, who has been Chief Minister five times, does not understand that the Government of India has no power to stop the civil war now taking place in Sri Lanka. If India intervenes in Sri Lanka's internal matter, neighbouring countries may later obtain an opportunity to intervene in our country's internal affairs.”** Can there be a greater betrayal than that?
- On the Cauvery issue, Jayalalithaa said on 21 March 1991: **“Even if the Tribunal gives an award, Cauvery water will come to Tamil Nadu only if they themselves open the dam in Karnataka. The Tribunal's award can be useful only if Karnataka agrees and adopts a fair and favourable decision.”** Was that betrayal, or was it not?
- Jayalalithaa said: **“Even if the Tribunal gives an award, Karnataka Chief Minister Bangarappa is under no compulsion to implement it.”** Was that a betrayal of Tamil Nadu, or was it not?
- On 9 June 1992 Union Irrigation Minister Shukla wrote to Jayalalithaa, then Chief Minister, seeking consent for a committee to implement the Tribunal's interim order. Jayalalithaa replied only nine months later, on 30 March 1993. Can there be a greater betrayal?
- Even after the Tribunal issued its interim order in 1991, Jayalalithaa remained Chief Minister until 1996 without securing the 205 TMC of water stipulated in the interim order in even one year. Was that betrayal, or was it not?
- When the Cauvery River Authority was constituted through the DMK's major efforts, Jayalalithaa said in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly on 2 April 2002: **“The Cauvery Authority is a non-functioning authority. We refused to accept that authority and withdrew from the BJP alliance.”** She called it a **“toothless authority”** and a **“non-functioning authority.”** Was that betrayal, or was it not?
- In an English statement issued on 21 September 1998, Jayalalithaa wrote exactly: **“The Kaveri River Authority headed by the Prime Minister is a very big fraud perpetrated on the farmers of the kauveri Delta in Tamil Nadu”**. Was that an indirect betrayal of Tamil Nadu, or was it not?
- At a Tamil Nadu Cabinet meeting on 21 June 2002, Jayalalithaa decided that Tamil Nadu would not participate in meetings of the Cauvery River Authority. Was that a betrayal of Tamil Nadu, or was it not?
- Jayalalithaa said she had no confidence in the Prime Minister, the Chairman of the Cauvery Authority; the situation reached the point where even the Supreme Court criticised the statement, and Jayalalithaa later had to express regret. Was that a betrayal of Tamil Nadu, or was it not?
- Referring to the period when the AIADMK was allied with the BJP, then Prime Minister Vajpayee said on 22 August 1999, according to the source, that **“the period of alliance with the AIADMK was the most painful period in my political life,”** and that even on the day before Independence Day Jayalalithaa had threatened to bring down the government, but they had not yielded to her blackmail tactics. Was that a betrayal of trust by Jayalalithaa, or was it not?
- And above all: in a letter she herself wrote to then Prime Minister Rajiv Gandhi about M.G.R.—the man who brought Jayalalithaa into politics, introduced her and made her who she was—she said, according to the source, that M.G.R. was jealous of her growth and could not tolerate her advancement. Can there be a greater outright betrayal than that?

Udanpirappē, I truly have no desire to write about all these matters or cause embarrassment to others. But the painful truth is that, day after day in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly, a few ministers themselves provoke us into writing in this way.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**10 April 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. The entire letter is structured as Kalaignar's political rebuttal to repeated accusations of **betrayal**. Every allegation, quotation and characterisation remains attributed to the source rather than presented as an independently established fact.
2. The source's exact English sentence from Jayalalithaa's 21 September 1998 statement is reproduced without correction, including **“Kaveri”** and the source spelling **“kauveri Delta”.**
3. The village proverb **“Ghee is dripping from finger millet”** is retained consistently with its earlier appearance in this volume; it mocks a claim the writer regards as implausible.
