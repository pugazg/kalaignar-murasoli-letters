**Udanpirappē,**

Chief Minister Jayalalithaa has spoken for an hour and a half in reply to the debate on the Governor's Address—no, no—she has read out her reply speech. At the very beginning she indirectly attacked me as “a senior Member of the Legislative Assembly who does not participate in the proceedings of the House,” denied DMK members permission to reply, and expelled all of them. Two days earlier, an issue of privilege had been hurriedly raised against the Leader of the Opposition, denying him even the opportunity to speak on the Governor's Address. Even the car and police protection provided to him as Leader of the Opposition were, with great magnanimity, not allowed. Having thus ensured that all of them stayed away from the House, and with no one there to question her, the Chief Minister completed her reply speech.

At the beginning of her speech she referred to what I had said at the Kanchipuram Veera Vanakka Naal public meeting—“It was not the Kazhagam that lost; it was you, the voters”—and claimed that this demeaned the voters of Tamil Nadu and the Legislative Assembly. Jayalalithaa points out that I do not participate in Assembly proceedings. Tamil Nadu knows that when she was in the opposition, she herself attended the proceedings of the House on only one or two days. Today, because the Assembly is being conducted in the old building out of obstinacy, even legislators in good health struggle to walk inside and reach their seats. Considering my physical condition, Members of the House themselves know very well that there is no facility for me to enter the Assembly in a wheelchair, take my place and speak.

In that Kanchipuram speech of mine, which the lady who has spoken both standing and sitting in the House now cites, where did I demean the voters or the Assembly? I did not even say anything there about today's ruling party! I spoke only about the language struggles. But when I wrote about the Governor's Address in my “Udanpirappu letter” dated 2 February 2012, I listed for two pages the wrongs of the AIADMK government. Where is the Chief Minister's reply to all that? I referred to Cabinet reshuffles, transfers of officials, court judgments, bus-fare increases, milk-price increases, irregularities in TASMAC auctions and sexual wrongdoing involving the police themselves. What is the Chief Minister's explanation for all those allegations? Instead of answering them, is it an answer merely to say that I do not come to the Assembly?

The Chief Minister says I spoke in some demeaning manner. Yet in her own speech yesterday she referred to an interview I gave in Cuddalore in which I said: “At times like these, I believe it is the duty of the opposition, whichever government is in office, to cooperate with that government. Therefore I have carried out that duty properly.” That is the political civility Anna taught me. But creating a condition in which I cannot even come to the Assembly, then mocking that very condition and using the House to attack me—that is the civility learned by today's Chief Minister.

In her long speech, with regard to farmers in Cuddalore and Villupuram districts affected by Cyclone Thane, she announced a special package containing concessions usually given at times of cyclones and floods. Even there, she compared it with relief assistance given under DMK rule during smaller natural calamities in 2000 and 2008, claiming that relief work was now being carried out on a much greater scale. Whatever the comparison, the DMK welcomes relief assistance reaching affected people.

Next, in her reply speech, Chief Minister Jayalalithaa said that the State Government would immediately appoint an expert committee to submit a report to the Central Government on the thoughts and fears prevailing among the people of the Kudankulam area about the nuclear power station. Yet in the letter she wrote to the Prime Minister on 19 September 2011, the same Jayalalithaa asked that appropriate instructions be issued to the authorities concerned not to continue any work at the Kudankulam Nuclear Power Station until an amicable solution was reached. If she is now announcing that the State Government will immediately appoint an expert committee, could she not have appointed that committee then itself? Was the State Government's letter to the Prime Minister then written merely to deceive the protesting people? Is her present announcement about appointing a State expert committee also only to deceive them?

Not only that. After the Kudankulam protest committee and, on behalf of the Central Government, Union Minister Honourable Narayanasamy met the Chief Minister, did not the Tamil Nadu Cabinet meet on 22 September 2011 and pass a resolution asking the Prime Minister to suspend work at the nuclear power station until the fears of the people of that area were removed? Was that resolution too passed only to deceive the people of the area?

Next, in her reply the Chief Minister said that from June onward the electricity shortage would gradually decrease and that by the middle of next year the shortage would be eliminated completely. Before the election, however, the AIADMK manifesto said that if they came to power they would end power cuts within four months. Now they say the electricity shortage will be eliminated fully only by the middle of next year. When replying to the Governor's Address last year too, Chief Minister Jayalalithaa spoke at length about the power shortage, and I wrote a detailed response in *Murasoli* on 18 June 2011 under the title “Will There Be an Electricity Shortage in the Future?” The Chief Minister has now repeated those same points. Jayalalithaa says that if the DMK government had implemented power projects during the previous five years, there would have been no shortage.

Let me explain what was done for electricity generation after the DMK government was formed in 2006.

A project to generate 600 MW through an Ennore Thermal Power Station extension was begun on 8 December 2006 at an estimated cost of ₹3,136 crore; its generation is to begin in August 2013.

At Mettur Thermal Power Station, a project capable of generating 600 MW was begun on 2 May 2007 at an estimated cost of ₹3,100 crore; its generation will soon come into operation.

At North Chennai Thermal Power Station, a project to generate 600 MW was begun on 26 June 2007 at an estimated cost of ₹2,475 crore; its generation will soon come into operation.

At Udangudi, under a joint project of BHEL and the Tamil Nadu Electricity Board, two projects, each capable of generating 800 MW, were begun on 15 October 2007 at an estimated cost of ₹8,362 crore. One unit will begin operation and generation in March 2013 and the other in September 2013.

At North Chennai Thermal Power Station, another project capable of producing 600 MW was begun on 14 July 2008 at an estimated cost of ₹2,475 crore.

At a cost of ₹1,126 crore, a project capable of generating 183 MW of cogenerated power from cooperative and public-sector sugar mills was begun on 14 December 2009. Altogether, projects for generating 4,183 MW were begun under DMK rule.

After the DMK government was formed in 2006, various measures were undertaken with future needs in mind. Through different power projects, arrangements were made for 1,400 MW in 2010–2011, 3,316 MW in 2011–2012, 1,222 MW in 2012–2013 and 1,860 MW in 2013–2014—a total of 7,798 MW.

I believe these statistics provide the necessary firm explanation to the accusation that no new power-generation projects were begun under DMK rule.

After the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam government was formed in Tamil Nadu in 2006, many new factories were started in different places, increasing electricity demand. Had efforts of this magnitude been made during the AIADMK government that ruled from 2001 to 2006 after calculating future requirements, the required generation would have begun by now and Tamil Nadu would not have faced this shortage.

Jayalalithaa takes pride in saying that during her 2001–2006 government Tamil Nadu was a State without power cuts. But during her 1991–1996 government, was there or was there not an electricity-control regime in Tamil Nadu until 16 October 1992? Likewise, from 3 October 1982 to 26 July 1984, during AIADMK rule, was there or was there not an electricity-control regime? Had we refrained from encouraging so many new industries after the DMK came to power in 2006, perhaps no electricity-control regime would have become necessary. But can a government exist without encouraging industrial development?

Because the AIADMK government did not begin enough power-generation projects, the shortage arose during DMK rule. But it can be confidently expected that the benefit of the generation projects begun during DMK rule will certainly eliminate the electricity shortage in Tamil Nadu in the future.

A few days ago, the Chief Minister said in the Assembly that the opposition would be stunned when they heard the reply she was going to give to the Governor's Address. That is true! Hearing her hour-and-a-half speech, even the ruling-party members themselves must have been stunned—wondering, “Is this the speech that was supposed to leave us stunned?” For there were no important opposition members there to hear it!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**6 February 2012**

## Letter-specific notes

1. `அசந்துதான் போனார்கள்` carries Kalaignar's closing reversal: the Chief Minister predicts the opposition will be stunned; he jokes that ruling-party members themselves must instead have been stunned by the speech. The English title preserves that turn.
2. `நான் அளித்த பேட்டியில்` is rendered as **“an interview I gave”**, following the scan-verified canonical reading.
3. All power-project dates, cost figures, megawatt capacities and projected generation figures are preserved as printed and are not externally reconciled.
4. Kudankulam statements and resolutions are translated as Kalaignar presents their chronology, without outside supplementation.

2001 முதல் 2006 வரையிலான தனது ஆட்சிக் காலத்தில், தமிழ்நாடு மின் வெட்டே இல்லாத மாநிலமாக இருந்தது என்று ஜெயலலிதா சொல்லி பெருமைப்பட்டுக் கொள்கிறார். 1991 முதல் 1996ஆம் ஆண்டு வரை ஜெயலலிதா ஆட்சிக் காலத்திலே 16-10-1992 வரை தமிழ்நாட்டில் மின் கட்டுப்பாட்டு முறை இருந்ததா? இல்லையா? அது போலவே 3-10-1982 முதல் 26-7-1984 வரை, அ.தி.மு.க. ஆட்சிக் காலத்திலே மின் கட்டுப்பாட்டு முறை இருந்ததா இல்லையா? 2006ஆம் ஆண்டு தி.மு.க. ஆட்சிக்கு வந்த பிறகு அதிக தொழிற்சாலைகளை தமிழகத்திலே தொடங்காமல் இருந்திருந்தால் மின் கட்டுப்பாட்டு முறை வந்திருக்காதுதான். அதற்காக தொழில் வளர்ச்சியை ஊக்குவிக்காமல் ஓர் அரசு இருக்க முடியுமா?

அ.தி.மு.க. ஆட்சியிலே மின் உற்பத்தி திட்டங்களை அதிகமாகத் தொடங்காத காரணத்தால் கழக ஆட்சியிலே மின் பற்றாக்குறை ஏற்பட்டது. ஆனால் தி.மு.கழக ஆட்சியில் மின் உற்பத்தித் திட்டங்களைத் தொடங்கியதன் பயன் நிச்சயமாக வருங்காலத்தில் மின் பற்றாக்குறையை தமிழகத்தில் இல்லாமல் செய்திடும் என்று உறுதியாக நம்பலாம்.

சில நாட்களுக்கு முன்பு பேரவையில் பேசிய முதலமைச்சர் ஆளுநர் உரைக்கு தான் ஆற்றவிருக்கின்ற பதிலுரை கேட்டு எதிர்க்கட்சியினர் அசந்து போவார்கள் என்று கூறியிருந்தார். அது உண்மைதான்! அவரது ஒன்றரை மணி நேர உரை கேட்டு ஆளுங்கட்சியினரே “இதுதானா அசந்துபோக வைக்கக் கூடிய உரை” என்று அசந்துதான் போயிருப்பார்கள்! ஏனென்றால் அவரது உரையைக் கேட்ட முக்கிய எதிர்க்கட்சியினரே இல்லையே?

அன்புள்ள,
