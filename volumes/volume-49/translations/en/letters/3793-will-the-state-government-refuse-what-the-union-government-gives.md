# 3793. Will the State Government Refuse What the Union Government Gives?

**Udanpirappē,**

When the DMK assumed office in 2006, I wrote to Prime Minister Manmohan Singh on 5 June requesting approval for an elevated expressway linking Chennai Port and Maduravoyal. The Union Government approved the project, and Prime Minister Manmohan Singh himself came and laid its foundation stone on 8 January 2009 at a ceremony held under my leadership as Chief Minister.

The work proceeded rapidly under the DMK Government. It was only because the AIADMK Government prevented it from continuing that Soma, the company executing the work, demanded compensation for the losses it had suffered.

A few days ago, journalists asked Union Minister for Road Transport Mr Fernandes what had happened to this important four-lane elevated road project. He replied: “Banks were refusing to finance the project in the absence of clear-cut information from the State Government about its stand and a delay in clearing encroachments.” In other words, he said that banks were refusing finance because the State Government had not clearly stated its position and had failed to overcome the delay in removing encroachments.

In an affidavit filed in the High Court on the Maduravoyal elevated-road project, the Project Director of the National Highways Authority stated:

> “The project work began after approvals had been obtained from the departments concerned. After the change of government, adequate cooperation was not received from the departments of the State Government. The necessary cooperation was not provided in land acquisition and the rehabilitation of affected people. The reason is not known. The State Government has shown no interest in the project.
>
> “Last January, the Chief Engineer of the Public Works Department’s Water Resources Organisation sent a letter saying that the National Highways Authority must obtain the approval of the Coastal Regulation Authority before carrying out certain works. There is no justification for this. The order was issued with an ulterior motive and is contrary to the public interest. It appears that the State Government is obstructing the project.
>
> “The Chief Engineer’s order should therefore be quashed and stayed. The State Government should be directed to cooperate so that the flyover project may continue and be completed within the stipulated period.”

Following these developments, newspapers including *The Times of India*, *The Hindu*, *Dinakaran*, *Daily Thanthi*, *Dinamani* and *Dinamalar* have reported over the past two or three days that the ₹1,815-crore Chennai Port–Maduravoyal elevated expressway is in danger of being abandoned. They say implementation has stopped and that, because the contractor is demanding heavy compensation for the delay, the National Highways Authority is being driven towards such a decision.

Yet the AIADMK Government appears unconcerned that a project of this scale in Tamil Nadu’s capital may be abandoned after several hundred crore rupees have already been spent, or that improved transport would greatly benefit South Chennai, North Chennai and Tiruvallur District. Indeed, it does not even seem as though a Government is functioning here.

Just as they wish to close down the Sethusamudram Project; just as they have kept the new Secretariat building locked for three years; just as they have failed properly to maintain the Perarignar Anna Centenary Library raised in Anna’s memory; and just as they have prevented the Central Institute of Classical Tamil and the Pavendhar Research Library from functioning properly, the AIADMK rulers now appear intent upon conducting a closing ceremony for the Maduravoyal–Port elevated-road project as well.

*Daily Thanthi*, which reported the matter, wrote:

> “The Port–Maduravoyal elevated expressway, undertaken at a cost of ₹1,815 crore to reduce traffic congestion in Chennai and now kept suspended, is in danger of being abandoned completely. The National Highways Authority has reached the point of abandoning the project because the contractor is demanding additional compensation of about ₹872 crore for the delay in carrying out the work.
>
> “Prime Minister Manmohan Singh laid the foundation stone for the project, which was conceived under the DMK Government in January 2009. Work began in September 2010. Massive pillars for the expressway rose one after another. Of the total 889 pillars, 120 have been erected, and the road deck has been constructed over fifteen of them.
>
> “After the change of government in Tamil Nadu, the State Government stopped the project in April last year, claiming that the fifteen pillars erected in the Cooum River would affect the flow of water.”

It continued:

> “No work has been carried out for a year. The company says that thirty per cent of the entire project has been completed and ₹500 crore spent. The National Highways Authority does not wish to pay compensation of about ₹872 crore. It has therefore reached the stage of considering whether to abandon the project entirely. There is a danger that the Authority may issue an announcement to that effect within a day or two.
>
> “If the project is abandoned, exporters and importers will be severely affected and the income of Chennai Port will fall drastically. Trucks bringing export containers from the southern districts now reach the Port through the Chennai bypass, Maduravoyal, Madhavaram, Puzhal and the Hundred Feet Road. Trucks from the northern districts travel through the Manali refinery road and Ennore High Road.
>
> “Because of congestion on the Minjur–Madhavaram road, these trucks can only crawl. Traffic congestion is multiplied. At times it takes trucks as long as two days to reach the Port from Manali High Road. Truck owners continually complain that the delay wastes both time and fuel. The Port–Maduravoyal expressway is the solution to all these problems.”

When officials of the National Highways Authority were asked about the risk of abandonment, they said, “The project will be continued when a suitable situation arises.” Other newspapers have devoted page after page to the same report, expressing alarm that a sound project intended to relieve traffic congestion may be abandoned and cause an enormous loss to Chennai.

The National Highways Authority said: “It was the ‘motivated, tainted’ action of the State Government which had led to the situation of stalling of the project work.” *The Hindu* reported that another document attached to the Authority’s court affidavit described the difficulties in obtaining land for several National Highways projects in Tamil Nadu and stated that existing projects would be extremely difficult to complete unless those obstacles were removed without delay.

Because of such artificial problems, it is said that the National Highways Authority is hesitant to undertake new projects. *The Hindu* further reported that, regarding the allocation of new projects to Tamil Nadu, the Authority had stated: “Unless the State Government signs its State Support Agreement and comes forward to provide assistance under it, approval cannot be granted for new projects in the State.”

Relying upon the Maduravoyal–Port road, numerous companies had planned factories in Chennai’s suburban areas. All those companies have now suspended their work. Chennai Port had planned a massive container terminal near Sriperumbudur at a cost of ₹3,600 crore and another dry-port project costing ₹500 crore. Both have reportedly been placed on hold. Anand, a journalist with *The Hindu*, reported this under the title “Two Chennai Port Trust Projects in Limbo.”

An official of the Chennai Port Trust said: “The stoppage of Maduravoyal Project is ruining us. We will be losing crores of rupees every year.” In an interview, Union Minister for Shipping, my younger brother G.K. Vasan, said:

> “The Maduravoyal road project has been blocked and stopped by the State Government. It is an important project for both the Port and the city. We have repeatedly asked the State Government to expedite it. Recently, the Union Secretary of this Department discussed the matter with the State Chief Secretary.”

The Union Government filed a case in the Madras High Court seeking removal of the AIADMK Government’s stay on the project. When the case came up on 12 August 2013, the Bench comprising Justice Agarwal and Justice Sathyanarayanan issued a severe warning:

> “The Tamil Nadu Government has been given several opportunities to file its counter-affidavit in this case. It has repeatedly sought further time. This deserves condemnation. The nineteenth is fixed as the final deadline. The Government must file its reply that day. If it fails, we shall ourselves decide the matter.”

Atulya Misra, IAS, the present Chairperson of the Chennai Port Trust, stated:

> “The port will take a big hit if the proposed 19 km Port - Maduravoyal project is to be dropped. Any more delay or cancellation of the project would put in jeopardy projects worth Rs.4,000 crore the port had embarked on, keeping the elevated highway in mind. It was the expected lifeline for the port. It was meant to be the solution for congestion faced by containers”.
>
> “The project was compared to other similar projects such as Mumbai trans-harbour project and the Hosur Road elevated expressway. Besides decongesting city roads, the project was to reduce the distance travelled by container trucks from 52 km to 33 km.”

A senior IAS officer has thus spoken with anguish. A report published by *The Times of India* on 10 August 2013, quoting a police officer on the Maduravoyal–Port project, stated that its implementation could save many lives; that thirty accidents currently occurred there every day; and that the new project could prevent those accidents.

Members of business organisations likewise said that the new road was absolutely essential for travelling to the Port and transporting their goods.

Thus every section of society, officials, every newspaper and the Union Government have spoken of the project’s urgent necessity. Yet because of the Tamil Nadu Chief Minister’s decision, a project on which several hundred crore rupees have been spent now lies paralysed.

The road project has been stopped merely because the Prime Minister of India and I, then Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu, participated in its inauguration. A case is now pending in the High Court, while the Tamil Nadu Government delays because it has no adequate basis upon which to reply.

To condemn this profoundly anti-people conduct of the Tamil Nadu Government, and to insist that it immediately remove the unjustified prohibition imposed upon the Maduravoyal–Port road works and extend its full cooperation to the project, the DMK has decided to conduct a massive protest at Nerkundram on 17 August 2013. It will be organised jointly by the South Chennai, North Chennai and Tiruvallur district units, under the leadership of DMK Deputy General Secretary V.P. Duraisamy and in the presence of District Secretary J. Anbazhagan and District Organisers R.T. Sekar and S. Sudarsanam.

Like the many righteous struggles the DMK continually conducts in the people’s interest, this peaceful struggle for the Maduravoyal–Port elevated-road project must also be made a success. I appeal to DMK members, the public, members of friendly parties and the business community to come forward and participate.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**

14 August 2013
