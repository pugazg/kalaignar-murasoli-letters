# 3539. Five-Year Achievements of the Health and Family Welfare Department! (1)

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

Whenever a Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam government is formed, it gives special importance to the Health Department and creates all the facilities needed to make quality, advanced medical care easily available to everyone living in Tamil Nadu, especially the poor, the simple and the disadvantaged. Various new schemes are also implemented. In that spirit, since this government was formed in 2006, it has introduced one new medical-welfare scheme after another—schemes praised by the Union Government and other State Governments and regarded by them as worthy of emulation—and has implemented them successfully.

As a result of the continuous measures taken by this government, Tamil Nadu has recorded many achievements in health at the all-India level and has continued to make progress. From 2006–07, a Family Welfare Insurance Scheme has been implemented to provide compensation to a family if a death occurs following a family-planning sterilisation operation.

### Financial allocation

After this government assumed office, greater funds were allocated to the Health Department and many new schemes were implemented. In place of the ₹1,487 crore allocated to this department in 2005–06, the allocation rose to ₹3,889 crore in 2010–11.

This government has been filling vacancies for doctors in government hospitals and Primary Health Centres without delay. Since 2006, through the Employment Exchange and the Tamil Nadu Public Service Commission, government hospitals and Primary Health Centres have appointed 7,438 doctors; 9,744 nurses; 1,799 Village Health Nurses; 703 pharmacists; 433 Grade-II laboratory technicians; and 129 Grade-III laboratory technicians on regular pay scales. Including 1,249 doctors and 5,478 nurses who had been working on consolidated pay, a total of 34,854 people have so far been appointed.

### Kalaignar Insurance Scheme for Life-Saving Advanced Treatment

Union Minister for Health and Family Welfare Thiru Ghulam Nabi Azad, praising Tamil Nadu by saying, “Tamil Nadu is the model for the whole of India in implementing healthcare schemes,” launched the Kalaignar Insurance Scheme for Life-Saving Advanced Treatment on 23 July 2009. Under this widely praised scheme—one among the many medical-welfare schemes being implemented by this government for the welfare of poor and ordinary people—membership identity cards have been issued to 1 crore 35 lakh families.

For 51 life-threatening diseases, the scheme enables patients to receive specialised treatment free of cost at accredited government hospitals and high-quality private hospitals. The entire insurance premium for the scheme is paid by the DMK government. Up to 7 March 2011, 2,90,291 patients had benefited from life-saving surgical treatment at 702 accredited hospitals at a cost of ₹750.28 crore.

### '108' Emergency Medical Ambulance Service Scheme

To ensure that poor rural people receive immediate medical assistance in emergencies, the “108 Emergency Medical Ambulance Service Scheme” is being operated in all districts as a free 24-hour service, 365 days a year, in partnership with the private organisation EMRI, with 415 ambulances. With World Bank assistance, the scheme is to be expanded with another 200 ambulances at a cost of ₹31 crore. A total of 632 vehicles will operate across all parts of Tamil Nadu.

When immediate medical help is needed—particularly during childbirth or in accidents—dialling 108 brings a medical ambulance with appropriate medical staff to the place where help is required in approximately 20 minutes. They administer first aid, take the patient to a nearby government or private hospital, and help ensure that the necessary emergency treatment is provided without delay. This scheme, launched on Anna's birthday, 15 September 2008, had by 7 March 2011 taken 8,62,576 patients to government and private hospitals for emergency treatment. In addition, 34,407 lives in critical condition had been saved. These included 2,20,294 deliveries. So far, ₹143.64 crore has been allocated.

### Dr. Muthulakshmi Reddy Maternity Financial Assistance Scheme

To enable poor pregnant women to obtain nutrition and compensate for loss of income during pregnancy, the Dr. Muthulakshmi Reddy Maternity Financial Assistance Scheme provides ₹6,000 to each beneficiary. Poor pregnant women are given ₹3,000 as the first instalment in the seventh month of pregnancy and ₹3,000 as the second instalment after the child is born. Under this scheme, 25,76,612 pregnant mothers have so far been given ₹1,389.42 crore, and the scheme continues to be implemented.

### Varumun Kappom Scheme

The Varumun Kappom Scheme was introduced by the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam government in 1997 so that diseases among people in rural and urban areas could be detected at an early stage and appropriate treatment provided.

The scheme, abandoned by the subsequent AIADMK government, was restarted on 30 December 2006. Up to 28 February 2011, 19,272 medical camps had been conducted throughout Tamil Nadu and 1 crore 81 lakh 66 thousand people had benefited. At the continuing medical camps under this scheme, those identified as requiring advanced treatment are provided surgical treatment entirely free of cost under the Kalaignar Insurance Scheme.

### Kannoli Kappom Scheme

Under the Kannoli Kappom Scheme, students studying in Classes 6 to 8 in 14,801 government and government-aided schools have benefited. Under the scheme, 30,04,749 students were given eye examinations by appropriate medical specialists using medical equipment, and free spectacles were provided to 1,60,903 students found to have defective vision.

From 2010–11, the scheme was extended to students studying from Classes 6 to 10. Up to 28 February 2011, 39,60,467 students had been examined; eye defects were identified in 1,70,803 students, and spectacles were provided to 1,02,779 of them.

### Heart Protection Scheme for Young Children and Schoolchildren

To protect children and school students from poor and ordinary families against heart disease, the “Young Children's Heart Surgery Scheme” has been implemented from December 2007 and the “Schoolchildren's Heart Surgery Scheme” from 3 June 2008.

Under these two schemes, poor children have received free heart surgery in 27 renowned private hospitals. At the Institute of Child Health and Hospital for Children in Egmore, Chennai, 1,800 children have so far undergone heart surgery free of cost.

Apart from these schemes, 4,700 children living below the poverty line have received free surgical treatment under the Kalaignar Insurance Scheme for Life-Saving Advanced Treatment.

Taking together the Kalaignar Insurance Scheme, the Young Children's Heart Protection Scheme and the Schoolchildren's Heart Protection Scheme, the total number of children who have so far undergone heart surgery is 9,800.

### Two pilot schemes
### Heart Disease Prevention Scheme

Under the “Heart Disease Prevention Scheme,” a pilot project implemented for the past three years in Sivaganga and Virudhunagar districts, approximately 8 lakh people over the age of 30 in Virudhunagar district and approximately 5 lakh people in Sivaganga district have been screened for high blood pressure and diabetes. They have also been advised about lifestyle practices to prevent heart disease, while those found to have heart disease receive appropriate treatment. The Tamil Nadu Health Project, implemented with World Bank financial assistance, is being extended to the other districts of Tamil Nadu with an allocation of ₹100 crore.

Another pilot project, the “Cervical Cancer Prevention Scheme,” implemented in Thanjavur and Theni districts, is to be expanded to all districts at a cost of ₹100 crore.

The Tamil Nadu State Health Society launched an innovative programme called “Nalamaana Tamizhagam” on 26 November 2010. Health inspectors and Village Health Nurses go from house to house in every village in Tamil Nadu and examine people over the age of 30 for high blood pressure and diabetes, with arrangements for treatment. If high blood pressure, diabetes or similar conditions are detected, arrangements are made for treatment at Primary Health Centres. So far, 45,982 camps have been held. A total of 21 lakh and 75 people participated. High blood pressure was identified in 3.18 lakh people, diabetes in 2.90 lakh, and both conditions in 1.82 lakh; in all, 7.90 lakh people were referred to Primary Health Centres.

### Primary Health Centres that are among the best functioning in India

To ensure that rural people can obtain maternity services free of cost at all times, 24-hour delivery services have been implemented in all 1,589 Primary Health Centres in Tamil Nadu. Three nurses have been appointed in each Primary Health Centre for this purpose. During the past four and a half years, funds from the National Rural Health Mission have been used to strengthen infrastructure and operating theatres at Primary Health Centres and to provide modern medical equipment.

As a result of the various measures taken by this government, the number of deliveries taking place in Primary Health Centres has risen year after year and is now more than three times what it was. As a consequence, the number of deliveries in private hospitals has been declining significantly. During AIADMK rule in 2005–06, the number of deliveries in Primary Health Centres was only 82,532. But this rose to 2,98,253 in 2009–10. In 2010–11, up to the end of February, 2,55,877 deliveries took place. In the past five years alone, 10,49,988 deliveries have taken place.

It is in Tamil Nadu that the largest number of deliveries and caesarean operations take place in Primary Health Centres in all of India. In 2009–10, 2,694 caesarean operations were performed. In 2010–11, up to February 2011, 4,363 caesarean operations took place. In the past five years alone, 8,708 caesarean operations have been successfully completed in Primary Health Centres.

Primary Health Centres previously had no facility for providing blood. Arrangements have now been ordered for blood-storage facilities, and blood-storage centres have so far been established in 255 Primary Health Centres. Washing machines have been provided at a cost of ₹9.66 crore so that clean, laundered clothes can be supplied to inpatients in Primary Health Centres.

Pregnant women who come to Primary Health Centres for examinations and women who undergo family-planning surgery are provided three meals a day free of cost for three days. Under this scheme, begun on 15 September 2008, 20,94,023 mothers had benefited up to 28 February 2011. It is only in Tamil Nadu that a scheme providing nutritious food to pregnant women at Primary Health Centres is being implemented. It is also only in Tamil Nadu that pregnant women at Primary Health Centres are tested for gestational diabetes and given treatment.

To make medical facilities easily available to rural people, 166 new Primary Health Centres have been opened since 2006 at a cost of ₹66.42 crore.

இலவசமாகக் கிடைக்க வேண்டும் என்பதற்காகத் தமிழகத்தில்

உள்ள 1,589 ஆரம்ப சுகாதார நிலையங்களிலும் 24 மணிநேர
பிரசவ சேவைத் திட்டம் நடைமுறைப்படுத்தப்பட்டுள்ளது.
இதற்காக ஒவ்வொரு ஆரம்ப சுகாதார நிலையத்திலும் மூன்று
செவிலியர்கள் நியமனம் செய்யப்பட்டுள்ளனர். கடந்த நான்கரை
ஆண்டுகளில் தேசிய ஊரக நலவாழ்வு இயக்கத்தின் நிதியில்
ஆரம்ப சுகாதார நிலையங்களின் கட்டமைப்புகள் மற்றும் அறுவை
அரங்குகள் வலுப்படுத்தப்பட்டு; நவீன மருத்துவக் கருவிகளும்
வழங்கப்பட்டுள்ளன. இந்த அரசு எடுத்துவரும் பல்வேறு
நடவடிக்கைகளால், ஆரம்ப சுகாதார நிலையங்களில் நிகழும்
பிரசவங்களின் எண்ணிக்கை ஆண்டுக்கு ஆண்டு உயர்ந்து
தற்போது மூன்று மடங்கிற்கு மேலாக அதிகரித்துள்ளது. இதன்
காரணமாக தனியார் மருத்துவமனைகளில் நிகழும் பிரசவங்களின்
எண்ணிக்கை கணிச மாகக் குறைந்து வருகிறது. ஆரம்ப சுகாதார
நிலையங்களில் 2005-06ஆம் ஆண்டில் அ.தி.மு.க. ஆட்சிக்
காலத்தில் நடந்த பிரசவங் களின் எண்ணிக்கை 82 ஆயிரத்து 532
மட்டுமே. ஆனால், இந்த எண்ணிக்கை 2009-10ஆம் ஆண்டில் 2
இலட்சத்து 98 ஆயிரத்து 253 ஆக உயர்ந்துள்ளது. 2010-11 ஆம்
ஆண்டில் பிப்ரவரி மாதம் முடிய 2 இலட்சத்து 55 ஆயிரத்து 877
பிரசவங்கள் நிகழ்ந்துள்ளன. கடந்த 5 ஆண்டுகளில் மட்டும் 10
இலட்சத்து 49 ஆயிரத்து 988 பிரசவங்கள் நிகழ்ந்துள்ளன.
இந்தியாவிலேயே தமிழ்நாட்டில்தான் ஆரம்ப சுகாதார
நிலையங்களில் அதிக அளவில் பிரசவங்களும், சிசேரியன் அறுவை
சிகிச்சையும் நடைபெறுகின்றது. 2009-10ஆம் ஆண்டில் 2,694
சிசேரியன் அறுவை சிகிச்சைகள் செய்யப்பட்டுள்ளன. 2010-
11ஆம் ஆண்டில் பிப்ரவரி-2011 வரை 4,363 சிசேரியன் அறுவை
சிகிச்சைகள் நடந்துள்ளன. இதுவரை கடந்த 5 ஆண்டுகளில்
மட்டும் 8,708 சிசேரியன் அறுவை சிகிச்சைகள் ஆரம்ப சுகாதார
நிலையங்களில் வெற்றிகரமாக நடத்தி முடிக்கப்பட்டுள்ளன.
ஆரம்ப சுகாதார நிலையங்களில் இரத்தம் அளிக்கும் வசதி
இல்லாமல் இருந்தது. தற்போது, இரத்தம் சேமித்து வைக்கும்
வசதிகளையும் ஏற்படுத்திட ஆணையிட்டு; இதுவரை, 255
ஆரம்ப சுகாதார நிலையங்களில் இரத்த சேமிப்பு நிலையங்கள்
ஏற்படுத்தப்பட்டுள்ளன. ஆரம்ப சுகாதார நிலையங்களில்
உள்நோயாளிகளுக்கு சுத்தமாக சலவை செய்து துணிகள் வழங்க
9 கோடியே 66 இலட்ச ரூபாய்ச் செலவில் சலவை இயந்திரங்கள்

வழங்கப்பட்டுள்ளன. ஆரம்ப சுகாதார நிலையங்களுக்குப்
பரிசோதனைக்காக வரும் கர்ப்பிணிப் பெண்கள் மற்றும் குடும்ப நல
அறுவை சிகிச்சை செய்து கொள்ளும் பெண்கள் ஆகியோர்க்குத்
தினமும் மூன்று வேளை உணவு, மூன்று நாட்களுக்கு இலவசமாக
வழங்கப்படுகிறது. 15.9.2008இல் தொடங்கப்பட்ட இந்தத்
திட்டத்தில் 28.2.2011 வரை 20 இலட்சத்து 94 ஆயிரத்து
23 தாய்மார்கள் பயனடைந்துள்ளனர். இந்தியாவிலேயே
தமிழ்நாட்டில்தான் ஆரம்ப சுகாதார நிலையங்களில் கர்ப்பிணிப்
பெண்களுக்கு சத்துணவு வழங்கும் திட்டம் செயல்படுத்தப்படு
கிறது. ஆரம்ப சுகாதார நிலையங்களில் கர்ப்பிணிப் பெண்களுக்குக்
கர்ப்பகாலச் சர்க்கரை நோய் உள்ளதா எனப் பரிசோதனை செய்து
சிகிச்சை அளிப்பதும் தமிழகத்தில் மட்டும்தான்.
கிராமப்பகுதி மக்களுக்கு எளிதில் மருத்துவ வசதிகள்
கிடைக்க வேண்டும் என்பதற்காக 2006க்குப் பின் 66 கோடியே 42
இலட்ச ரூபாய்ச் செலவில் 166 புதிய ஆரம்ப சுகாதார நிலையங்கள்
தொடங்கப்பட்டுள்ளன.
