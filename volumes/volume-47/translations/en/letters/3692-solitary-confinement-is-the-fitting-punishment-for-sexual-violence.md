# 3692. Solitary Confinement Is the Fitting Punishment for Sexual Violence!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This thought-preserving translation retains the source's list of reported sexual-violence cases, punishment argument, criticism of the State Government, legal references, figures, quotations and rhetorical force. Tamil remains authoritative. `Udanpirappē` is retained as Kalaignar's distinctive address.

## English translation

Udanpirappē,

In Thoothukudi, student Punitha was subjected to sexual violence and brutally murdered.

In Thatharavalasai village near Harur, tribal woman Menaka was sexually assaulted and murdered.

Near Tirupattur, nine-year-old girl Gayathri was raped and murdered.

Suguna near Virudhachalam;

twenty-one-year-old Sandhya in Chidambaram;

Mariammal, aged 27, in Thoothukudi, sexually assaulted;

Vimala near Nannilam;

a young woman named Malar in Koyambedu—

just as in the horrific incident involving a student in Delhi, reports of sexual assault have continued to emerge from Tamil Nadu over the past several days.

A public-interest petition was filed asking the High Court to take suo motu cognisance of these continuing incidents of sexual violence in Tamil Nadu. The petition sought a detailed inquiry into the measures taken by the police in connection with the continuing sexual-assault cases. Justices Venkataraman and Vasuki, who heard the case, issued notice on 28 December 2012 directing the Tamil Nadu Government to reply by 4 January 2013.

When I was shocked to hear that the medical-college student in Delhi who had been subjected to sexual violence had died, I issued a condolence message on 30 December 2012. Even then I said:

> “Not merely in some isolated place, but across India—and particularly in Tamil Nadu in recent times—reports have continuously appeared of students and poor women being subjected to such cruel sexual violence. The severe punishment for those who engage in such acts should be to confine them in solitary imprisonment for the rest of their lives. The Central and State Governments must take a firm decision on this.”

While people belonging to various political parties have been saying that those who commit sexual atrocities should receive the death penalty, I alone expressed the view that the severe punishment for such offenders should be solitary confinement for life, and that the Central and State Governments should take a firm decision on it. If I say this, will Amma accept it? She immediately says sexual offenders should be detained under the Goondas Act. If arrested under that Act, those offenders will come out within a year. There is no guarantee that, after coming out, they will not commit another sexual offence. Like social thinkers and legal experts, I have never accepted the death penalty. That is why I stated my view that the punishment appropriate to this crime is “solitary confinement for life.” Even if the State Government refuses to weigh this view carefully, I expect the Central Government to take appropriate action.

But as far as the Tamil Nadu Government is concerned, action in these sexual-violence cases appears to stop at merely registering a case at the police station for form's sake. In the case of Punitha, a thirteen-year-old seventh-standard student in Thoothukudi district who was raped and murdered, the DMK announced and conducted a protest. On behalf of the party, ₹2 lakh was provided; district secretary Periyasamy gave ₹50,000; Member of Parliament Jeyadurai gave ₹1 lakh; and Rajya Sabha member and poet Kanimozhi personally went, gave assistance and consoled the girl's mother. Only after that did the Tamil Nadu Government wake up and send some minister. Apart from that one incident, in the other sexual-assault cases I do not know of the Tamil Nadu Government taking any action beyond registering cases.

The CPM has submitted a request to the District Collector that ₹10 lakh in relief be provided to the family of tribal girl Menaka, who was subjected to sexual violence in Hosur on 18 December 2012. Likewise, the Tamil Nadu Government should quickly provide appropriate relief to all families affected by sexual violence in the State. Instead, if a thirteen-point plan is merely announced on paper, a few statements welcoming it may appear in newspapers—but whether it will provide real relief to those affected or prevent sexual atrocities from occurring remains a question.

After Jayalalithaa left for Kodanadu on 1 January 2013, a lengthy announcement appeared in her name. As always, the announcement of a thirteen-point plan to suppress crimes against women was followed by praise and publicity! At the beginning of this letter I listed the sexual assaults and murders that had occurred during the previous few days. In this situation, Amma now says she is going to make Tamil Nadu a State without sexual assault! This too can be added to the list of many promises that have not been fulfilled.

The first three points in the Chief Minister's thirteen-point plan are administrative announcements about who will investigate and take action in sexual-violence cases—both future cases and those already pending. The fourth point is the “Goondas Act.” That law is already suffering badly under this Government. It has been invoked against those they dislike and against opposition members, who have been arrested, imprisoned and tormented, only to obtain justice through the courts. Now, in the name of sexual assault, they propose to amend the Goondas Act further, opening another provision that can be used to settle cruel scores against everyone they dislike.

The Goondas Act has fallen into the hands of our Chief Minister and is suffering terribly. Whatever the issue in Tamil Nadu, it is the Goondas Act. When she came to power earlier, she held the POTA law in her hand, arrested those who opposed her and happily kept them in prison for six or nine months. Those who were imprisoned then now seem to seek absolution for their sins by remembering it and praising “Amma.” Generally, the Goondas Act had been used against those who repeatedly committed offences—people for whom committing crime had become habitual. A few days ago she announced that the law would be amended so that even a person committing for the first time some offence said to disturb public order could be detained under the Goondas Act, and that those committing “cyber” crimes would also be brought under it. That amendment has not even been enacted yet. Before that, she has said the Goondas Act can also be applied to those committing sexual violence. Instead of invoking the Goondas Act separately for everything, why not consider one blanket amendment allowing our Chief Minister to use it against everyone she dislikes? Even if such a decision were taken, there are people in Tamil Nadu ready to issue statements welcoming it and to publish large advertisements and news reports in the newspapers.

This is not merely my view. In an article in the weekly *Kalki*, a writer named Priyan discussed the Goondas Act in Tamil Nadu. The article quotes Madras High Court advocate Balu as saying: “The Chief Minister's announcement has already created a stir in judicial circles. Even now the Goondas Act is being used for political vendetta. Therefore, intensive consultation is necessary before bringing in this amendment.” The *Kalki* article goes on to say:

> “The Goondas Act has been in force since 1982, during M.G.R.'s rule. Fundamentally, if a person repeatedly engages in various offences—such as brewing illicit liquor, selling ganja, selling banned lottery tickets, or disturbing peace in public places—the law enables that person to be arrested and imprisoned for up to one year without being allowed to come out on bail. But what happens in practice? This law is used according to the likes and dislikes of ruling politicians and police officials.”

One point in the Chief Minister's announcement says steps will be taken to establish women's fast-track courts in every district to hear sexual-violence cases. Another says the Government will bear medical-treatment expenses for women affected by sexual violence. In addition to bearing such medical expenses, this Government should come forward to provide compensation to women who have already been affected by sexual violence during its tenure.

This thirteen-point plan should not become merely one more announcement among the announcements issued every day like daily newspapers. This Government must genuinely come forward to provide proper assistance to people affected by sexual atrocities and to prevent such incidents. Let us wait a few more days and see how this Government acts!

Considering the AIADMK rulers' past record of failing to respect the law, failing to give due regard to the administration of justice, turning legal rules upside down and displaying a special talent for misusing laws, is it not natural for anyone to wonder how they are going to implement these announcements concerning sexual offences?

With affection,
M.K.
06-01-2013
