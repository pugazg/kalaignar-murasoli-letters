# 3482. The Lady’s Baseless Accusation and the Firm Reply!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This is Part 1 of the volume’s closing two-letter rebuttal to Jayalalithaa’s Coimbatore speech. It preserves the author’s political accusations as source-attributed claims and, importantly, carries the complete comparative data series: departmental allocations, new institutions, food-grain and paddy production, agricultural concessions and fertiliser distribution. Figures are reproduced individually rather than summarised.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3482](../../../chapters/3482-ammaniyin-aathaaramatra-kutrachattum-aanitharamaana-pathilum.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 386](../../../pages/page-386.md)–[PDF 393](../../../pages/page-393.md)  
**Date:** 16 July 2010

**Udanpirappē,**

After the 2006 Legislative Assembly general election, the Leader of the Opposition Jayalalithaa spent the past four years resting alternately at the Kodanadu Estate and Siruthavur mansion, “six months in the forest and six months in the country,” as the source puts it, and treating the daily signing of statements written by someone else as her political work. As next year’s election approaches, the author says, she had a long article written in the name of a speech, like the old dramatic opening “Aho, come, child,” convened a protest public meeting in Coimbatore and read it there for more than an hour without pause.

Because the World Classical Tamil Conference had been held in Coimbatore with learned people, elders, scholars and intellectuals from many countries, bringing Tamils from around the world together and drawing lakhs of people who stayed for five days while important resolutions and decisions were made, Jayalalithaa chose Coimbatore itself for her protest meeting and brought party workers there from across Tamil Nadu, the source says.

That is her party-building work. But, with the sense that it is the duty of those in Government to answer what the author describes as her many baseless and false allegations, I wish both to show the people what kind of accusations she raised and to set out a summary of the achievements of the DMK Government during the past four and a half years.

First, in all her statements during the past four years, Jayalalithaa has described the DMK Government as a “minority DMK Government.” She repeated the phrase two or three times in her Coimbatore speech. If the DMK Government truly was a minority Government, the author asks, why did she not, if she had the courage, bring a no-confidence motion against it and prove her majority? What standing does she have to call it a minority Government without doing so? The people of Tamil Nadu may judge.

Next, Jayalalithaa says Tamil Nadu has not developed under DMK rule, the Tamil language has not developed, and Tamils have not developed. Is that true?

The author says one need only compare how much was allocated to each department under Jayalalithaa’s Government with how much is allocated in the current year to see whether Tamil Nadu has developed.

**Agriculture**
- 2005–06 under Jayalalithaa: **₹801 crore**
- 2009–10 under the DMK Government: **₹1,836 crore**

**Rural Development**
- 2005–06: **₹2,828 crore**
- 2009–10: **₹5,665 crore**

**School Education**
- 2005–06: **₹4,110 crore**
- 2009–10: **₹9,148 crore**

**Social Welfare**
- 2005–06: **₹654 crore**
- 2009–10: **₹2,167 crore**

**Higher Education**
- 2005–06: **₹745 crore**
- 2009–10: **₹1,463 crore**

**Health and Family Welfare**
- 2005–06: **₹1,487 crore**
- 2009–10: **₹3,391 crore**

**Adi Dravidar Welfare**
- 2005–06: **₹487 crore**
- 2009–10: **₹876 crore**

**Highways**
- 2005–06: **₹2,381 crore**
- 2009–10: **₹4,383 crore**

**Police**
- 2005–06: **₹1,346 crore**
- 2009–10: **₹2,855 crore**

Although departmental allocations normally increase year by year, the author argues that the scale of the increase in four years of DMK rule itself shows Tamil Nadu’s development to anyone who understands the details.

After 2006, the source lists four new Anna Universities of Technology at Coimbatore, Tiruchirappalli, Tirunelveli and Madurai; **14 new Government Arts and Science Colleges** at Orathanadu, Perambalur, Valparai, Surandai, Kulithalai, Lalgudi, Mettur, Pudukkottai, Theni, Tiruvannamalai, Villupuram, Pennagaram, Tirupattur (Vellore) and Vedaranyam; **six new medical colleges** at Villupuram, Tiruvarur, Dharmapuri, Sivaganga, Perambalur and Tiruvannamalai; and new Government engineering colleges in **11 districts** — Tindivanam, Villupuram, Panruti, Ariyalur, Thirukkuvalai, Ramanathapuram, Tiruvannamalai, Thanjavur, Dindigul, Thoothukudi and Kanyakumari.

It also lists the law making Tamil compulsory in schools through Standard X, Tamil’s classical-language status, the Central Institute of Classical Tamil in Chennai, the World Classical Tamil Conference, the elevation of Erode, Tiruppur, Vellore and Thoothukudi municipalities into corporations, and the new Ariyalur and Tiruppur districts. Are all these, the author asks, signs that Tamil Nadu, Tamil and Tamils have not developed? He says an angry Jayalalithaa must explain.

Jayalalithaa, in her speech, said agricultural production had sharply declined because there was no irrigation water; that water due from neighbouring States had not come; that no effort had been made to obtain Cauvery water; and that the reason was that members of Karunanidhi’s family were doing business there and pressure on Karnataka would harm their businesses. The author asks what connection there is between members of his family conducting business in Karnataka and asking the Karnataka Government for water.

In fact, he says, agricultural production declined during Jayalalithaa’s Government, while after the DMK assumed office production increased year by year. For the public to know the truth, he gives the following total food-production figures:

- **2000–01, DMK:** 86.17 lakh metric tonnes
- **2001–02, AIADMK:** 76.89 lakh metric tonnes
- **2002–03, AIADMK:** 44.61 lakh metric tonnes
- **2003–04, AIADMK:** 43.12 lakh metric tonnes
- **2004–05, AIADMK:** 61.46 lakh metric tonnes
- **2005–06, AIADMK:** 61.17 lakh metric tonnes

The author argues that food production remained low throughout those five AIADMK years. Turning Jayalalithaa’s style of argument back on her, he asks whether someone could therefore say the fall occurred because she failed to secure Cauvery water properly from neighbouring States and, being ancestrally from Karnataka, did not press that Government for water.

Jayalalithaa, the author says, repeatedly accuses his family and relatives in petty fashion. What is to be done? He has a family: some members are in politics, some in business. If being in politics is wrong and being in business is wrong, what are those family members supposed to do? Although Jayalalithaa may not have what he calls a family of her own, did she not make another family her family and concern herself with its advancement? Did she not give one member of that family a Rajya Sabha seat and make that person a leading party figure? Did she not call another a foster son and conduct his wedding like a conference?

After the DMK Government returned in 2006, the source credits measures including the **₹7,000-crore agricultural-loan waiver**, interest-free crop loans, incentive-linked higher procurement prices for paddy and sugarcane, and interest waiver on farmers’ non-farm loans. It gives the following food-production series:

- **2006–07:** 82.64 lakh metric tonnes
- **2007–08:** 84.46 lakh metric tonnes
- **2008–09:** 91.11 lakh metric tonnes
- **2009–10:** 95.60 lakh metric tonnes

The author argues that these figures show a step-by-step rise, not the fall described in the Coimbatore speech.

He then gives paddy production separately. Under the DMK in **2000–01**, paddy production was **73.66 lakh tonnes**. Under the AIADMK it was:

- **2001–02:** 65.84 lakh metric tonnes
- **2002–03:** 35.77 lakh metric tonnes
- **2003–04:** 32.23 lakh metric tonnes
- **2004–05:** 50.62 lakh metric tonnes
- **2005–06:** 52.10 lakh metric tonnes

After the DMK Government took office in 2006, the source says paddy production rose to **66.11 lakh metric tonnes in 2006–07** and to **71.50 lakh metric tonnes in 2009–10**.

Likewise, the author says statistics show that production of small millets and pulses was higher during DMK rule than under the AIADMK. With the truth standing thus, he asks whether Tamil Nadu’s people will not recognise the Coimbatore claims as a bundle of falsehoods.

Fertiliser plays an important role in increasing agricultural production. The source says Tamil Nadu farmers received more fertiliser than under the previous AIADMK Government. Tamil Nadu uses on average **20 to 24 lakh metric tonnes** of urea, DAP, potash and complex fertilisers each year.

For the five AIADMK years **2001–02 to 2005–06**, the source gives average distribution of **7.3 lakh metric tonnes of urea**, **2.24 lakh metric tonnes of DAP**, **2.94 lakh metric tonnes of potash** and **3.54 lakh metric tonnes of complex fertiliser**.

For the four DMK years **2006–07 to 2009–10**, it gives averages of **9.88 lakh metric tonnes of urea**, **3.43 lakh metric tonnes of DAP**, **4.99 lakh metric tonnes of potash** and **3.93 lakh metric tonnes of complex fertilisers**.

Thus, compared with the five AIADMK years, the four DMK years distributed an additional **2.54 lakh metric tonnes of urea**, **1.19 lakh metric tonnes of DAP**, **2.05 lakh metric tonnes of potash** and **0.40 lakh metric tonnes of complex fertiliser**. The author concludes that this shows the Government supplied farmers’ key input generously and concentrated on improving food-grain production in Tamil Nadu.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**16-7-2010**

## Letter-specific notes

1. All claims about Jayalalithaa, her political conduct, family connections, Karnataka and agricultural causation are translated as the author’s claims and rhetorical rebuttal.
2. Departmental allocations are kept as a nine-category comparison rather than compressed.
3. Total-food-production and paddy-production series are preserved year by year.
4. The canonical PDF 393 prints `3.54 மெட்ரிக் டன்` for the earlier-period complex-fertiliser average, while the surrounding series uses lakh metric tonnes and later comparisons use `0.40 இலட்சம்`; the Tamil appendix preserves the canonical wording exactly. The English renders the apparent intended unit as **3.54 lakh metric tonnes** only within the source’s statistical series and does not alter canonical Tamil.
