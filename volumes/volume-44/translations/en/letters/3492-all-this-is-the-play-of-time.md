**Date:** 18 August 2010

**Udanpirappē,**

In 1994, a private individual filed a case in the Supreme Court of India contending that the law enacted in Tamil Nadu to provide 69 per cent reservation was invalid. Nearly sixteen years later, only on 13-7-2010, did the Supreme Court deliver an interim judgment.

In that interim judgment it was stated:

> “It has been laid down that if a
> State wants to exceed 50 percent reservation, then it is required to base
> its decision on the quantifiable data. In the present case, this exercise
> has not been done. Therefore, keeping in mind the said parameter,
> we direct the State to place the quantifiable data before the Tamil
> Nadu State Backward Classes Commission and, on the basis of such
> quantifiable data amongst other things, the Commission will decide the
> quantum of Reservation”

That is to say, if a State wishes to exceed the 50 per cent level of reservation, its decision must be based on statistical data needed to justify doing so. In this case the work of collecting such data had not been undertaken. Therefore, keeping in mind the already stated criterion, the State government was directed to collect the necessary data and place it before the Tamil Nadu State Backward Classes Commission; and, on the basis of caste-wise statistical data, among other matters, the Commission was to determine the quantum of reservation.

If the Tamil Nadu government is to collect these statistics separately, we need caste-wise population data. For that, a caste-wise census — `(Caste-Based Census)` — must be conducted.

If such a caste-wise census were to be conducted in Tamil Nadu alone, around ₹400 crore would be needed. The Tamil Nadu Backward Classes Commission has expressed the view that this funding could be sought from the Union Government. While the Tamil Nadu government has been considering this, the Union Government has itself been examining the conduct of a caste-wise census on an all-India basis, and matters have progressed to the point of considering acceptance of it. If the Union Government itself conducts a caste-wise census, we must consider whether Tamil Nadu needs a separate census or whether Tamil Nadu’s figures would be covered in the Union census. There are also two differing views as to whether the Union Government’s census should proceed on the basis of a biometric census or as a caste-wise census.

Even on this point, the founder of the PMK is reported in today’s *Dina Thanthi* as having said at a function that “Chief Minister Karunanidhi has said that the caste-wise census may be started under the biometric census method.” I do not know on what basis he has expressed that view. As far as we are concerned, the moment this issue arose, I wrote to the Prime Minister of India on 14-8-2010, even before anyone asked me to do so, and before those who claim to have raised their voices for social justice before me had announced that they would hold demonstrations and agitations. In that letter I wrote:

> “I firmly
> believe that caste-based census will certainly go a long way in ensuring
> that the fruits of the various developmental schemes implemented by
> the Union Government and the State Govern-ment fully percolate
> down to the lower strata of the Society. Hence the urgent need of the
> hour is to incorporate the Caste-based details in the on-going Census
> 2011 itself without postponing it to bio-metric stage. I am given to
> understand that a few changes in the existing census format by the
> experts concerned will be sufficient to achieve the object of caste-
> based census and accomplish the task early”

In other words, I clearly wrote that I firmly believed a caste-wise census was essential if the benefits of the various development schemes implemented by the State and Union governments were to reach fully even the lowest strata; that the urgent need was to incorporate caste-wise enumeration into the census already under way rather than postpone it in the name of a biometric census; and that I understood the necessary caste-wise information could be collected by making only a few changes to the existing census form. Thus I stressed both the necessity and urgency of the caste-wise census.

Justice M. N. Rao, Chairman of the National Commission for Backward Classes, has also said: “The inequalities created by caste can be removed only by conducting a caste-wise census. If such a census is held, the statistics will show that even sixty-three years after Independence many communities have not advanced. Opposition to a caste-wise census arises because, if this becomes known, those communities will demand further concessions.” We may keep that in mind as well.

Scholars who recently participated in a seminar in Bengaluru have explained that the caste-wise population census should be entrusted to the Census organisation itself and should not be combined with the biometric process. In the letter they sent to the Union Government on the basis of those explanations, they stated that where outside organisations have scope to participate in the biometric process, there may also be opportunities for those organisations to compromise the caste-wise population-enumeration work.

The reason, they said, is that the enormous task of compiling biometric statistics has been entrusted to those organisations. If the work of compiling caste-wise population statistics were compromised, it would certainly defeat the purpose for which those statistics were being collected. All the census work would be completed by March 2011, whereas biometric data compilation might continue for some further time. Therefore, they said, the National Census organisation was the proper body to undertake the caste-wise population census, and the house-to-house population enumeration scheduled from 9 February to 28 February 2011 was the proper time to do it. The caste-wise census too should be entrusted to the tested and reliable Census organisation, and the struggles that could arise from linking it with the biometric-data process should be avoided. That view too cannot simply be brushed aside.

Dr. Ramadoss has demanded two per cent reservation for Vanniyars in Union Government employment. When his son Dr. Anbumani was a Union Minister, did he ever open his mouth on this matter in Parliament or in a Cabinet meeting?

Newspapers have reported Dr. Ramadoss as asking, “Can Karunanidhi speak about social justice?” and “Can Chief Minister Karunanidhi, or other leaders of Dravidian parties, speak about social justice?”, and as saying that only after the Vanniyar Sangam was formed, major agitations were conducted and thirty lives were sacrificed were the Most Backward Classes separately classified and twenty per cent reservation granted. He may indeed have struggled for that twenty per cent reservation. But although Dr. Ramadoss conceals the history that the agitation was conducted during the AIADMK period and that, once the DMK came to power, it respected that agitation, called him for talks and granted a separate twenty per cent reservation for the Most Backward Classes, those who have benefited from that twenty per cent reservation will certainly not forget that family financial assistance was provided to the families of the thirty who died in that agitation and monthly pensions were arranged for them.

Dr. Ramadoss may, if he wishes, ask whether leaders of Dravidian parties can speak about social justice. But the party born for social justice itself was the Justice Party, the root party of the Dravidian movements. It was under Justice Party rule that, for the first time, the historic reservation order known as the first Communal G.O. was issued in 1921. If neither I, nor Tamilar Thalaivar Veeramani, nor others belonging to the Dravidian movement are to speak about social justice, Dr. Ramadoss himself could have understood who else is qualified to speak about it had he paused to think before speaking.

When I was Leader of the Opposition, Dr. Ramadoss took me to Villupuram, placed a single chair for me, and proclaimed that social justice could be protected only if Kalaignar became Chief Minister. If that same Dr. Ramadoss now asks whether Karunanidhi may speak about social justice, all this is indeed the play of time!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**18-8-2010**
