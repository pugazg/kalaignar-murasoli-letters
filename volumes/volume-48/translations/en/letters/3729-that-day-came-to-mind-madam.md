# 3729. That Day Came Back to Mind, Madam!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 9 April 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

In a statement read under Rule 110 in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly on 4 April 2013, Chief Minister Jayalalithaa announced with pleasure that, taking account of the growing housing need in Madurai district, an integrated **satellite town** would be developed on 586.86 acres belonging to the Tamil Nadu Housing Board in Thoppur and Uchapatti villages, on the Madurai–Tirunelveli four-lane road near the airport, about fifteen kilometres from Madurai. The statement said that 19,500 housing plots would be created there, and continued at length.

No sooner had the Chief Minister read this great announcement and sat down than representatives of the Republican Party of India, Forward Bloc, All India Samathuva Makkal Katchi, Manithaneya Makkal Katchi and Communist Party of India rose to praise the announcement and express their thanks. It did not end there. The minister responsible for the department stood up and heaped thanks for the announcement concerning his own department at the golden feet of **“Amma.”** Even then it did not end. The Speaker rose and joined his own thanks to the congratulations offered for the Chief Minister's announcement.

As usual, every newspaper in Tamil Nadu published the announcement prominently.

Reading it brought a few memories flowing through my mind.

When the AIADMK came to power in 2011, a similar headline appeared in all the Tamil Nadu newspapers on 9 September 2011: **“Housing on 311 Acres at Thirumazhisai—Satellite Town Near Chennai—Jayalalithaa Announcement in the Assembly.”**

As far as we are concerned, whether at Thirumazhisai or Madurai, we are happy that a satellite town is to be created. After all, the land on which they propose to create the Thirumazhisai satellite town was acquired by the Housing Board during the DMK government.

There was one curiosity even then. Jayalalithaa announced in the Assembly on 9 September 2011 that a satellite town would be developed on 311 acres at Thirumazhisai, and representatives of all parties immediately stood up and praised it. But the project had already been announced on 25 August 2011 in the policy note placed before the Assembly by Housing Minister R. Vaithilingam. Poor man—if he announced it, who would take notice? Only when **“J”** announced it did it acquire great “value” and become known to the outside world.

Thus on 9 September 2011 Chief Minister Jayalalithaa announced under Rule 110, as though creating and announcing it afresh that very day, a project that had already appeared earlier in the department's policy note.

But what became of that announcement? Has the satellite town at Thirumazhisai been built? Two years have passed. As far as we know, even the foundation-stone ceremony has not yet taken place.

The idea of a satellite town adjoining Chennai did not arise only then. It arose during the DMK government too. Soon after the DMK assumed office in 2006, a statement was made in the Assembly on 1 September 2006. It said there were 1.40 lakh acres south of the Vandalur–Kelambakkam road, between Old Mahabalipuram Road and National Highway 45. The Chennai Metropolitan Development Authority had provisionally identified 30,000 acres. About 13,000 families lived there. The 270-square-kilometre area had a clean and good environment suitable for a new town. Most of the land was uncultivated and fallow. When acquiring land, water-resource areas, residential areas and forest areas would be excluded; only fallow and dry lands would be acquired. Landowners would be paid a fair market price. Residents would not be displaced and could continue living where they were. Employment created by the new town would benefit local people and help improve their economic position.

No sooner was this announcement read in the Assembly during the DMK government than Jayalalithaa hurriedly issued a statement the very next day, 2 September 2006. In it she said, among other things:

> “Because ruling power has fallen into the hands of arrogant, petty creatures, anti-democratic and anti-people disorder has become a continuing occurrence in tradition-rich Tamil Nadu. Another example is the satellite-town announcement made near Chennai by Karunanidhi, Chief Minister of the minority DMK government.
>
> “When a Chief Minister lacks foresight and acts arbitrarily in this manner, these become base acts that make the heart tremble, and Karunanidhi and his party will have to pay the price. Karunanidhi says this satellite town is to be created to reduce crowding in Chennai city. First let his wife and children, grandchildren, relatives of his late first wife and those related through them, relatives of his present wife, and relatives through the companion he acquired all leave Gopalapuram and CIT Colony and reduce the crowding in those areas. Karunanidhi, who is so concerned about population congestion, should first recognise the congestion created by his own family members and send them out of Chennai city.
>
> “Karunanidhi seems very fond of the word ‘companion’ [*thunai*]; perhaps that is why he is searching for a ‘companion’ [satellite] for Chennai city too. Karunanidhi, who lives in luxury, has no opportunity to know the anguish of the people who are screaming in distress as soon as the satellite-town announcement appears. Reports keep coming that the Chennai satellite-town plan is being designed and created only to benefit his family, relatives, and grandsons Dayanidhi Maran and Kalanithi Maran.”

In that statement, Jayalalithaa spoke without giving even the slightest place to civility or culture, the letter says. Her statement remains available in old newspapers. Yet forgetting that very statement, she later announced a **satellite town** at Thirumazhisai and has now announced another at Madurai.

There is another irony. Even before Jayalalithaa issued her statement opposing the satellite town announced by the DMK, Dr. Ramadoss of the PMK, then an allied party of ours, had civilly said that such a satellite town could affect agricultural land and cause hardship to local residents. Within two days, on 2 September 2006, I issued a statement saying that the satellite-town plan adjoining Chennai—which had been announced in the DMK election manifesto, the Governor's Address and the Budget—was being dropped on the basis of goodwill, after considering the advice of an allied party, and also out of precaution so that innocent members of the public would not become victims of the openly planned violence of certain selfish, harmful forces.

Nevertheless, on 3 September 2006, the PMK's official newspaper *Tamil Osai* wrote an article under the headline **“Jayalalithaa's Statement—the Height of Incivility.”**

*Theekkathir*, newspaper of the Communist Party of India (Marxist), which is now an ally of the AIADMK, wrote in an editorial on 4 September 2006:

> “Chief Minister Karunanidhi has announced that the plan to build a satellite town near Chennai is being cancelled. He has said the decision was taken in a spirit of goodwill and with respect for the feelings of allied parties. But when the previous AIADMK government tried to demolish Queen Mary's College and build the Secretariat on that site, students protested and every section of the public opposed it. Yet Jayalalithaa's government arrogantly and obstinately refused to abandon the plan. Comparison of that conduct with Chief Minister Kalaignar's present announcement is unavoidable.
>
> “It is painful that Jayalalithaa's statement opposing the satellite-town scheme was the height of political incivility. Opposition parties have every right to oppose a government scheme, but political civility must be maintained. In her statement Jayalalithaa used disgusting and uncivil words about Chief Minister Karunanidhi's family. The statement reveals only her hostility and distortion. That may be why even AIADMK members could not bear it when the Chief Minister [Kalaignar] read the statement aloud in the Assembly. The Legislative Assembly and the political arena are places for civilised debate, not for expressing rage and defamation. The words and actions of Jayalalithaa and her party prove that they have still not learned and reformed themselves after the people's verdict.”

At this moment I cannot help remembering those words.

Because I have mentioned all this, Jayalalithaa may immediately say: **“I opposed only the satellite town near Mahabalipuram; later I announced one at Thirumazhisai.”** But even at Thirumazhisai, the DMK government began efforts to establish a satellite town after assuming office in 2006. People of the area, farmers, opposition parties and allied parties expressed objections. Accepting their requests, the Tamil Nadu government announced on 21 December 2006 that wet and dry agricultural lands would not be acquired for the satellite-town project.

Had Jayalalithaa and some others not opposed the DMK's satellite-town announcement in 2006, a satellite town might have arisen during the DMK government itself and Chennai's congestion might have been reduced to some extent. When the DMK government announced a satellite town then, it was opposed. Now that Jayalalithaa has announced under Rule 110 that a satellite town will be built in Madurai, all her allied parties have welcomed and praised it.

Let us wait and see whether the Madurai satellite town comes into being quickly instead of suffering delay like Thirumazhisai. But I do not wish to issue a statement about Jayalalithaa's present **“satellite”** town in her own style—using the word *thunai* to vulgarise and ridicule it, as she did the day after the DMK government announced a satellite town. Instead, I have simply recalled the statement she herself issued then.

I feel only like singing: **“That day came back to mind—Madam! Madam!”**

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**9 April 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. The Tamil word `துணை` can mean **companion** and also functions in `துணை நகரம்`, **satellite town**. Jayalalithaa's quoted 2006 statement uses that double meaning as a personal taunt; Kalaignar's closing deliberately turns the wordplay back on her. The translation makes the pun explicit rather than inventing a different English insult.
2. The long 2006 statements attributed to Jayalalithaa, Kalaignar, *Tamil Osai* and *Theekkathir* are translated as source quotations. Their characterisations and political allegations are not independent assertions of this translation.
3. Land areas, family counts and the 586.86-acre / 19,500-plot Madurai figures are preserved exactly as stated in the source.
4. The final song-like line is translated only to the extent quoted in the source and provides the title's refrain.

சொல்லக்கூடும்! ஆனால் அதே திருமழிசையில் தி.மு.கழக ஆட்சிக்
காலத்திலேயே ஒரு துணை நகரம் அமைப்பதற்கான முயற்சிகளில்
2006ஆம் ஆண்டு பொறுப்புக்கு வந்ததும் தொடங்கினோம்.
அந்தப் பகுதி மக்களும், விவசாயிகளும், எதிர்க்கட்சிகளும்,

தோழமைக் கட்சிகளும் தங்கள் எதிர்ப்பினைத் தெரிவித்தார்கள்.
எனவே அவர்களின் கோரிக்கைகளை ஏற்று, 21-12-2006 அன்று
தமிழக அரசின் சார்பில் துணை நகரம் அமைக்கும் திட்டத்திற்காக
நஞ்செய், புஞ்செய் நிலங்களை கையகப்படுத்துவதில்லை என்று
தெரிவிக்கப்பட்டது.
இவ்வாறு 2006ஆம் ஆண்டு தி.மு. கழகம்; துணை நகரத்
திட்டத்தை அறிவித்தபோது ஜெயலலிதாவும், வேறு சிலரும்
அதனை எதிர்க்காமல் இருந்திருந்தால் கழக ஆட்சியிலேயே
துணை நகரமும் வந்திருக்கும், சென்னையின் இட நெரிசலும்
ஓரளவு குறைந்திருக்கும். ஆனால் அன்றைக்குத் தி.மு.கழக அரசு
துணை நகரத்தை அறிவித்தபோது எதிர்ப்பு கூறப்பட்டாலும்,
தற்போது ஜெயலலிதா அம்மையார் பேரவையில் 110வது
விதியின் கீழ் மதுரையிலே துணை நகரம் அமைக்கப் போவதாக
அறிவித்திருப்பதை அவருடைய தோழமைக் கட்சிகள்
எல்லாம் வரவேற்றுப் பாராட்டியிருக்கின்றன. மதுரை துணை
நகரமும், திருமழிசைபோல் காலதாமதமாகி விடாமல் விரைவில்
நடைமுறைக்கு வருகிறதா என்பதைப் பொறுத்திருந்து
பார்ப்போம்! ஆனால் தி.மு.க. ஆட்சியில் “துணை நகரத்தை”
அறிவித்தபோது, மறுநாளே அதுபற்றி “அநாகரிகமாக”
“கொச்சைப்படுத்தி”; “துணை” என்ற வார்த்தையை வைத்து
அறிக்கை விடுத்த ஜெயலலிதா அம்மையாரின் தற்போதைய
“துணை” நகரத்தைப் பற்றி நான் அவர் “பாணி”யிலே அறிக்கைவிட
விரும்பவில்லை. மாறாக அவர் விடுத்த அறிக்கையைத்தான்
தற்போது ஞாபகப்படுத்தியிருக்கிறேன். “அந்த நாள் ஞாபகம்
வந்ததே; அம்மையே! அம்மையே!” என்றுதான் பாடிடத்
தோன்றுகிறது.
