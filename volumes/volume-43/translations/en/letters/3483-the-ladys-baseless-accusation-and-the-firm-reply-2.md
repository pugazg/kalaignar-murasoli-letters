# 3483. The Lady’s Baseless Accusation and the Firm Reply! (2)

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This final source letter in Volume 43 continues the rebuttal begun in 3482. It addresses petroleum pricing and inflation, food subsidy and public-distribution measures, historical commodity-price comparisons, a newspaper report of falling pulse/oil prices, grocery packets, free LPG connections and rice quality. Political interpretations remain source-attributed; all quantities, prices, percentages and dates are preserved.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3483](../../../chapters/3483-ammaniyin-aathaaramatra-kutrachattum-aanitharamaana-pathilum-2.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 394](../../../pages/page-394.md)–[PDF 400](../../../pages/page-400.md)  
**Date:** 17 July 2010

**Udanpirappē,**

In her angry speech — forgive me, in her Coimbatore speech — Jayalalithaa spent a large portion talking about the rise in prices, particularly the increase in petroleum-product prices and the resulting rise in prices of other goods. Even an ordinary politician knows, the author says, that primary responsibility for general price rises and petroleum-price increases lies with the Union Government, while the State Government has only a limited share of responsibility.

Yet Jayalalithaa, after speaking at length about price rises and petroleum prices, did not say a single word about the Union Government responsible for them or about the Congress party holding the principal responsibility in that Government. Some major newspapers themselves noted, “Jayalalithaa did not criticise the Congress in her speech; she also avoided criticising the Central Government.” From this, the author says, one may understand her underlying intention. More importantly, he says, Left friends who say that because the DMK is allied with the Congress they cannot have a relationship with the DMK should understand it.

Let her attempt to deceive the Left later. In the Coimbatore speech, even while seeking to deceive her own party workers, she assured them that she herself would take care of the alliance question. At the same time she avoided criticising the Congress and the Union Government. She may think she can deceive the Left and party workers in this way. But Congress friends, the author says, will not so easily have forgotten her earlier criticism of Mrs Sonia Gandhi as lacking devotion to her husband and being unfit to rule India.

As a principal criticism of the State DMK Government, Jayalalithaa said in Coimbatore that human beings cannot live without food; that people go to the market for ingredients to cook; that rice alone is not enough and they need pulses, salt, chillies, spices and vegetables; and that kerosene or cooking gas is needed to cook them. She said the prices of all these goods had risen two or three times over the past four years. The author mocks this as a “great discovery” written out for her to read at the meeting.

Price rises, he says, are generally present in growing economies in all countries. They are not confined to Tamil Nadu but exist in many Indian States and indeed worldwide. At the same time, he says, one must also acknowledge that people’s purchasing power is increasing.

To protect ordinary poor people and middle-income groups from food-price increases, the DMK Government, from **15.9.2008**, has been providing rice through the Public Distribution System at **₹1 per kilogram**, described by the source as the lowest price in India.

Closely monitoring rises in pulses and cooking-oil prices, from May 2007 the Government began providing **toor dal, urad dal and palm oil** through fair-price shops at subsidised rates. The source says toor dal and palm oil were imported from abroad and purchased from other States to ensure steady supply in Tamil Nadu. VAT exemption on pulses and cooking oil has also continued.

Further, under the “low-priced grocery commodities scheme,” **10 grocery items are provided for ₹50**. The author asks whether Jayalalithaa forgot these measures when she said in Coimbatore that people need pulses and salt, or whether she assumed the people had forgotten them.

At a scale the author says no other State in the country had undertaken, the Government allocated **₹4,000 crore in 2009–10** to provide subsidised food commodities through fair-price shops under the Public Distribution System to all family-card holders.

Taking into account declining prices of pulses and sugar, **₹3,750 crore** has been allocated for food subsidy this year. By contrast, in the last year of Jayalalithaa’s Government, **2005–06**, the allocation was only **₹1,200 crore**, the source says.

To contain the rise in sugar prices, although the Union Government supplied only **10,832 tonnes** of sugar at levy price, the State Government has been buying the additional sugar needed by people at open-market prices and supplying **34,000 tonnes a month** to all cardholders at **₹13.50 per kilogram**.

What, the author asks, happened to prices during the Jayalalithaa Government whose former Chief Minister now speaks of price rises under this Government?

- Ponni boiled rice was **₹14/kg in 2001** when Jayalalithaa assumed office and rose to **₹20/kg by 2005** under her Government.
- Toor dal was **₹27/kg in 2001** and rose to **₹33 by 2004**.
- Groundnut oil was **₹38/kg in 2001** and rose to **₹56 by 2003**.
- Gingelly oil was **₹34 in 2001** and rose to **₹65 in 2003**.
- Coconut oil was **₹45 in 2001** and rose to **₹88 in 2005**.
- Tamarind was **₹22/kg in 2001** and rose to **₹41 in 2005**.

Thus prices rose year after year during Jayalalithaa’s Government too. I do not call that itself wrong, the author says. But when prices rose in this way, were measures taken under that Government to control them, as the DMK Government is now doing?

Indeed, the author says, because of measures taken by the DMK Government the prices of some essential commodities were declining. He reproduces the gist of a report that appeared two days earlier in what he calls a neutral newspaper, under the headline: **“Pulse, oil prices continue to fall — relief for housewives.”**

The report said that protests and blockades had occurred nationwide demanding control of price rises; that the Union Government banned online hoarding and allowed imports from abroad; and that essential-commodity prices consequently came somewhat under control.

It reported, among other things:

- toor dal that had sold at **₹100/kg** fell to **₹65**;
- sugar fell to **₹30**;
- first-grade toor dal that sold the previous month at **₹6,900/quintal** fell to **₹6,200**, while second-grade fell from **₹6,500 to ₹5,700**;
- first-grade urad dal fell from **₹8,000 to ₹7,600/quintal**, with first grade available at **₹79 retail**;
- first-grade moong dal fell from **₹9,400 to ₹8,000/quintal**;
- palm oil fell from **₹44 to ₹42**;
- sunflower oil from **₹52 to ₹49**;
- Agmark gingelly oil from **₹110/kg to ₹105**;
- coconut oil from **₹75/kg to ₹70**.

The author says Jayalalithaa concealed all such news, spoke at length in Coimbatore about price rises and blamed the DMK Government.

He then cites Government statistics to say that compared with the previous year the prices of several commodities had fallen: toor dal by **26.67 per cent**, chilli by **22.22 per cent**, coriander by **20.83 per cent**, mustard by **16.67 per cent**, among others.

Jayalalithaa also said that supplying rice alone was insufficient. This Government, the author replies, does not provide rice alone. With the stated aim and responsibility of protecting people from the impact of prices, every family card receives under the Special Public Distribution Scheme toor dal, urad dal and palm oil, together with a subsidised packet of ten grocery items: turmeric powder, chilli powder, coriander powder, bengal gram, fenugreek, mustard, fennel or white urad dal, cumin, pepper, cinnamon, clove or garam masala. So far, **1.80 crore packets** have been distributed.

Under the free LPG-connection scheme, from **2006–07 through 2009–10**, stoves with free gas connections were provided to **23 lakh beneficiaries** at a cost of **₹521.45 crore**. In **2010–11**, work was under way to provide **6 lakh** further free gas connections at a cost of **₹140 crore**.

For the Public Distribution System, the Union Government provides **2.96 lakh tonnes of rice every month** from the central pool. The source says this rice is supplied through the PDS only after it has been tested and found to meet the prescribed quality. In addition, when paddy procured in the Cauvery-delta districts of Thanjavur, Tiruvarur and Nagapattinam is milled and adjusted against the central pool, it too is supplied only after prescribed quality is tested.

Therefore, the rice supplied under the Public Distribution System is not low-quality rice, the author concludes. Tamil Nadu’s poor people, who obtain this rice every day and eat their fill, understand very well both the quality of the rice and, he says rhetorically, the quality of Jayalalithaa who criticises it.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**17-07-2010**
