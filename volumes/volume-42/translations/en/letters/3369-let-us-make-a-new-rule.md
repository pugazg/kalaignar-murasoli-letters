# 3369. Let Us Make a New Rule!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This letter turns on the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly’s Rule 110 and the distinction between the existing rule and any future amendment to it. The rule text, quoted Assembly proceedings, procedural claims and political criticism are translated in source order. The complete audited Tamil is reproduced below and remains authoritative.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3369](../../../chapters/3369-puthiyathor-vithi-seivom.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 50](../../../pages/page-050.md)–[PDF 55](../../../pages/page-055.md)  
**Date:** 22 February 2009

**Udanpirappē,**

You may often have seen news reports saying, “A statement was placed before the House under Legislative Assembly Rule 110.” What is this Assembly Rule 110? The Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly Rules book, dealing with how the Assembly is to function and the rules to be followed at that time, prints each rule in order and is given to all Members. We have followed those rules for many years. What does Rule 110 say in that sequence?

**Rule 110:**

**(1)** A Minister may, with the consent of the Speaker, make a statement on a matter of public importance.

**(2)** There shall be no debate on that statement at that time.

**(3)** A Minister who wishes to make a statement under sub-rule (1) shall, in advance, send to the Secretary of the Assembly the date on which the Minister wishes to make the statement and a copy of it for the Speaker’s consideration.

That is Assembly Rule 110. It is under this rule that the Chief Minister or other Ministers have placed before the House many statements concerning matters of public importance.

Indeed, during Jayalalithaa’s previous government, even if a day passed without something else happening, it would not pass without a statement under Rule 110. Whatever announcement the government wished to make, it would place a statement under Rule 110. The reason was that no one could ask a question on it. If any Member rose to ask about it, the Speaker would immediately say: “The statement has been placed under Rule 110. No one is permitted to ask questions on it. Sit down.” The Members would sit down. There are many date-wise examples of this in the Assembly proceedings. Anyone may look them up.

To give one immediate example, it can be seen on page 152 of the Assembly proceedings dated 1.4.2002. What did the Speaker say then?

> “Hon’ble Speaker: Mr. C. Gnanasekaran, Mr. Polur Varadhan, Mr. E. V. Velu and Mr. La. Santhanam have given notices as a special calling-attention matter regarding Kuwaiti woman Dalal Azmi, who has been arrested and lodged in Vellore prison in connection with the police-passport issue. Mr. J. Hemachandran, Mr. T. Mani, Ms. Balabharathi, Mr. Vai. Subramaniam and Mr. G. K. Mani have given notices as a calling-attention matter. Considering the importance of this issue, the Hon’ble Chief Minister (Jayalalithaa) will make a statement under Rule 110.”

After that, then Chief Minister Jayalalithaa rose and read a statement under Rule 110. Was any Member then permitted by the Speaker to raise a question or speak about that statement or the issue? No.

But the PMK and the two Communist parties that raised the issue in the House yesterday were, during Jayalalithaa’s government, the very parties whose Members were present in the House and had given calling-attention notices on that same issue. Were their Members then permitted to debate the statement read under Rule 110? They were not. Did they accept it then? They did. Why then do they rise now and ask so many questions, insist on a debate, and behave to the point of being removed from the House? In 2002, under Jayalalithaa’s government, when a statement was placed under Rule 110, the ruling AIADMK did not permit any debate or speech on it. But now, when under the same Rule 110 a statement is made during the DMK government, those same people rise and create an issue demanding permission to speak. Is that right? Is it just? That is the question before the people today.

The PMK friends who argued that a statement should not be read under Rule 110 and that the matter should instead be debated are now conducting a protest outside with their mouths covered. When Jayalalithaa read statements under Rule 110, rather than rising and seeking a debate they simply kept their mouths shut. They themselves must recognise the difference between that silence then and the speed with which they are protesting now by literally covering their mouths.

It is said that one PMK Member even remarked that, had Chief Minister Kalaignar been in the House, he would have called us, who had been removed, back into the House.

That is true. I myself would have called them back. Or, if the Members had told me they wished to speak on this matter, I would have allowed them to speak on it for a little while and then had the statement under Rule 110 delivered as the reply. I would have shown some flexibility instead of stubbornly insisting that the rule alone must be followed.

But in my absence, the ruling party and the Speaker evidently upheld the rule firmly. There is no fault in that on their part. One can say it arose from their concern that the traditions of the House should be followed. The Members who created the commotion are not people unaware of the Assembly rules. Yet they behaved this way. That is what shocks and surprises me! What can be done? When politics intervenes, that too has to be borne.

If Members give a motion under Assembly Rule 56 asking for a discussion on an event of public importance, is there any rule saying that it must necessarily be taken up? There is no such rule. It is stated clearly that the Speaker may refuse consent to raise the specified matter in the House; that if the Speaker decides to reject the motion, the Speaker’s decision is final; and that no attempt may be made to raise the same matter before the House in any other form.

When the AIADMK was in government in Tamil Nadu, on any issue they used Rule 110 to make statements precisely so that Members would not be given an opportunity to speak. After it was already known yesterday that a Minister was going to read a statement on this matter, how can it be fair to demand that even Question Hour be postponed in order to permit a debate?

If Question Hour is to be postponed, a motion has to be brought and can be postponed only if that motion is unanimously accepted. Even if one person objects, Question Hour cannot be postponed. But yesterday, while Question Hour had already begun and was under way, they demanded that it be postponed and that the calling-attention motion be taken up for discussion, and created an issue over it. Even the statement that the Members were “removed” appears to mean that, because they continued shouting while Question Hour was under way and would not allow it to proceed, the Speaker was apparently placed in an unavoidable situation where those shouting had to be removed so that the Assembly could proceed properly and peacefully.

In between, even the Speaker is reported in the *Dinamani* newspaper to have said that anything could be taken up after Question Hour. Yet the paper also reports that the Members were not satisfied with this answer from the Speaker and continued shouting. If they wished to express any view about the statement read under Rule 110 and were unable to do so at that moment, then later the same day, while the debate on the Financial Statement was taking place, they could have had whoever was speaking on behalf of their party express that view.

Whatever the case, what happened has happened. At least from now on, I ask all parties to cooperate so that the House may function peacefully and properly.

The government has taken steps for a CBI inquiry into the incidents that occurred in the court. A proper inquiry will take place. Action will be taken against those who did wrong. Let us act with the thought that, without distinguishing between lawyers and police personnel, both are our own people. One wears khaki; the other wears black — both are ours!

The present Assembly rules were framed many years ago. If some changes are needed, the committee that frames rules for the Assembly can meet, discuss them and prepare new rules. Therefore: let us make a new rule — and for now, let us respect the rule that exists!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**22-2-2009**
