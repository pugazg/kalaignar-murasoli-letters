# 3717. The Maduravoyal Project Too—Arokara! Why? Why?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 10 March 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

In the manifesto issued by the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam for the 2006 Tamil Nadu Assembly election, we said that an **Elevated Highway** scheme would be implemented in parts of Chennai city suffering heavily from traffic congestion. After the DMK assumed office, in a letter I wrote to Prime Minister Manmohan Singh on 5 June 2006, I requested approval for a grade-separated elevated road to Chennai Port.

Through the continuing efforts of people such as Thambi T.R. Baalu, the Union Government approved construction of an elevated road connecting Chennai Port and the Maduravoyal junction at a cost of ₹1,655 crore. Prime Minister Manmohan Singh himself came in person and, under my leadership as Chief Minister, laid the foundation stone for the project on 8 January 2009.

The project cost was later fixed at ₹1,815 crore, with the larger share of funding coming from the Union Government. It was announced even then that this would become the longest elevated highway in India.

To enable uninterrupted container movement from Chennai Port, the Tamil Nadu government gave in-principle approval on 22 June 2007 to the National Highways Authority of India for an elevated expressway along one bank of the Cooum River.

The Tamil Nadu government also agreed that the cost of land acquisition needed for the elevated road, together with expenditure on rehabilitation and resettlement of people affected by the project, would be borne in a 50:50 ratio by the Chennai Port Trust and the Tamil Nadu government. The State government's share would therefore amount to ₹130 crore.

In addition, the State government assured the National Highways Authority of India of full cooperation in matters such as obtaining clearances under pollution-control law and shifting drinking-water lines, sewer lines and electricity poles necessitated by construction of the elevated highway.

Whenever it is a major project like this that was begun or completed under a DMK government, one wonders what goes through the mind of today's Tamil Nadu Chief Minister. She seems only to look for a way to conduct its closing ceremony! Is not the new Secretariat a major example? A building conceived and planned specifically to house the Secretariat and the Legislative Assembly, with rooms for ministers built in sequence, was inaugurated under my leadership as Chief Minister after bringing the Prime Minister and Mrs. Sonia Gandhi to the function. Everyone who saw it admired and praised it. Now, we are told, it is to be converted into a hospital! It must not function as the Secretariat, but it may function as a hospital! If the building is suitable to function as a hospital, why should it not function as the Secretariat? What further evidence is needed of the mentality of the person Tamil Nadu has received as its Chief Minister?

The Sethu Samudram project, the long-standing dream of Tamils, has met the same fate as the Secretariat. The Prime Minister and Mrs. Sonia Gandhi came in person to Madurai and, under my leadership, laid the foundation stone. The AIADMK too had said in its election manifesto that this project should be implemented. Yet now the AIADMK government is thinking of every possible obstacle to place in its way. In the same line comes this Maduravoyal road project. The Prime Minister himself came and laid its foundation stone—how could Amma possibly tolerate that?

When opposition to this project was taken to court, Chief Justice Iqbal and Justice Sivagnanam of the Madras High Court ruled that the project could continue. The present Tamil Nadu government did not attach much importance even to that judgment. While work had been progressing rapidly, the AIADMK government stopped it in March 2012, citing some reason.

A report in *The Hindu* dated 29 January 2013 said that the Tamil Nadu government was engaged in efforts relating to continuation of the project and had written to the National Highways Authority of India, which had been implementing it. The report concluded with these exact English words:

> “The Tamil Nadu Government is yet to complete its share of the work in terms of relief
> and rehabilitation of dwellers along the Cooum River and acquisition
> of the land”

Reports about work on the Chennai Port–Maduravoyal project appeared in *The Hindu* on the 6th, *The Times of India* on the 7th and *Dina Thanthi* on the 8th. According to those reports, for the past year there had not even been a sign that work would restart.

The Tamil Nadu government's Water Resources Department, which stopped the project, wrote to the National Highways Authority saying that fresh permission for the project's alignment and fresh Coastal Regulation Zone clearance had to be obtained. The NHAI replied that since there had been no change whatever in the project's alignment, there was no need to obtain new permission or fresh CRZ clearance.

The NHAI further stated that the project would lead to dredging of the Cooum and removal of encroachments; increase the river's carrying capacity; prevent future encroachment along its banks; and provide rehabilitation for people living on the Cooum riverbank.

Because the AIADMK government prevented the project from continuing, the contractor, Soma, has now demanded ₹668 crore as compensation from the National Highways Authority of India. In its letter, the company said that the Tamil Nadu government had undertaken to acquire the necessary land and relocate 6,100 families living along the Cooum riverbank and 458 commercial buildings in Chintadripet. Since this was not done and the land was not handed over, project work had been delayed. The Tamil Nadu government and Chennai Port were the parties that stood to benefit most from the project, the company said, and therefore the State government should cooperate; otherwise the works would be severely affected and construction costs and other losses would increase.

Had the project been carried out according to the schedule planned under the DMK government, the work would have been completed by the coming September and traffic would have begun on a four-lane high-speed elevated road between Chennai Port and Maduravoyal. Instead, ₹500 crore has already been spent and only 20 per cent of the work has been completed.

In this situation, newspapers have reported that the Maduravoyal elevated-expressway dispute is to be taken to court. A public-interest case seeking removal of the obstacles to implementation and arrangements for completing the project rapidly within the prescribed time remains pending in the High Court. Meanwhile, reports say the NHAI too is preparing to approach the court over the same issue, with its officials stating that **“there appears to be no way other than following the legal route to revive and complete the project.”**

The National Highways Authority of India is deeply troubled by the Tamil Nadu government's approach. Large infrastructure projects of this kind are progressing rapidly in other States without obstruction, with the willing cooperation and assistance of their State governments. Only in Tamil Nadu, because of what the letter calls the State government's negative approach, is the project being delayed.

Whenever the State government raised one doubt after another, the NHAI supplied the required explanations; yet the delay already caused has continued. Union Government official A.K. Upadhyay and Prime Minister's Adviser T.K.A. Nair met the Tamil Nadu Chief Secretary and gave all the necessary explanations about why the project needed to be accelerated, but even that did not prevent further delay.

*The Times of India* reported that **if this dedicated elevated road is not created for Chennai Port, a situation in which Chennai Port itself may have to be closed could become unavoidable.**

It is in this situation that Soma, the company carrying out the works, is pressing for compensation of ₹668 crore because of the delay caused by the Tamil Nadu government. Who is to pay such an enormous sum? Will not this loss fall upon the head of the Tamil Nadu government that placed obstacles in the project's path out of political hostility? Because of this “politics of hatred” of the AIADMK government, Tamil Nadu suffers not only financial loss; the benefits that the people of Tamil Nadu were to receive from the project are also being harmed. If the people of Tamil Nadu understand this at least now, perhaps Tamil Nadu can prosper from here on!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**10 March 2013**
