# 3800. The Food Security Act — Gain or Loss?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

Twisting facts and loudly proclaiming false information are skills Chief Minister Jayalalithaa has mastered. In that manner, she has issued a long statement lamenting that, because the DMK supported the Food Security Bill, “the DMK’s double role has been exposed.” She has worked very hard to create the illusion that the Union Government enacted the National Food Security Act specifically against Tamil Nadu and that the DMK, by supporting it, committed a great betrayal of Tamil Nadu’s people.

Tamil Nadu’s total population is 7 crore 21 lakh. According to the figures accepted by the Union Government’s National Sample Survey Office, poor and ordinary people constitute 65.55 per cent of the rural population and 37.79 per cent of the urban population in Tamil Nadu. On that basis, under the National Food Security Act, 232.70 lakh poor people in rural Tamil Nadu and 131.88 lakh in urban areas will benefit. Altogether, 364.58 lakh poor people across the State’s towns and villages will benefit under this Act.

Under the Food Security Act, there will be no change in the rice presently supplied to the 18.65 lakh poorest people covered by the Antyodaya Anna Yojana (AAY): 35 kilograms per family each month, amounting to 65,000 tonnes a month.

Of Tamil Nadu’s 364.58 lakh poor people in urban and rural areas, after excluding the 18.65 lakh poorest people, the remaining 345.93 lakh people will each receive five kilograms a month under the new Food Security Act. The quantity required will be 1,72,900 tonnes of rice a month.

Until now, the Union Government supplied Tamil Nadu with 65,000 tonnes of rice a month at ₹3 a kilogram for people covered by AAY, and 1,05,000 tonnes at ₹5.65 a kilogram for those below the poverty line (BPL)—a total of 1,70,000 tonnes.

Rice supplied to the poor and the poorest, which until now stood at 1,70,000 tonnes a month, will henceforth rise to 2,37,900 tonnes a month—1,72,900 tonnes plus 65,000 tonnes. Its price will be only ₹3 a kilogram.

In other words, because of the new Act Tamil Nadu will receive 67,900 tonnes more rice than it has so far received for these categories.

Will this additional rice bring Tamil Nadu a gain or a loss? Anyone can understand the answer easily. What can anyone do if Jayalalithaa refuses to understand it?

Under the new Food Security Act, purchasing 2,37,900 tonnes of rice a month from the Union Government at the subsidised rate of ₹3 a kilogram will cost only ₹856.44 crore a year.

Until now, the Union Government supplied the Tamil Nadu Government with 65,000 tonnes a month under AAY at ₹3 a kilogram; 1,05,000 tonnes a month under the BPL programme at ₹5.65 a kilogram; and 1,26,000 tonnes a month for those above the poverty line (APL) at ₹8.30 a kilogram.

Until now, the Union Government annually supplied 20,40,000 tonnes of rice for people below the poverty line, at ₹3 and ₹5.65 a kilogram. Under the new arrangement, it will supply Tamil Nadu with 28,54,000 tonnes a year for people below the poverty line, all at ₹3 a kilogram.

Thus, under the new Act, the Union Government will supply Tamil Nadu with an additional 8,14,000 tonnes of rice every year for people below the poverty line.

Even someone who knows only elementary arithmetic can understand whether this is a gain or a loss for Tamil Nadu. What can anyone do if Jayalalithaa does not understand it?

Until now, for people above the poverty line, the Tamil Nadu Government bought 1,26,000 tonnes of rice a month from the Union Government at ₹8.30 a kilogram and distributed 15,12,000 tonnes a year.

Henceforth, the Tamil Nadu Government will need to buy only 58,100 tonnes a month at ₹8.30 a kilogram—just 6,97,000 tonnes a year. Neutral observers must say whether that is a gain or a loss for Tamil Nadu. Let Jayalalithaa answer this direct question directly.

The annual cost to the Tamil Nadu Government of the rice required for people above the poverty line will be ₹578 crore. The annual cost of the rice required for people below the poverty line will be ₹856 crore. Together, under the new Food Security Act, the total amount Tamil Nadu must pay for rice allocated by the Union Government will be ₹1,434 crore a year.

At present, however, the Tamil Nadu Government pays ₹2,231 crore for the rice obtained under Union allocations. The Food Security Act will therefore reduce the State Government’s annual expenditure by ₹797 crore—₹2,231 crore minus ₹1,434 crore. Is this a gain or a loss for Tamil Nadu? Was the DMK right or wrong to support the new Act? Let Jayalalithaa answer these direct questions directly.

Concealing all this, and apparently believing no one will bring the facts into the light, Jayalalithaa says that the Food Security Act will impose an additional expenditure of ₹1,000 crore on Tamil Nadu. That is a blatant falsehood.

The only ground Jayalalithaa offers for opposing the Act is that rice supplied for people above the poverty line should continue to be given at ₹8.30 a kilogram. She raised this demand on 25 August 2013.

But even before she did so, on 21 August 2013, Thambi T.R. Baalu, leader of the DMK parliamentary party, had written to Union Food Minister K.V. Thomas insisting that the price should remain ₹8.30 a kilogram. On 22 August, the Minister replied that T.R. Baalu’s request would be accepted, that a proposal had been sent for the Union Cabinet’s approval, and that a decision would be taken shortly. The letter signed by the Union Food Minister stated:

> Dear Thiru T.R. Baalu, Please refer to your letter dated 21-8-2013 regarding National Food Security Bill, 2013. As regards the issue prices for additional allocations to be made to the State Governments over and above their entitlements under the Bill, my Ministry has sent a proposal for Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs for fixing this price at the current APL prices, i.e., Rs. 6/10 per kg for wheat and Rs. 8/30 per kg. for rice. A decision in this matter will be taken shortly.

He also confirmed this while speaking in the Lok Sabha.

T.R. Baalu made the request on 21 August and the Union Minister gave his assurance in reply on 22 August. Only on 25 August did Chief Minister Jayalalithaa write to the Prime Minister pressing the same demand. She now asks why the Minister’s assurance was not incorporated as a statutory amendment and says that the entire Act must therefore be opposed. The Union Government has neither increased the existing price of ₹8.30 a kilogram for rice supplied for those above the poverty line nor issued any order to do so. Yet Jayalalithaa says the DMK has betrayed Tamil Nadu. The people of Tamil Nadu must say where the betrayal lies.

A special correspondent of *Dinamalar*, concluding an article published on 30 August 2013 under the title “Is the Food Security Act a Gain or a Loss for Tamil Nadu?”, wrote clearly: “With the Food Security Act coming into force, the Tamil Nadu Government will save ₹40.28 crore a month on rice procurement, according to government sources.” If even *Dinamalar* says that the Bill will save the Tamil Nadu Government ₹483.36 crore a year, the people of Tamil Nadu may judge how much truth there is in Jayalalithaa’s allegation that the DMK betrayed the State.

Without necessity, Jayalalithaa has also written in this statement as though I betrayed Tamil Nadu over the Sri Lankan Tamil question, Katchatheevu, the Cauvery and Mullaperiyar. Perhaps she writes this so that I may list, with evidence, the betrayals she herself repeatedly committed on those issues, thereby giving her the pleasure of reading them! Whenever she cannot answer the issue directly, she repeats these old allegations to vent her anger.

It is a wonder beyond wonders that Jayalalithaa has now begun speaking of “State autonomy.” It was the DMK Government that gave life to that expression by appointing a committee headed by Justice Rajamannar, receiving its report and acting on the question of State autonomy.

The concessions and rights Tamil Nadu already enjoyed will not be affected by the Food Security Act. On the contrary, the DMK made every effort and secured an arrangement that will reduce Tamil Nadu’s annual expenditure by ₹797 crore. Chief Minister Jayalalithaa issues statements like this only to conceal that truth and to oppose the Union Government for political reasons.

Jayalalithaa now issues elaborate statements, but has she forgotten that, when there was neither a Food Security Act nor additional financial assistance from the Union Government, the DMK Government supplied one kilogram of rice for one rupee? Has she forgotten that, when she was Chief Minister in 2003, the AIADMK Government issued family cards marked “H” and took away the rice received by lakhs of poor and lower-middle-class people? Does she imagine the people of Tamil Nadu have forgotten that cruelty?

The DMK directly demanded that the annual average of 36,78,000 tonnes of rice so far supplied to Tamil Nadu should not be reduced under any circumstances. Chief Minister Jayalalithaa made the same request in a letter to the Prime Minister. The Union Government accepted it and incorporated it into the Food Security Bill through an official amendment. Yet, as usual, Jayalalithaa speaks as though the amendment was made only because she insisted upon it. When Tamil Nadu’s demand has been fulfilled, what could be wrong in the DMK supporting the Act?

The quantity of rice Tamil Nadu has so far received has not been reduced even slightly. Nor will there be any change in the price of ₹8.30 a kilogram for rice supplied for those above the poverty line. The Union Food Minister stated this firmly in his letter to Thambi T.R. Baalu, the leader of the DMK parliamentary party.

The Union Government enacted this law to remove hunger among poor and ordinary people throughout India. The AIADMK did not say that it opposed the law itself; it merely insisted that amendments be accepted. We must not forget that even the BJP, the principal opposition party in Parliament, supported the law. Had the AIADMK rejected it, why did its members not walk out when the Bill was put to a voice vote in Parliament, saying that they would not accept it? Why did they not do so?

Who is playing a double role? Is the Food Security Act a gain or a loss for Tamil Nadu?

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**

3 September 2013
