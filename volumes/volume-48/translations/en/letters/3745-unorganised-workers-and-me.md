# 3745. Unorganised Workers—and Me!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

Workers in Tamil Nadu will not have forgotten that during the period of DMK rule, with the specific purpose of providing benefits and safeguarding the welfare of unorganised workers, 35 welfare boards were established. Through those boards, workers in the respective occupations were enrolled as members, the welfare assistance they needed was provided, and they and their families were protected. Everyone knows that whenever workers of a particular occupation came together during DMK rule and requested a separate board for themselves, those requests were accepted and boards were established for them. To become a member of those boards, it was enough for an application to come through a trade union; membership cards would immediately be issued through that trade union itself.

Advisory committees were appointed for unorganised workers, and representatives of all trade unions had places on the respective committees during DMK rule. But now, under AIADMK rule, even the advisory committees have not been renewed. All the unorganised-workers welfare boards have been paralysed and rendered inactive for the sole reason that they were begun under DMK rule. Now, to register as a member, the worker must appear in person; a Village Administrative Officer must issue a certificate; a bank account must be opened—such severe conditions have been imposed.

At this point I wish to explain briefly the scale of assistance provided through the various welfare boards during the fifth period of DMK rule, from 13 May 2006 to 19 April 2011.

In the Tamil Nadu Construction Workers Welfare Board, 10 lakh 59 thousand 969 workers were newly enrolled, and assistance amounting to ₹100 crore 54 lakh 504 was provided to the families of 3 lakh 46 thousand 44 workers.

In the Tamil Nadu Manual Workers Welfare Board, which had been established by the then DMK government in 1999 and continued functioning, 4 lakh 49 thousand 938 workers were registered; assistance of ₹67 crore 33 lakh 7 thousand 113 was provided to the families of 2 lakh 87 thousand 724 workers.

The previous AIADMK government had merged nine unorganised-sector welfare boards—including the Tamil Nadu Autorickshaw and Taxi Drivers Welfare Board, created by the DMK government of 2000–2001—with the Tamil Nadu Manual Workers Welfare Board.

But because the DMK government wished to give special attention to the welfare of poor worker families engaged in those nine categories of unorganised work, it ordered that all nine once again function as separate welfare boards from 1 September 2006. It also created several more unorganised-workers welfare boards, enrolled workers through them and provided various kinds of welfare assistance.

Accordingly:

- In the Tamil Nadu Autorickshaw and Taxi Drivers Welfare Board, of 1 lakh 619 registered workers, assistance of ₹3 crore 92 lakh 11 thousand 633 was provided to the families of 17 thousand 46 workers.
- In the Tamil Nadu Laundry Workers Welfare Board, of 34 thousand 182 registered workers, assistance of ₹3 crore 14 lakh 60 thousand 732 was provided to the families of 16 thousand 634 workers.
- In the Tamil Nadu Barbers Welfare Board, of 25 thousand 397 registered workers, assistance of ₹2 crore 8 lakh 89 thousand 826 was provided to the families of 9 thousand 332 workers.
- In the Tamil Nadu Tailoring Workers Welfare Board, of 4 lakh 42 thousand 288 registered workers, assistance of ₹61 crore 25 lakh 95 thousand 684 was provided to the families of 2 lakh 27 thousand 850 workers.
- In the Tamil Nadu Handicraft Workers Welfare Board, of 82 thousand 61 registered workers, assistance of ₹9 crore 21 lakh 50 thousand 489 was provided to the families of 29 thousand 65 workers.
- In the Tamil Nadu Palmyra Workers Welfare Board, of 33 thousand 416 registered workers, assistance of ₹1 crore 55 lakh 49 thousand 745 was provided to the families of 7 thousand 401 workers.
- In the Tamil Nadu Handloom and Handloom-Silk Weaving Workers Welfare Board, of 1 lakh 80 thousand 116 registered workers, assistance of ₹23 crore 88 lakh 20 thousand 61 was provided to the families of 1 lakh 9 thousand 185 workers.
- In the Tamil Nadu Footwear-Leather Products Manufacturing and Leather Tanning Workers Welfare Board, of 11 thousand 225 registered workers, assistance of ₹89 lakh 59 thousand 583 was provided to the families of 3 thousand 61 workers.

Likewise, new boards were established for various kinds of workers under names such as the Tamil Nadu Painters Welfare Board, Tamil Nadu Goldsmiths Welfare Board, Tamil Nadu Pottery Workers Welfare Board, Tamil Nadu Domestic Workers Welfare Board, Tamil Nadu Powerloom Weavers Welfare Board, the welfare board for street vendors and workers in shops and establishments, and the Cooks Welfare Board. Welfare assistance was provided to their families.

If we consider the functioning of another 18 unorganised-workers welfare boards operating under departments other than the Labour Welfare Department, the Cable TV Workers Welfare Board was established on 28 March 2008 with the welfare of people engaged in the television trade in mind.

The Tamil Nadu Agricultural Labourers–Farmers Social Security and Welfare Scheme was started on 7 August 2006, and the Agricultural Labourers Welfare Board was started on 18 January 2007. Under this board, 1 crore 74 lakh 65 thousand 498 workers were enrolled as members. Of them, assistance of ₹684 crore 2 lakh 55 thousand 686 was provided to the families of 8 lakh 72 thousand 981 agricultural labourers and farmers.

The “Tamil Nadu Folk Artists Welfare Board” was started on 20 April 2007. An initial fund of ₹1 crore was sanctioned to provide welfare assistance to members. In this board, 25 thousand 192 artistes were enrolled, and up to 15 March 2011 assistance of ₹66 lakh 31 thousand 567 was provided to the families of 4 thousand 869 artistes.

The “Tamil Nadu Narikuravar Welfare Board” was started from 27 May 2008. Up to 15 March 2011, 9 thousand 762 people registered as members. A fund of ₹1 crore was allocated for welfare assistance. Assistance of ₹1 crore 97 lakh 11 thousand was provided to the families of 3 thousand 593 Narikuravars.

The “Tamil Nadu Village Temple Priests Welfare Board” was started on 22 April 2007 and 65 thousand 326 people were enrolled. Up to 15 March 2011, assistance of ₹63 lakh 21 thousand 45 was provided to the families of 2 thousand 30 priests.

The “Tamil Nadu Welfare Board for Persons with Disabilities” was started from 24 April 2007. Up to 15 March 2011, 52 thousand people were enrolled, and assistance of ₹1 crore 62 lakh 75 thousand 478 was provided to 3 thousand 632 people.

The “Tamil Nadu Aravanis Welfare Board” was started on 10 April 2008. 2 thousand 86 people were enrolled as members, and assistance of ₹3 lakh 83 thousand 880 was provided to 775 beneficiaries.

The “Tamil Nadu Tribal Welfare Board” was started on 20 April 2007; the Tamil Nadu Ulema and Employees Welfare Board on 24 August 2009; the Tamil Nadu Pudhirai Vannar Welfare Board on 31 August 2010; and the “Tamil Nadu Denotified Communities Welfare Board,” under the chairmanship of the Backward Classes Welfare Minister, on 20 April 2007. Welfare assistance was provided through them.

In the Tamil Nadu Sanitation Workers Welfare Board, 43 thousand 351 people were enrolled and, up to 15 March 2011, assistance of ₹17 lakh 86 thousand 440 was provided to 1,133 people.

The “Tamil Nadu Fishermen Welfare Board” was established on 29 June 2007 with the aim of achieving planned, assured improvement in the social, economic and educational conditions of Tamil Nadu's fishermen. 3 lakh 85 thousand 756 fishing workers were enrolled as members, and up to 15 March 2011 assistance of ₹5 crore 22 lakh 1 thousand 411 was provided to the families of 5 thousand 60 members.

The Tamil Nadu Film Industry Welfare Board was started on 28 October 2009; the Tamil Nadu Goat Rearers Welfare Board on 16 May 2010; the Tamil Nadu Khadi Yarn Spinners and Weavers Welfare Board on 6 August 2010; and the Tamil Nadu Publishers, Booksellers and Employees Welfare Board on 17 August 2010. With the welfare of document writers and their families throughout the State in mind, the Document Writers Welfare Board was established on 28 October 2010.

Thus, across the 35 unorganised-workers welfare boards, 2 crore 13 lakh 55 thousand 884 members were enrolled. Of them, assistance of ₹1,011 crore 56 lakh 1 thousand 87 was provided during DMK rule to 21 lakh 41 thousand 279 unorganised-worker families.

There is now a situation in which it is not even known whether all the assistance that had been provided under DMK rule continues to be given, or what has become of those boards. To examine all this, a meeting of all unions was held in Chennai on the 23rd at the AITUC union office under the chairmanship of trade-union leader Comrade S.S. Thiagarasan. Representatives of the Labour Progressive Federation, AITUC, CITU, BMS and HMS participated. On behalf of the Labour Progressive Federation, DMK Labour Wing Secretary Thambi Singara Rathina Sabapathy also took part.

Condemning what they describe as the AIADMK government's anti-unorganised-worker actions, ten trade unions have come forward to hold protest demonstrations at about 10 a.m. on 7 May 2013 in front of district offices across Tamil Nadu. I too strongly appeal that everyone belonging to the unorganised sector of the Labour Progressive Federation take part in these demonstrations.

This year, on the occasion of May Day, my expectation and desire—as one who was responsible for starting these boards—is that the Chief Minister's Rule 110 statement in the Assembly should announce the revival of all 35 boards and once again bring light into the lives of several lakh unorganised workers.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**30 April 2013**
