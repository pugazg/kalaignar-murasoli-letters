# 3404. This Pen Makes Its Appeal!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> The letter uses the author’s childhood choice of a pen as a framing image for a long list of policies and welfare measures he attributes to his governments, before ending in an explicit election-day request for votes under alliance symbols. The policy claims and final appeal are preserved as historical source material, not independently verified or endorsed here. The complete audited Tamil is reproduced below and remains authoritative.

**Source pages:** [PDF 268](../../../pages/page-268.md)–[PDF 271](../../../pages/page-271.md)  
**Date:** 13 May 2009

**Udanpirappē,**

By an old custom in our homes, when a child reaches the crawling stage, a “pal kozhukattai ceremony” is held. A lamp is lit in the hall, a kolam is drawn, and on a large tray on the floor are placed kozhukattai, vadai, jewellery, a writing pen, a small knife and other objects. People watch to see which of those objects the crawling child picks up.

My family, it seems, held such a ceremony for me when I was a child. I crawled and moved towards the tray and picked up the pen that was on it.

In the days immediately after celebrating Mother’s Day, recalling that event is an enjoyable experience for me.

A young artist named Rajapani once heard me tell this story. He drew me in the form of a writing pen and, in another imaginative touch, drew Mother Anjugam Ammayar rocking the cradle. That picture is still in my home. It is the picture referred to here.

But the pen swinging in the cradle never slept. It still works:

for the poor and simple —

for ordinary people who know little —

for labourers who wear out their bodies in work —

for farmers of upright heart —

for the people at the very bottom, layer after layer of those described as the lowest strata —

for the Tamil language that is my breath —

for the ethnic feeling that is the beat of my heart —

and to struggle for the rights of Tamils wherever they live.

The pen I picked up before my mother has still not completed its work.

It was this pen that signed for those who had lost their sight.

It was this pen that framed a rehabilitation scheme for those who had begged with bowls in their hands.

Though the cruelty of one human being pulling another in a hand-rickshaw still existed in Kolkata, the capital of a Communist-ruled State, it was this pen that signed to abolish it completely in Tamil Nadu and provide free cycle-rickshaws.

It was this pen that created the Slum Clearance Board.

It was this pen that created the Water Supply Board.

It was this pen that gave schemes for persons with disabilities.

It was this pen that brought the house-site law.

It was this pen that created the land-ceiling law.

It was this pen that provided free electricity to farmers and weavers.

It was this pen that framed the Anna Renaissance Scheme.

It was this pen that established Periyar Memorial Samathuvapurams.

It was this pen that made Kamarajar’s birthday Education Development Day by law.

It was this pen that made the first day of Thai Tamil New Year’s Day by law.

It was this pen that ordered a beautiful 133-foot statue for Ayyan Thiruvalluvar at the tip of Kanyakumari.

It was this pen that ordered rice to be supplied at one rupee a kilogram, on a scale the source says was unmatched elsewhere in India.

It was this pen that arranged free colour television sets for all families, described as a measure to increase happiness in women’s lives.

It was this pen that, on the very day of assuming office, ordered the complete waiver of ₹7,000 crore in cooperative loans owed by farmers.

It was this pen that laid the foundation for a scheme giving ₹20,000 free assistance for the marriages of poor women.

It was this pen that made provision to give ₹6,000 each to pregnant women.

It was this pen that approved the Kalaignar Insurance Scheme for life-saving advanced treatment for one crore poor people.

It was this pen that signed for minority rights; for the advancement of Arunthathiyars; for Tamil as a Classical Language; and for the Sethusamudram project.

It was this pen that arranged memorials, memorial halls and statues in honour of national martyrs and their sacrifices.

Thus, to frame schemes worth many thousands of crores —

to relieve the hunger of many crores of people —

this child has continued to work, taking my mother’s lullaby as the highest praise. Yes — your own household’s child.

Dear mothers whose hearts have rejoiced in celebrating Mother’s Day! Elders of their families!

With the point of this pen, great achievements — great schemes — have been carried out with the support of close Udanpirappugal so that the mother who bore me and the golden land in which I was born may rejoice.

Think for a moment about my request, the author says, and let May 13 be the day on which you can express the gratitude:

> “He picked up the pen in childhood;  
> because of that, neither the country nor we were wasted.”

This pen — which, the source says, has done all this and intends to do still more — asks you to cast your precious votes under the Rising Sun, Hand and Star symbols.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**13-5-2009**

## Letter-specific notes

1. The long policy list is translated as the author’s own account; figures and claimed effects are not independently verified here.
2. “Rising Sun — Hand — Star” is preserved as the source’s alliance-symbol election appeal.
3. The recurring “this pen” structure is retained to preserve the letter’s rhetorical form.

3404. இந்தப் பேனா வேண்டுகிறது!

உடன்பிறப்பே,

பழமை வழக்கமாக நம் வீடுகளில் குழந்தை தவழும் பருவத்தில் “பல் கொழுக்கட்டை விழா” நிகழ்ச்சி ஒன்று நடத்துவார்கள். கூடத்தில் விளக்கேற்றி வைத்து - கோலமிட்டு - தரையில் ஒரு பெரிய தாம்பாளத்தில் கொழுக்கட்டை, வடை, நகை நட்டுகள், எழுதுகின்ற பேனா, சிறிய கத்தி முதலியவற்றை வைத்து அங்கே தவழ்ந்து செல்கின்ற குழந்தை அந்தப் பொருள்களில் எதை எடுக்கிறது என்று கவனிப்பார்கள்.

எனக்கு இப்படி குழந்தை பருவத்தில் பல் கொழுக்கட்டை நிகழ்ச்சியை என் வீட்டார் நடத்தியபோது - நான் ஊர்ந்து, நகர்ந்து, தவழ்ந்து குறிப்பிட்ட தாம்பாளத்தை அடைந்து - அதிலேயிருந்த பேனாவை எடுத்தேனாம்.

“அன்னையர் தினம்” கொண்டாடி அடுத்தடுத்த நாட்களில் அந்த நினைவு எனக்கு வருவது ஒரு சுவையான அனுபவம்!

அந்த நிகழ்ச்சியை என் வாயிலாக ஒரு முறை கேள்விப்பட்ட இளம் ஓவியர் ராஜபாணி என்பார், என்னை எழுதுகோலாகச் சித்திரித்து, அன்னை அஞ்சுகம் அம்மையார் ஆட்டுகின்ற தொட்டிலையும் ஓவியமாகத் தீட்டி ஒரு அற்புதக் கற்பனையைச் செய்து தந்தார். அந்த ஓவியம் இன்னமும் என் வீட்டில் இருக்கிறது. அது தான் இந்த ஓவியம்.

ஆனால், தொட்டிலில் ஆடும் பேனா தூங்கவே இல்லை. இன்னமும்;

ஏழை எளியவர்களுக்காக -

எதுமறியா பாமரர்களுக்காக -

உடல் ஓடிய உழைக்கின்ற தொழிலாளர்களுக்காக -

உத்தம நெஞ்சம் கொண்ட உழவர் பெருமக்களுக்காக -
