# 3814. Does a “Majority” Government Mean It Can Do Anything?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 8 October 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

Murders and robberies continue without interruption under the AIADMK Government in Tamil Nadu. In its issue dated 29 September 2013, *Dinamalar* carried a half-page report under the headline “Chennai Tops in Murder and Robbery.” It said:

> Cases connected with murder, robbery, theft and chain-snatching are increasing in Tamil Nadu. On the 19th alone, twelve murders took place across the State in a single day. Across Tamil Nadu, 1,806 people were killed last year, and 1,044 people were killed in the current year up to July. According to the statistics, on average between 100 and 120 people are brutally murdered every month. Among the districts of Tamil Nadu, Chennai stands first in offences such as murder, attempted murder, theft, highway robbery, robbery and causing injury. In Chennai, there were eighty-five property-related murders last year and seventy up to July this year. Up to July this year, Chennai witnessed 116 murders, 1,261 thefts, 300 robberies and fifty-four incidents of highway robbery. Apart from these, Chennai also stands first in chain-snatching incidents in Tamil Nadu.

That is the tribute *Dinamalar* has written for the AIADMK Government!

On the same day, 29 September 2013, the Tamil daily *The Hindu* carried a report under the headline “Vellore Becomes a City of Terror—Successive Murder Attempts on the Same Day.” It reported:

> The public have been frightened by two successive attempted murders in Vellore, one of them involving an AIADMK municipal councillor. These attacks, carried out in places with heavy public movement, have caused great alarm. In October last year, BJP State Medical Wing Secretary Dr Aravind Reddy, and on 1 July 2013 Hindu Munnani State Secretary Vellaiyappan, were chased and hacked to death in the middle of the road in Vellore. These two murders shocked the public. Last Friday, many people were eating tiffin at Hotel Durga Bhavan near Vellore’s new bus stand. A ten-member gang arriving on motorcycles entered the hotel directly. They began arguing with men already seated at one of the tables. Suddenly the gang attacked those who were eating and, in the blink of an eye, drew concealed knives and began hacking them. When three people sustained cut injuries, the other diners fled the hotel screaming. Amid the confusion, the gang abducted one of the injured men. The hotel was thrown into turmoil.
>
> Before that commotion had subsided, at about eight in the evening, two men wearing “monkey caps” attacked and hacked Sukumar, municipal councillor for Ward 49, in the Thorapadi area and escaped. Though these two successive attempted murders on a single day created terror among the public, they have also somewhat unsettled the police.

Only two days before this report, an AIADMK minister who had gone to Delhi read out the Chief Minister’s speech there, declaring: “Law and order is excellent in Tamil Nadu. Tamil Nadu shines as a garden of peace.” The text of that speech was then sent to every newspaper here and caused to be published.

Next, it is being reported that gutka, zarda and similar products are being sold freely in violation of the ban under this Government, while officials hesitate to take action. The Government warned that anyone selling such products in breach of the ban would have the goods confiscated and could be fined up to five lakh rupees. Yet more than four months after the ban was imposed, reports say that gutka and zarda are being sold in abundance and at prices many times higher than before. Under the AIADMK Government, the only things flourishing are sales of gutka and zarda—and sales through TASMAC! The ruling party may celebrate this too as one of its much-proclaimed “centenary achievements.” It may even advertise it!

In this splendid setting, you will also have read the stream of reports appearing for the past fifteen days about confusion and irregularity in the administration of the Police Department.

When sandalwood smuggler Veerappan was killed by the Special Task Force in 2004, the AIADMK Government then in office selected about one thousand personnel—from constables to Inspectors General—said to have served in the STF. It granted each of them three lakh rupees in cash and free house sites in the towns and district headquarters where they served. Through Police Department Government Orders No. 1252/2004 dated 29 October 2004 and No. 1396/2004 dated 6 December 2004, that Government also gave all one thousand personnel a one-time special promotion.

Other members of the Police Department alleged that the one thousand beneficiaries included people favoured by the AIADMK Government and by police officers close to the rulers of that time. Charges were raised from several quarters that many recipients had never gone anywhere near the forest and had instead been doing maintenance work at Poes Garden.

In a Police Department with more than one lakh employees, granting special promotion to only one thousand people created numerous administrative complications. Personnel with greater seniority than the promoted group—from constables to superintendents—were made to suffer the denial of promotions for no fault of their own. A large number of police friends complained directly to me, when I was Chief Minister, that because the AIADMK Government had promoted the thousand people it preferred, the promotions due to them at the proper time had been blocked. I raised the issue with senior officials and asked them to find a way to remove their grievance.

Officials pointed out that the Tamil Nadu Police Service Rules and the Tamil Nadu Police Subordinate Service Rules contained no provision for promotions of this kind. Even so, the DMK Government did not cancel the promotions already granted. Acting magnanimously, it reviewed the seniority of both the special promotees and the other employees and, in accordance with the law, issued a Government Order on 3 October 2007. It provided that the STF personnel would retain only one special promotion and would receive any further promotion only when their original seniority entitled them to it.

Those who had obtained special promotions challenged this Government Order by filing several writ petitions in the Madras High Court. On 14 October 2009, the Honourable Justice Venkataraman dismissed their petitions and held that the DMK Government’s order was valid. Writ appeals were then filed against that judgment.

Meanwhile, after returning to power in 2011, the AIADMK Government issued another Government Order on 3 May 2012, unlawfully enabling the same one thousand police personnel who had already received a special promotion to receive yet another promotion. A Division Bench of the Madras High Court heard the writ appeals and dismissed them on 5 April 2013. In that judgment, the Court quashed the Government Order issued by the AIADMK Government on 3 May 2012 and criticised the Government for issuing it. The Court upheld the Government Order made during the DMK administration and directed that police personnel be promoted in accordance with it.

Instead of implementing the High Court’s direction, the AIADMK Government once again moved to give two promotions to police officers who had served in the Veerappan operation. As a result, thirty-two people, including police officer Radhakrishnan, filed a case against the Government in the Madras High Court. Justice Kirubakaran heard the matter and, at the end of last month, directed the Government Pleader to fill all eighty-eight vacancies and report back to the Court, failing which the Court would issue appropriate orders on its own.

The Government Pleader, however, stated that promotions to the rank of Deputy Superintendent of Police could not be granted at that stage. Counsel for the petitioners argued that DSPs had been denied promotion for the past three years, that many had retired without receiving it, and that promotions should be granted immediately.

Delivering judgment on 27 September 2013, Justice Kirubakaran said:

> More than one hundred persons serving as DSPs have retired without receiving promotion. At present there are seventy-eight vacancies in the rank of Additional Superintendent of Police. The Government must fill these vacancies immediately. Otherwise, by the end of this year, fifty-three DSPs will retire without promotion. They must be promoted within two weeks. These promotions shall be temporary and subject to the final judgment in the case. While granting these promotions, the Government must also consider the names of DSPs who participated in the Veerappan search operation and issue appropriate orders.

After this judgment, on Sunday, 29 September 2013, the Tamil Nadu Government hurriedly issued an order promoting ninety-eight DSPs as Additional SPs. The list included persons said to have received promotion on the claim that they had served in the Veerappan search operation: Puzhal Assistant Commissioner Kandasamy; Chennai City Police Welfare Wing Assistant Commissioner Thirumalachamy; Chief Minister’s Security Wing DSP Perumalsamy; and Chief Minister’s Special Security Group DSP Veeraperumal.

Reports said that the order contained numerous irregularities. For example, Rajan was already serving as Additional SP at the Cuddalore district headquarters, yet the Government treated that post as vacant and appointed Murali to it. In the same district, Jacob Manickaraj was serving as Additional SP in the Prohibition Enforcement Wing, but Srinivasan was appointed to his post.

The Court had quashed the AIADMK Government’s order and ruled that a second promotion could not be granted. Yet because promotion orders were issued to twenty persons in defiance of that ruling, contempt proceedings were initiated against the Home Secretary and the Director General of Police. Inspector Gauthaman, who filed the case, was reportedly placed on the waiting list a few days ago. Another Inspector who filed the case, Yaqoob, is also said to have been abruptly transferred.

Some friends in the Police Department say that Rajarajan, Perumalsamy—who performed security duty for the Chief Minister—and Veeraperumal were granted promotions on the claim that they had served in the Veerappan Special Task Force. They allege that, solely to give these men further promotion, Government Order No. 550/2013 was issued by amending the Tamil Nadu police service rules, without even implementing the High Court’s order and with the intention of disregarding that judgment.

Against this amendment order, other affected police officers filed five writ petitions and one contempt petition. While those cases remained pending, the AIADMK Government hurriedly promoted Deputy Superintendents of Police last month. Newspapers have reported for the past ten days that many beneficiaries were security officers attached to the Chief Minister and that a great many irregularities occurred in the haste with which these promotions were made.

There is another similar case. In 2011, the Tamil Nadu Public Service Commission selected fourteen DSPs. A case was filed in the High Court alleging malpractice in the selection. The Court accepted the challenge and ruled that the appointments were invalid. The fourteen candidates appealed, but the appeal was dismissed. They have since appealed to the Supreme Court. The Supreme Court has not stayed the High Court’s judgment; it has merely adjourned the hearing. Nevertheless, all fourteen DSPs continue to serve in violation of the High Court’s ruling. After the High Court had declared their appointments invalid, allowing them to continue in service was itself a breach of the Court’s order. It is now said that the AIADMK Government has even promoted those fourteen DSPs as Additional SPs. That too has been challenged in the High Court.

I believe these examples are sufficient to show how the Police Department is being made to function at the Government’s bidding. Had these wrongs occurred under a DMK Government while today’s Chief Minister Jayalalithaa was Leader of the Opposition, how fiercely would she have attacked the DMK Government in her statements? She would have thundered: “Under this minority Government, the person responsible for the Police Department must resign today itself!”

Does having a “majority” Government mean that it may do anything it pleases?

**With affection,**  
**M. K.**
8 October 2013
