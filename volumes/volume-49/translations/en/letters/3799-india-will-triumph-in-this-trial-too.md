# 3799. India Will Triumph in This Trial Too!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 30 August 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

The value of the Indian rupee against the United States dollar has continued to fall and has reached 68.80. It is said that the United States, seeking to protect itself from economic crisis, has taken steps to strengthen the dollar, and that the currencies of countries such as India, Indonesia, Brazil, South Africa and Turkey have consequently been falling rapidly.

Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru served as India’s Prime Minister for seventeen years. During his time, the rupee stood at 4.76 to the dollar. Indira Gandhi served as Prime Minister for fifteen years, and the exchange rate did not go beyond 11.36. It was 16.22 during Rajiv Gandhi’s prime ministership, 35.43 under P.V. Narasimha Rao and 45.31 under Vajpayee. Now, during the nine years of Prime Minister Manmohan Singh’s Government, the rupee has suffered an unprecedented and alarming fall to 68.80.

Last Friday, 23 August 2013, the rupee recovered slightly from its decline and stood at 64. On 26 August it was 64.31. On 27 August it fell to 66.24, losing 193 paise in a single day—a three per cent fall, the steepest daily decline in ten years.

There was no improvement on 28 August. The decline only continued. In the morning, the rupee fell to 67.06 against the dollar, then moved through 67.18, 67.35, 67.37 and 67.40 before depreciating to 68.80. Experts have warned that unless India’s weakened economy is lifted and strengthened, the rupee may fall to 75 to the dollar. Altogether, since January this year, the rupee has lost more than 19.50 per cent against the United States dollar. It is a darkness of a kind never before seen or heard of in the Indian economy.

Nor has the fall been confined to the dollar. The rupee has also weakened against the British pound, falling to 106 to the pound. It has fallen to 92 against the euro, ₹75 against the Swiss franc, ₹65 against the Canadian dollar, ₹60 against the Australian dollar and about ₹50 against both the New Zealand and Singapore dollars. The Kuwaiti dinar has risen beyond ₹240. *The Economist* has described the Indian rupee as the most undervalued currency in the international market at present.

A chain of consequences from the rupee’s fall is unavoidable. The stock market too has declined over the past several days. The value of investors’ shares has fallen by 170 lakh crore.

Although the Union Government says it is taking various measures to arrest the rupee’s decline, none appears to be producing an immediate result. Public-sector oil companies and importing firms continue to purchase dollars in large quantities and at high cost.

Because oil companies buy large quantities of United States dollars, the rupee is falling in overseas markets. The conflict in Syria caused crude-oil prices in the London market to rise by 0.4 per cent on 27 August 2013, and prices are expected to rise further. India imports eighty per cent of its crude-oil requirement. A rise in oil prices therefore raises the cost of imports and sends still more dollars out of the country. It will also increase the Union Government’s petroleum-subsidy burden.

Many products manufactured in India depend upon components purchased abroad. The rupee’s decline will therefore raise the prices of televisions, mobile phones, laptops, printers and similar goods. Electronics manufacturers are said to have decided on price increases of five to seven per cent. Sony is said to have decided to raise its prices by four to six per cent, while computer and printer manufacturers have decided on increases of eight per cent.

Companies manufacturing consumer appliances import half the inputs required for the goods they sell. Higher import costs for crude oil, pulses, cooking oil, fertiliser and coal will push inflation still higher. In July, wholesale-price inflation had risen to 5.79 per cent and retail-price inflation to 9.64 per cent.

Metro-rail construction is under way in Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, Bengaluru and Hyderabad. Since foreign companies are carrying out these projects, the rupee’s fall has created a need to allocate more money than originally provided. Those companies and personnel such as engineers are paid in dollars.

Former Union Minister and BJP leader Yashwant Sinha has said that the Union Government must accept responsibility for the rupee’s severe fall and resign; that the crisis can be managed only if the Government resigns; that the Congress-led coalition’s time in office is over; that the economy will recover only after it leaves; and that investors have lost faith in the Indian economy. Several other Opposition leaders have said that the UPA Government has suffered a catastrophic economic failure and no longer deserves to remain in office. They have alleged that an economic “tsunami” has struck India and that Manmohan Singh, P. Chidambaram and Montek Singh Ahluwalia are responsible.

Prime Minister Manmohan Singh certainly possesses profound economic knowledge. He was the principal architect of the new economic reforms introduced twenty years ago. But when old economic strategies cease to produce results, new approaches must be adopted and a solution found without delay.

Union Finance Minister P. Chidambaram has said that the Government is taking vigorous action to control the rupee’s decline and is seeking to reduce the fiscal deficit to 4.8 per cent of gross domestic product. He told journalists:

> “The rupee has overshot its target; it is undervalued. We must be patient and we must be firm. We are doing whatever is necessary; the rupee will reach its proper value.”

He also said that the economic situation had been extremely bad when he assumed office as Finance Minister; that several steps taken by the previous Finance Minister had increased the Government’s economic burden; and that neither industrial growth nor agricultural development had occurred to the expected extent. Even he, however, said that no deadline could be fixed for when the rupee would regain strength or rise.

The Finance Minister’s ten-point programme for improving India’s economy lists the following objectives: contain the fiscal deficit within 4.8 per cent; contain the current-account deficit within 7,000 crore dollars; improve the management of financial resources; increase capital; activate stalled projects; improve the performance of public-sector enterprises; increase capital expenditure; strengthen the performance of public-sector banks; promote exports; and increase industrial production.

Responding to him in an article in today’s *Dinamani*, S. Gurumurthy has criticised the Finance Minister in these words:

> “After watching in the silence of a sage for eighteen months while the Indian rupee fell sharply, Union Finance Minister P. Chidambaram announced on 12 August that he would reduce the current-account deficit in order to stabilise the rupee.”

Our Finance Minister has said the current-account deficit must be contained within 7,000 crore dollars. We should understand what the “Current Account Deficit” means. It is the gap between the total foreign exchange we spend on imports and the foreign exchange we earn through exports; depending on the balance, there is either a current-account deficit or a current-account surplus. When we export more, the current account produces a surplus. When we import more, it produces a deficit. When the deficit becomes too large, it creates an economic crisis. The current-account deficit has now grown to 8,900 crore dollars.

The Government says the deficit has grown so large because of heavy imports of crude oil and gold. That explanation alone is not the whole truth.

When discussing imports, we must also understand imported production inputs. When a manufacturer imports material in order to produce an item, it is an import of capital goods or production inputs. By importing such inputs, manufacturing new goods and exporting them at a higher price, the current-account deficit created by imports can be offset.

In 2004–05, imports of such materials were worth about 2,550 crore dollars. They are now imported to the value of 58,700 crore dollars.

For reasons such as these, the dollar has risen sharply and the external value of the Indian rupee against it has fallen to an unprecedented level. Since May this year, the rupee has declined by eighteen per cent. As a result, economic growth during this financial year is expected to fall below five per cent.

> “The life of one who lives without knowing the measure  
> will seem to exist—then vanish without a trace.”

Kalaignar’s commentary on this *Tirukkural* explains:

> “Unless plans are framed with an understanding of the measure of what exists, what can be created and what can yet be earned, apparent strength or wealth will disappear until nothing remains.”

Economic plans must be framed with this warning from Valluvar in mind. Our natural resources are boundless. Our human resources are incomparable. Since attaining independence, India has faced many challenges and continued to advance. Let us hope with confidence that India will certainly triumph in this economic trial too.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**

30 August 2013

நடப்புக் கணக்குப் பற்றாக்குறையை ஈடுகட்ட முடியும்.

2004-2005இல் மூலப் பொருள்களின் இறக்குமதி 2550
கோடி டாலர் அளவுக்கு இருந்தது. தற்போது 58700 கோடி
டாலருக்கு மூலப் பொருள்கள் இறக்குமதி செய்யப்படுகின்றன.

இதுபோன்ற காரணங்களால்தான் டாலர் மதிப்பு மிகவும்
உயர்ந்து, அதற்கு நிகரான இந்திய ரூபாயின் வெளிமதிப்பு
வரலாறுகாணாத அளவிற்கு வீழ்ச்சி அடைந்து வருகிறது.
இந்த ஆண்டு மே மாதத்திலிருந்து இதுவரை ரூபாய் மதிப்பு 18
சதவிகிதம் வீழ்ச்சி அடைந்துள்ளது. இதன் காரணமாக இந்த
நிதி ஆண்டில் பொருளாதார வளர்ச்சி 5 சதவிகிதத்திற்கும் கீழே
செல்லும் என்று கூறப்படுகிறது.

“அளவறிந்து வாழாதான் வாழ்க்கை உளபோல
இல்லாகித் தோன்றாக் கெடும்”

அதாவது, “இருப்பது, இயற்றக்கூடியது, இனியும்
ஈட்டக்கூடியது ஆகியவற்றின் அளவு அறிந்து செயல்
திட்டங்களை வகுத்துக் கொள்ளாவிட்டால், வலிமையோ
அல்லது வளமோ இருப்பதுபோல் தோன்றினாலும் கூட
இல்லாமல் மறைந்து போய்விடும்”

(திருக்குறள்-கலைஞர் உரை) என்ற அய்யன் வள்ளுவரின்
எச்சரிக்கையைக் கருத்திலே கொண்டு பொருளாதாரத்
திட்டங்கள் வகுக்கப்பட வேண்டும். நமது இயற்கை வளங்கள்
எல்லையற்றவை. நமது மனித வளம் ஈடுஇணையற்றது. இந்தியா
விடுதலை அடைந்ததற்குப் பின்னர் எத்தனையோ சவால்களைச்
சந்தித்து முன்னேறி வருகிறது. தற்போது ஏற்பட்டுள்ள இந்தப்
பொருளாதாரச் சோதனையிலும் இந்தியா நிச்சயம் வெற்றிபெறும்
என எதிர்பார்ப்போம்!
