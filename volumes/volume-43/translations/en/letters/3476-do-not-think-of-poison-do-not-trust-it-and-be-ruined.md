# 3476. Do Not Think of Poison; Do Not Trust It and Be Ruined!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This conference invitation moves through Bharathi and Bharathidasan, Dravidian/Tamil cultural imagery and a direct appeal to the Udanpirappu, then answers the charge that the World Classical Tamil Conference was intended for the author’s self-publicity. It contrasts A.R. Rahman’s `பிறப்பொக்கும்` conference song with the praise song used at the 1995 Thanjavur conference and closes on the source’s `தஞ்சை` / `நஞ்சை` wordplay. The political comparison is translated as the author’s argument; no outside account is imported.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3476](../../../chapters/3476-nanjai-ennaadhe-nambik-kedaadhe.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 354](../../../pages/page-354.md)–[PDF 357](../../../pages/page-357.md)  
**Date:** 17 June 2010

**Udanpirappē,**

For the Tamil of which Bharathi sang, “Among all the languages we know, nowhere do we see one as sweet as Tamil” —

for the Tamil of which Pavendhar sang, “Tamil is milk for our youth” —

to add strength and splendour to it —

to gather the greetings offered by the people of the world —

we are holding a celebration in the Kongu region where benefactors like Kumanan once lived.

Yes; a celebration for Tamil!

A celebration so that the Tamil people shall not fall!

That celebration, Udanpirappē, is the World Classical Tamil Conference!

More than fifty distinguished scholars from foreign countries, and language experts living in every State of India, are all coming south like bees swarming around a pot of honey.

To drink in Dravidam —

Dravidian civilisation —

their art —

their culture —

their heritage —

their customs and practices —

the sweet Tamil segments, like the jackfruit of the South, ripening in Dravidam; yes, the segments of literature, the segments of artistically graceful poetry — and to melt at their sweetness —

not only are thousands upon thousands of Tamil scholars coming;

they are coming also to hear the arguments put forward by those among them rich in Tamil learning and linguistic learning —

and, with the strength given by the Tamil milk drunk at the mother’s breast —

to become a spear that climbs the mountain peak and fells the opponent —

to straighten even the hunchbacked posture of the futile who, their minds confused, walk according to fate —

to win many victories — to write upon the brow of the Himalaya the history of our Tamil’s victory —

to let every country know that all the Tamils who have come here stand with hearts of steel held upright —

to add strength to my Tamil Mother, who has neither beginning nor end —

and to hold aloft the banner of fame proclaiming that, among the languages of the world, then, now and forever hereafter, this is the first language.

“Come! Come!” I call the cuckoos of colourful Tamil Nadu to the city of Coimbatore!

Are you not the lion that rises in revolt —

as a spear that springs forward,

as a sword that gathers fury,

saying, “We shall pluck out and cast away hostility”?

I am not calling you to such a battlefield.

My dear one! I call you to the beautiful, knowledge-filled halls where eminent poets, scholars and great men of learning will sit and attend closely.

You know the purity soaked into my invitation!

You know the expression of affection that fills it!

Is not a letter written by one Udanpirappu to another a living portrait drawn by squeezing out one’s very life?

That is why, unable to come to you in person, I am sending this living portrait to you!

Do not remain there surrendering your heart to the beauty of the portrait — set out on your journey, my affectionate Udanpirappu!

Do you hear a voice from somewhere? They call the cry of a certain animal “Kambodhi,” do they not? It is that “Kambodhi” that is being heard!

The “World Classical Tamil Conference,” it says, is supposedly being held so that I may publicise my own name! I know you will not believe it! But have you forgotten who, at the World Tamil Conference held one day in Thanjavur, had a “Tamil Thai Vazhthu” prepared in her own name and sung? If you have forgotten, remember it now!

“Avvai and Andal, those noble women too,
sang beautifully and nurtured Tamil;
Mother Chief Minister Jayalalithaa,
remaining in office, nurtures it!
As Mother India and Mother Tamil
shine together in harmony,
a great gem that came though it was not expected —
long live Mother Jayalalithaa!”

For those who composed a song calling themselves “Tamil Mother” and “the great gem that came though it was not expected,” had it sung, listened to it and rejoiced — where is the tribute-song I offered, set to music by Thambi A.R. Rahman, beginning with `Pirappokkum` and bringing together the songs of Sangam literature in honour of our ancestors of undying fame?

And where is the self-praise song they prepared for the Thanjavur conference?

Are you saying, “Anna, why think of a bad thing in the midst of a good feast?” Forgive me! I could not avoid telling the old story.

Those innocent multitudes of disciples must not be allowed to forget it, must they?

That is why I reminded you of Thanjavur — *Thanjai*!

Tamil Nadu, do not think of poison — *nanjai*!

Tamil Nadu, do not trust it and be ruined!

Does not your Udanpirappu have the right to say this?

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**17-6-2010**

## Letter-specific notes

1. The title and close retain the source’s `தஞ்சை` / `நஞ்சை` sound-play; the English cannot reproduce the sound exactly, so both forms are made visible at the point of the wordplay.
2. The four-line 1995 conference praise-song is translated from the canonical Tamil as printed; no outside version is substituted.
3. `காம்போதி` is retained as **Kambodhi** because the source explicitly presents it as the name used for an animal’s cry and deploys it rhetorically.
4. The completed second visual/textual-fidelity pass established that the actual title matches the printed contents. The earlier provisional title-discrepancy claim remains superseded.
