# 3813. This Is the DMK Nurtured by So Many Samikkannus!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 30 September 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

You know that I am ninety years old. Even now, I do not fail to come to Anna Arivalayam every day. By the time I finish writing either an *Udanpirappu* letter or questions and answers for the morning edition of *Murasoli*, it is already 10:30. Only then do I have breakfast; only after that do I come to Arivalayam. By the time I finish meeting visitors—the movement’s front-line functionaries, district secretaries, those who come to contribute election funds, and those wishing to bring local problems to my attention—it is already one in the afternoon. Around one, after watching the news, I come outside and find more movement comrades and reporters waiting there. The reporters say, “Just one question! Just one question!” When I call them closer, they ask nine questions. When I remind them that they said there would be only one, they reply, “One question from each of us, Ayya!” I answer as far as I can. Even when I think of getting past them and leaving, a few more movement comrades remain. It was in this way, a few days ago, as I was preparing to leave, that an elderly man came near me.

“Who are you? What do you need?” I asked.

“I am a party man, Ayya. I need nothing. I only wanted to see you—that is all,” he said. I called him over near the car.

“Leader, I am about to enter my hundredth year. From the time I first became conscious of the world, I have belonged to the DMK. I am from Kanchipuram district. My only wish was to receive your blessings,” he said.

I blessed him and left. The old man was filled with deep satisfaction.

I read the petition he had placed in my hand. It said:

> Ayya, greetings. I am a poor man belonging to the Adi Dravidar community and residing in Kanchipuram district. I am now one hundred years old. From the beginning until today, I have served as a member of the DMK’s Esur branch. My entire family belongs to the DMK. The people of our area know that I have remained committed to the principles of the DMK until this day. On 27 September 2013 I shall enter my hundredth year. I submit this petition seeking your good wishes. We are also going to celebrate my hundredth birthday in our village on 27 September 2013. I most humbly request that you bless me. Yours always, a DMK volunteer. I have enclosed my membership card.

When I learned that his hundredth birthday was on 27 September 2013, I asked younger brother Stalin to arrange for the district secretary to go personally to the man’s home and convey greetings. I handed the petition to Stalin and departed. Stalin took Samikkannu and his wife into his room, honoured them with a shawl, gave them one thousand rupees, and sent them on their way.

As I had asked, Stalin also contacted Kanchipuram district secretary T. M. Anbarasan and requested that he go to Samikkannu’s home on his birthday and honour him on behalf of the movement.

Perhaps because that hundred-year-old Samikkannu had said “Kanchipuram district” at Anna Arivalayam, the moment I set out by car for home, Anna entered my stream of memories. After him came C. V. M.; then C. V. Rajagopal, K. T. S. Mani, Kanchi Kalyanasundaram, Kambarasapuram Rajagopal, Venga, Madurantakam Arumugam, Poonamallee Rajarathinam, T. M. Parthasarathy who wrote the history of the DMK, Kundrathur T. Mohanalingam, T. N. Sambandam of the same Kundrathur, and Manimozhiyar. One after another, they appeared before my mind’s eye and enquired after my well-being.

To Anna, who had come into my memory, I said, “I have grown old, Anna.”

“What do you mean, grown old?” he replied. “When I left you in 1969, did I not say that the work of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam would be needed for at least another fifty years? Have you forgotten? Those fifty years take us only to 2019. Even after that, the work of our movement will still be needed by Tamil Nadu.”

As I travelled home, I turned over in my mind the many occasions on which, while Anna was alive, we had gone to Kanchipuram to meet him, the intimacy with which we had moved together, and the many ideas we had heard from him. Then I remembered again that all these memories had been stirred by the fact that Samikkannu of Kanchipuram district had reached the age of one hundred.

On the 27th, Kanchipuram district secretary Anbarasan and other front-line district functionaries went to Samikkannu’s home. They garlanded the couple, conveyed my birthday greetings, presented a purse of money on behalf of the district organisation, photographed the occasion, and sent the photograph to me.

The elderly man is Thiru Samikkannu. He lives in the Adi Dravidar Colony at Esur village in Chithamur Union, Kanchipuram district. His wife is Kasiyammal. Samikkannu is an agricultural labourer. Kasiyammal cuts and sells firewood and also works as an agricultural labourer. The couple have two sons, Govindan and Ashok, and two daughters, Devi and Sathyavani.

For many years, it had been Samikkannu’s dream—he who held an intense attachment to the DMK—to meet me and speak to me in person. Yet he had passed the years wondering whether such a meeting would ever be possible.

He is said to tell movement comrades who visit him that in the 2016 Assembly election they must make Leader Kalaignar Chief Minister for the sixth time. He is said to have attended every conference conducted by the DMK. Whenever younger brother Stalin and I addressed public meetings in Chennai or Kanchipuram district, he never failed to attend. He never thought of seeking even the smallest position. His joy, he says, lies simply in being a volunteer.

The second son of this hundred-year-old movement comrade, Ashok, was living in Puzhuthivakkam, Chennai, when a fire broke out on 25 February 2009. Ashok, his wife Sivasakthi, their sons Seeman and Suresh, and their daughter Suganya were all burned to death. Though this tragedy deeply affected Samikkannu, he did not stop his movement work and has continued it to this day.

Samikkannu is not the only one of his kind in our movement. He has never held any post. He has never even paused to ask what benefit the party had given him. The Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam is a movement built and nurtured by countless Samikkannus. When people like him joined this movement and began building it, they did not expect that one day our party would come to power in Tamil Nadu, or that its members would become legislators, Members of Parliament and ministers. Their sole aim was to build the movement.

On 27 February 2012, on behalf of the DMK, we observed the inauguration of the centenary of the Dravidian movement. I took part along with General Secretary Perasiriyar, Dravidar Kazhagam President K. Veeramani, Justice Mohan, Professor Ma. Nannan and Professor Suba Veerapandian. Our movement is sustained by innumerable volunteers like Samikkannu—the prop roots of the banyan tree called the Dravidian movement. The growth of that movement is our very life.

On behalf of the Dravidian movement, which itself is more than a hundred years old, I wholeheartedly greet Samikkannu—an Adi Dravidar, one hundred years of age, an unwavering member of the DMK from the beginning until today—who proclaims even in his hundredth year that he knows nothing beyond the growth of this movement. I also greet his wife. As far as we know, and according to the information now available, I believe Kanchipuram Samikkannu to be the senior-most living volunteer of our movement. Let all of us join together in greeting that veteran comrade.

Long live Samikkannu; may his service continue to grow!

**With affection,**  
**M. K.**
30 September 2013

மற்றும் காஞ்சிபுரம் மாவட்டக் கழக முன்னணியினரும் 27ஆம்
தேதியன்று சாமிக்கண்ணுவின் இல்லத்திற்கே சென்று அந்தத்
தம்பதியினருக்கு மாலை மரியாதை செய்து, என் சார்பில் பிறந்த
நாள் வாழ்த்துகளையும் தெரிவித்து, மாவட்டக் கழகத்தின் சார்பில்
பொற்கிழியும் வழங்கி, அதைப் புகைப்படம் எடுத்து எனக்கு

துணைவியார் காசியம்மாள். சாமிக்கண்ணு விவசாயக் கூலித்

அவரும், அவருடைய துணைவியார் சிவசக்தி என்பவரும்

ஆகியோரும் தீயில் கருகி இறந்துவிட்டார்களாம். சாமிக்கண்ணுவை
இந்தச் சம்பவம் பெரிதும் பாதித்த போதிலும், கழகப் பணியை

எத்தனையோ சாமிக்கண்ணுகள் இணைந்து வளர்த்த கட்சிதான்
