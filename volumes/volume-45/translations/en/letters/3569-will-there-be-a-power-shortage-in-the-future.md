# 3569. Will There Be a Power Shortage in the Future?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 18 June 2011

**Udanpirappē,**

Replying to the debate on the Governor's Address, Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Jayalalithaa spoke at length about the power shortage. She said that if the DMK government had continued the efforts undertaken while she was in office, Tamil Nadu could have avoided the power shortage; that from July the present three-hour power cut in Tamil Nadu would be reduced to two hours; and that during the DMK period nothing had been done to increase electricity generation. She showered statistics in support of these charges. Since I was the Chief Minister responsible for the government during the past five years, I wish to reply to her accusations.

Let me explain what was done for power generation after the DMK government came to office in **2006**.

A project to generate **600 MW** through the Ennore Thermal Power Station expansion was begun on **8-12-2006** at an estimated cost of **₹3,136 crore**; generation from it is to begin in **August 2013**.

At the **Mettur Thermal Power Station**, a project capable of generating **600 MW**, estimated at **₹3,100 crore**, was begun on **2-5-2007**; its generation is to come into operation from **July 2011**.

At the **North Chennai Thermal Power Station**, a project to generate **600 MW** at an estimated **₹2,475 crore** was begun on **26-6-2007**. Its generation should have come into operation in **May 2011**; it is now expected in September.

At **Udangudi**, under a joint project of **BHEL** and the Tamil Nadu Electricity Board, two units each capable of generating **800 MW** were begun on **15-10-2007** at an estimated **₹8,362 crore**. One unit is to begin generation in **March 2013**, and the other in **September 2013**.

At the North Chennai Thermal Power Station itself, another project capable of generating **600 MW** was begun on **14-7-2008** at an estimated **₹2,475 crore**. Its generation is to come into operation from **August 2011**.

At a cost of **₹1,126 crore**, a project capable of producing **183 MW of co-generated power** from cooperative and public-sector sugar mills was begun on **14-12-2009**. Its generation is to be brought into operation from **July 2011**.

Thus projects to generate a total of **4,183 MW** were begun under the DMK government. After the DMK government was formed in 2006, various measures were taken with future demand in mind. Through different power projects, provision was made for **1,400 MW in 2010-2011**, **3,316 MW in 2011-2012**, **1,222 MW in 2012-2013**, and **1,860 MW in 2013-2014**—a total of **7,798 MW**.

I believe these statistics provide a firm answer to the charge that the DMK government did not begin any new electricity-generation projects.

After the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam government was formed in Tamil Nadu in **2006**, electricity demand increased because many new industries were established in different parts of the State. After May 2006, through the efforts of **TIDCO**, memoranda of understanding were signed for **27 industries** to be started.

Through **24 Government Orders**, permission was given for **24 industries** to be established. Of these, twelve factories have so far been opened: **Motorola** on 27-4-2008; **Apollo Radial Tyres** on 10-2-2011; **Samsung India** on 13-11-2008; **Sanmina (SCI) Corporation** in December 2007; the second division of **Hyundai Motor** on 2-2-2008; **Dell Computers** on 22-4-2008; the source-named **Grothling** factory on 16-7-2008; **Nokia Siemens** on 16-10-2008; **Hansen Transmissions International** on 24-1-2010; the **Ford expansion plant** on 5-2-2010; the **Nissan India car factory** on 17-3-2010; and **ADC Tyres**.

Apart from these, assistance was provided through **SIPCOT** and the **Tamil Nadu Industrial Investment Corporation** for establishing new industries. As a result, industries multiplied in Tamil Nadu and the State faced the need to supply the electricity they required.

Tamil Nadu's estimated peak demand was **11,000 MW**, while generation during the DMK government was about **7,300 MW**. The shortage was therefore **3,700 MW**. Every effort was made under the DMK government to manage this shortage. **2,200 MW** was purchased from power producers, and power was also purchased from the electricity market.

The Tamil Nadu government encouraged the establishment of coal-based thermal power stations as private merchant-power projects. As a result, private companies came forward to establish **ten coastal thermal-power projects** capable of generating **18,140 MW** in Tamil Nadu. One private company began preliminary work for a **1,200 MW** project, expected to begin generation in **2011-2012**.

Taking all this into account, the explanation given was that if efforts on this scale had been undertaken during the preceding AIADMK government itself, calculating future requirements, the generation now required would already have begun and Tamil Nadu would not have faced a shortage.

Apart from thermal, hydro, nuclear, gas and wind power, Tamil Nadu also generated electricity through co-generation, biomass and captive-use generating stations. Under the DMK government, **487 MW** was available through co-generation, **127 MW** through biomass generation, and **851 MW** through captive generation.

In addition, we requested and obtained **2,350 MW** from the Union Government's allocation. An additional **75 MW** was obtained from **26-3-2008** to meet peak-hour demand.

The total installed capacity of windmills connected to the grid was then **4,888 MW**. Private windmills added **578 MW in 2006-2007**, **381 MW in 2007-2008**, **431 MW in 2008-2009**, and **600 MW in 2009-2010** to the grid.

Another accusation made by Jayalalithaa was that **“during the period from 2006 to 2011—that is, during the DMK government—these thermal power stations did not receive any award of any kind.”**

The **Thoothukudi Thermal Power Station** received from the Government of India the award for excellent transportation based on high imports through Thoothukudi Port for the years **2006-2007, 2007-2008 and 2008-2009**. Under the DMK government, the fourth unit of the **Mettur Thermal Power Station** generated electricity continuously for **300 days up to 1-10-2009**, surpassing the third unit's previous record of **272 days**. The first unit of the **North Chennai Thermal Power Station** generated electricity continuously for **214 days** during 2006 and 2007.

Jayalalithaa next referred to **Vallur** and the **North Chennai Thermal Power Station** during her previous term and said that if the DMK government had continued the efforts made by her government, the power shortage would not have arisen.

The total value of the **Vallur Thermal Power Station project** is **₹8,000 crore**. Work on Units 1 and 2 was begun under the DMK government on **13-8-2007**. Work on Unit 3 began on **28-7-2009**. The boiler for Unit 1 has been installed and turbine-installation work is under way. Apart from the main machinery, work to install other equipment is proceeding for both units. The contract for purchasing the main machinery for Unit 3 was also awarded to **Bharat Heavy Electricals**, and that work too is under way.

Units 1, 2 and 3 are planned to be connected to the grid respectively in **August 2011, October 2011 and September 2012**, and to be commissioned respectively in **October 2011, December 2011 and November 2012**.

As for the **Thoothukudi Thermal Power Station**, the contract for the main machinery was awarded to Bharat Heavy Electricals on **28-1-2009**, during the DMK government itself. The project is expected to be commissioned in **August 2012**.

The first unit of the **Udangudi Thermal Power Station**, valued here in the source at **₹8,700 crore**, is expected to be commissioned in **March 2013**, and the second unit in **September 2013**.

Thus, after the DMK government conceived the projects, prepared project reports, followed the procedures required for implementation, and brought the power projects toward the generation stage, the AIADMK has come to office.

Is there a power shortage only in Tamil Nadu? Is there no power shortage in other States?

Statistics say there is a shortage of **5,200 MW in Maharashtra**, **2,384 MW in Uttar Pradesh**, **1,200 MW in Kolkata**, **1,500 MW in Punjab**, **1,120 MW in Karnataka**, **964 MW in Andhra**, **364 MW in Kerala**, **880 MW in Haryana**, **800 MW in Rajasthan**, and **1,178 MW in Tamil Nadu**.

In Andhra there is rotational load-shedding for **four hours a day**. In Karnataka there is rotational load-shedding for **three hours a day**. In Tamil Nadu too there is rotational load-shedding for **three hours a day**.

For agriculture, three-phase supply is given for a total of **seven hours a day in Andhra**, only **six hours a day in Karnataka**, and a total of **nine hours a day in Tamil Nadu**.

For industries, Andhra generally has power cuts on **eight days a month**, amounting to approximately **28 million units a day**. Karnataka has **three hours of daily power cuts for industries**, amounting to approximately **28 million units a day**. In Tamil Nadu, high-tension industries and commercial consumers have only a **30 per cent cut in demand and consumption**, amounting to approximately **20 million units a day**.

Thus, instead of power shortage being confined to only a few States such as Tamil Nadu, many States across India were severely affected by shortages. This also created a situation in which Tamil Nadu could not obtain the electricity it needed from those States.

Jayalalithaa takes pride in saying that during her **2001–2006** term Tamil Nadu was a State without any power cuts. But during Jayalalithaa's **1991–1996** government, was there or was there not a power-control system in Tamil Nadu up to **16-10-1992**? Likewise, during the AIADMK government from **3-10-1982 to 26-7-1984**, was there or was there not a power-control system?

If, after the DMK came to office in 2006, we had refrained from bringing more industries to Tamil Nadu, power-control measures might indeed not have become necessary. But can a government exist without encouraging industrial growth?

Because the AIADMK government did not start enough power-generation projects, a power shortage arose during the DMK government. But we can confidently believe that the benefit of the power-generation projects begun under the DMK government will certainly eliminate Tamil Nadu's power shortage in the future.

The AIADMK government may take pride in claiming that it was through its own ability that Tamil Nadu reached a condition without power shortage. However it happens, **it is enough if the people get electricity!** If uninterrupted, quality electricity reaches the Tamil people, it will always be a matter of happiness for everyone!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**18-6-2011**
