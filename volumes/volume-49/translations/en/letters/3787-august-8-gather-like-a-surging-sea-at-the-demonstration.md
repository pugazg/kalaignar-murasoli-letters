# 3787. August 8: Gather Like a Surging Sea at the Demonstration!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

An election to Sri Lanka’s Northern Provincial Council is to be held this coming September. Sri Lanka’s Chief Elections Commissioner, Mahinda Deshapriya, held consultations with representatives of all parties on 12 July 2013 regarding the arrangements. After that meeting, M. A. Sumanthiran, senior Member of Parliament of the Tamil National Alliance, said that the heavy concentration of the military in the Northern Province prevented the people from moving freely. The Alliance had long demanded a gradual reduction of the troops and had urged the Commissioner to reduce their numbers before the September election. The Commission rejected that request, though the Elections Commissioner assured them that military interference in administrative work would be reduced and the police would be used for such duties instead.

Rohan Hettiarachchi, head of an election-monitoring organisation, has said that the Commissioner agreed to invite observers from Commonwealth and Asian countries in order to help ensure a fair election.

Meanwhile, a delegation of six Members of the European Parliament has urged Sri Lankan Foreign Minister Peiris to conduct the election fairly.

International opinion has repeatedly called for a reduction in the immense military presence in the Northern Province. Yet the Sri Lankan Government refuses to withdraw the forces, advancing “security” as its excuse. If the election is to take place freely under these conditions, the troops concentrated there must be removed from the region.

C. V. Wigneswaran, the Tamil National Alliance’s candidate for Chief Minister in the Northern Provincial Council election, has said that devolution is impossible unless police powers and control of land in Tamil areas are transferred to the people there. A former judge of the Supreme Court of Sri Lanka, he said it would be politically suicidal to exclude police and land powers from the authority of the Provincial Council, and that responsibility for law and order should rest with the respective Council.

The police and military personnel now stationed in the Northern Province are all Sinhalese. Fifteen army divisions are encamped there. Of Sri Lanka’s total army strength of two lakh personnel, one lakh and fifty thousand are said to be stationed in the North alone. This enormous military concentration has created great fear among the people.

The resolutions adopted at the Tamil Eelam Supporters Organisation conference on 12 August 2012 are highly relevant to both the Sinhala occupation of traditionally Tamil lands and homes and the extensive military movement in the Northern Province. One resolution stated that the lands and homes of the Tamil people in the Eelam regions had been forcibly seized. In places including Sampur, Mannar and Vavuniya, many thousands of acres belonging to Tamils had been taken away. The conference called on the United Nations to act immediately to end the cruelty by which Tamil Eelam people, dispossessed in the very regions where they had lived for generations, were forced to live as refugees on their own soil.

Another resolution explained that military dominance had replaced democratic government in Sri Lanka. The armed forces had taken possession of numerous Tamil-owned houses for military use and refused to vacate even empty homes under their control. No social event could be held in Tamil homes in the North and East without military permission. Military officers themselves were being appointed as District Collectors and civil administrators. Present-day Tamil Eelam, the resolution said, presented the appearance of a military camp.

There are also reports that, among those contesting the Northern Provincial election, is Ananthi, wife of Ezhilan, a former LTTE leader in the Trincomalee district. She is said to have filed her nomination in Jaffna. Douglas Devananda, now a Minister representing the ruling United People’s Freedom Alliance, is also reported to be interested in contesting as a Chief Ministerial candidate. Former MPs Dharmalingam and Sivalingam and others have reportedly filed nominations in Jaffna as well. With competition increasing among the Tamil parties, reports say that the Eelam People’s Democratic Party is actively engaged in selecting a suitable candidate.

Sri Lanka’s Thirteenth Constitutional Amendment, intended to provide a measure of devolution to the provinces, arose from the Rajiv Gandhi–Jayewardene Accord. With the support of the Rajapaksa Government, Sinhala organisations oppose the Amendment and demand its repeal. They continually claim that the Accord was imposed through India’s threat and pressure. Although we do not accept the Rajiv–Jayewardene Accord in its entirety, the fact remains that the Indian and Sri Lankan Governments discussed it for more than a year—from August 1985 to December 1986—and signed it only after deep consideration and many rounds of consultation.

The idea of elected provincial institutions did not suddenly originate in 1987. Even while Sri Lanka was under British rule, the recommendation of the Donoughmore Commission was accepted in 1940 and agreement was reached in principle to establish elected provincial councils, though it was not implemented. After independence, the Choksy Commission in 1955 recommended regional councils. The Bandaranaike–Chelvanayakam agreement of 1957 also accepted the concept of elected regional councils. Thus, “elected provincial councils” were not a new proposal born with the Rajiv–Jayewardene Accord; they had been debated and accepted by Sri Lankan authorities from 1940 onward.

It is in this situation—Tamil-owned land and homes being occupied by Sinhalese, a massive army remaining in the North, the Thirteenth Amendment being diluted, and the Rajapaksa Government moving to conduct an election—that United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights Mrs Navanethem Pillay is to visit Sri Lanka.

During her visit she is expected to examine reconstruction in the Tamil regions. Prathiba Mahanamahewa, Secretary of Sri Lanka’s Human Rights Commission, has said that the UN High Commissioner would inspect rehabilitation work in war-affected areas and submit a report at the following year’s human-rights meeting in Geneva.

Tamils throughout the world expect Mrs Pillay to examine directly the irregularities deliberately unleashed before the Northern Provincial election and the internationally discussed complaint that relief intended for Tamils is not reaching them properly, and to initiate corrective action through the United Nations.

One example concerns the fifty thousand houses to be built with Indian funding and allotted to displaced Tamils affected by the war. Sri Lanka has enacted a rule that a house will be allotted only to a family with at least four members. The result is that, instead of the houses being allotted to Tamils, many of the homes built with Indian money may go predominantly to Sinhalese families.

I have already pointed out the replacement of Tamil place-names in Tamil regions with Sinhala names. The Rajapaksa Government has created a new district called “Weli Oya,” using a Sinhala name, in a way that breaks the historic link between Vavuniya district in the Northern Province and Trincomalee district in the Eastern Province, connects the area to the Sinhala-majority Anuradhapura district, and settles only Sinhalese there. TESO has already drawn attention to these developments. It has also pointed out that, although a UN Human Rights Council resolution clearly said that during the period of a general election no new districts should be created, boundaries altered or new settlements encouraged, the Sinhala Government is acting in violation of that principle.

Taking all these matters together, a **Mass Tamil Demonstration** will be held on 8 August in every district headquarters across Tamil Nadu. Its purposes are to insist on full implementation of the Thirteenth Amendment; demand that India not participate in the Commonwealth conference to be held in Sri Lanka; call for the protection of Tamil Nadu fishermen; oppose Sinhala settlement in the Tamil regions; bring the TESO resolutions to the attention of the people of Tamil Nadu and the Union Government; and unite a Tamil uprising against Sinhala domination.

As Tamils across our motherland prepare to march in these demonstrations, I too will join them at the demonstration in Chennai, the capital. I ask you to come without fail.

Dear eyes of the movement, who have never hesitated to raise the voice of ethnic awakening for the Sri Lankan Tamils: come, come in a surging sea to the demonstration on 8 August!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**1 August 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. **TESO** is the Tamil Eelam Supporters Organisation.
2. The source uses **Tamil Eelam** both geographically and politically when describing the Tamil regions of Sri Lanka; the translation preserves that usage rather than replacing it with a different formulation.
3. **Lakh** is retained as an Indian numbering unit equal to 100,000.
4. The correction from `வேட்பாளரைச்` to the scan-visible `வேட்பாளரைத்` affects only the canonical Tamil transcription and not the meaning of the English translation.

முழுமையாக நிறைவேற்ற வலியுறுத்தியும், இலங்கையில்
நடைபெறவுள்ள “காமன்வெல்த்” மாநாட்டில் இந்தியா கலந்து
கொள்ளக் கூடாது என்பதை வற்புறுத்தியும், தமிழக மீனவர்களைப்
பாதுகாக்க வேண்டும் என்பதைச் சுட்டிக்காட்டியும், இலங்கையில்
தமிழர் பகுதிகளில் சிங்களக் குடியேற்றத்தைத் தடுக்க
வேண்டும் என்பதை எடுத்துக்காட்டியும், “டெசோ” கூட்டத்தில்
நிறைவேற்றப்பட்டுள்ள தீர்மானங்களை தமிழக மக்களின்
கவனத்திற்குக் கொண்டு செல்லவும், இந்திய மத்திய அரசின்
கவனத்தை ஈர்க்கவும், சிங்கள ஆதிக்க வெறிக்கு எதிராகத் தமிழர்
எழுச்சியை ஒன்று திரட்டவும்தான் வருகின்ற ஆகஸ்ட் 8ஆம்
தேதியன்று தமிழகம் முழுதும் உள்ள மாவட்டத் தலைநகரங்களில்
“தமிழர் பெருந்திரள் ஆர்ப்பாட்டம்” நடைபெறவுள்ளது. இந்த
ஆர்ப்பாட்டத்தில் அணிவகுக்க தாய்த் தமிழகத்திலே உள்ள
