# 3435. A Serial That Has Ended; A History That Cannot End!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation follows the audited canonical Tamil in source order. It preserves the letter’s literary-historical framing, quoted prefaces and greetings, named people and places, source chronology and the long fictional closing episode from *Paayum Puli Pandaraga Vanniyan*. Historical claims remain source-framed and are not reconciled with outside accounts.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3435](../../../chapters/3435-mudindha-thodarkathai-mudiyaatha-varalaaru.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 76](../../../pages/page-076.md)–[PDF 86](../../../pages/page-086.md)  
**Date:** 26 November 2009

**Udanpirappē,**

The historical portrait *Paayum Puli Pandaraga Vanniyan*, which had been appearing in *Murasoli* for the second time, has come to an end.

No matter how many times the history of that warrior appears, that heroic epic will continue to live.

This letter is to explain that.

In my stirring historical novel *Paayum Puli Pandaraga Vanniyan*, published around 1991, I portrayed the characters I created — Pandaraga Vanniyan; Kuruvichi Nachiyar, the beloved who captured his heart; and his dear younger sisters Nalla Nachi and Oomaichi Nachi — as people who fought to defend the rights of Tamils on Sri Lankan soil.

In one place in the foreword to that novel, our Professor referred to the words spoken by the betrayed Nalla Nachiyar to the villain Kakkai Vanniyan, who says, “Am I not a Tamil too?”:

> “You are a Tamil indeed! By race, by language, by the blood that runs in your body, you are a Tamil! But a Tamil without heat or a sense of shame! A Tamil who performs worship at someone’s feet for money! A Tamil who pawns his honour for office!”

The Professor then observed that one is led to wonder how many people in today’s Tamil land those angry words would fit, and wrote that the dominant feeling is the anguish that the thought itself will not leave the mind.

He then turned to another battlefield, in Tamil Nadu, which Pandaraga Vanniyan cherished. At Kayatharu, when a British Major accused Veerapandiya Kattabomman, the following words are given to Kattabomman in this book:

> “If there is a people in this world who failed to prosper because of lack of unity, it is the Tamil people! Even without enemies having to struggle to divide them, the people who on their own go to the extreme of bitterness, jealousy and rancour and stand divided are also the Tamil people! The result is here before you: Veerapandiyan stands in the cage, while so many Tamil petty kings stand watching the spectacle. Is this not the sacred political tableau of today’s Tamil Nadu!”

In his foreword, the Professor showed how painfully these words of Kalaignar in the book reverberated in his own heart.

After referring to my introduction titled “Thorana Vayil,” with which that heroic epic *Pandaraga Vanniyan* begins, I wish in this short series to set down a few historical notes as the next stage.

In 1982, the Vavuniya District Development Council in Sri Lanka organised a function at a place called Karsilai Madu in Vavuniya to erect a statue to Pandaraga Vanniyan. For the commemorative publication issued for that function, I sent the following greeting:

> “To the same extent that Veerapandiya Kattabomman in Tamil Nadu fought the British and sacrificed his life, the martyr and great warrior Pandaraga Vanniyan, in the Vanni country of Sri Lanka, raised the slogan ‘Freedom if there is to be life; otherwise there is no life!’ and fought until his final breath and gave his life.
>
> I am immensely happy to learn that dear friend and Member of Parliament T. Sivasithambaram and the committee have decided to erect a statue to him in Vavuniya and, in connection with it, to celebrate a Muthamizh festival. Even if the present fails to give sacrifice its due value, this statue-opening ceremony is worthy of praise as a sign that the future will certainly give it its value.”

From that very time, I developed an eagerness to collect information about that great warrior through many people. Yet those notes were not easy to obtain.

Professor Dr Vidyanandan, Vice-Chancellor of the University of Jaffna, who sent greetings for the statue-opening ceremony, wrote:

> “The great warrior Pandaraga Vanniyan fought foreigners until his last breath to defend the honour of Adangapattru, the Vanni country. He ruled patriotically with the welfare of the people in his mind. The British military commander Ondriberg erected a memorial stone to Pandaraga Vanniyan at Karsilai Madu in order to perpetuate his own fame. To proclaim Vanniyan’s courage, the Vavuniya District Development Council is erecting a statue in Vavuniya. One may expect this statue to make confidence, patriotism and the voice of rights rise not only among the people of the Vanni region but among all Eelam Tamils who have lost confidence and become weary. May this statue pave the way for people to desire and recognise good leaders, and for good leaders to arise!”

Was that freedom fighter truly defeated? Did his battle cry end with that? Or did that lion rise roaring again? The serial portrait *Paayum Puli Pandaraga Vanniyan* does indeed answer all these questions — a clear answer, an answer overflowing with courage.

When the statue was erected for that king who defended honour, Navalar Mr Amirthalingam, then Leader of the Opposition in the Sri Lankan Parliament, said in his greeting:

> “When the invasions of the British caused Tamil kings and Sinhala kings who ruled various parts of Sri Lanka to lose their sovereignties, the last king of Vanni, the great warrior Pandaraga Vanniyan, fought with all his breath until the end to preserve the sovereignty of the people under his rule. The cause of his fall was not merely British aggression; an important cause was also the base act of betrayal by one called Kakkai, who was like a disease born along with a person only to kill him.”

That pure man who had to encounter traitors was not without good friends. Kannusamy, known as Vikrama Raja Singhan, who ruled Kandy until 1815, was captured by the British, imprisoned for sixteen years in Vellore prison in Tamil Nadu and died in that prison; he was Pandaraga Vanniyan’s close friend.

For that great Tamil whose heart had been hurt by informers, there was also a maiden called Kuruvichi Nachiyar who slightly transformed his volcanic heart and became a cool shade in which he could rest. That palace of love, built on strength of mind, was a palace of resolve — a palace of sacrifice.

With the entry of the Portuguese, the Dutch and the British into Sri Lanka, the kingdoms of Tamil kings and Sinhala kings fell one after another. Yet Pandaraga Vanniyan’s kingdom and the kingdom of the King of Kandy alone remained, in the end, with the strength to resist the British who had established their dominance.

Pandaraga Vanniyan administered the region bounded by the Jaffna lagoon to the north, the Aruvi River to the south, Mannar district to the west and Trincomalee to the east.

He had to withstand both Dutch opposition and British opposition. Historical notes say that his sisters also governed and protected the frontiers in support of him: Nalla Nachi on the Trincomalee frontier and Oomaichi Nachi in the Panangamam region on the Mannar frontier. When one sees this, how fitting it is that the Tamils who lived on that soil worshipped Kannagi as their deity then and continue to worship her today.

In Tamil Nadu, at ancient Vanji, Cheran Senguttuvan took a stone from the Himalayas for Kannagi, the goddess of chastity, had the defeated Kanaka-Vijayars who had fought him carry it on their heads, and erected a statue. *Silappathikaram* says that at that time the Eelam king Kayavagu introduced that worship of the chaste woman in Eelam. There is a story still current in that region that Kannagi, whose anger did not subside even after burning Madurai, came to rest at the tenth place; it was called “Paththappalai,” and through change in usage became today’s “Vattrappalai.”

The warrior bearing the title Pandara Vanniyan, whose sisters and beloved Kuruvichi Nachiyar were portrayed as descendants of that Kannagi tradition, assumed the responsibility of rule at a very young age and, at only thirty-four, planted his fame through death on the battlefield.

He is referred to in historical notes as Pandara Vanniyan and in the title of this serial as Pandaraga Vanniyan. His personal name was Kulasekaram Vairamuthu.

The sword-warrior Vairamuthu was a descendant in the line of kings bearing the clan name Kulasekaram.

At the statue-opening ceremony, Tamil United Liberation Front leader Mr M. Sivasithambaram said:

> “Pandar Vanniyan’s life clearly shows us that, whatever the form of imperialism, when that imperialism attempts to take away a people’s sovereignty, that people will rise with valour and fight for its rights. I am proud, on this occasion, to point out that the people who live as his heirs on the soil where Pandara Vanniyan lived have expressed their political feeling in Sri Lanka’s political history as a people thirsting for freedom. The honour being given today does not belong only to the great warrior Pandara Vanniyan. What is being honoured today is his ideal and his desire for freedom.”

Even Sri Lankan Prime Minister Premadasa, in his greeting for the statue-opening ceremony, stated:

> “Honouring those who deserve honour is a principle of moral conduct,”

and:

> “I firmly say that the ceremony to erect a statue honouring the great warrior Pandara Vanniyan is an entirely proper act.”

Those words proclaim the warrior’s fame.

Pandaraga Vanniyan, who used a battle-sword as the emblem on his flag and sprang like a tiger through many battlefields — his appearance was:

Tall stature!

Broad chest!

Narrow waist!

Wide forehead!

Hardened shoulders!

Piercing gaze!

If you read how I fashioned the end of the war-story I offered by stringing together like a garland the heroes, warriors and martyrs of that soil of courage, born in the footsteps of that fearless hero’s life, along with their histories — here, read it for yourself!

Kuruvichi Nachiyar became somewhat confused. Edward, a British commander, had told her that Pandaraga Vanniyan had been defeated by the enemy, had made a peace agreement with him and was seated in his tent eating a feast. Hearing this, Kuruvichi Nachiyar was confused. Yet she could not completely deny the possibility that such a defeat might have occurred to Pandaragan amid the modern weapons of the British forces.

So, saying, “All right, I will come,” Kuruvichi Nachiyar put her sword into its sheath and followed Edward to meet Pandaragan. Two of Edward’s soldiers and two of Kuruvichi’s soldiers followed behind them from the front of the Mullaitivu palace. The six horses passed through many streets of Mullaitivu and were moving along a long, narrow road when a British soldier came rapidly from the opposite direction on horseback and stopped before Edward.

Edward looked at the soldier arrogantly and asked, “What?”

The soldier handed a rolled letter to Edward. Edward read the message with agitation, silently to himself:

> “Dear Edward! Pandaraga Vanniyan, together with fifty of his soldiers, has been surrounded and taken prisoner by our forces in the Ottusuttan area near the Nedunkani road. Friend! You must immediately go to Panangamam and defeat Kuruvichi Nachiyar, who is fighting us there with great fury. — Vandri Berg.”

So spoke the letter. Edward rejoiced inwardly. Delighted that, without even having to go to Panangamam, he had deceived Kuruvichi and was taking her with him, he congratulated himself. So that Kuruvichi would not suspect anything, he smiled at her and said, “You asked earlier where the tent was. That information has come in this letter.”

“Where is it?” asked Kuruvichi.

“It seems to be in the Ottukkattan area, near the Nedunkeni road!”

Edward rolled up the letter and threw it back to the soldier who had brought it. The soldier caught it deftly.

A grove thick with tall trees. Inside that grove, a tent. On each tree adjoining the tent, a warrior of Mullaitivu is bound in chains. Fifty warriors are tied in this way. In their midst, Pandaraga Vanniyan is chained to a large, strong tree.

The moment she saw that cruel sight, Kuruvichi forgot herself, ran to Pandaragan, embraced him and cried aloud. Stroking her hair, Pandaragan comforted her.

“Do not worry! They suddenly surrounded us while we were resting in this grove and now celebrate as though they have defeated us.”

Pandaragan growled like a tiger.

“They brought me here saying that you had reached an agreement with them!”

“An agreement? Do you believe that while this life remains, my head would nod to an agreement that enslaves the soil of Sri Lanka and Tamil Eelam to the foreign British?”

Kuruvichi stood without speaking. She thought deeply about something. Then, as one who had reached a decision, she looked at Vandri Berg and Edward and said:

“He will speak like that — but I can bring him around to my way. Even if we have not made an agreement with you until now, I am ready to make one from now on! He too will not reject what I say!”

As she said this, Kuruvichi winked at Pandaragan and asked, “You must not reject what I say! Is that right?” Pandaragan understood Kuruvichi’s mind and remained silent.

“Silence is a sign of consent! We have grown tired from fighting the British all this time. Our old weapons are wonderful! Powerful! Yet they cannot stand before your modern weapons! Even without weapons we can perform many feats! With nothing more than the sword and spear, we can perform feats that this world will celebrate!”

As Kuruvichi was speaking, Vandri Berg interrupted:

“With swords and spears, what feats can you perform that the world would celebrate?” he asked in astonishment.

“We will divide our fifty warriors into two groups and make them stand on either side. They will have swords in their hands. I will stand in the middle with a large fruit pierced by a spear and placed above my head. Our fifty warriors will charge at one another with swords from opposite sides and, without causing a single injury to anyone, will cut the fruit on the spear-point above my head into fifty pieces in a single stroke. Only one piece of the fruit will remain on the spear above my head.”

As soon as Kuruvichi said this, Vandri Berg asked, “Is that so?” and ordered the soldiers: “Hey! Untie everyone except Pandaraga Vanniyan! Let us see them perform that astonishing feat.”

At the words “except Pandaragan,” Kuruvichi was deeply disappointed. But she managed herself.

She fixed a large fruit on a spear, placed it above her head and stood in the middle. The Mullaitivu warriors who had been untied — all except Pandaragan — sat on horses with swords, twenty-five on each side.

“Come! Charge!” Kuruvichi cried, raising her hand and clapping it. In that instant the fifty warriors clashed with the British soldiers who had been standing there watching. Amid the great tumult, Pandaraga Vanniyan’s bonds were cut. Pandaragan sprang like a tiger onto a horse and stabbed Edward down. The bodies of hundreds of British soldiers were scattered in pieces.

Edward, fallen on the ground and breathing his last, raised his gun. The bullet flew toward the centre of Kuruvichi’s forehead. Pandaraga Vanniyan charged toward her on horseback to lift her away. But before he could do so, the bullets had drunk her life.

Without realising that her breathing had stopped, Pandaraga Vanniyan held her close on the horse and escaped from the British. His surviving warriors followed him.

She who had yearned to adorn herself as a bride and know the taste of life — as the sacred lamp of an ideal, a flame of sacrifice extinguished yet unextinguished — now wore the form of death, lay in Pandaragan’s lap within his tight embrace, and travelled swiftly on horseback.

Believing her still alive, he and the Tamil warriors following him travelled a long distance along the forest path.

The path they showed that day through the forest was the path of heroic warriors! Pandaraga Vanniyan was not one man alone; his history did not fail to help many like him — people of resolve, the light of truth and feeling — to arise. Therefore, it is a living history!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**26-11-2009**

பழத்தை வைத்துக்கொண்டு நடுவில் நிற்பேன். எங்கள் ஐம்பது
வீரர்களும் எதிரும் புதிருமாக வாளுடன் பாய்ந்து யாருக்கும் ஒரு
காயமில்லாமல் என் தலையில் ஈட்டி முனையில் உள்ள பழத்தை

ஐம்பது துண்டுகளாக ஒரே வெட்டில் வெட்டுவார்கள். ஒரே ஒரு
பழத்துண்டு மட்டும் ஈட்டியுடன் என் தலைமீது எஞ்சியிருக்கும்.”

குருவிச்சி இதைச் சொன்னவுடன், “அப்படியா?” என்ற
கேள்வியுடன் வான்ட்ரி பெர்க், வீரர்களைப் பார்த்து “ஏய்! பண்டாரக
வன்னியனைத் தவிர மற்றவர்களை அவிழ்த்து விடுங்கள்! அந்த
அதிசய சாதனையை அவர்கள் நிகழ்த்தட்டும் பார்க்கலாம்” என
ஆணையிட்டான்.

“பண்டாரகனைத் தவிர” என்றதும் குருவிச்சிக்குப் பெரும்
ஏமாற்றம்தான்! ஆனாலும் சமாளித்துக் கொண்டாள்.

பெரிய பழமொன்றை ஈட்டியில் பொருத்தி, தன் தலை
மீது வைத்துக் கொண்டு நடுவில் நின்றாள். பண்டாரகனைத்
தவிர கட்டவிழ்த்து விடப்பட்ட முல்லைத் தீவின் வீரர்கள் ஒரு
பக்கத்துக்கு இருபத்தைந்து பேராக வாட்களுடன் குதிரைகளில்
அமர்ந்திருந்தனர்.

“உம்! பாயலாம்!” என்று குருவிச்சி தனது கையை ஓங்கித்
தட்டியதுதான் தாமதம். அந்த ஐம்பது வீரர்களும் அங்கே
வேடிக்கை பார்த்துக் கொண்டிருந்த ஆங்கிலேய வீரர்களுடன்
மோதினர். பெரும் அமளிக்கிடையே பண்டாரக வன்னியனின்
கட்டுக்கள் களையப்பட்டன. பண்டாரகன், பாயும் புலியாகவே
ஒரு குதிரையிலேறி எட்வர்டைக் குத்திச் சாய்த்தான்.
நூற்றுக்கணக்கான ஆங்கிலேய வீரர்களின் உடல்கள் துண்டு
துண்டுகளாகச் சிதறின.

கீழே சாய்ந்த எட்வர்டு, மரண மூச்சு விட்டுக்கொண்டே தனது
துப்பாக்கியைத் தூக்கினான். துப்பாக்கிக் குண்டு, குருவிச்சியின்
நெற்றிப் பொட்டை நோக்கிப் பாய்ந்தது. அதற்குள் அவளைத்
தூக்கிக் கொண்டு போகப் பண்டாரக வன்னியன் குதிரையுடன்
அவளிடம் பாய்ந்தான். ஆனால் அதற்குள் துப்பாக்கிக் குண்டுகள்
அவள் உயிரைக் குடித்துவிட்டன.

அவள் மூச்சு நின்றுபோனது தெரியாமலே குதிரை
மீது அவளை அணைத்தவாறு பண்டாரக வன்னியன்,
ஆங்கிலேயரிடமிருந்து தப்பிவிட்டான். எஞ்சிய அவனது வீரர்களும்
அவனைப் பின் தொடர்ந்தனர்.

மணக்கோலம் பூண்டு வாழ்வின் சுவை அறியத் துடித்தவள்
- இலட்சியத் திருவிளக்காய் - அணைந்தும் அணையாத தியாகச்
சுடர்விளக்காய் - பிணக்கோலம் பூண்டு, பண்டாரகனின் மடியில்
படுத்துக் கொண்டு - அவனது இறுக்கமான தழுவலுடன்
குதிரையில் வேக வேகமாகப் போய்க் கொண்டிருந்தாள்.
அவள் உயிருடனிருப்பதாகவே கருதிக் கொண்டு அவனும்,
அவனைப் பின்தொடர்ந்த தமிழ் வீரர்களும் காட்டுப் பாதையில்
நெடுந்தூரம் சென்று கொண்டிருந்தனர்.
காட்டுப் பாதையில் சென்று அவர்கள் அன்று காட்டிய பாதை
வீரமறவர்களின் பாதை! பண்டாரக வன்னியன் ஒருவனல்ல;
அவனைப் போல பலர்; உறுதியும் வாய்மை ஒளியும் உணர்வும்
கொண்டவர்கள் தோன்றிட - அந்த மாவீரனின் வரலாறு பயன்படத்
தவறவில்லை. எனவே அது வாழும் வரலாறு!
