# 3750. Let Us Work to Make Ezhuchi Naal a Success!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

On 7 May 2013 I wrote about the statements which opponents claimed had been made by NASA concerning the Sethu Samudram project, and about the replies given by Thambi T.R. Baalu when he was a Union Minister. As further evidence, this is what the source says NASA stated:

> “NASA had issued the following disclaimer when it was brought
> to their notice that certain statements were attributed to them - “The
> image may be ours, but their interpretation is not certainly ours........
> Remote sensing images or photographs from orbit cannot provide direct
> information about the origin or age of a chain of islands, and certainly
> cannot determine whether humans were involved in producing any of
> the pattern seen”

Therefore the truth, according to the source, is that neither scientifically nor through deep-sea chemical analysis is there any basis or evidence that a bridge created by human beings existed there.

Dr Annamalai Magizhnan of Australia has written in an article that “Rama’s Bridge is a fabricated story.”

On 20 March 2007, during a discussion held on behalf of the Union Government about the Sethu Samudram project, N.K. Raghupathi, I.A.S., chairman and managing director of the project, explained with the relevant documents—before the Union Minister, departmental officials and representatives of religious organisations—that scientific and technical studies by various private and government institutions had found no evidence of a human-built bridge in the project area.

The delegation led by Swami Paramananda Saraswati, secretary of the Hindu Dharma Acharya Sabha, which participated in that meeting, expressed satisfaction with the explanation given by the officials.

They also told journalists that they supported the economic development of that region.

What did Mr Raghupathi, who had been chairman of the Thoothukudi Port Trust, say about the kind of transformation the Sethu Canal project would bring to the economy of India, including Tamil Nadu as a whole?

> “Just as the Suez Canal links the Red Sea and Madagascar, the Panama Canal links the Pacific and Atlantic oceans, and the Kiel Canal links the North Sea and Baltic Sea, the Sethu Samudram project, by linking the Gulf of Mannar and the Palk Strait through a canal and enabling the movement of large ships, will certainly bring industrial and commercial development and economic growth to Tamil Nadu.
>
> “This is a rare gift received by Tamil Nadu.”

From this, those who think with a sense of balance can understand the justification for the Sethu Samudram project. It is equally clear that those acting with the intention of stopping the project will never recognise that justification.

The Panama Canal and the Suez Canal were made by excavating and deepening land so that ships could pass through them. But the Sethu Canal project is completely different from those two canals! Different! Distinct! The Panama Canal was cut through land between the two countries of North America and South America; the Suez Canal through land between Egypt and Europe. The Sethu Canal, however, is planned entirely within sea territory belonging to India, near Pamban, by deepening to forty feet the rock formations known as Adam’s Bridge so that ships can pass.

Even before the DMK came to office, Perarignar Anna delivered a long speech in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly on 9 October 1960 on the Third Five-Year Plan. Speaking about the Sethu Samudram project, he said:

> “What we must ask the Union Government categorically is this: which plans are you going to implement definitely for the State government? Are you going to implement the Sethu Samudram project and the development of Thoothukudi harbour, or are you not? We must ask this categorically. At this moment I humbly ask the Cabinet: the people of the country know your ability very well. From now on they must judge your ability by whether you succeed in this. To investigate and submit a report, the Government of India appointed one of the foremost intellectuals of our State, Mr Ramasamy Mudaliar. After he studied the matter and submitted his report, are you going to keep quarrelling over minor details—whether it is ten crore or fifteen crore—and say you do not know whether it can be included in the Third Five-Year Plan? I once again ask this Cabinet of Tamil Nadu. This government must boldly ask the Union Government whether all these projects are going to be implemented—or whether we are to withdraw from this.”

Anna spoke in this manner in the Legislative Assembly about the Sethu Samudram project as far back as 1960—fifty years ago. At DMK conferences held in Thiruparankundram in 1961 and Virugambakkam in 1966, the Sethu Samudram project was pressed through resolutions. After the DMK came to office in 1967, Anna observed Ezhuchi Naal and pressed not only for the Sethu Samudram project and the Thoothukudi deep-sea harbour but continued to speak for them during the Assembly session as well.

When work on the Thoothukudi deep-sea harbour slowed and an attempt was made to turn it into a fishing harbour, Union Shipping Minister V.K.R.V. Rao visited Chennai in 1967. Anna and I, as Public Works Minister, went to the place where he was staying and met him. Anna also took with him former Chief Minister the elder statesman Bhaktavatsalam and former Industries Minister R. Venkataraman, and gave an assurance that if the port were to operate at a loss the State government itself would bear that loss. Perarignar Anna worked extraordinarily hard to secure the Thoothukudi deep-sea harbour.

In 1972, at the entrance to Thoothukudi port, Prime Minister Mrs Indira Gandhi unveiled the statue of the “Tamil Helmsman”, V.O.C., with me, then Chief Minister, presiding. On that occasion too, I said that the Sethu Canal project was absolutely essential for the usefulness of Thoothukudi port to grow further.

In the DMK manifesto for the 2004 parliamentary election we stated:

> “We will urge the Union Government to allocate the necessary funds and quickly begin and implement the Sethu Canal project, which has for a long time remained only at the stage of a promise.”

The source notes that this appeared on page 13. Like us, the AIADMK manifesto too, on page 33, then insisted that the Sethu Samudram project was necessary.

The Sethu Samudram project is a ₹2,427 crore project. The source gives the financing as follows: government equity ₹495 crore; Shipping Corporation of India ₹50 crore; Thoothukudi Port ₹50 crore; Dredging Corporation of India ₹30 crore; Ennore Port ₹30 crore; Paradip Port ₹30 crore; Chennai Port ₹30 crore; Visakhapatnam Port ₹30 crore; public share ₹226 crore; and government-guaranteed debt ₹1,456 crore—total ₹2,427 crore. Work worth about ₹800 crore had already been completed.

The Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs approved the project. Its chairman was the Prime Minister. The source states that Pranab Mukherjee, P. Chidambaram, Sharad Pawar, Lalu Prasad Yadav, T.R. Baalu, Dayanidhi Maran, Kamal Nath and Mani Shankar Aiyar were also members of that committee and approved the project.

The Sethu Samudram project, the source says, would generate eight crore person-days of employment every year. It would create large numbers of direct and indirect jobs. Development of Rameswaram and Nagapattinam ports would provide direct employment to about 10,000 people. The source further states that every tonne of cargo handled in the ports would provide employment to eleven people.

All the doubts raised by environmentalists about the Sethu Samudram project have been answered. Additional environmental tests costing ₹3 crore were carried out under the project. The project, it says, would make an operating profit from the very first year and would be directly profitable.

Every study conducted from 1955 until today has demonstrated technically that the project is feasible. There has not been even the slightest doubt about this.

Will the project damage coral reefs?

Except for the Adam’s Bridge section of the Gulf of Mannar, no other part of the Gulf of Mannar is to be dredged under this project. In the Gulf of Mannar, between Thoothukudi and Pamban Island, the coastal sea area has been declared the Gulf of Mannar Marine National Park. The Tamil Nadu government has declared the areas extending ten kilometres from this park the Gulf of Mannar Marine Biosphere Sanctuary. Coral reefs lie in the shallow waters around these twenty-one islands. There are no coral reefs in the proposed sea route or in the Adam’s Bridge area that is to be deepened—this, the source says, cannot be denied. Waste from ships travelling through this sea route would not be permitted under international law and Indian maritime law; therefore, it argues, there would not be even a slight possibility of waste, especially oil spills, affecting the coral reefs.

The land-based structures are proposed on 12.5 acres in the Dhanushkodi area and 2.5 acres in the Kodiyakarai area. Efforts are being made to use only land belonging to the Tamil Nadu government. Therefore there is no possibility of fishermen being displaced because of the infrastructure. The source adds that even if some fishermen live in huts on government land, the project company will bear the entire cost of their relocation.

Udanpirappē, it is against the continuation of such a distinguished Sethu Samudram project that the AIADMK government of Tamil Nadu has filed a petition in the Supreme Court. Even though the DMK is in opposition, we have announced **Ezhuchi Naal protest meetings throughout Tamil Nadu on the 15th** in order to explain to the people the necessity of the project.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**8 May 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. The NASA disclaimer is intentional English in the source and is reproduced exactly, including its punctuation and grammar.
2. The source’s comparison sentence about the Suez, Panama and Kiel canals is translated as printed; no external geographic correction has been inserted into the body. In particular, the source itself describes North America and South America as “the two countries”; that source wording is preserved here rather than silently corrected.
3. Environmental, employment, profitability and financing claims are source-attributed project arguments and figures; they are not independently reconciled here.

எடுக்கப்பட்டு வருகின்றன. எனவே கட்டமைப்புகள் ஏற்படுவதால் மீனவர்கள் இடமாற்றம் செய்யப்படும் வாய்ப்பும் இல்லை. சில

நிறுவனமே ஏற்கும் என்று சொல்லப்பட்டுள்ளது.
உடன்பிறப்பே, இத்தகைய சிறப்பான சேது சமுத்திரத்
திட்டத்தைத் தொடரக் கூடாது என்றுதான் தமிழக அ.தி.மு.க.
