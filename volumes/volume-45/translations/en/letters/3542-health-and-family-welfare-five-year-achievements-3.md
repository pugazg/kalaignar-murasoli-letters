# 3542. Five-Year Achievements of the Health and Family Welfare Department (3)

> **Translator’s note**
>

Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

I continue to write about the achievements and schemes carried out by the DMK government in the Health and Family Welfare Department during the past five years.

### HIV / AIDS control

Because the prevalence of AIDS stood at 1.13 per cent in 2001, Tamil Nadu was classified as a State with a high prevalence of HIV/AIDS. Through the awareness and preventive measures taken by this government from 2006, it has now fallen to 0.25 per cent. It is only in Tamil Nadu, among the States of India, that the number of people affected by HIV has declined and been stabilised in this manner.

For the welfare of children affected by AIDS, the Tamil Nadu Government established a trust on 6 April 2009 with an investment of ₹5 crore and has been providing assistance through it. In the 2009–10 financial year, educational assistance of ₹44.50 lakh was provided to 1,549 affected children who had lost their parents to AIDS and were left without support.

To provide quality food to mental-health patients receiving treatment in mental-health institutions, this government raised the daily food allowance from ₹13.70 to ₹20 from 18 June 2009, and from ₹20 to ₹40 from 28 August 2010.

In Tamil Nadu, first-line treatment has been provided to 12,961 HIV patients. The tablets for one person cost ₹6,000 a year. Second-line combination treatment is now being provided to 700 people at Tambaram, at an expenditure of ₹36,000 per patient per year. Second-line combination treatment has also been started in Salem and will shortly be provided in Madurai.

The reasons for the rapid decline in HIV prevalence in Tamil Nadu include:

- Tamil Nadu has 874 Integrated Counselling and Testing Centres (ICTCs), the highest number in India.
- 41 antiretroviral-therapy (ART) centres are functioning.
- Number registered at these centres: **1,62,029**.
- Number who began treatment: **73,850**.
- Number currently receiving treatment: **47,279**.

### Medical Beneficiaries Welfare Societies

“Medical Beneficiaries Welfare Societies” have been established in 1,539 Primary Health Centres, 264 government district and taluk hospitals, 15 medical-college hospitals and their affiliated hospitals. With ₹81.12 crore provided to these societies, facilities have been created to repair government hospitals and Primary Health Centres and to purchase urgently needed instruments and equipment, enabling uninterrupted quality medical service to the public.

In addition, ₹60.86 crore has been released as annual maintenance funding for all Primary Health Centres and sub-health centres under the National Health Programme, and ₹55.57 crore has been provided to Primary Health Centres and sub-health centres.

Village Health and Drinking-Water Sanitation Committees have been formed in 12,618 village Panchayats, and 2,540 such committees have been formed in 561 Town Panchayats. From 2007 onwards, ₹10,000 has been provided each year to each committee for health activities, amounting so far to ₹60.63 crore.

### Birth and death registration at Primary Health Centres

All Primary Health Centres have been declared birth-and-death registration centres, and children born there are issued birth certificates immediately. Every year during April and May, health inspectors go to the homes of children up to five years of age and provide their birth certificates.

### Action against fake and expired medicines

During a recent surprise inspection, the Drug Control Department identified a fake medicine called `ரிநர்வ்` and a fake adulterated cough medicine under the name `பென்னாட்ரல் காப் பார்முலா`, and discovered that expired medicines were being sent back for sale. On the government's immediate orders, the Drug Control Department and Police Department have jointly been arresting those involved in such activities. Fake and expired medicines have also been detected and seized. Forty-one people have been arrested in connection with them, of whom nineteen have been detained under the Goondas Act.

In order to provide the public with information about the nature of medicines, how they are to be used, dosage, allergies and related matters, a pharmaceutical-awareness centre—the first of its kind in India—was opened at the Drug Control headquarters on 7 December 2007 and has been functioning. Steps are also being taken to appoint 50 Drug Inspectors and 10 Junior Analysts in order to strengthen the Drug Control Department further.

The Tamil Nadu Government created a new Brain-Death Organ Donation unit in October 2008. After legally obtaining certification from those who suffer “brain death,” arrangements are made to retrieve organs such as kidneys, liver, heart and eyes and immediately transplant them into patients who need them. So far, from 163 people who suffered brain death, 29 hearts, 4 lungs, 143 livers, 304 kidneys and 196 heart valves have been donated, and these have been successfully transplanted into 942 people. It is in Tamil Nadu, the source states, that the programme of obtaining organs from people who suffer brain death and transplanting them into those who need them is being implemented successfully in India.

### National Programme for Prevention of Blindness

Under the blindness-prevention programme, 27.48 lakh people have undergone cataract surgery since 2006. Eye examinations have been conducted for 84.54 lakh schoolchildren; visual impairment was identified in 3.88 lakh of them, and 1.39 lakh were provided free spectacles.

### Public-private partnership in healthcare

To increase the use of Primary Health Centres and government hospitals, this government encourages private participation. With assistance from the charitable organisation `Catalyst Trust`, Medavakkam Primary Health Centre has been upgraded into a 30-bed Primary Health Centre at a cost of ₹2 crore.

Vadamalayan Hospital, Madurai, has constructed a maternity ward at Melakkal Primary Health Centre at a cost of ₹12 lakh. At Nandivaram Primary Health Centre, a landscaped park and a delivery chair have been provided at a cost of ₹5 lakh. A mechanised laundry has been established in Vellore district at a cost of ₹4 lakh. Wipro G Healthcare has provided medical equipment worth ₹40 lakh to Panavaram Primary Health Centre.

The `Times Foundation` has built and handed over a 30-bed Primary Health Centre at Semmancheri in Kanchipuram district at a cost of ₹10 crore, and a 50-bed hospital has also been established at Tiruvottiyur at a cost of ₹5 crore.

### Hearse service

A free hearse-service scheme costing ₹10 crore is operating with 180 hearses through the Indian Red Cross Society with assistance from Tamil Nadu EMRI. It is intended to remove the burden on poor and ordinary families who otherwise have to pay high charges to private vehicles to take home the body of a family member who dies after being admitted to a government hospital for treatment. The first phase was launched on 26 February 2011 with ten hearses. The telephone number for this scheme is **155377**.

### Stipends for medical students

For medical students in government medical colleges:

- the monthly stipend for medical interns was raised from ₹4,500 to ₹7,000;
- the monthly stipend for first-year non-service postgraduate medical students was raised from ₹8,000 to ₹15,000;
- for second-year postgraduate students, the monthly stipend of ₹8,000 plus a research allowance of ₹500 was raised to ₹16,000;
- for third-year postgraduate students, the monthly stipend of ₹8,000 plus a research allowance of ₹1,000 was raised to ₹17,000;
- for first-year super-speciality postgraduate medical students, the monthly stipend was raised from ₹9,500 to ₹18,000;
- for second-year super-speciality postgraduate medical students, it was raised from ₹10,000 to ₹19,000;
- for third-year super-speciality postgraduate medical students, it was raised from ₹10,000 to ₹20,000;
- for fourth-year neurosurgery postgraduate medical students, the monthly stipend of ₹9,000 plus a research allowance of ₹1,000 was raised to ₹18,000; and
- in the fifth year, the stipend was fixed at ₹19,000.

These increases have been provided from 1 July 2009 at an annual cost of ₹9.60 crore.

### Awards received by the Health Department

Honourable Union Health Minister Thiru Ghulam Nabi Azad has on many occasions praised Tamil Nadu, saying, “Tamil Nadu is the model for the whole of India in implementing health schemes.” For the outstanding implementation of National Rural Health programmes in Tamil Nadu, the Union Government selected Tamil Nadu as the leading State. The award was presented by the Vice-President of India at a function held in New Delhi on 12 April 2010 in the presence of Union Health Minister Thiru Ghulam Nabi Azad, and was received by the Principal Secretary of the Health and Family Welfare Department.

For the various reforms being carried out by the Tamil Nadu Government in healthcare, for implementing many excellent new schemes, and for successfully carrying out various programmes in partnership with private institutions, the Health Department of the Tamil Nadu Government received the first prize for 2009 from the Federation of Indian Chambers of Commerce and Industry (FICCI). For schemes introduced by the Tamil Nadu Government in 2009, the Centre for Science Development and Media Education gave the award “e India 2009.” Tamil Nadu received the second award for registration of births and deaths.

Tamil Nadu has received the “e-India 2010” award for implementing the Kalaignar Insurance Scheme.

For rendering good service to the public, the King Institute of Preventive Medicine, Guindy, received the Federation of Indian Chambers of Commerce and Industry (FICCI) award for 2010.

### ISO certification for 48 Primary Health Centres

At a cost of ₹4.86 crore, 48 Primary Health Centres were upgraded, and ISO certificates were issued to all 48 in the presence of officials of the Indian standards institution on 26 February 2011.

U.S. consular official Thiru Andrew T. Simkin visited some Primary Health Centres in Kanchipuram and Vellore districts on 1 August 2009. He praised the Tamil Nadu Government for providing excellent medical treatment to rural people, and said that he would report the healthcare facilities provided in Tamil Nadu to the U.S. Government and that steps would be taken to provide similar healthcare facilities to rural people there.

The World Bank, in the September 2010 issue of its *World Bank* publication, praised Tamil Nadu in a lead article titled **“Tamil Nadu Shows the way in Tackling Some of India’s Pertinent Health Issues”**. It said that Tamil Nadu serves as an example in India in providing excellent public-health services to the people and has effectively expanded healthcare in rural areas by integrating district- and taluk-level hospitals, Primary Health Centres and sub-health centres.

Udanpirappē, the DMK government has been implementing so many outstanding schemes through the Health Department. Because of the exceptional work of the department's Minister, younger brother M.R.K. Panneerselvam, all these schemes have been implemented in an excellent manner. Take all these schemes to the people so that they may continue to be implemented by electing a DMK government once again! Ensure the victory of our alliance!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**16-03-2011**
