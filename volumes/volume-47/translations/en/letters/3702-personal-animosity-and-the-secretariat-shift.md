# 3702. Personal Animosity and the Secretariat Shift!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This thought-preserving translation retains the source's chronology of Secretariat proposals, construction figures, court proceedings, political criticism and rhetorical questions. Tamil remains authoritative. `Udanpirappē` is retained as Kalaignar's distinctive address.

**Date:** 2 February 2013

## English translation

Udanpirappē,

Now that court decisions have emerged concerning the new Secretariat, I think it is in the public interest to recall what happened at different stages, and I therefore write this letter.

As early as 1983, a site was being sought for a new Tamil Nadu Secretariat. In 1983 M.G.R. considered whether it could be built in Tiruchirappalli. In 2003 Jayalalithaa considered sites at Thiruvidandhai and Thaiyur.

At that time the Secretariat functioned from Fort St. George. Because the Indian Army too is located there, security restrictions have long made it difficult for ordinary people to enter freely. In a democracy, everyone knows that the centre of government should be somewhere people can approach as a matter of right. Even minor changes to buildings there—and, figuratively speaking, even cutting and removing grass—require approval from the Government of India. In addition, Government offices are scattered across the city, resulting in crores of rupees being continuously spent as rent. For these reasons, the AIADMK Government itself first searched for a site where all offices could be brought into one complex.

Jayalalithaa then said that Namakkal Kavignar Maaligai, built during DMK rule and inaugurated on 14 August 1975, had cracks and leakage and that the Secretariat had to be shifted immediately from the Fort.

During her Government, she decided to build a new Secretariat on the Queen Mary's College campus on Chennai's beach road. The AIADMK Government said that despite the State's financial difficulties, with long-term vision it would build the new Secretariat there. At the time, it was stated that the existing Secretariat was unsuitable, that the Queen Mary's College buildings were in poor condition and should be demolished, and that a new Secretariat complex was essential. Because teachers, students and parents of the college opposed the move, the plan was abandoned.

Later it was announced that the Secretariat would be built at Kotturpuram. A foundation-stone ceremony was held with religious rituals, attended by then Chief Minister Jayalalithaa herself. That proposal too did not materialise.

Keeping all this in mind, and as a continuation of steps taken by the previous Government, during DMK rule in 2007 a site was selected and the new Secretariat was created.

At the Omandurar Government Estate, we set out to provide the Legislative Assembly, proposed Legislative Council and offices of several departments associated with the Secretariat, and to modernise Kalaivanar Arangam. In the roughly 86,450-square-metre Block A, the Assembly chamber, Speaker's office, Legislative Council chamber, Assembly library, meeting hall, Governor's office, Chief Minister's office and Secretariat, Leader of Opposition's office, ministers' offices, Cabinet meeting hall, Chief Secretary's office, and offices of six major departments—Legislature, Public, Finance, Home, Law, and Planning and Development—were constructed and completed attractively.

On 13 March 2010, Prime Minister Manmohan Singh, Sonia Gandhi and I participated in the inauguration of the new Secretariat and Assembly buildings.

The complex had been specially designed for Secretariat use. The overall plan was around ₹1,000 crore, of which ₹581.80 crore had been spent on the completed portion. After it was built and inaugurated, why, immediately after the AIADMK came to power, did its Cabinet decide on 19 August 2011 to convert this newly built Secretariat into a multi-speciality hospital instead of using it for its intended purpose?

The new Secretariat had been built and opened; Assembly sessions had been held there; seven Cabinet meetings had taken place there; and the Chief Minister and other ministers had shifted there. Yet when Jayalalithaa came to power in May 2011, she announced that the Secretariat would continue at the old location and that the new building would not be used. Name boards there were pulled down and the “Jaya” Government set about putting up new ones.

It was in the Omandurar Secretariat created during DMK rule that the Governor of Tripura met me; Mahendra Singh Dhoni, captain of the Indian cricket team, was honoured with a special award for winning the World Cup; Union ministers P. Chidambaram, G.K. Vasan and Narayanasamy, Governor Surjit Singh Barnala and leaders of several political parties visited and praised the design of the complex.

AIADMK MLAs themselves attended the Assembly chamber in the new Secretariat for many days and participated in debates. If the AIADMK truly believed the complex was unsuitable, should its members not have boycotted the proceedings even then? They did not; they participated. Jayalalithaa alone never came there even once. After returning to power, she ignored appeals by leaders of many parties, rejected the building, removed overnight the Paavendhar Classical Tamil Research Library that had been functioning in the old Secretariat, and resumed Assembly sittings there.

This makes it clear that the sole reason for her hostility is that the Omandurar complex was built during DMK rule and inaugurated by the Prime Minister, and that claims about administrative inconvenience merely conceal the feeling in her mind.

If being built under the DMK is itself the objection, can Namakkal Kavignar Maaligai—also built under DMK rule—be renovated and used? Many flyovers across Tamil Nadu were built during DMK Governments. Jayalalithaa's car travels over them. Has she ever stopped and said, “Karunanidhi built this flyover, so I will not travel on it”? If the building cannot function as a Secretariat, how can it function as a multi-speciality hospital?

To convert it into a hospital, facilities such as oxygen-supply areas, ramps and wards have to be created. These changes themselves require many crores of rupees. If the AIADMK Government is initially prepared to spend ₹30 crore on this conversion, is that not expenditure caused by Amma's insistence? Is it not a waste of the people's tax money?

When the issue reached the Madras High Court, Justices Murugesan and Janarthana Raja asked whether environmental clearance had been obtained, whether tenders had been issued, and whether the Government could continue taking action while the case was pending. They declared that the matter required examination. The Government advocate argued that the conversion was a “policy decision.” The judges then imposed an interim stay on converting the new Secretariat into a hospital.

A Bench comprising Justices D. Murugesan and K.K. Sasidharan later recommended that the matter be placed before a different Bench. A new Bench of Justices K.N. Basha and N. Paul Vasanthakumar was constituted. Arguments before it included that many crores had already been spent on the Secretariat, that further alterations would cause additional expense, that several hospitals already existed nearby, and that traffic congestion would result. The Bench nevertheless dismissed the petitions, saying that Chennai did not have a Government multi-speciality hospital like AIIMS in Delhi and that allegations of ulterior motive could not be accepted without evidence.

Within one hour of the High Court delivering that judgment, work began inside the new Secretariat to convert it into a hospital. Why such haste? Had the hospital's inauguration already been announced in advance? Why this urgency? What necessity existed to convert a building designed for the Secretariat into a hospital? Is not the single animosity that it was built during DMK rule the real reason?

If another Secretariat has to be built in future, how much will that cost? Do we need any better example of how one individual's animosity and stubbornness, under the name of a “policy decision,” wastes—and will continue to waste—the tax money of the people of Tamil Nadu? Yes, of course, a “policy decision” can be taken by any Government at any time, can it not?

With affection,
M.K.
2-2-2013
