# 3763. Everyone Knows All the Truth!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

I had written in detail welcoming the fact that the Chief Minister was to inaugurate the Hogenakkal drinking-water project on 29 May 2013 from the Secretariat through “video conferencing”, and I had expressed my happiness.

You may also have seen that the government issued full-page advertisements in every newspaper about the inauguration, and that after the project was inaugurated it was not members of the public but the minister of that very department who placed another full-page advertisement thanking the Chief Minister. In repayment for receiving the advertisements, all those newspapers published the Chief Minister's speech in full. Was that not what the full-page advertisements were for?

In the speech she delivered while inaugurating the project by video conference, the Chief Minister, as usual, lunged at and clawed at the DMK government. She said in particular that a project report had been prepared in 1994 to implement the scheme at a cost of ₹350 crore and that the project could not be implemented because adequate financial assistance was unavailable. If the AIADMK government could not raise sufficient funds, why was the project report prepared?

She further said that after she assumed office for the second time, a proposal seeking financial assistance for the scheme was sent to the Union Government on 18 August 2005. She assumed office for the second time in 2001. Why did she spend four years—from 2001 until 18 August 2005—without sending a proposal to the Union Government seeking financial assistance for the project?

The Chief Minister also said that the foundation stone for the project was laid in 2008. But she did not mention which government was in office then. In 2008, the DMK government was in office. It was during that government that the foundation stone for the scheme was laid.

Jayalalithaa said that after the foundation was laid, the BJP held protests in Karnataka; that AIADMK announced counter-protests; that resolutions seeking the Union Government's cooperation were adopted in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly on 27 March 2008 and 1 April 2008; and that after this I announced on 5 April 2008, **“Let the Karnataka election be completed; afterward we will discuss it together and, if necessary, enter the field,”** thereby, according to her, stopping the project. She also said that when she tried to ask a question about this in the Assembly she was not allowed, so she walked out and demanded that I, then Chief Minister, resign.

I must explain the facts.

When the foundation stone for the Hogenakkal scheme was laid, there was no elected government in Karnataka. Governor's Rule was in force and preparations for a general election were under way. At that time the Karnataka BJP organised protests there, and Tamils living in Karnataka were severely attacked. Tamil-owned shops were destroyed. Buses were burned. Theatres showing Tamil films were attacked.

Even after two resolutions seeking the Union Government's cooperation were passed in the Tamil Nadu Assembly, the attacks on Tamils did not stop. Condemning the Karnataka BJP's actions, the Tamil film industry held a fast at Chepauk on 4 April 2008 under the leadership of K.R.G., then president of the South Indian Film Chamber of Commerce. Among those who participated were Superstar Rajinikanth, Kalaignani Kamal Haasan, Vijayakanth, Ajith Kumar, Kaviperarasu Vairamuthu, directors Rama Narayanan, T. Rajendar, P. Vasu, Muktha Srinivasan, Cheran and K.S. Ravikumar, and actors including S.S.R., Sarathkumar, Dhanush, Jiiva, Simbu, Manorama, Khushbu, Sneha, Nayanthara and Ramya Krishnan, along with many others.

Because the issue had created a situation in which law and order could be affected in both States, and because Tamils living in Karnataka were suffering greatly, with only a few days remaining before a government was to be formed there, it is true that as Chief Minister I issued a statement saying that we would maintain peace temporarily on this issue until then, and that if the situation continued afterward there would be no need to remain patient.

Had I not issued such a statement and instead remained silent, serious harm might have been inflicted on Tamils living in Karnataka. The preventive action I took to protect them from harm was portrayed by the Chief Minister in her speech yesterday as a major accusation, as though I had abandoned the project itself.

*The Hindu* had, at the time itself, published an editorial on 7 April 2008 about this decision of mine. The Tamil translation printed in the letter praises the intervention as one of political maturity, saying it immediately reduced tension in both Karnataka and Tamil Nadu, deprived violent elements on both sides of a cause, and that meaningful talks on Hogenakkal could in any case be held only with an elected government in Karnataka.

The source then reproduces the English editorial passage exactly as printed:

That editorial written by *The Hindu* at the time is itself the answer to the accusation made against me by Chief Minister Jayalalithaa while inaugurating the project yesterday. Jayalalithaa has demonstrated that the explanation and criticism *The Hindu* gave about her then still applies today.

Jayalalithaa also asked whether I could issue a statement saying that the project should be kept on hold for a few months without announcing it in the Assembly and without consulting everyone. She asked this very question in 2008, and I answered it then itself.

The Assembly was on a three-day continuous holiday. If I had waited to consult all-party leaders and make an announcement in the Assembly, the delay could have allowed deaths and destruction of property in both States. To avert an emergency, the statement was therefore issued urgently and without delay.

She also complained that she tried to raise a question in the Assembly about my statement at the time but was not given permission. I will explain that too.

When the Assembly is in session, whether a member or a party wants to seek an explanation, or raise a question of privilege or an adjournment matter, Assembly rules clearly provide that it can be raised only after the House begins in the morning and Question Hour is completed. But when Jayalalithaa and members of her party, as soon as the House assembled, tried to speak saying they wished to raise a privilege or adjournment issue, they were told: **raise the matter after Question Hour and the ruling party will give an explanation.** They did not have the patience even to wait that one hour in accordance with Assembly rules; having to leave town, they walked out and now say they were denied permission in the House.

Tamil Nadu knows very well the extent to which opposition parties are given permission under their government today. Those who still believe in democracy and understand legislative traditions are learning this through experience.

Chief Minister Jayalalithaa said that the Hogenakkal scheme was accelerated only after the AIADMK took office. The people of that region have directly witnessed under whose government the project was planned rapidly, accelerated and brought toward completion.

When DMK Treasurer Thalapathi M.K. Stalin visited Krishnagiri in February, he said that a project that could have been completed by December 2012 had not been inaugurated because of delay caused by the AIADMK government. Chief Minister Jayalalithaa called that false propaganda.

A single timetable had been prepared for this scheme, and on that basis work under DMK rule was organised so that the project could be completed by December 2012. The work proceeded accordingly. M.K. Stalin spoke at Krishnagiri on the basis of having repeatedly visited the works and monitored them directly as the minister responsible; he was not engaging in false propaganda.

The Chief Minister said it was laughable that Stalin claimed he would complete in two months while out of office what had not been done in two years while the DMK was in office. The truth is that because the DMK government energetically accelerated the works while in office, the present Chief Minister was able to conduct the inauguration now. What the AIADMK has done is reap the harvest of work undertaken during DMK rule. The reality, the letter argues, is that the AIADMK has no right to celebrate the benefits of this project as though it had created them in the way the DMK did.

I had pointed to a photograph published by *Dinamalar* as evidence that the Hogenakkal works were not yet fully complete and were still under way, and asked why the scheme was being inaugurated in such haste. For some reason, the Chief Minister gave no answer to that question.

As if to prove that what I said was true, today's *The Hindu* itself reports: **“People in many rural areas were upset that the water did not reach them”**—that people in many rural areas were deeply disappointed because drinking water had not reached them.

Poor Chief Minister—what could she do? She could have read an answer only if the officials had written one for her! And why did the officials not write one? **Because they know all the truths!**

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**31 May 2013**
