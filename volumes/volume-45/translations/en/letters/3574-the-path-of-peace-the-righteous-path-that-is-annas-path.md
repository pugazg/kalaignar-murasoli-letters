# 3574. The Path of Peace—the Righteous Path—That Is Anna's Path!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 28 July 2011

**Udanpirappē,**

Counsel for the Tamil Nadu government has argued before the Supreme Court that the DMK government had introduced the **Samacheer Kalvi** scheme in great haste and that, because time was needed to improve the quality of the textbooks and to constitute a high-level committee to examine the matter, it was not possible to introduce Samacheer Kalvi in Tamil Nadu this year.

The Samacheer Kalvi scheme was **not** introduced in haste under the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam government.

During the **2006 general election**, the DMK election manifesto declared that we would make arrangements to provide all the people of Tamil Nadu with a Samacheer Kalvi system that would give education of the same standard in all schools. After winning that election and coming to power, we did not immediately enact a law and announce Samacheer Kalvi in the very first session of the Legislative Assembly.

On **8.9.2006**, the Tamil Nadu government constituted a committee of educationists headed by former Bharathidasan University Vice-Chancellor **Dr S. Muthukumaran** to make recommendations to the government on Samacheer Kalvi. That committee studied the issue thoroughly and submitted its study report for the government's consideration. Even after the committee submitted its recommendations, the Tamil Nadu government did not simply accept them as they stood and immediately bring a law for Samacheer Kalvi.

To re-examine those recommendations and advise the government, the Tamil Nadu government appointed **M.P. Vijayakumar, I.A.S. (Retired)** as a one-man committee. That one-man committee examined Dr S. Muthukumaran's report and gave the government recommendations on implementing Samacheer Kalvi.

Even after that, on **30.10.2008**, the government constituted a committee of educationists and officials to study and examine the procedures for implementing Samacheer Kalvi in Tamil Nadu, to study the syllabuses and examination systems in operation in other States and to report to the government. That committee travelled in person to **Karnataka, Gujarat, Kerala and Maharashtra**, studied the systems there and submitted its report to the government.

Only after all these committees had been constituted, had spent many months studying the issue and had submitted all their recommendations—that is, only after approximately **four years of consideration**—was legislation enacted in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly on **1.2.2010** to enable implementation of Samacheer Kalvi in the State.

Even after the law was enacted, at a meeting held on **1.3.2010** under the chairmanship of the then School Education Minister, the reports and recommendations of educationists were considered and the procedures for implementing Samacheer Kalvi in Tamil Nadu in the **2010-2011 academic year** were examined in detail. It was decided that Samacheer Kalvi could be implemented in two stages: first, for **Classes 1 and 6** in 2010-2011; and then, in the **2011-12 academic year**, for all the remaining classes.

Some private schools and associations filed cases in the Madras High Court seeking to have the Samacheer Kalvi Act struck down. In its judgment dated **30.4.2010**, the High Court upheld the validity of the law enacted by the Tamil Nadu government.

Appeals were then filed in the Supreme Court in New Delhi seeking an interim stay of the Madras High Court judgment favourable to Samacheer Kalvi. Those appeals too were dismissed.

As the first stage, syllabuses were prescribed and textbooks prepared for **Classes 1 and 6** in the 2010-2011 academic year. The Tamil Nadu Textbook Corporation printed **61 lakh textbooks** required for Class 1 and **84 lakh textbooks** required for Class 6. They were distributed to the students, who studied those books and completed the previous academic year.

Under the government's policy decision, to continue implementing Samacheer Kalvi from the **2011-2012 academic year**, textbooks for the remaining **Classes 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9 and 10** were printed at a cost of **₹200 crore** and had already been sent to the districts. The syllabuses for these classes were prepared by education experts and published on the School Education Department website so that the public, parents and students could know them.

At a time when people speak of equality in everything and equal rights for everyone, the position of the DMK—and, why, of most educationists in Tamil Nadu—is that equality must be established in education too. It was precisely to put that principle into practice that the scheme was not arbitrarily announced at the outset. Expert committees, committees of educationists and committees of officials were appointed, they were sent on study tours to other States, and only after that was Samacheer Kalvi introduced step by step under the DMK government. It was welcomed by all sections.

Advocate **K. Shyam Sundar** filed a public-interest petition in the Madras High Court challenging the amendment law brought by the AIADMK government on Samacheer Kalvi. Justices **S. Rajeswaran** and **K.P.K. Vasuki** observed:

> “The objective of the Samacheer Kalvi Act is very clear. A study committee consisting of distinguished experts examined the matter in detail and recommended implementation of the Samacheer Kalvi system. The recommendations of that committee cannot simply be ignored. Further, when a large amount has already been spent, is it necessary to spend another large amount? The Advocate General should give the government appropriate advice on all these matters.”

When the Tamil Nadu government appealed this ruling in the Supreme Court, Justices **P.S. Chauhan** and **Swatanter Kumar** ruled:

> “Samacheer Kalvi, introduced last year for Classes 1 and 6, must continue to be implemented. If it is stopped midway, confusion will arise. Samacheer Kalvi must be implemented this very year for Classes 2 to 5 and Classes 7 to 10. If there are defects in that syllabus, an expert committee must be appointed to examine them, and that expert committee must submit its report to the Madras High Court within two weeks. Considering the importance of this case, the High Court must conduct hearings every day and deliver judgment within one week.”

In accordance with that ruling, the AIADMK government constituted a committee of educationists. Many people said that the committee included only those opposed to Samacheer Kalvi. Without concerning themselves with such criticism, the rulers had that very committee prepare a report and submitted it to the High Court.

After explaining that committee's report in detail, the Chief Justice of the High Court and another judge stated in their judgment:

> **“We have no hesitation to hold that the State has exceeded in its powers in bringing the Amending Act to postpone an enactment which is already come into force”**

The source then separately renders that English statement in Tamil as: **“We have no hesitation in saying that the State government has exceeded the limits of its authority.”**

The judges further said that, apart from Classes 1 and 6, Samacheer textbooks required for the other classes had already been printed and substantial work had been completed; that they had also been published on the internet; that, in these circumstances, amending the Samacheer Kalvi Act would harm students' welfare; and therefore the amendment made by the Tamil Nadu government to Section 3 of that Act was invalid and was being struck down. They said that textbooks for education under the Samacheer Act must be distributed to students immediately, and expressed the expectation that, keeping the future of students and the welfare of the country in mind, the State government would take immediate action to implement Samacheer Kalvi.

Even after all that, the Tamil Nadu government did not respect the ruling. Leaders of all parties in Tamil Nadu requested that the government, in the interest of students, implement the High Court judgment instead of appealing to the Supreme Court. Ignoring that request too, the government went to the Supreme Court.

In its interim order, the Supreme Court said it could not stay the Madras High Court judgment and directed that Samacheer textbooks be distributed by **2 August**. Even five days after that, the Tamil Nadu government had still not begun distributing the books to students. Instead, counsel arguing for the Tamil Nadu government, contrary to the Supreme Court ruling, is reported to have said that there was no possibility of implementing Samacheer Kalvi in the current year. That makes it clear that the Tamil Nadu government has no concern for the condition of Tamil Nadu's students.

Schools have been open for two months, yet students and their parents are suffering without even knowing what textbooks they are to study this year. The Tamil Nadu government, instead of worrying about this, has turned the matter into a question of prestige. It refuses to respect court judgments, refuses to listen to the views of all-party leaders and stubbornly insists, like the saying, that **“the hare I caught has only three legs.”**

That is why, on behalf of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam, students have been asked to boycott schools and colleges across Tamil Nadu on **Friday, 29-7-2011**, and express their opposition.

I ask everyone to cooperate in making this righteous protest a success and in proving that when students resolve upon something, victory is possible in anything.

**The path of peace—the righteous path! That is Anna's path!**

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**28-7-2011**

## Letter-specific notes

1. `Samacheer Kalvi` is retained as the established name of the uniform-school-education scheme; the source's committee, litigation and implementation chronology is translated without external reconciliation.
2. The English High Court quotation printed on PDF 273 is reproduced verbatim, and the source's separate Tamil rendering is then represented separately, following the bilingual-source policy.
3. The closing `அமைதி வழி - அறவழி` is rendered as **the path of peace—the righteous path**, preserving the movement slogan rather than replacing it with a generic call for protest.
