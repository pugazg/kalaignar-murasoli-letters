# 3538. Five-Year Achievements of the Rural Development and Panchayat Raj Department (3)

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

I continue to write about the achievements and schemes carried out by the DMK government in the Rural Development and Panchayat Raj Department during the past five years.

With the aim of creating unity and a sense of group solidarity among members of women's self-help groups, various competitions are conducted at block and district levels. From 2007–08 onwards, ₹1.60 crore has been allocated each year for conducting these competitions. They have received an enthusiastic response among self-help groups.

To help women's self-help groups gain recognition in society and enable them to approach banks and government institutions more easily and benefit from them, identity cards are being issued in stages to self-help groups under the women's development programme.

In 2008–09, ₹20 crore was provided to train 25,000 young people.

Through leading institutions such as Nokia, TVS and IL&FS, the Tamil Nadu Corporation for Development of Women has so far provided employment-linked vocational training to 40,123 young people.

For 2009–10, the target was to provide vocational training to 25,000 young people; by 31 March 2010, 25,386 young people were receiving vocational training.

For 2010–11, the target was again to train 25,000 young people; by 30 September 2010, 14,134 young people were receiving vocational training.

The Vazhnthu Kattuvom Project is an empowerment and poverty-reduction programme being implemented for six years with World Bank assistance at a cost of ₹717 crore. In 16 districts, covering 2,509 Panchayats in 70 of the most backward blocks, project work was taken up in phases: first, from October 2006 in 578 village Panchayats in 15 blocks; second, from April 2007 in 584 village Panchayats in 15 blocks; third, from November 2008 in 635 village Panchayats in 20 blocks; and fourth, from February 2009 in 712 village Panchayats. The project is to be extended additionally to 1,661 village Panchayats in 50 blocks. In addition, the project has been extended until 2014 in Panchayats where it is already operating.

Special efforts have been undertaken to involve rural youth constructively in development work, build their skills and create employment opportunities. With the assistance of reputed training institutions and industrial companies, 1,15,838 young people have been given vocational training and 95,120 have been placed in jobs. In addition, 24,352 young people are currently receiving vocational training.

For providing vocational training and employment to young people, the project has established links with reputed industrial companies such as Nokia, Foxconn, MRF, Ashok Leyland and Intimate Fashions.

Youth sub-groups have been formed in 2,736 village Panchayats. In addition, 20 per cent of the funds provided to Village Poverty Reduction Committees has been set aside for youth development; by involving young people through this allocation, the committees have begun useful activities. These activities include education centres, employment guidance centres, village information centres and physical-fitness centres.

### Tsunami Emergency Assistance Project (TEAP), undertaken with Asian Development Bank financial assistance

The project began on 6 April 2005. Yet only ₹4.95 crore had been spent up to 13 May 2006. When the project was completed on 31 October 2009, however, ₹292.94 crore had been spent. It was implemented in 257 coastal village Panchayats comprising 1,602 habitations in 12 rural coastal districts of the State.

Under the livelihood component, the target was achieved 100 per cent in providing revolving funds to self-help groups, economic-development assistance and grant assistance to persons with disabilities to start self-employment activities.

Financial assistance amounting to ₹154 crore 99 lakh 49 thousand has been provided to 70,655 persons with disabilities.

People with disabilities find it difficult to obtain bank loans to increase their income. Therefore, persons with disabilities in coastal districts have each been given a grant of ₹10,000 to enable them to undertake a self-employment activity capable of generating continuing income. Under this scheme, approximately ₹34.63 crore has been provided as grants to 34,634 persons with disabilities.

To provide the necessary skill-development training to self-help groups, fishers, artisans, weavers and other sections in the coastal districts, and to create infrastructure for them, the “Mother Teresa Women's Complex” built for self-help groups near Valluvar Kottam in Chennai at an estimated cost of ₹15.33 crore, together with six projects, has been completed at an estimated cost of approximately ₹26.38 crore.

Under the Sea Safety Training Scheme, training in marine safety methods was begun for fishers and members of their families at an estimated cost of ₹1.13 crore. So far, 31,959 women and 36,890 fishers have received training.

Working capital of ₹2.96 crore has been provided to 93 fishermen's associations in seven coastal districts. Bank credit has also been arranged through the Federation of South Indian Fishermen Associations. This has prevented them from having to borrow at high interest from moneylenders and has enabled the associations to function independently. Through this, about 5,000 members of fishermen's associations have benefited directly, while about 20,000 fishers have benefited through the beneficiaries.

Four direct procurement centres in Thanjavur district and eleven direct paddy procurement centres in Nagapattinam district have been completed at an estimated cost of ₹1.62 crore and handed over to the Tamil Nadu Civil Supplies Corporation.

At a cost of ₹4.06 crore, nine milk-chilling centres and 37 milk-distribution centres have been completed in eleven coastal districts and are in use. In addition, 160 milk cooperative societies have been established or renovated. Members of self-help groups engaged in this occupation have been trained. So far, 3,645 people have been trained in milk production and maintenance of milch cattle, and 400 people have been trained in marketing milk and milk-based products. The 160 milk cooperative societies have been renovated and supplied equipment worth ₹80 lakh.

Under the Handloom Export Development Scheme, 250 weavers in Kanyakumari district have been given skill-development training and their handlooms have been improved and renovated at an estimated cost of ₹61.09 lakh. Renovation work has been completed in eight work-centre buildings.

In Villupuram district, work on an artisan village benefiting approximately 1,000 artisans has been completed at a cost of ₹67.49 lakh.

In coastal Panchayats, 610 road works covering about 699 kilometres were undertaken at a cost of ₹89 crore, and all the works have been completed.

A total of 196 works, including renovation of coastal Panchayat council buildings and construction of meeting halls, were undertaken at an estimated cost of ₹5.54 crore, and all the works have been completed.

To create awareness among rural people about use of individual toilets, personal cleanliness and sanitation, 21 awareness publicity films have been produced at an estimated cost of ₹67.28 lakh.

Under the Rajiv Gandhi Rehabilitation Project, the government took up construction of 2,687 houses in Kanchipuram, Tiruvallur, Villupuram, Nagapattinam and Thoothukudi districts after voluntary organisations withdrew without constructing them. The work, estimated at about ₹65.81 crore, has been completed for all 2,687 houses.

In these areas, basic facilities such as cement-concrete roads, street lights, public buildings and electricity connections to houses have been undertaken at an estimated cost of about ₹10 crore.

In areas where voluntary organisations built houses, basic facilities have been undertaken under the Rajiv Gandhi Rehabilitation Project at an estimated cost of ₹57.34 crore.

Works comprising 133 internal cement-concrete roads at an estimated cost of ₹40.59 crore, 5,495 sanitary toilet facilities at an estimated cost of ₹5.49 crore, earth-filling in low-lying areas at an estimated cost of ₹2.76 crore, and street lights and electricity connections to houses at an estimated cost of ₹5.47 crore have all been completed.

In eleven coastal districts, approximately 18,299 vulnerable houses located within 0–200 metres of the high-tide line, including houses built by the government under the Indira Memorial Housing Scheme and Fishermen's Housing Schemes within 1,000 metres and houses that were ten years old as of 1 January 2006, are being rapidly rebuilt under the Rajiv Gandhi Reconstruction Project at a cost of about ₹499.22 crore. So far, 17,094 houses have been completed. The remaining 1,205 houses are progressing at various stages.

In the areas where these houses are being built, 3,251 basic-infrastructure works—cement roads, tar roads, drinking-water supply, street lights, pond renovation, burial-ground improvement, construction of anganwadi centres and kitchens, and similar works—were taken up at an estimated cost of ₹68.14 crore in accordance with the guidelines of the Anaithu Grama Anna Marumalarchi Thittam. All these works have been completed.

To restore the livelihoods of people in tsunami-affected areas of Tamil Nadu and rebuild damaged houses, the Tamil Nadu Government has established the Emergency Tsunami Reconstruction Project with technical and financial assistance from the World Bank.

Under this project, the government decided to rebuild houses identified as vulnerable within 200 to 1,000 metres of the sea's high-tide line so as to protect them against future disasters such as tsunamis. Through this, a coastal community capable of recovering from disaster is being created. The Tamil Nadu Government and the World Bank signed the agreement for this project on 18 August 2009.

Under the Emergency Tsunami Reconstruction Project, the department has planned the following construction works (`ETRP-VRCCP`):

**I.** Rebuild approximately 17,000 houses identified as vulnerable in coastal areas between 200 and 1,000 metres from the high-tide line.

**II.** Construct evacuation roads needed for people to escape easily during an emergency, and install direction boards on those roads to guide them to safe areas.

In the eleven coastal districts, beneficiary lists selected by Revenue Department officials were re-examined through voluntary organisations appointed as third-level facilitators. Accordingly, 15,056 houses were found eligible for reconstruction. The construction of these 15,056 new houses was taken up in 157 packages. So far, 14,429 vulnerable houses have been demolished and construction has begun on 13,070 houses, with work in progress. Action is being taken to complete all houses within the project implementation period ending in December 2011.

**Udanpirappē!**

With the help of the leaders and members of local bodies—which serve like training grounds for preparing young people capable of rendering great service by participating in both the State and Union Governments—and because of the unceasing efforts of Deputy Chief Minister Thambi M.K. Stalin, this government has created in rural areas roads, drinking-water facilities, electricity, education, sports, transport, sanitation, veterinary facilities and many other amenities. In the past five years, you yourself have known from experience that this government has created, and continues to create, a condition in which one can say, “Every facility is available everywhere!” When you go among the people during the coming general election, can you not hold your head high, point to these achievements and ask for their votes?

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**13-03-2011**
