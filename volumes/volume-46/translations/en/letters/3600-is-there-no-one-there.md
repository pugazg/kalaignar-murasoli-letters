# 3600. Is There No One There?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation follows the locked Volume 46 conventions. Kalaignar's sarcasm, repeated rhetorical questions, historical sequence, figures and direct political criticism are retained rather than neutralised. The complete audited Tamil follows and remains authoritative. Bilingual source alignment has been verified.

**Udanpirappē,**

There is a proverb: “The hand of one who has gripped iron, and the hand of one afflicted with scabies, will never stay still.” In just that way, whenever our Chief Minister Jayalalithaa assumes office, she takes pleasure in finding out what good things useful to the people were done by the previous government and conducting closing ceremonies for every one of them. They say some people derive a satisfaction from watching the suffering of others. In English they call such a person a “sadist.” It is in that manner that she is now trying to find a peculiar pleasure in sending home people who had worked for many years, earned wages and sustained their humble families.

Removing symbols of culture—demolishing things that endure in memory—imprisoning those she dislikes—filing false cases against those who voice a different opinion—treating all government employees like slaves—saying she will get work out of government officials by wielding the whip—sending ministers home once every six months—tossing officials about once every six weeks—saying that all government revenue is being spent only on government employees—snatching away freedom of speech and freedom of writing: the Jayalalithaa government, which has been carrying on these things as its achievements, announced yesterday yet another closing ceremony in that sequence—the achievement of mercilessly sending 13,000 Makkal Nala Paniyalargal, people's welfare workers, home.

When the DMK government was formed again in 1989, a scheme was begun to provide employment to educated unemployed youth in Tamil Nadu: the scheme appointing people's welfare workers. When the scheme began, they were appointed on an honorarium of ₹750 a month. But within two years, when Jayalalithaa became Chief Minister in 1991, owing to her “large-heartedness” she dismissed all 13,000 people's welfare workers and sent them home with effect from 13 July 1991.

When the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam government was formed in 1996, we restored employment to all 13,000 people's welfare workers who had lost their posts. In the same way, employment was provided to 10,000 road workers. When Jayalalithaa again assumed office as Chief Minister in 2001, she—possessing, of course, no vindictiveness whatsoever—“compassionately” sent home the 13,000 people's welfare workers appointed during DMK rule and earned their burning anguish. Rural Development Department Government Order No. 149 of the Jayalalithaa government dismissed all the people's welfare workers with effect from 1 June 2001. Of the 13,247 people who lost their jobs, about half were women.

Those families had worked for five years and received a monthly wage. If suddenly there was no job, what were they to do for their livelihood? Many would have married relying upon that wage. Many would have married off their daughters. What would have become of all of them? What concern was that to Jayalalithaa? Many died by suicide. For many whose marriages had been arranged in reliance on this employment, the marriages were stopped when the jobs disappeared, leaving many women who had depended on them in a helpless condition.

To give only a few examples: Veerappan, a people's welfare worker from the village of Vairavankattalai near Keezhaiyur in Nagapattinam district, died by suicide after drinking poison on 11 June 2001 because he had lost his job. Prabha, wife of C. Kandasamy, a people's welfare worker in Oddanchatram Union, Dindigul district, died by suicide after drinking poison on 11 June 2001 because her husband's job had been taken away. On 12 June 2001, Thilagar, who had been working as a people's welfare worker in Natham Union, unable to bear the anguish, also took his own life. At that very time, ₹25,000 each was given to these families as relief from the DMK. Ramamurthi of Aruppukkottai, after losing his job, remained without food in grief and died. Ilangovan of Tharangambadi in Nagapattinam district died by suicide after drinking poison. In Ramanathapuram district, Mandapam Kabilan, after losing his job, went without food and died—the list goes on. The rulers paid no heed to any of the protests conducted by the dismissed people's welfare workers.

When the DMK again assumed office in 2006, those dismissed people's welfare workers met me and expressed their anguish.

From 1 June 2006, all of them were once again appointed by the DMK government. Not merely were they given work: with the intention of bringing people's welfare workers under a time-scale of pay, the DMK government issued an order with effect from 1 June 2009 fixing ₹2,500–5,000 with grade pay of ₹500. Because of this order, 12,653 people's welfare workers benefited.

The people's welfare workers carried out duties in spreading adult education, making people aware of the evils of drinking, maintaining streetlights and looking after nutritious-meal centres. Whenever the AIADMK government assumes office, it acts as though all government employees are somehow enemies. For some reason, it forgets that they too are a part of the government. Government employees themselves know well that when the AIADMK was in power in 2002, emergency laws called ESMA and TESMA were enacted specifically to suppress government employees, and that all those laws were later repealed under the DMK government.

As far as the people's welfare workers are concerned, at present they have not taken part in any agitation against the government. Why, they have not even placed any demand. In this situation, is it necessary to dismiss them? From 1989 onward, like “six months in the country, six months in the forest,” if this cruelty continues—remaining in employment during five years of DMK rule and all being sent home whenever an AIADMK government is formed—what will become of their lives? At this age, can they seek some other employment? What condition will their families be reduced to? Should not a government think about all this? Is it to ask, “What price compassion?” Even if new employment is not provided, if everyone already working is sent home, where will they go? Are they not citizens of Tamil Nadu too? For that matter, did they not also vote, enabling Jayalalithaa to become Chief Minister today?

Before signing their dismissal order, should not the Chief Minister have thought for one minute about their future lives? Are all of them mitta mirasus? Are they capitalists? Were they not people who had to receive the wage that came on the first of every month and run their lives with it? Where will all of them suddenly go?

When they were dismissed during the previous Jayalalithaa government, did not many die from starvation and suicide? Can that situation be created again? Should not those holding responsibility in government think about all that? Do they not say that the tears shed by the poor never go in vain? Do they not say those tears are equal to a mighty army? Can a government strike at the stomachs of those poor people in this manner?

Leaving unused the Secretariat built by spending crores of rupees of government money—saying that a children's hospital will be started in the library building bearing Anna's name—foisting false cases upon former ministers, imprisoning them and taking pleasure in it: are these the characteristics proper to a government? Is there no one there who can offer advice about all this?

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**10 November 2011**

## Letter-specific notes

1. `மக்கள் நலப் பணியாளர்கள்` is rendered descriptively as **people's welfare workers**, with the Tamil administrative identity noted at first occurrence.
2. Kalaignar's ironic descriptions of Jayalalithaa's `பரந்த உள்ளம்`, absence of `காழ்ப்புணர்ச்சி`, and `கருணை உள்ளம்` are retained as irony rather than flattened into literal praise.
3. `நாடாறு மாதம், காடாறு மாதம்` is kept close to its source imagery as “six months in the country, six months in the forest.”
4. `மிட்டா மிராசுகள்` is retained as **mitta mirasus**, a source-specific landed-elite expression, rather than replaced by an unrelated English class label.
