# 3431. Which of These Is Helplessness?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation follows the audited canonical Tamil in source order. It preserves the letter’s land-reform figures, Dalit framing, political accusations, historical comparison, quoted remarks and rhetorical questions without reconciling them against outside material. The complete audited Tamil is reproduced below and remains authoritative.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3431](../../../chapters/3431-ithil-ethu-kaiyaalaakaathanam.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 49](../../../pages/page-049.md)–[PDF 53](../../../pages/page-053.md)  
**Date:** 10 November 2009

**Udanpirappē,**

In Kaverirajapuram village in Tiruvallur district, Karnataka Chief Justice P. D. Dinakaran was alleged to have illegally occupied government poramboke and fallow lands. When it was announced that those lands would be recovered directly and distributed to Dalit people, and an attempt was thereby made to disturb law, order and peace in the district, the government intervened to prevent it and to protect law and order. For doing so, the General Secretary of the Marxist Communist Party issued a statement condemning the government.

K. Balakrishnan, General Secretary of the Tamil Nadu Farmers’ Association, has also issued a statement on the issue saying that the government is hesitant to recover illegally occupied lands and distribute them under the two-acre land distribution scheme. That is not true. It was the DMK government that introduced, in the 2006–2007 Budget, the very scheme of distributing government lands and poramboke lands, up to two acres, to landless poor farmers, most of them Dalits. It is also noteworthy that this scheme was launched in this very Tiruvallur district on 17-9-2006, the birthday of Thanthai Periyar.

Under this scheme, this government has so far distributed free of cost 2 lakh 10 thousand 747 acres of land to 1 lakh 75 thousand 798 families of landless poor agricultural labourers, particularly Dalits. If the friends of the Marxist Communist Party conceal this, like hiding a whole pumpkin in a serving of rice, and then issue statements saying that this government is reluctant and hesitant to take action under the free-land scheme, one can see how far they are prepared to go in order to mislead the people.

The DMK government has been working with the aim of removing social inequalities by fixing ceilings on landholdings, distributing surplus land to landless poor people and ensuring that land belongs to the cultivator. It was during DMK rule, in 1970, that a law was suddenly brought in fixing the ceiling at 15 standard acres. Because of that law, 1 lakh 78 thousand 880 acres were taken over as surplus land and distributed to 1 lakh 37 thousand 236 eligible landless poor farmers. Of them, 61 thousand 985 were Dalits and 204 were tribal people.

Under the Farmers’ Social Security Welfare Assistance Scheme, 1 crore 70 lakh 38 thousand 112 members have so far been enrolled, and assistance amounting to ₹247 crore 49 lakh 360 has so far been given to 3 lakh 75 thousand 390 farming families. This too was done with the intention of helping farmers and, in large measure, Dalit people.

In 1971, under the DMK government, the “Kudiyiruppu Occupants (Conferment of Ownership) Act” was enacted for the first time in India, making the house sites on which agricultural labourers — mostly Dalits — were living their own property. A major function was held for this at Thiruthuraipoondi. At that function, veteran Communist leader Manali Kandasami said of the law:

> “What could not be achieved even after shedding blood for many years has been achieved by Chief Minister Kalaignar with a single drop of ink. Tonight, every farmer will go to sleep with the joyous feeling that we are sleeping in our own homes.”

Even if the friends of the Communist Party have forgotten those words, I have not.

For the past several days, heavy rain has fallen across the State and people have suffered greatly. Ministers and Members of Parliament and the Legislative Assembly have gone to the districts and are engaged in relief work. Schools have been closed. At such a time, when comrades of the Marxist Communist Party — who on earlier occasions would always engage in relief work and public service — put aside the public work demanded by the rains and instead try to conduct a protest in the name of land recovery against one individual, and devote their attention to that, it can only be called the height of politics.

Against whom are they conducting this protest? He too is a Dalit. He is a judge. In a special interview given today to *The Times of India*, he has said that because this complaint concerning the land was made against him, his elevation to the Supreme Court has been blocked. In that interview, he has also said that he has not occupied any government land or public land. But the Marxists say that the judge has encroached there.

In this situation, a government cannot act in a rash “pick it up and overturn it” manner. If it did, the same party might immediately conduct a protest asking why this government acted so hastily to seize the land of a Dalit citizen. Even if an individual has encroached upon land, it is the government that must take action against it; if the encroachment is proved true, the government must first recover the lands and only then can they be distributed to others. If a political party says it will itself recover the land and itself distribute it, is that proper for law, order and peace?

If every party were to take the law into its own hands in this fashion, seize lands or buildings and distribute them to people of its choice, what would this be — Tamil Nadu or West Bengal?

When a party tries to act in a way that disturbs law, order and peace, and says it will defy the prohibition and proceed, the government may have to take action against those involved. Yet the General Secretary of the Marxist Communist Party has issued a statement saying that this is an example of the Police Department’s “helplessness.” What is helplessness? If arresting those who violated the law is helplessness, was releasing them immediately and sending them home not an even greater helplessness? Is that not friend Varadarajan’s assessment?

These same Marxist Communist friends are in such a hurry to conduct an agitation saying that Justice Dinakaran has occupied government land, that they will recover it and distribute it to Dalits. Yet many months ago, did they not say that in Siruthavur, Jayalalithaa had seized and occupied government land that had been allotted for Dalits? Why have they not, all these months, come forward to conduct a recovery agitation there? What is the motive behind showing urgency only here?

It is not the intention of this government that friends of the Marxist Communist Party should be arrested in this matter. On 15 August 1970, when the Communist Party of India announced that it would conduct a similar land-recovery movement, thousands belonging to that movement were arrested across Tamil Nadu. When reporters met me in Tiruchi at the time and asked about it, I said:

> “The policy that land should be given to those who have none is not the policy of the Communists alone; it is a policy shared by all progressive parties, including the DMK. The DMK government is acting according to law to implement it. Even so, without showing any party prejudice, it will take action to protect law and peace. I am deeply sorry that an unavoidable situation arose in which friends of the Communist Party had to be arrested. They have the right to express their feelings. How much better it would have been if they had accepted the request of this government, which is prepared to put that same feeling into effect peacefully and lawfully.”

That is what I wish to recall even now.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**10-11-2009**

## Letter-specific notes

1. `தலித்` is retained as **Dalit**, matching the source’s own political/social terminology.
2. `ஸ்டாண்டர்ட் ஏக்கர்` is translated as **standard acre** without importing an outside land-ceiling definition.
3. The source’s “whole pumpkin in a serving of rice” image and “Tamil Nadu or West Bengal?” rhetorical contrast are retained rather than neutralised.
4. Historical land-reform claims and figures remain source-framed and are not externally reconciled.
