# 3617. Even ‘All Are Equal by Birth’ Is Not Allowed, It Seems!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This draft follows the locked Volume 46 conventions. The Assembly exchange, court observations, Education Department circular, textbook page numbers, names, programme titles, quoted textbook passages, poetry and Kalaignar’s sarcasm are preserved in source order. **Source pages:** [PDF 196](../../../pages/page-196.md)–[PDF 205](../../../pages/page-205.md)  
**Date:** 21 April 2012

**Udanpirappē,**

When the demand for grants for the School Education Department was taken up for discussion in the Assembly, Thangam Thennarasu, speaking for the DMK in his capacity as former minister of that department, had barely begun by saying that he would list as headlines the achievements made in the department. The present minister immediately interrupted him and, without necessity, dragged me and my family into his reply by asking, “Is reading autobiographies about the DMK leader and Kanimozhi what you call Samacheer Kalvi?” The Speaker permitted this and had it entered in the Assembly record. This news appeared in all the newspapers.

The AIADMK Government opposed the Samacheer Kalvi scheme simply because it had been introduced by the DMK Government. Concerning this scheme, High Court Judges Mr. S. Rajeswaran and Mrs. K.B.K. Vasuki asked:

> “The purpose of the Samacheer Kalvi Act is very clear. A study committee consisting of eminent experts examined the matter in detail and recommended implementation of the Samacheer Kalvi system. Those recommendations cannot simply be ignored. Apart from this, when a large amount has already been spent, is it necessary to spend another large amount? The Advocate General must give the Government proper advice on all this. Further, can an Act already in force—an Act upheld by this Court—be suspended merely through a policy decision of the Cabinet?”

Later, after the Supreme Court ruled that the Samacheer Kalvi scheme had to be implemented from that very year, the AIADMK Government had no alternative but to accept it.

At that time, in a circular sent by the Education Department to all Chief Educational Officers, orders were issued that portions to be removed from Samacheer Kalvi textbooks should be covered with stickers, and in some places struck out with a black marker pen, before the books were distributed.

Will the minister who asked, “Is reading the autobiographies of the DMK leader and Kanimozhi what you call Samacheer Kalvi?”, the Chief Minister who allowed it, or the Speaker who caused it to be entered in the Assembly record tell us in which Samacheer Kalvi lesson my autobiography or Kanimozhi’s autobiography appears? If such passages existed, should not those who sent the Education Department circular have ordered them too to be hidden with stickers? What, in fact, did they hide from the Samacheer Kalvi syllabus?

In the Standard I Tamil textbook, four entire pages—69, 70, 79 and 80—were to be removed! Do you know what was on those pages? Page 69 carried a picture of an apple under the heading “I Will Colour It.” Half the apple had been coloured red, and the pupil was to colour the remaining half. Even that sheet had to be removed. Why? Because black and red are said to be the colours of the DMK. Apparently there is no black or red in the AIADMK flag! What intelligent people... hee... hee... hee...

Page 80 listed the names of the Tamil months. Because the months began with Thai, they ordered that the entire page be torn out.

In the Standard III Tamil textbook, the circular said that on page 67, “two pictures and the cultural programme and procession scenes from the World Classical Tamil Conference held from 23–27 June 2010 must be covered with stickers.”

In the Standard III English textbook, the last paragraph on page 26 had to be removed. What did it say? “The World Tamil Conference was held in Coimbatore from 23 to 27 June 2010.” That passage had to be erased. And these are the people who present the Tamil Thai Award, it seems!

On page 27 of the same book, the passage saying “The Chief Minister presided over that conference. The conference exhibition was useful” had to be erased. It did not even say that I was the conference president; it merely said “the Chief Minister.” Yet the Tamil Nadu AIADMK Government ordered that it be erased. Is this what Minister Sivapathi calls my autobiography?

On page 75, the first paragraph had to be removed. The circular ordered that the entire paragraph beginning “Among classical languages, our refined Tamil is a distinguished language” should be struck out with black marker. Do you know what that paragraph actually says? “Among classical languages, our refined Tamil is a distinguished language. A procession was held at the first World Classical Tamil Conference conducted by the Tamil Nadu Government at Coimbatore. Decorated floats under the name ‘Iniyavai Naarpathu’ took part in the procession. They displayed the antiquity and cultural distinction of the Tamil language.” The Tamil people themselves must say whether even one word in this passage praises the DMK or me.

In the Standard IV Social Science book, the passage on Chennai Sangamam on page 111 had to be covered with a sticker. That is, the Education Department ordered that the passage saying folk arts refresh people and offer a feast to artistic feeling, and that the Tamil Nadu Government annually conducts Chennai Sangamam to develop folk arts and encourage folk artists, should be struck out with black marker. I do not know whether encouraging folk artists is a crime. They ordered these lines erased. Is this what the Tamil Nadu Education Minister calls an autobiography of Kanimozhi?

In Standard V Social Science, the third picture on page 80 had to be removed. It was a picture of the new Secretariat building, which had to be covered with a sticker. The picture was so small that one could scarcely identify it as the new Secretariat. If they thought even such a picture must not appear in a textbook, one can understand how deeply animosity has taken root in the heart of the AIADMK rulers.

In the Standard VI Tamil textbook, the Government order said that the whole of page 56, containing Abdul Rahman’s modern poem “Thirst,” must be covered with a sticker. The poem says:

> “Gardener who cut the branches of mine  
> that stretched their heads beyond the fence,  
> what will you do with my roots  
> slipping beneath the fence? — Abdul Rahman”

I do not know why they ordered this poem hidden with a sticker. Anger against Kavikko?

In the same book, page 129 had to be removed in full because it contained the words “Tamil New Year Special Issue — Poem — Welcome, Thai Tamil New Year.” Next, page 130 contained a free travel-pass application form asking the student to fill in name, school name, starting place and destination. I have been unable to discover why that entire page too was ordered removed.

In the Standard VI English book, the whole passage on “Thappattam” in the second paragraph of page 53 had to be removed. The paragraph explained what Thappattam is and noted that it existed in the age of *Silappathikaram*. At the end was a sentence asking, “Have you seen Thappattam performed at Chennai Sangamam during the Pongal festival?” Because of that one sentence, the entire paragraph was ordered removed.

In the Standard VI Science textbook, the picture of a bar magnet on page 81 had to be hidden. Why? Apparently the colours in the picture looked like black and red.

In the Standard VI Social Science textbook, page 11 said: “Through the financial assistance of the Kalaignar Life-Saving Treatment Scheme recently introduced by the Tamil Nadu Government, disadvantaged people everywhere are receiving quality treatment even in expensive, high-standard hospitals.” That had to be covered with a sticker!

On page 12, the words “Anaithu Grama Anna Marumalarchi Thittam” in the third line had to be erased with permanent marker. On page 17 too, the words “Anaithu Grama Anna Marumalarchi Thittam” and “Vaazhndhu Kaattuvom Thittam” had to be erased with permanent marker. Under a Government that carries Anna’s name, has the very name “Anna” now become objectionable?

On page 24, the words “It is noteworthy that the person implementing it is Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Kalaignar” had to be erased. The book actually says: “To ensure good nutritious food for pregnant mothers, the Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Kalaignar has announced and is implementing the good scheme under which the Government provides ₹1,000 per month for three months before delivery and three months after delivery, totalling ₹6,000.” If, following the Government’s instruction, the final words are removed, the sentence will simply end unfinished at “implementing...” That, this Government says, is what students must read.

On page 82, the words “Accepting the view of Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Kalaignar, in 2004 the Central Government declared Tamil a Classical Language. From this the incomparable distinction of Tamil is evident” had to be erased. Readers themselves can see what is wrong in these words. On the same page, a map of Tamil Nadu had to be hidden. Why? The area where the Cholas lived was shown in black, the region ruled by the Cheras in red, and the region ruled by the Pandyas in blue. Since black and red appeared among the three colours, apparently the whole picture had to be hidden!

In Standard VII, page 98 stated that Ko. Po. Krishnasamy Pavalar, Kandasamy, N.S. Krishnan, Arignar Anna, Kalaignar M. Karunanidhi, the T.K.S. Brothers, R.S. Manohar and others followed the earlier pioneers and helped the dramatic arts to grow. The circular ordered that this passage be struck out with a black marker. Under the heading “Pioneers of Tamil Drama,” the book had written in detail about Parithimar Kalaignar, Sankaradas Swamigal, Pammal Sambandhanar and others, and in the final paragraph simply added the names listed above as others who helped Tamil drama grow. Because my name too was included, an order was issued under a Government bearing Anna’s name that Anna, N.S. Krishnan and all the others must be blacked out along with me. Is that not shameful?

In the Standard IX Tamil textbook, the circular ordered that the phrase “Beginning Festival of the Tamil New Year” on page 203 be struck out with a black marker. What does the passage say? “Pongal festival, the festival of Tamils; the festival of the day of Thai; the beginning festival of Tamil New Year; the distinctive festival of the Tamil people; the great festival of culture.” The phrase saying that the first day of Thai is the beginning festival of Tamil New Year had to be blacked out.

In the Standard X Tamil textbook, page 17, the circular said that the phrase “was brought to completion by Honourable Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Kalaignar M. Karunanidhi” must be struck out with black marker. Why? The complete passage said: “The effort to have Tamil declared a Classical Language began in 1901 and continued until 2004. The Union Government recognised Tamil as a Classical Language in October 2004. This continuing effort was brought to completion by Honourable Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Kalaignar M. Karunanidhi.” They ordered only the final phrase, naming me, to be blacked out.

On page 89 of the same book, the circular said that the entire “Let Us Know” section had to be removed. A poem written by me appeared there, and the whole thing had to go. On page 100, the circular ordered: “Read and understand Kalaignar’s pleasant prose. From Flag Day... through ‘Victory will seek you out and garland you,’ the passage must be covered with a sticker.” It was a prose passage I wrote about Flag Day. Because I wrote it, they ordered it hidden. I feel no sorrow about their hiding any of these things.

But here comes a spectacle even amid the pain! A picture of Ayyan Thiruvalluvar had been printed in every textbook. Beneath it were the words of Thiruvalluvar: “பிறப்பொக்கும் எல்லா உயிர்க்கும்” — “All living beings are equal by birth.” Because those words appeared in my handwriting, the AIADMK Government ordered those very words removed. And today a minister stands in the Assembly and says that my autobiography and my daughter’s autobiography were placed in the Samacheer Kalvi textbooks. Do we need a better example of how they utter falsehoods contrary to truth?

On page 239, the circular said that the sentence “The Pavendhar Classical Tamil Research Library is functioning at Fort St. George, Chennai” must be struck out with a black marker. Perhaps because theirs is the Government that removed the Pavendhar Classical Tamil Research Library from Fort St. George overnight, they ordered the sentence removed as well!

Generally, the *Semmozhi Vaazhthu* appearing in textbooks of all standards had to be hidden with stickers. Do you know what words it contained that made it necessary to hide the song?

> Equal is the birth of all living beings—  
> once born, every place is our town, everyone our kin!  
> A measure of food, two pieces of clothing,  
> one dwelling place—  
> so we declared and lived;  
> so shall we labour and live.  
> “Good and evil do not come from others”—  
> that noble saying is our golden saying!  
> Casting war aside,  
> making wealth common,  
> the loving language that shows the path of peace  
> is the spoken wisdom of Ayyan Valluvar!  
> From beings of one sense to beings of six senses,  
> dividing and describing the bodily forms they possess—  
> the Tolkappiyam of undying fame,  
> the lofty culture spoken by the peerless Kural,  
> the resounding *Silambu* and *Mekalai*,  
> with *Chintamani*, *Valayapathi*,  
> and *Kundalakesi*—  
> ours is Tamil, the Classical Language!  
> Kamban, lord of the Tamil land,  
> and queen of poets Avvai, the good woman,  
> and the many thousands of weavers of verse  
> praised by every faith—  
> the language that stands as the seed  
> for every new garment they give!  
> Dividing life beautifully  
> into *agam* and *puram*,  
> the sweet language without beginning or end—  
> the living world-language that grows as it is learned—  
> our language, our language—it alone  
> is the Classical Language, the Classical Language—our Tamil language!  
> Long may it live! Long may it live!  
> Long may it live!

Is there even one word here that students must not read, one word that could ruin their future if they read it?

The sole offence is that I wrote this Classical Tamil benediction. Look at the Classical Tamil benediction I wrote—and at the same time look at the following words in the World Tamil Conference song from the conference Jayalalithaa conducted at Thanjavur when she was Chief Minister:

> “Like southern Tamil that lives for ever,  
> like Tamil literature that forever tastes sweet,  
> to add eternal fame to the language,  
> long live the gracious Chief Minister!”

> “Avvai, Andal and Ammaiyar too  
> sang beautifully and nurtured Tamil;  
> Mother-Chief-Minister Jayalalithaa  
> remains in rule and nurtures it.”

> “As Mother India and Mother Tamil  
> join together in harmonious joy,  
> the great jewel who came when none would come—  
> long live Mother Jayalalithaa!”

In the World Tamil Conference benediction, instead of blessing Tamil, Jayalalithaa’s praise was thus magnified many times over. In the Classical Tamil benediction composed for the conference conducted under DMK rule, is there even one word praising the Chief Minister? Yet they said it must be torn out!

Udanpirappē, those who did all this today conceal every bit of it and a minister himself has stated falsely in the Assembly that my autobiography and my daughter Kanimozhi’s autobiography appeared in Samacheer Kalvi. DMK members were not given an opportunity to deny that false statement. That is why, when there is no opportunity to say in the Legislative Assembly what must be said there, the DMK has come forward to say it before the people’s assembly.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**21 April 2012**
