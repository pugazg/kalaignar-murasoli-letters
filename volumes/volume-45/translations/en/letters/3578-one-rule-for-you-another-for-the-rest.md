# 3578. One Rule for You; Another for Everyone Else?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 5 August 2011

**Udanpirappē,**

I want to explain to you one more example of the extent to which the AIADMK government is engaged in retaliatory false-case action. Mr Jaffer Sait, IPS, a member of the Muslim community, served as Additional DGP in the Intelligence Department during the previous DMK government. It is true that he served the government with the degree of honesty and loyalty expected of him. But the present rulers treated the very fact that he had worked honestly and efficiently as an offence: “How can you be loyal to a government? Is that not wrong? So the place where you belong is the Mandapam camp! I am transferring you there.” Not content with hurling him away in this fashion, the government instructed senior officers to take some action against him so as to nourish its desire for revenge.

The senior officers reportedly said: he is a good officer; whichever party is in government, he is capable of serving that government loyally; there are many officers like him; if action is taken against him, the norms of the police service will be damaged. But the government would not listen. It said, “Are all of you loyal only to Karunanidhi? Some action must immediately be taken against Jaffer Sait.” And thus action has been taken—the weapon of revenge has been fired.

The allegation against this officer holding the rank of Additional DGP is that he obtained an allotment of a Tamil Nadu Housing Board plot and thereby earned several crores of rupees! In the matter of allotting Housing Board plots, the decision that government could allot them under its discretionary quota was itself taken during AIADMK rule. The Government Order was issued on 25-1-1979. When the system was first introduced, only 10 per cent of houses or plots could be allotted at the government's discretion. But after the AIADMK returned to power in 1991, it was that government which decided to raise the discretionary quota from 10 per cent to 15 per cent.

When complaints arose during DMK rule about Housing Board plots being allotted on a discretionary basis, the long-standing system of government discretionary allotment of houses and plots was abolished during the DMK government itself, and this was announced in the Governor's Address. While the DMK was still in power, Mr Subramanian Swamy made this very allegation, and I gave a detailed explanation in the Assembly under Rule 110; it was published in the newspapers too.

The long-followed practice was that 85 per cent of Housing Board houses or plots were allotted by lot and the remaining 15 per cent by the government under its discretionary quota. The government used that 15 per cent discretionary quota to make allotments, under both DMK and AIADMK governments, to unmarried women, women deserted by husbands, widows, persons with disabilities, social workers, distinguished persons in society, elderly persons living alone, employees of public institutions and specified public-sector institutions, employees of the Union Government and Union Government institutions, journalists, employees of nationalised banks, employees of nationalised insurance institutions, employees of all Boards in Tamil Nadu other than the Tamil Nadu Housing Board, employees of local bodies and municipalities, serving and former military personnel, freedom fighters, language martyrs and government employees of unblemished record.

Houses or plots allotted under the government's discretionary quota are not sold at a concessional price. The same amount collected from persons receiving allotments by lot—that is, the market rate—is collected from discretionary allottees too. Indeed, during the recent DMK government there were many examples of people who had received plots under the discretionary quota returning those plots to the Housing Board because the amount they had to pay as the price of the land was too high for them to afford.

No rule was violated in allotting these plots during DMK rule. Not one of the procedures followed during AIADMK rule was altered during the DMK period. On 7-12-2010, younger brother I. Periasamy, then Minister in charge of the department, told reporters: “Government allotments will be made within the rules. If rules have been violated, and a specific complaint is made, the government will examine it and take action if the complaint is found true.” This was reported in the newspapers.

Who received Housing Board houses or plots under the discretionary quota during Jayalalithaa's government?

- N. Narayanan, IAS, then Chief Secretary—4,115 square feet in 1993.
- K.S. Karthikeyan, son of present Minister K.A. Sengottaiyan—4,535 square feet in Besant Nagar in 1995.
- Noor Jameela, wife of former Minister Nagore Meeran—2,559 square feet in Kottivakkam in 1993.
- Dr Bhanumathi Thambidurai, wife of MP Thambidurai—seven grounds of land in Anna Nagar.
- AIADMK's trade-union federation—three grounds of land in Anna Nagar.
- Mrs Bhanumathi, wife of AIADMK's S.M. Velusamy—a house in Coimbatore in 1993.
- Among police officers: Devaram, K. Vijayakumar, R. Nataraj, P. Kalimuthu, Iqbal Mohammed, A.P. Mohammed Ali, K. Radhakrishnan, V. Balachandran, C. Sylendra Babu, S.V. Karuppasamy, Chitrarasu, R. Thirunavukkarasu, F.M. Hussain, G. Sampath Kumar, Senthamarai Kannan, K. Shanmugavel, P. Chinnasamy, Sanjay Arora, Tamil Selvan, Gopalakrishnan, Periyayya, Ashok Kumar and others received Housing Board plots or houses. The DMK government did not point to any of this as wrongdoing.
- Among IAS officers: Malik Feroz Khan, P.A. Ramaiah, S. Kabilan, Raghavan, Shyam Sundar, Srinivasan, Lal Raina Silo, V. Ramu, V. Varaprasad Rao, T. Natarajan—who had served as Deputy Secretary to Jayalalithaa; the DMK government did not raid his house—M. Devaraj, Syed Munir Hoda and other officers received houses or plots. Devaraj and Syed Munir Hoda had bought Housing Board plots during AIADMK rule; yet without considering that as a disqualification, when I was Chief Minister they served as my Secretaries.
- Apart from these there is a separate list of politicians. There is also a list of people who received two plots.

**Udanpirappē,**

So many people received Housing Board residential plots both during AIADMK rule and later when the DMK assumed office. Is it not an act of revenge for Jayalalithaa's government now to pick out only a few people and take action against them?

Newspapers publish stories that these officers used the plots, entered joint arrangements with private parties, built large buildings and sold them, earning crores of rupees. Once the amount fixed by the Housing Board for these residential plots is paid in full, the plots become the property of the respective allottees. No one can dictate that privately owned land must thereafter be used only in some particular manner; there is no such condition. The owner may use the plot as he wishes. Moreover, when the plots themselves cost about ₹1 crore, what are government officers expected to do to pay that amount and construct a house? Building houses on such land through a joint venture with a private company is something the Housing Board ordinarily permits. There is no violation of rules in this. Many people have built houses in this way. What could be wrong in it?

That is not all. Additional DGP Jaffer Sait first obtained this plot in his daughter's name, then returned it, and afterwards obtained the same plot in his wife's name. It would be wrong if separate plots were bought in the name of each person. But what great wrongdoing is there in transferring the same single plot from one family member's name to another because of family circumstances? If even the names of wife and daughter are dragged into a case, is that not deliberate retaliation against the entire family?

The government did not stop with filing a case against that officer. At midnight it placed him under temporary suspension. Let me give one more example of how far this revenge has gone. At the very height of vindictiveness, after suspending this particular officer, the government has ordered that he must remain at the Mandapam camp during the period of suspension and must not stay with his family in Chennai. What should such an order be called?

He belongs to the Muslim minority community. This is their fasting period. How proper is it to order that even at such a time he may not see his family? Why such retaliatory measures? If officers loyal to a government are punished, and other officers begin to think, “If we serve a government loyally, the next government may retaliate against us,” can they discharge their duties properly and fully?

We are told there are associations for IAS and IPS officers. Do those associations permit such actions? IAS and IPS officers are said to be officers under the control of the Union Government. Will that Union Government at least pay attention in situations such as these? When will there be an end to such vindictive action? Administrative history will certainly not accept attempts to display administrative skill through revenge!

**With affection, M.K.**  
5-8-2011
