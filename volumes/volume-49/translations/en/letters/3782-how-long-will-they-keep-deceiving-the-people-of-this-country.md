# 3782. How Long Will They Keep Deceiving the People of This Country!

> **Translator’s note**
>

It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. **Udanpirappē,**

Every Tamil Nadu newspaper has carried, in banner headlines, a statement by the Chief Minister under the title: “₹2,950 crore allotted for road-development works — Jayalalithaa orders.” One might naturally marvel: “Ah! Even while staying at Kodanadu, our Chief Minister has allocated nearly ₹3,000 crore solely for road-development works! What a great announcement!”

The reason is this. In the Budget presented on behalf of the AIADMK Government in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly on 21 March 2013, the Finance Minister announced:

> “The allocation for the Highways Department, which stood at ₹5,615.71 crore in 2012–13, has been increased by this Government to ₹6,452.77 crore in 2013–14.”

A further ₹2,032 crore was allotted for the Comprehensive Road Infrastructure Development Programme. During the debate on the department’s demand for grants in April, the Highways Minister also explained in detail which schemes would be carried out during the year.

He told the Assembly that, in the first phase, 1,678 kilometres of roads would be improved at a cost of ₹8,580 crore; that 5,000 kilometres of roads would be renewed during the year at a cost of ₹1,088 crore; that work on forty-one railway road-overbridges was under way at a cost of ₹1,062 crore; that administrative approval had been obtained for road-improvement works and pedestrian subways in Chennai at a cost of ₹385.68 crore; and that ₹549 crore had been allotted for roads required by industries in the Sriperumbudur area.

Even before the demand for grants for this department was taken up for debate, the Chief Minister hurriedly read a statement under Rule 110 in the Assembly on 1 April 2013. She announced allocations of ₹168 crore to widen the road from Tiruninravur to Padi into six lanes; ₹98 crore for a Thanjavur bypass; ₹300 crore for 131 bridges on Government and Panchayat Union roads; ₹100 crore for sixty-seven Government roads; and ₹202 crore for preliminary work, including site studies, for eighteen railway overbridges in eleven districts. She said these projects would be undertaken.

Were all the projects announced by the Chief Minister under Rule 110 already included in the Budget and the demand for grants? Or were they new projects? It is impossible to tell.

On 15 April 2013, the Chief Minister read another statement under Rule 110 announcing that a ₹400-crore scheme would be created to maintain village-panchayat and Panchayat Union tar roads.

Paragraph 76 of the Budget for 2013–14 stated that 168 kilometres of intermediate-lane State highways would be upgraded to two-lane roads; that 1,000 kilometres of single-lane major district roads would be upgraded to intermediate-lane roads under the Comprehensive Road Infrastructure Development Programme; that a further 3,500 kilometres of roads would be strengthened; and that ₹2,032 crore had been allotted for the programme in the 2013–14 Budget.

After allocating ₹2,032 crore in the Budget for these schemes, the Chief Minister issued an order on 24 July 2013—before even four months had passed—allocating ₹2,950 crore under the Comprehensive Road Infrastructure Development Programme for widening roads and improving the quality and capacity of road infrastructure.

Is this a new announcement? Or is it merely the same allocation announced once again after having already been included in the Budget? If it is not new, should the Government not have said that this was an order implementing what had already been announced in the Budget? If it is new, have all the schemes mentioned in the Budget been completed within these four months? Only the newspapers that gave the Chief Minister’s statement such prominence can shed light on this!

And what of the numerous announcements she made under Rule 110? Have funds been allotted and Government Orders issued for them? If this is not a new announcement but only an order allocating money for schemes already announced, why did every newspaper report it as though the Chief Minister had unveiled something entirely new? Should the Government not explain to the people which account is true?

If the Chief Minister has indeed announced genuinely new schemes involving an allocation of ₹2,950 crore, should the Assembly’s approval not be obtained? The Budget debate ended only in May. Is it proper to announce an allocation of this scale just two months later? What explanation does this Government offer?

That is not all. When schemes involving such a vast amount are announced, should they not receive the Cabinet’s approval? When were these schemes discussed in the Cabinet? If they were discussed, should the decision not have been announced to the newspapers immediately after the Cabinet meeting?

In this fine state of affairs, the Highways Minister’s policy note to the Assembly says that many schemes undertaken under the previous year’s allocation remain pending. For example, it says that road-widening and improvement works, bridges and minor bridges worth ₹1,136 crore, sanctioned in 2012–13, are still being implemented as pending works.

Does this not make it clear that many schemes from 2012–13 remain unfinished even while more and more schemes of this magnitude are continuously being announced?

The Highways Minister told the Assembly that the Chief Minister had allotted ₹1,088 crore for road maintenance in 2013–14. The Chief Minister has now announced that she has ordered an allocation of ₹1,021 crore, ₹26 lakh and ₹56,000 through the State Highways Department. Does this amount fall within the Minister’s announcement, or is it an additional allocation beyond what he said had been allotted?

Because yesterday’s statement by the Chief Minister speaks of “the ₹2,950 crore allotted this year,” it does not appear to be a new announcement in addition to an earlier allocation. What had already been announced has been presented as though it were new—like **old toddy in a new pot**—and the newspapers have reported it as a fresh declaration.

At a time when the Chief Minister has made this announcement on behalf of the Highways Department, I am reminded that it was the DMK Government which created a separate Highways Department in the Secretariat on 1 August 1996 and a separate Ministry for Highways in Tamil Nadu from 26 March 1998.

If one statistic is to be offered in comparison with the AIADMK Government: between 2001 and 2006, under the AIADMK, 46,017 kilometres of roads were improved at a cost of ₹5,569 crore. In the five years of DMK rule from 2006 to 2011, 65,886 kilometres of roads were improved at a cost of ₹13,529 crore.

Between 2001 and 2006, the AIADMK Government built 871 bridges and 814 minor bridges at a cost of ₹497 crore. By contrast, between 2006 and 2011 the DMK Government built 4,019 minor bridges at a cost of ₹1,385 crore. Only six bypasses were built under the AIADMK from 2001 to 2006; forty-eight bypasses were built under the DMK from 2006 to 2011.

Why go so far? During the DMK Government, we initiated the new four-lane elevated expressway from Chennai Port to Maduravoyal, invited the Prime Minister, and held the foundation ceremony in my presence. After several crores of rupees had been spent, it was the AIADMK Government that blocked the road project and kept it suspended!

It is said that many schemes announced by this Government never reach implementation, while some are subjected to long delay. For example, *The Times of India* reported on the fifteenth that approval had been granted the previous December for a flyover linking Old Mahabalipuram Road and East Coast Road, but that it had taken all these months merely to decide its location. The project is estimated at ₹400 crore. The same newspaper wrote that flyovers planned at Porur, Moolakadai and Tirumangalam had been delayed because of land acquisition.

The Chief Minister announces that roads will be improved at a cost of nearly ₹3,000 crore. But *The Hindu* itself has reported in detail on the condition of roads in the towns around Chennai.

Its report dated 12 July 2013 stated:

> “Roads in Tondiarpet, New Washermenpet, Perambur and Moolakadai were completely inundated. Kilpauk, Villivakkam and Mogappair West also suffered with Balfour Road and Barnaby Road among those flooded. Residents of Kilpauk said roads in the area became flooded even after a brief spell of rains, forcing them to walk on the road as pavements were completely encroached by parked vehicles. Several roads in north Chennai already in bad shape, have worsened now. In Moolakadai, road users had to battle not just rain-ravaged roads but also traffic snarls. Walking on Madhavaram High Road requires real skill. There are no pavements and the roadside is either slushy or waterlogged.”

On 17 July too, *The Hindu* wrote at length about the condition of Tamil Nadu’s roads. Perhaps because *The Hindu* wrote on two days in this manner, the Chief Minister hurriedly announced an allocation of nearly ₹3,000 crore for a road-improvement scheme.

What certificate has the Union Government’s National Highways Authority of India given the Tamil Nadu Highways Department? *The Hindu* carried it prominently on 14 July 2013. Tamil newspapers that magnify even a tiny item praising the Chief Minister did not publish what the National Highways Authority said.

The headline in *The Hindu* was: **“NHAI blames State for delay in ongoing projects.”** The report said:

> “As many as seven ongoing important road widening works valued over Rs. 3,000 crore were moving at a snail’s pace in Tamil Nadu due to lack of support from the State Government, said National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) officials.”

In other words, NHAI officials stated that seven major road-widening projects worth more than ₹3,000 crore were moving at a snail’s pace in Tamil Nadu because the State Government had failed to provide proper cooperation.

It was the same *Hindu* that had written a hymn of praise claiming that the AIADMK Government led by Jayalalithaa was performing better in many respects than the United Progressive Alliance Government at the Centre and the earlier DMK Government. Yet that newspaper itself has described the miserable condition of roads under the AIADMK Government in these terms.

Suppressing all of this, the Chief Minister continues to issue new announcements, and Tamil Nadu’s newspapers continue to give them prominence. The same announcement is made once in the Budget, once during the debate on the demand for grants, once in a statement read by the Chief Minister under Rule 110—and, as though even that were insufficient, schemes worth several crores are repeatedly presented in the name of fresh announcements, diverting, persuading and deceiving the people as though each were new.

Seeing all this, one is reminded of the song from the film *Malaikkallan*: **“How long will they keep deceiving the people of this country?”**

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**26 July 2013**
