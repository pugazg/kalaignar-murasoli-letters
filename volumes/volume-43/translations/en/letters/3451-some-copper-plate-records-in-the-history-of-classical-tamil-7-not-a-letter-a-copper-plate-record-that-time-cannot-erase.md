# 3451. Some Copper-Plate Records in the History of Classical Tamil — 7: Not a Letter; a Copper-Plate Record That Time Cannot Erase!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This letter closes the seven-part classical-Tamil-history sequence by tracing the DMK’s political and organisational campaign through the Union Government’s declaration of Tamil as a classical language. Historical and political claims remain source-framed. Sonia Gandhi’s 8 November 2005 letter is reproduced exactly as the English source wording printed in the canonical Tamil record.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3451](../../../chapters/3451-semmoli-varalatril-sila-seppedugal-7-kadithamalla-kaalathaal-azhikka-mudiyaatha-seppedu.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 199](../../../pages/page-199.md)–[PDF 207](../../../pages/page-207.md)  
**Date:** 31 December 2009

**Udanpirappē,**

Whether it has been in office or functioning as the opposition, the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam has always continued to raise its voice for the ideals it has embraced; the history of the DMK contains countless documentary proofs of this. It was an ideal of the DMK that Tamil should be declared a classical language. To fulfil that ideal, the party continued, even while in opposition, the efforts it had undertaken while in government, without allowing its action to slacken.

As President of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam, on 22.4.2003 I wrote a letter to Prime Minister Vajpayee on the need to declare Tamil a classical language. In that letter I wrote:

> “It is my heartfelt request that Tamil be declared a classical language. I once again bring to your attention this demand which I have been pressing for many years. No scholar has so far discovered when the Tamil language came into being. Many scholars have said that Tamil is a language that arose even before stone appeared and before soil appeared. This itself proclaims Tamil’s antiquity. I have for a long time been urging the Union Government to declare such a glorious Tamil a classical language.
>
> “I have appealed to the Union Government many times regarding the demand that Tamil be declared a classical language. In this situation, I trust that the National Democratic Alliance Government under your leadership will fulfil this long-awaited demand.”

Thus I again reminded and pressed the Union Government.

The Villupuram district DMK conference held on 20 and 21 September 2003 was conducted in a manner that created awareness, generated an upsurge and advancement, and yielded benefit. At that conference a resolution was adopted pointing out the lethargic approach of the Union Government which, although the Tamil language possessed every qualification to be declared a classical language and to find a place as one of the Union’s official languages, and although this demand had repeatedly been pressed before the Union Government and in Parliament, had still not accepted and announced it; the resolution urged the Union Government to take immediate action.

Following this resolution, the DMK Headquarters Literary Wing conducted seminars on Tamil as a classical language and official language in fifteen district headquarters across Tamil Nadu. Dr M. P. Manivendan and the State office-bearers led these programmes.

Across Tamil Nadu, explanatory meetings were held on the Villupuram conference resolutions concerning Tamil as an official language and classical language, the Cauvery Authority, the Sethusamudram Project, and the POTA, ESMA and DESMA laws, with DMK speakers addressing them. To press these resolutions, the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam conducted a civil-disobedience protest in front of Union and State government offices on 1.11.2003.

Through the efforts of the Karanthai Tamil Sangam, a meeting of Tamil scholars was held on 10.1.2004. It was decided that the demand for classical-language status should be known not only to Tamil scholars but also to ordinary people; that they too should support the demand; and that a one-crore-signature movement should therefore be conducted to press the Union Government. Its inaugural function was held on Monday, 12.1.2004, in the main hall of the Karanthai Tamil Sangam, with Tanjavur Municipal Chairman K. Sultan affixing the first signature. Because of the intensive efforts of everyone concerned and of Tamil enthusiasts, the movement reached its target within a very short time.

In the election manifesto issued by the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam for the May 2004 parliamentary election, the purpose of the classical-language demand was explained for the first time and the following pledge was included: “In order to make it possible to establish a department for Tamil in every university in India, for the Union Government to allocate funds every year for Tamil research, and for Tamil research to be undertaken in universities throughout the world (Global), the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam will continue to urge the Union Government to declare the Tamil language, which possesses all the qualifications of a classical language, an exalted and distinct classical language.”

Parliamentary elections were held. The United Progressive Alliance formed under the leadership of that lamp of sacrifice, Mrs Sonia Gandhi, won, and a government was formed under Dr Manmohan Singh. The Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam was among the parties in the United Progressive Alliance. As a result of our having continuously explained this matter to Mrs Sonia Gandhi even before the parliamentary elections, the National Common Minimum Programme issued after the government was formed at the Centre included the programme: “Tamil will be declared as a Classical Language.” This made the world realise that the history of the classical-language demand had reached a distinguished stage.

On 7.6.2004, addressing the joint sitting of Parliament, President of India Dr A. P. J. Abdul Kalam announced, “Tamil will be declared as a Classical Language.” That announcement immersed Tamil scholars, Tamil enthusiasts and Tamils living throughout the world in an ocean of joy.

At the special conference of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam held in Salem city on 21 and 22 August 2004, a resolution stated that for the past century many Tamil scholars had supplied appropriate evidence and insisted that Tamil was an exalted and distinct classical language; that even while he was Chief Minister Kalaignar had repeatedly pressed Prime Minister Vajpayee both through letters and in person to have that special status accepted by the Union Government; that the DMK had included it in its parliamentary election manifesto and continued to press for Tamil to be declared a classical language; and that, accordingly, the United Progressive Alliance Government at the Centre had accepted the demand and announced it in the President’s Address. The conference welcomed the announcement and requested that the appropriate government order be issued quickly. Professor M. P. Balasubramaniam moved the resolution, S. Amudhan seconded it, and it was adopted unanimously.

The United Progressive Alliance Government at the Centre, functioning under the guidance of Mrs Sonia Gandhi and the leadership of Dr Manmohan Singh, issued a notification on 12.10.2004 declaring Tamil a classical language. Udanpirappē, you who know me completely will certainly understand how jubilant I would have been when that notification was issued, how greatly my heart would have leapt with joy, and how much pride would have sprung within me. A joy equal to what I felt whenever Thanthai Periyar patted me in appreciation, and whenever Arignar Anna embraced me, kissed my forehead and showered affection upon me, took residence within me then.

As an expression of that joy were the speeches I delivered at the felicitation function conducted by the Tamil Language Academy in Chennai on 10.10.2004 and at the felicitation function jointly conducted at Madurai on 17.10.2004 by the Madurai Tamil Sangam and more than thirty associations devoted to Tamil. All Tamil enthusiasts know this. Though those speeches are wholly relevant to this series of articles, I give only a few portions below. Here they are:

> “We have all gathered here to celebrate Tamil having been declared a classical language. I cannot accept the description of this as a felicitation function, or the specification that the felicitation is for me. When I think of the great scholar Caldwell, who produced the comparative grammar of the Dravidian languages; of Parithimar Kalaignar, Surya Narayana Sastri, who after a gap of many years raised the first voice that Tamil should be made a classical language; and, after them, of the many Tamil Sangams, many Tamil poets and many great Tamil scholars — hundreds and thousands who laboured for us to attain this position — I regard what you have done today as conveying through my mouth the appreciation and gratitude that should be offered to all of them. You have not tied gratitude into a bundle and placed it upon my head as though commanding me, ‘Take this and deliver it to them.’
>
> “I am an instrument. The people of Tamil Nadu made use of this instrument. Tamil poets and great Tamil scholars have made use of it. If a chisel is used to carve a sculpture, can it be understood that the chisel itself carved the sculpture? The work was done by the sculptor. In the same way, I am only the chisel; there are many sculptors — many thousands. At this moment I offer my bowed salutations and gratitude to all those sculptors.
>
> “If we look a little more closely, any language can grow only in relation to a government. If you turn the pages of history, you will understand that for a language to grow it needs a government, the authority of that government, and the support of those who run it. Seen that way, the truth is that a government arose in India which created the path for Tamil to become a classical language. Tamil has today been made a classical language as a duty of that government. On behalf of this great celebration, I am duty-bound to thank Dr Manmohan Singh, who discharged that responsibility, and Mrs Sonia Gandhi, who has assumed responsibility for the guiding group.
>
> “At the same time, I was born in 1924, eighty years ago, in a small village called Tirukkuvalai somewhere on the soil of Tanjavur in Tamil Nadu, as the son of Muthuvelar and Anjugam Ammaiyar. If today, at this great celebration, with every Tamil heart blessing me, I have become a person receiving so much praise, acclaim and honour, I dedicate this gratitude to the mother and father who gave birth to me; to Thanthai Periyar, who raised me and made me what I am; to elder brother Perarignar Anna, who guided me and brought me forward; to all of you who cooperate with me and stand by me; and to all the Udanpirappugal.
>
> “In the past, for Tamil to become a classical language and one of the official languages, we had to adopt a resolution at the Villupuram conference and struggle. One lakh members of this movement under my leadership had to go to prison. Even then, from the government of that time we did not receive even the consoling words, ‘It will be considered; we shall see.’ But this government, the government ruling India today, understood our feeling and announced in the President’s Address that Tamil would immediately be made a classical language. We pressed and pressed, asking when the announcement would be implemented, and today it has constructively received Cabinet approval. We see Tamil, as a classical language, crawling and playing in our laps.
>
> “This is not the labour of one person. The trust placed in us by the voters of Tamil Nadu has not gone in vain. The desire and affection we hold for Tamil have not gone in vain. The credit for protecting them from going in vain is not mine. It belongs to the voters of this country who made forty people under my leadership victorious. It belongs to Tamils. Therefore this felicitation and this function of gratitude belong to them.”

Later, it was while I spoke at the DMK conference held in Tiruchirappalli on 5.3.2006 in the presence of Mrs Sonia Gandhi that the feelings swelling within me poured forth like the Courtallam waterfalls. I give below a portion of that speech so that, while writing this, I may once again experience those feelings, and that you too may experience them when you read it:

> “For Tamil to be made a classical language, the demand of Parithimar Kalaignar — Surya Narayana Sastri — had for one hundred and fifty years flowered, borne fruit and ripened, while we feared that it might rot away. The present Union Government, saying, ‘We are here; do not be afraid,’ accepted the request we made and declared Tamil a classical language.
>
> “After declaring Tamil a classical language, Ammaiyar, you yourself wrote me a letter on November 8. In that letter:
>
> November 8, 2005
> Dear Thiru Karunanidhiji,
> I have received your letter of 28th October. I am glad that all the
> formalities for declaring Tamil as a Classical Language have now
> been completed. This is an achievement for all the constituents of
> the UPA Government, but particular credit goes to you and your
> Party.
> With Regards,
> Yours sincerely,
> Sonia Gandhi.
>
> “That is, you wrote that all the formalities and procedures required for making Tamil a classical language had been completed and the achievement fulfilled; that although the parties of the United Progressive Alliance were responsible for this achievement, particularly and especially I was responsible, and the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam under my leadership was responsible.
>
> “I tell you: this is not merely a letter. I am one who passionately wants it to become something that, a hundred years or two hundred years from now, my great-grandson may take up and read and praise, saying, ‘This is the copper-plate record that our grandfather safeguarded for us through the ages.’
>
> “In my youth I began political life carrying a flag bearing the tiger, bow and fish and proclaiming, ‘Long live Tamil! Let Tamils triumph!’ That political life continues to this day. If there is a history to Tamil becoming a classical language, then the letter you wrote to me, giving me that history and that pride, is something that should be engraved in golden letters, set with diamonds, and in the future mounted upon my grave. It is something that should remain in my memorial. With that fullness of heart, I convey to you my gratitude, salutations, appreciation and good wishes for the letter you wrote.”

Every word of that speech of gratitude will continue to echo in my heart as long as I live; why, even after I am gone, it will continue to echo in my grave!

The voice first raised by Tamil scholars demanding that Tamil be declared a classical language echoed organisationally, became the voice of Tamil hearts, rose in the political arena, gained strength through government, and, because it was properly presented by those qualified to do so to the appropriate people at the right moment, Mother Tamil today sits upon a golden chariot and proceeds in procession across the earth!

Time ripened. The century-old dream of Tamils became reality. Tamil was proclaimed a classical language — the first part of the history of classical-language status has come to a distinguished completion. The tasks that we must next undertake for the classical language stand before us in ranks. Let us prepare ourselves for them!

Long live Tamil, the classical language!  
May the welfare of the refined Tamil people flourish!  
May glorious Tamil Nadu prosper and grow in splendour!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**31-12-2009**
