# 3774. Does Tamil Not Deserve the Right That Hindi Has?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 15 July 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

A report has appeared that yesterday, 12 July 2013, the Madurai Bench of the High Court refused permission to argue in Tamil in a petition seeking the rescue of a husband stranded in Saudi Arabia. Saying that “permission cannot be granted to argue in Tamil in the High Court”, the Court dismissed the petition.

A woman named Aisha Banu of Kovilpatti in Thoothukudi district had filed the petition before the Madurai Bench. She said that her husband, Fakkeer Mydeen, had gone abroad for work; that the company owner had taken away his passport; that because he was not literate he was unable to contact Indian Embassy officials; that he had been suffering for the past twenty-one months; and that the authorities should be directed to take appropriate action to rescue him and bring him back to India.

The lawyer who wished to appear for her asked to present his arguments in Tamil and sought the Court's permission. The High Court judge refused, saying that a Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court had held that arguments in the High Courts and the Supreme Court must be conducted in English. The judge showed the lawyer a copy of that judgment and also dismissed Aisha Banu's petition.

I do not come forward to find fault with the judge. In his order, he referred to Article 348 of the Constitution, which states that English shall be the language used in proceedings before the Supreme Court and the High Courts.

In one case, Raj Narain appeared in the Supreme Court and argued in Hindi. M. C. Setalvad's successor as Attorney General, C. K. Daphtary, objected; some judges on the Bench also objected to Hindi being used. The judges then refused permission to speak in Hindi. They ordered that the litigant could speak in English, submit his argument in writing or appoint a lawyer to argue on his behalf. On that occasion too, the judges cited Article 348 and stated that English was the language of court proceedings.

When the DMK was in office, I told journalists who met me on 22 November 2006:

> “English has so far been the language used in all proceedings of the Madras High Court. Article 348 of the Constitution of India states that all proceedings in the High Courts and the Supreme Court shall be conducted in English. Accordingly, English has been used in all proceedings of the Madras High Court.
>
> “However, Article 348(2) provides that, if High Court proceedings are to be conducted in the State language, the State Government may take the appropriate legal steps with the consent of the President of India. Using that constitutional provision, the Tamil Nadu Government has decided to introduce our mother tongue, Tamil, as the language of proceedings in all matters before the Madras High Court.”

This announcement was also issued as a press release of the Tamil Nadu Government. The High Court and the Bar welcomed it. The then Chief Justice of the High Court, the Honourable A. P. Shah, supported the decision and himself explained the procedures that had to be followed. I immediately wrote to thank him.

As soon as the announcement was made, the “then” Dr Ramadoss issued a statement praising me: “Who other than Kalaignar can realise the principle of Tamil everywhere and in everything? He has now made the beginning.”

In September 2001, the Tamil Forum of lawyers in the High Court filed a writ petition on this very issue. The High Court dismissed it. The reason given was that, under Article 348(2), only the Governor had the authority to issue the necessary order; therefore, the Governor had to act, and there was no use merely arguing the matter in court. The AIADMK was in office then, but it took no follow-up action.

Following my announcement of 22 November 2006, on 6 December 2006 I myself moved in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly the Government resolution to make Tamil a language of proceedings in the High Court. The resolution stated:

> “English has so far been the language of all proceedings in the Madras High Court. Article 348(1) of the Constitution of India provides that all proceedings in the High Courts and the Supreme Court shall be conducted in English.
>
> “However, Article 7 of the Official Languages Act, 1963, read with Article 348(2) of the Constitution, enables a State Government, with the consent of the Honourable President of India, to take appropriate action for all proceedings in the High Court to be conducted in the official language of the State.
>
> “On the basis of the policy that all administrative proceedings in our State must be conducted in Tamil, the Tamil Nadu Government has decided, under Article 7 of the Official Languages Act, 1963, read with Article 348(2) of the Constitution, to introduce Tamil, the official language of our State, in all proceedings of the Madras High Court, including judgments and decrees.
>
> “This Legislative Assembly unanimously resolves to request the Honourable President of India to grant early consent for implementing this decision, which deserves to be inscribed in letters of gold in the history of Tamil Nadu.”

The resolution on “Tamil as a language of proceedings in the High Court”, unanimously adopted by the Legislative Assembly under the DMK Government on 6 December 2006, was accompanied by the recommendation of the Governor of Tamil Nadu. I personally met President A. P. J. Abdul Kalam at his residence, handed the resolution to him and requested permission to implement it in the Madras High Court. The President assured me that he would consider it.

But in a letter dated 27 February 2007, the relevant division of the Union Ministry of Law and Justice said that the matter had been considered in consultation with the Supreme Court, and that the Chief Justice of India was of the view that it would not at that time be appropriate to introduce a regional language in High Court orders, judgments and other proceedings.

On 11 March 2007, I wrote detailed letters on the matter to Prime Minister Manmohan Singh, Home Minister Shivraj Patil and Law Minister H. R. Bhardwaj. I pointed to the precedents of Hindi, the regional language, being used in the High Courts of Rajasthan, Bihar, Uttar Pradesh and Madhya Pradesh. I finally asked for urgent action to give effect to the long-standing desire of the people of Tamil Nadu and to secure the President's consent without delay.

The very next day, 12 March, Union Law Minister H. R. Bhardwaj replied:

> “I have carefully read your letter. Keeping in mind the points raised by you, I have asked my Department to reconsider the matter afresh and to obtain the consent of the President of India at the earliest.”

On 12 March 2007, Union Ministers P. Chidambaram, T. R. Baalu, A. Raja, S. Regupathy, K. Venkatapathy and Subbulakshmi Jagadeesan, together with Members of Parliament from Tamil Nadu, signed a petition, met the Prime Minister and submitted it.

As a continuation of the steps taken by the DMK Government, the World Classical Tamil Conference held in Coimbatore in June 2010 adopted this resolution:

> “In order that Tamil be recognised as a language of use in the Madras High Court, the Tamil Nadu Government in 2006 unanimously adopted a resolution in the Legislative Assembly, obtained the recommendations of the Honourable Governor of Tamil Nadu and the Honourable Chief Justice of the Madras High Court, and forwarded it to the Union Government. This Conference therefore urges the Union Government to recognise Tamil as a language of use in the Madras High Court immediately and without further delay.”

At the time this resolution was adopted, former Minister and lawyer Madurai Pon. Muthuramalingam argued an entire case in Tamil in the Madras High Court. *The Hindu* reported it in detail under the headline, “Judges Impressed: First case argued entirely in Tamil”:

> “Pon. Muthuramalingam, a former Minister and lawyer, argued the criminal appeal, filed by two convicts in a 2007 murder case, before a Division Bench comprising Justice M. Chockalingam and Justice M. Duraiswamy. The Judges recorded their appreciation of the counsel's effort.”

Even after all this, because the Supreme Court had raised an objection, the Union Government could not then grant approval to the Tamil Nadu Government's resolution. Nevertheless, because a DMK Government was in office in Tamil Nadu at the time, the hearings in a large number of cases in the Madras High Court took place in Tamil. M. Y. Eqbal, who was Chief Justice in 2010 during the DMK Government's tenure, also issued an order permitting arguments in Tamil in the High Court.

Now, however, a High Court judge has denied a lawyer permission to argue in Tamil and has also stated that the Constitution does not permit arguments in Tamil. Lawyers have therefore launched a protest demanding that Tamil be declared a language of proceedings in the High Court.

The Union and State Governments must understand that their protest is entirely just. In particular, the Union Government must intervene. Just as Hindi is already permitted in practice as a language of proceedings in certain northern High Courts, it must obtain the Supreme Court's approval and secure the President's consent for the proposal pending since the period of the DMK Government, so that Tamil may lawfully be used as a language of proceedings in the High Court in Tamil Nadu.

I urge this again and again.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**15 July 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. The Tamil source describes Fakkeer Mydeen as having gone to Dubai for work while the case is introduced as concerning a husband stranded in Saudi Arabia. The translation preserves both source statements without silently reconciling them.
2. **Language of proceedings** is used for `வழக்கு மொழி` / `வழக்காடு மொழி`, meaning the language legally permitted for arguments and court business.
3. Article 348 and the Official Languages Act are translated as cited and framed in Kalaignar's letter; the Tamil source remains authoritative for the historical argument.
4. The intentional English newspaper headline and quotation from *The Hindu* are retained.

3ஆம் தேதியன்று ஒரு வழக்கறிஞரிடம் தமிழில் வாதம் செய்ய
அனுமதி மறுத்ததோடு; தமிழில் வாதம் செய்ய அரசியல் சட்டத்தில்
அனுமதி வழங்கப்படவில்லை என்றும் கருத்துக்கூறியுள்ளார்.
இதனை எதிர்த்துத்தான் உயர்நீதிமன்றத்தில் வழக்காடு மொழியாக

தமிழை அறிவிக்கக்கோரி, வழக்கறிஞர்கள் போராட்டத்தில்
ஈடுபட்டார்கள். அவர்களின் போராட்டம் மிகவும் நியாயமான
போராட்டம் என்பதை மத்திய, மாநில அரசுகள் உணர்ந்துகொள்ள
வேண்டும். குறிப்பாக மத்திய அரசு இந்தப் பிரச்சினையிலே
தலையிட்டு, வேறு சில வட இந்திய மாநிலங்களில்
தற்போது நடைமுறையிலே இந்தி வழக்காடு மொழியாக
அனுமதிக்கப்பட்டுள்ளதைப் போலவே, தமிழகத்திலே உள்ள
உயர் நீதிமன்றத்திலும் வழக்காடும் மொழியாகத் தமிழை சட்டப்படி
கையாளுவதற்கு, உச்ச நீதிமன்றத்தின் ஒப்புதலைப் பெற்று,
குடியரசுத் தலைவரிடம் தி.மு.கழக ஆட்சிக் காலத்திலிருந்து
ஒப்புதலுக்காக நிலுவையிலே உள்ள சட்டத் திருத்தத்திற்கு
அனுமதி பெற்றுத் தர வேண்டும் என்று மீண்டும் மீண்டும்
வலியுறுத்திக் கேட்டுக்கொள்கிறேன்.
