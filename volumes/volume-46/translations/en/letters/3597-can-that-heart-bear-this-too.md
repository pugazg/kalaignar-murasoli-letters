# 3597. Can That Heart Bear This Too?

**Udanpirappē,**

Those who dedicated themselves to language and country and gained an undying place in history; learned Tamil savants; warriors of principle; mountains of character whom the world honoured; scholars and artists praised by the whole world; and venerable elders who achieved great things in public service—to keep their rare names alive in the hearts of society like lamps of light is one of the fundamental duties of a government that honours culture. That is why, whenever the DMK government blossomed in Tamil Nadu, that culture was honoured and preserved, as everyone knows.

Though it was the policy of the M.G.R. government that no government building should be named after a living person, during his own period in office the Medical University was named after him. But by the time its inaugural function was held, M.G.R. had passed away. After that, neither during Janaki Ammal's government nor during Governor's rule was even a proper site allotted to that university. It functioned like an orphan in a small rented house at Taramani.

But after I assumed office as Chief Minister, at a function organised by Kilpauk Government Medical College at the President Hotel in Chennai on 24 June 1989, all those who spoke before me kept referring merely to “the Medical University,” and not to “the M.G.R. Medical University.” Pointing this out, I announced in my speech: “Very soon, Dr. M.G.R. Medical University will take shape in fine, lofty, mansion-like buildings, worthy of having its name engraved in letters of gold.”

As I had announced, the inauguration of Tamil Nadu Dr. M.G.R. Medical University was held on 31 August 1989 at the University of Madras Centenary Auditorium. The function was presided over by Mr. Alexander, then Governor of Tamil Nadu and recently deceased. Mr. R. Venkataraman, then President of India, inaugurated the University and addressed the gathering. When I spoke, I began my address by saying:

> “My dear friend, the late Chief Minister Dr. M.G.R., who stood shoulder to shoulder with me for forty years in both the fields of art and politics, has had this University begun in his name—but until now its formal inauguration had not taken place, and it is taking place today.
>
> “In accordance with Valluvar's Kural, ‘Friendship is not the smile of the face; friendship is the smile that blossoms within the heart,’ engraving the meaning of friendship upon our hearts, today the inauguration of Dr. M.G.R. Medical University is being held on behalf of the DMK government of Tamil Nadu.”

I myself even drew the design for the University's building. But because the DMK government was dismissed the very next year after the foundation-stone function for M.G.R. University, perhaps because the foundation stone had been laid during my government, the work did not move quickly under the Jayalalithaa government that followed. After I returned to office for the fourth time in 1996, I accelerated the construction work, and on 31 July 1998 invited then Vice-President Mr. Krishan Kant to inaugurate the M.G.R. University buildings constructed at a cost of ₹6 crore. I unveiled the statue of M.G.R. installed at the building frontage and spoke there.

Further, honouring the memory of my dear friend M.G.R., I gladly accepted the long-standing request of the film fraternity and named the Tamil Nadu government's Film City after him.

With this same civilised and generous spirit, I had the long road then known simply as the “Beach Road” named “Kamarajar Salai” after Perunthalaivar Kamarajar. Going further back, nobody can forget that during the early period when the DMK held the office of Mayor of Chennai, a statue of Kamarajar was unveiled in the city. I announced that Kamarajar's birthday should be celebrated across Tamil Nadu in every educational institution as “Education Development Day,” and, precisely so that governments coming later could not change it, I enacted it into law.

To remove caste and religious divisions and enable people of all castes to live harmoniously in one place, the Samathuvapuram scheme implemented under the DMK government was named the “Periyar Memorial Samathuvapuram Scheme,” and I had a statue of Periyar installed in each Samathuvapuram. We also implemented countless measures in the DMK government in honour of Periyar's memory, including property rights for women and the scheme enabling people of all castes to become temple priests.

In the name of Omandur Ramasamy Reddiar, who assumed office as Chief Minister in Tamil Nadu after Independence and was hailed as the “beardless Periyar,” I named the Government Estate in Chennai “Omandurar Estate,” had a memorial built for him at Omandur, and named the Chennai road where ministers reside “Omandurar Salai.”

I named the ten-storey building constructed during the DMK government near Fort St. George after national poet Namakkal Kavignar and had it called “Namakkal Kavignar Maaligai.” At its summit I had illuminated the stirring lines written by him: “Say you are a Tamil; stand with your head held high.”

To create a span from Kottam to Kumari, I built “Valluvar Kottam” in Chennai and a beautiful 133-foot statue of Valluvar at Cape Kumari; at Poompuhar, I built the Silappathikaram Art Gallery with its seven-storeyed structure, doing what I could to display the excellence of sculpture, one of the arts that grew together with Tamil Nadu's history.

At Panchalankurichi I built a fort so that the memories of Veerapandiya Kattabomman, Oomaithurai and others who fought the foreigner would endure; and at Governagiri I created a beautiful settlement in the name of warrior Sundaralingam.

Among the memorials created were:

- Ambedkar Mani Mandapam in Chennai;
- Kamarajar Mani Mandapam in Kumari;
- the memorial for statesman Rajaji;
- martyr Viswanathadas Memorial House at Thirumangalam;
- Poolithevan Memorial Mandapam at Nerkattumseval;
- Jeeva Mani Mandapam for the communist fighter at Nagercoil;
- People's Poet Kalyanasundaram Mani Mandapam at Pattukkottai;
- Rationalist poet Narayanakavi Mani Mandapam at Udumalai;
- the Information Technology Highway in the name of young leader Rajiv Gandhi;
- elder Bhaktavatsalam Memorial House in Chennai;
- scholar Caldwell Memorial at Idaiyangudi;
- colleges and a grand statue in the name of Pasumpon Thevar;
- Thavathiru Kundrakudi Adigalar Memorial Mandapam at Kundrakudi;
- Maruthu Pandiyar memorial at Tiruppattur;
- Thillaiyadi Valliammai Memorial House;
- Bharathiyar Mani Mandapam at Ettayapuram;
- memorial halls for the language-war guardians, martyrs, and the honourable Quaid-e-Millath in the Gandhi Mandapam complex in Chennai;
- poet Ramalinganar Memorial House at Namakkal;
- martyr Kakkan Memorial Mandapam at Thumbaipatti in Madurai district;
- Vallalar Ramalinga Adigalar Memorial House at Maruthur;
- and statues honouring the memories of countless others such as Netaji, thinker-sculptor Singaravelar, Dheeran Chinnamalai, martyr Sankaralinganar, martyr Arya alias Bhashyam, Pudukkottai ruler Rajagopala Tondaiman, Ramasamy Padaiyatchiar and Nadigar Thilagam Sivaji Ganesan.

Bridges too were named after great figures such as Perarignar Anna, Maraimalai Adigalar, Thiru. Vi. Ka., Kalaivanar and Moopanar.

All these are memorials established during DMK rule!

None of these was done to elevate my fame—or to advertise the DMK government. They were created so that those who laboured and stood by Tamil Nadu's history, civilisation, art and culture—rich in courage and compassion—might be remembered with hearts fragrant with gratitude by people in this age and in every age, so that these things might flourish and never die.

Among those who spread all these feelings to me and to self-respecting Tamil people wherever they live, who laboured without fatigue to protect the Tamil people and the Tamil language, who possessed a sacred heart softer than a flower petal, who shaped people like me, who stood in Periyar's path and rose like a full moon to drive away the darkness of superstition, was Perarignar Anna. With an unquenchable thirst that Anna's memory should live forever in Tamil hearts, I created so many memorials in his name.

When Mr. V. P. Singh, then Prime Minister and a guardian of social justice, came to participate in an event in February 1990, I had the Chennai Meenambakkam domestic airport named after Perunthalaivar Kamarajar and the international airport named after Perarignar Anna, right there on the function stage.

Yet, as though placing a crown upon all those memorials, I built in Chennai, at a cost of ₹172 crore, the Anna Centenary Memorial Library—one of the foremost in Asia—as a celebrated field of knowledge honouring his memory.

Seeing and hearing how people coming here from many parts of the world marvelled at and praised it, and how that library stood not merely as a beautiful edifice but as a centre in which the knowledge of our dear Dravidian people might grow, gave me immense joy.

When Perarignar Anna lay on his deathbed at the Adyar Cancer Institute and doctors called him for surgery, he said that a few pages remained in the book he was reading and that he would come after finishing them. Having personally known the depth of his interest in reading books, I wished that, as the memorial marking the completion of his centenary, nothing could be more fitting than a library.

A few days after the library opened, when I went there to see it, about one hundred students who were studying there surrounded me, expressed the overflowing happiness in their hearts and thanked me. But if today's Chief Minister has announced that, out of animosity, she will convert that library into a hospital, one can understand the extent to which hostility has taken residence in her.

In the six months since she assumed office, she has continuously held closing ceremonies for many schemes begun under the DMK government, including:

- the Samacheer Kalvi scheme;
- the new Secretariat building;
- the Pavendar Classical Tamil Research Library;
- abolition of the Anna Universities;
- freezing of Tolkappiya Poonga;
- cancellation of the Chennai Suburban Police Commissionerate;
- concealment of the name board of Semmozhi Poonga;

and in this manner she is destroying one Tamil cultural symbol after another.

Anna said that one must have a heart capable of bearing anything. But—Anna! Can that heart bear this too?

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**4 November 2011**

அதனைப் படித்து முடித்து விட்டு வருவதாகவும் சொன்னதை அறிந்து,
புத்தகங்கள் படிப்பதில் அவருக்கிருந்த ஆர்வத்தைப் பற்றி நன்கு
அறிந்திருந்த காரணத்தால், அவரது நூற்றாண்டு நிறைவைக் குறிக்கின்ற
நினைவுச் சின்னமாக ஒரு நூலகத்தை விடச் சிறப்பானது வேறு எதுவும்
இருக்க முடியாது என்று விரும்பினேன்.

அந்த நூலகத்தைத் தொடங்கிய சில நாட்களுக்குப் பிறகு நான்
அங்கே சென்று பார்த்தபோது, அங்கே படித்துக் கொண்டிருந்த சுமார்
நூறு மாணவர்கள் என்னைச் சுற்றி வளைத்துக் கொண்டு, தங்கள்
உள்ளத்தில் பொங்கிய மகிழ்ச்சியைத் தெரிவித்து நன்றி கூறினார்கள்.
ஆனால் இன்றைய முதல் அமைச்சர் அந்த நூலகத்தையே ஒரு மருத்துவ
மனையாக மாற்றுவேன் என்று காழ்ப்புணர்ச்சியோடு அறிவித்திருக்கிறார்
என்றால், அவரது உள்ளத்தில் எந்த அளவுக்கு பகைமை குடி கொண்டுள்ளது
என்பதைப் புரிந்து கொள்ளலாம்.

அவர் ஆட்சிப் பொறுப்பேற்று ஆறு மாதங்களில் தொடர்ந்து கழக
ஆட்சியில் தொடங்கப்பட்ட பல திட்டங்களுக்கு மூடு விழா நடத்திக்
கொண்டிருக்கிறார். சமச்சீர் கல்வித் திட்டம்; புதிய தலைமைச்
செயலகக் கட்டிடம்; பாவேந்தர் செம்மொழி ஆய்வு நூலகம்; அண்ணா
பல்கலைக்கழகங்களை ரத்து செய்தது; தொல்காப்பியப் பூங்காவை
முடக்கியது; சென்னை புறநகர் காவல் ஆணையரகத்தை ரத்து செய்தது;
செம்மொழிப் பூங்காவின் பெயர்ப் பலகையை மறைத்தது; இவ்வாறு
தமிழ் கலாச்சாரச் சின்னங்கள் ஒவ்வொன்றாகச் சிதைக்கப்படுகின்றன.
