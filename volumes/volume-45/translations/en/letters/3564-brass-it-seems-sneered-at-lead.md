# 3564. “Brass, It Seems, Sneered at Lead!”

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 3 June 2011

**Udanpirappē,**

When, in earlier years, the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam implemented many free schemes, some opposition parties and some people who ran newspapers showered arrows of condemnation upon me, saying that I was ruining people's very lives and making them lazy instead of allowing them to progress on their own. Yet now, when Jayalalithaa under the AIADMK government begins a scheme to provide **twenty kilograms of rice free**, they praise and applaud it.

At the end of last March, before the election, when **CNN-IBN** interviewed me while I was Chief Minister, they asked a question about the free schemes announced in the election manifesto:

> “You announce many free schemes in the election manifesto. In the 2006 election manifesto you gave free colour television sets. Now you have announced in the manifesto that laptops, grinders and other things will be given free. What is your view of the claim that the promise of free concessions is what determines victory or defeat in an election?”

I replied:

> “Free concessions are not for obtaining votes. There are very many poor and ordinary people in Tamil Nadu. The aim of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam government is to enable them to live. That is not something that began yesterday—the movement itself was founded to make those poor people live. **‘Let us see God in the smile of the poor’** was the slogan of our leader Anna. These free schemes are among the efforts we have undertaken precisely to see that smile—to see that smile on the faces of the poor.”

In an editorial published on **25-3-2011** under the title **“Lead, It Seems, Was Sneered At.....”**, **Dinamani** wrote:

> “Suppose one roadside vendor spreads out his wares and shouts, ‘Buy two, get one free,’ trying to attract consumers, and another vendor selling the same goods opposite him shouts, ‘Buy one, get one free.’ What would that be like? That is exactly what the election manifesto issued by All India Anna Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam general secretary Jayalalithaa looks like! If one were to describe the AIADMK election manifesto, it could be finished in a single line: Jayalalithaa has said, ‘I will shower more freebies than Chief Minister Karunanidhi has promised in the DMK manifesto.’
>
> “When the DMK manifesto in the 2006 Tamil Nadu Assembly election spoke of free television sets and cooking gas, AIADMK general secretary Jayalalithaa completely opposed such freebies. But she had to face defeat in that election.”

Dinamani had thus stated categorically that Jayalalithaa herself had opposed freebies. Yet that same Jayalalithaa, almost immediately after coming to power, has participated in the inauguration of the free-rice scheme.

In another article in Dinamani it was written that the DMK government borrowed excessively in order to implement free schemes. While I was Chief Minister, I repeatedly explained the borrowings of the Tamil Nadu government. In the Assembly too, Finance Minister Perasiriyar clearly answered the matter while replying to the Budget debate. Even so, Dinamani published an attack intended to find fault with the DMK government. But the people of Tamil Nadu are watching how the same newspaper now keeps its mouth closed about the free schemes announced by the AIADMK government.

No government has a goal of somehow borrowing merely for the sake of borrowing. The truth is that, in the financial circumstances that existed while the DMK was in office, if the government was to devise many good schemes for the people, it had to borrow to carry out those schemes. I had also written in detail and with explanation that the Tamil Nadu government did **not** borrow for free schemes; it borrowed only for capital expenditure such as construction works, and I had set out which schemes the loans were for, how much was borrowed, and from whom. Newspapers such as Dinamani attacked the DMK government in sweeping terms without even reading those explanations.

It would also be good for that writer to understand that borrowing under the DMK government remained within the limits prescribed by the Union Government, and that the loans were taken only for essential works.

From the moment Jayalalithaa assumed office, she repeatedly said that the DMK government had left **one lakh crore** in debt and that it had to be put right. I assume that those responsible for the Finance Department would have given her the explanation.

Every government concerned about public welfare has a responsibility to act as a protective fortress for poor and ordinary people, the destitute and the elderly by implementing social-security schemes. Even developed European countries implement free education, old-age pensions, assistance to the unemployed, food coupons and national health-security schemes. In some countries, assistance is given directly in cash to the poor.

It is on this basis that the DMK government in Tamil Nadu implemented innumerable social-security schemes: one kilogram of rice for one rupee for food security; free dhotis and sarees for people below the poverty line; free electricity for farmers who increase food production; pensions for the elderly and destitute; marriage assistance for poor women; insurance for life-saving advanced treatment for the poor; concrete houses for people living in huts; and free tuition fees from school and college education up to professional education.

The AIADMK government has now come forward to implement, with some expansion, a few schemes that the DMK government had already been implementing. In particular, when the DMK government came to power in 2006, it announced and began supplying one kilogram of rice for **₹2**, and after a few months expanded the same scheme further by supplying one kilogram for **₹1**. Under that scheme, a family received **20 kilograms a month for ₹20**. Jayalalithaa's government has now announced that the same 20 kilograms will be given free, without collecting the ₹20.

When, during the DMK government, we discussed with officials whether those same 20 kilograms could be given free, the officials said: **“When all the opposition parties are already condemning even the supply of rice at one rupee per kilogram, it is not good to give the rice entirely free.”** Those officials with a conscience will not have forgotten it.

The marriage-assistance scheme for women from poor and ordinary families was also a DMK government scheme. The AIADMK government has now announced a higher amount under that same scheme.

In addition, schemes intended to improve the standard of living of the poor—such as the free cooking-gas scheme and the colour television distribution scheme—had been implemented by the DMK government. Those who condemned all these as “free schemes” that would obstruct the country's economic growth have now come forward to implement such schemes themselves. Those who receive these free benefits may at least think: somehow, fortunately, the achievements begun by the DMK government have not been stopped and are continuing.

One cannot assume that a country's economic growth will benefit every section of society from the very beginning. It is only by providing assistance through various welfare schemes that the gap in living standards between the poor and others is reduced and the poor are given an opportunity for economic advancement.

For example, because the DMK government extended free education for first-generation students up to professional education, the number of students entering colleges and polytechnics rose from **2.85 lakh in 2005-2006** to **6.9 lakh in 2010-2011**. Because the DMK government spent **₹4,000 crore** on one-rupee rice and subsidised pulses, oil and other commodities, poor families were protected from the impact of price rises, food security became possible, and Tamil Nadu under the DMK government reached a condition in which there were no starvation deaths.

Likewise, in the past five years under the DMK government, **₹922 crore** was provided through the poor-women's marriage-assistance scheme and **₹4,251 crore** through various pension schemes, among many other forms of assistance that reached poor people. Will the AIADMK government continue with the same speed the insurance scheme under which about **2 lakh 62 thousand poor people** received free advanced treatment, the **108 ambulance scheme** that has saved many lives, and similar schemes? Or will it stop those too—as with the Secretariat, the Classical Tamil centre and the Samacheer Kalvi scheme? That is something we will know only from now on.

If a government ignores such social-security schemes and implements only growth schemes, it will be a government that ignores the welfare of the poor and ordinary people.

The argument that the government's debt burden rose because these schemes were implemented is also not correct. It is nothing new for a government to borrow for both development schemes and social-security schemes. As has already been explained many times, countries such as the United States and European countries also borrow to implement schemes. Other States in India too borrow to implement development and welfare-protection schemes. It has been clarified many times that Tamil Nadu's debt responsibility is very low compared with that of other States.

Under the limit fixed by the Union Government, a State's debt responsibility may be within **25 per cent** of the value of that State's production. Tamil Nadu's debt responsibility is only **19.58 per cent**.

Further, although the government had continuously implemented various social-security schemes from **2006-2007**, until **2008-2009** the State's revenue receipts were higher than its revenue expenditure. The reason a revenue deficit arose in Tamil Nadu from **2009-2010** was the decline in tax revenue and the revised pay scales given to government employees. In the coming years, Tamil Nadu's finances would again become strong enough to reach a revenue surplus. Therefore, the false propaganda that Tamil Nadu's debt burden rose only because of free schemes is an attempt to obstruct the government from providing such welfare assistance to poor people.

In an editorial, Dinamani criticised the financial position under the DMK government with the phrase **“Surplus on one side, debt on the other.”** The Tamil Nadu government's debt liabilities were clearly stated in page 55, paragraph 4 of the interim financial statement. On **31.3.2006**, during the AIADMK government, the State's total debt liability was **₹57,457 crore**, equal to **22.29 per cent** of the then Gross State Domestic Product. Although total debt liability rose to **₹1,01,541 crore in 2010-11**, it was only **19.58 per cent** of Gross State Domestic Product.

The Union Finance Commission and the Union Government permitted every State to maintain debt liabilities up to **25 per cent** of its gross product. Tamil Nadu's debt liability was therefore within the level permitted by the Union Government.

None of the loans obtained by the Tamil Nadu government was spent extravagantly. All of them were spent on capital works. From **2006-07 through 2010-11**, the State government's borrowing totalled **₹44,083 crore**. During the same period, the DMK government spent **₹44,666 crore** on capital works. The claim that the DMK government borrowed money to implement free schemes is wrong.

During the AIADMK period, from **2001-02 to 2005-06**, total borrowing was **₹28,721 crore**, while the amount spent by the AIADMK government on capital works during the same period was only **₹15,613 crore**.

The DMK government borrowed from the World Bank for capital works such as a **₹2,442 crore road-development project** and a **₹1,224 crore health project**. With Japanese financial assistance, it took up the **Hogenakkal combined water-supply scheme** at **₹1,928 crore**; and through NABARD it borrowed **₹4,113 crore** over the past five years for rural roads, development of irrigation facilities and infrastructure. Thus everyone can understand that the DMK government borrowed responsibly and spent the money on capital works.

Speaking in Chennai on **9.4.2011**, Union Finance Minister **Pranab Mukherjee** said:

> “The Thirteenth Finance Commission, in its recently submitted report, has recommended that no State should borrow more than **3 to 3.5 per cent** of its Gross State Domestic Product in 2011-12. The Tamil Nadu government's borrowing level is much lower than the recommended level.
>
> “Tamil Nadu is among the few States in India that satisfactorily maintain fiscal discipline and stability.
>
> “As of **24 March ‘011’** [source as printed], the Tamil Nadu government's cash balance was **₹13,537 crore**. The Tamil Nadu government has not had to obtain an overdraft from the Reserve Bank of India even for a single day because of having no money in its account. Many States, however, have taken overdrafts.”

If one takes note of this statement too, one can understand both that the DMK government did not borrow beyond proper limits and that it did not borrow money in order to implement all its free schemes.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**3-6-2011**
