# 3553. “If One Looks for Faults, There Will Be No Circle of Kin!”

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3553](../../../chapters/3553-kutram-paarkin-sutram-illai.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 145](../../../pages/page-145.md)–[PDF 149](../../../pages/page-149.md)  
**Date:** 5 April 2011

**Udanpirappē,**

Giving rest itself a rest, travelling alternately through harsh heat and cruel cold, I have been going from place to place to introduce the candidates of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam, Congress, Pattali Makkal Katchi, Viduthalai Chiruthaigal, Indian Union Muslim League, Kongunadu Munnetra Kazhagam, Moovendar Munnetra Kazhagam and Perunthalaivar Makkal Katchi who have been fielded to hold aloft the victory banner of our alliance; to remind people of the social work those candidates have been doing and enable that work to continue; and to ask the activists of the alliance to work without pause as spearheads for the victory of our Democratic Progressive Alliance, whose fundamental strength lies in these candidates.

At this moment, when I call upon all of you, my Udanpirappus—“Come! Come! Accept their request and mine, and come to raise the flag of victory!”—I ought to feel the weariness of my tour falling away and my energy growing still more so that I can join you and work with even greater vigour. Instead, here and there, troubling events occurring among you, my Udanpirappus, in the field are causing me some hesitation in my journey. For the present welfare and future growth of the DMK, I want to speak about this openly.

I write because I believe that saying it openly will itself be a precious remedy even for my small anxiety.

In any movement, differences of opinion—and the bitterness that arises from them—naturally appear like clusters of foam among waves. But because of them the sea-waves do not stop, nor does the sea itself dry up completely.

The sea—  
the wave—  
the foam—

these are permanent. And because of that, no one comes to hate the sea, the wave or the foam; no one casts them aside saying they are unwanted.

Thinking and acting with this in mind is the affectionate call I make to the activists of every party in this alliance.

I hear of things—  
why, I have seen them directly—  
without anyone telling me, I have sensed them too—

This tour has helped me see tens of thousands of voters and the incomparable workers of the movements. Yet, in the glittering silk cloth, here and there, the little snags of resentment that can catch and spoil it have not failed to trouble my eyes and my thoughts.

If a crooked thought arises in the heart—“If this person is made to win, what profit is there for us? What profit do we get by working for that victory?”—will I ever obtain the silk cloth I wish to weave by joining all of you together? Will I be able to fashion it properly? That anxiety has been troubling me.

Is not the broad feeling that whoever wins within this alliance brings advancement and victory to our country itself the truly great and honourable feeling? Eleven people won the World Cup and held the trophy in joy and celebration. Yet all Indians in the world—why, Tamils in particular—are celebrating with delight. Do they dance with such joy only after asking what share of that victory personally came to them, or after calculating it?

No—certainly not! It is a feeling, a feeling greater than life itself. Only those eleven could literally win the victory achieved by those eleven. Could the tens of thousands who watched and enjoyed it personally receive the trophy? Yet does not each person rejoice as though the victory were his or her own?

In the same way, in 234 constituencies, only 234 people can be the winners. Anyone who thinks, “We are not in that group, so what does it matter who wins?”—who sulks or fumes in that way—would surely be regarded as someone who does not understand the greatness of devotion to the country.

In a sporting field, victory is built when people run from their respective positions, help one another, display enthusiasm and encourage each other, lifting up the total form of victory together. In exactly the same way, in an election field where democracy is being protected, all who stand in one formation must cooperate, and the competition should be over **“I will get there first—you get there first”** in helping the alliance wear the garland of victory. If a ball struck hard and flying high is left uncaught by the fielder because he thinks the glory will belong only to the person who bowled it, would that not become an affliction for the whole team?

At this moment when the World Cup cricket victory has been won, I use this example because I believe it will settle deeply in the minds of many Udanpirappus.

I too can sense that here and there my Udanpirappus, who are dearer to me than life, have recognised both the pulse of truth hidden in this and the performance of falsehood that rubs against it as it passes. Though my heart is hurt a little by that, I stand upright like a mountain.

The reason for this strength is what I saw while travelling through so many places: my Udanpirappus, their own elder and younger brothers, elder and younger sisters, relatives and kin, and the mothers and fathers who gave birth to them, standing all along the route and singing blessings. They looked at me and said, **“You who gave rice to remove the hunger of the poor, you who came in the path of Vallalar of Vadalur, who withered whenever he saw a withered crop!”** They poured out brave words: **“Go with confidence—victory is ours.”** How could I forget that?

I have a body that is not well; I do not have the strength for this travel. Yet I run and run to come and see you and my Tamil people. Why?

Because they are people who do not forget gratitude—and I am one who does not forget to continue doing good for them.

Wherever I am made to sit, I will seek out Anna's words, **“Let us see God in the smile of the poor,”** and carve those words in colour along the edge of the sky, until a lasting path to life is opened for the helpless and the poor dance with joy before every watching eye. Whether we live or fall, we shall struggle until then. I press that vow into my heart, stand upright and call to you with affection: **Come, my precious ones—come and fight!**

Let us turn the proverb **“If a fox is given authority over the country, it will demand eight goats from the herd”** into a warning for the voters, and the proverb **“Those who endure will rule the earth”** into a weapon of struggle for our alliance. In this democratic righteous battle now taking place, let us march to victory with the slogan **“A strong homeland—a prosperous Tamil Nadu.”** Come! Come!

You have surely read Anna's treasure of thought: **“If one looks for faults, there will be no circle of kin.”** Understand that it applies to my letter today as well—and rise and come!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**5-4-2011**

## Letter-specific notes

1. The sea–wave–foam and silk-weaving metaphors are preserved as the source develops them rather than being reduced to a summary about alliance unity.
2. The World Cup cricket comparison is retained in full, including the source's fielding analogy and the 234-constituency comparison.
3. The closing proverbs and Anna quotation are translated as source rhetoric; no outside proverb version is substituted.

பிணியாகிவிடும் அல்லவா?

உலகக் கோப்பை கிரிக்கெட் வெற்றியைப் பெற்றுள்ள இந்த
நேரத்தில் உடன்பிறப்புகள் பலருக்கும் இது உள்ளத்தில் பதியும்
என்பதற்காக இதை உதாரணமாகக் குறிப்பிடுகிறேன்.

இதில் ஒளிந்து கிடக்கும் உண்மையின் துடிப்பையும் -
உரசிக் கொண்டு செல்லும் பொய்மையின் நடிப்பையும் என
உயிரினும் மேலான உடன்பிறப்புகள் அனைவரும் இங்கொன்றும்
அங்கொன்றுமாக உணர்ந்திருப்பதை நானும் உணருகிறேன்.
அதனால் மனம் சற்று உடையினும், மலை போல் நிமிர்ந்து
நிற்கிறேன்.

இந்த வலிமைக்குக் காரணம் ஊர் பல சுற்றி உலா வந்தபோது
என் உடன்பிறப்புகளும் அவர்தம் உடன் பிறந்த அண்ணன்
தம்பிகளும் - அக்காள் தங்கைகளும் - உற்றார் உறவினரும்
- அவர்கள் அனைவரையும் பெற்றெடுத்த தாய்மார்களும்,
தந்தையரும் - வழி நெடுக வாழ்த்திப்பாடி - என்னைப் பார்த்து
“ஏழைகளின் பசி போக்க அரிசி கொடுத்து, வாடிய பயிரைக் கண்ட
போதெல்லாம் வாடிய வடலூர் வள்ளலாரின் வழி வந்தவனே!”
என்று வாழ்த்தியதும் - “நம்பிக்கையாகப் போ - வெற்றிதான்
நமக்கு” என வீரமொழி பொழிந்ததையும்தான் நான் மறக்க
இயலுமா?

எனக்கோ நலமில்லாத உடல் - பலமும் இல்லை பயணம்
செய்வதற்கு - இருந்தாலும் உன்னையும் என் தமிழ் மக்களையும்
கண்டிட ஓடோடி வருகிறேனே, ஏன்?

அவர்கள் எல்லாம் நன்றி மறவாதவர்கள் - நானும்
அவர்களுக்கு தொடர்ந்து நன்மை செய்ய மறவாதவன்.

என்னை எங்கே உட்கார வைத்தாலும் - “ஏழையின் சிரிப்பில்
இறைவனைக் காண்போம்” என்ற அண்ணாவின் வரிகளைத் தேடிக்
கண்டு பிடித்து - திக்கற்றோர் வாழ்ந்திட - அந்த வரிகளை வான
விளிம்பில் வண்ணமாய்ச் செதுக்கி - காணும் விழிகளில் எல்லாம்
ஏழைபாழைகள் களி நடனம் புரிந்திட, வாழும் வழி நிலையாகத்
திறக்கப்படும் வரை - நாம் வாழினும், வீழினும் போரிடுவோம் -
என்ற சூளுரையை நெஞ்சில் பதித்து நிமிர்ந்து நின்று போராட
வா என் கண்மணியே! என்று வாஞ்சையுள்ள உடன்பிறப்புகளே
உங்களையெல்லாம் அழைக்கின்றேன்.

“நரிக்கு நாட்டாண்மை கொடுத்தால், அது கிடைக்கு
எட்டு ஆடு கேட்கும்” என்ற பழமொழியை வாக்காளப்
பெருமக்களுக்கு எச்சரிக்கையாக்கி; - “பொறுத்தார் பூமியாள்வார்”
என்ற பழமொழியை நமது அணியினருக்குப் போர்க் கருவியாக

வழங்கி - நடக்கின்ற இந்த ஜனநாயக அறப்போரில் - வலிவான
தாயகம் - வளமான தமிழகம் என்ற முழக்கத்தோடு வெற்றி நடை
போடுவோம், வா! வா! “குற்றம் பார்க்கின் சுற்றம் இல்லை” என்ற
அண்ணாவின் கருத்துப் புதையலைப் படித்திருப்பாயே; அது
இன்றைய என் கடிதத்திற்கும் பொருந்தும் என்று உணர்ந்து
எழுந்து வா!
