# 3478. Corruption! Is Your Name Jayalalithaa?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This letter answers an allegation concerning new liquor factories and TASMAC orders by reconstructing the author’s account of Golden Distilleries/Midas Distilleries under the earlier AIADMK Government. All licensing claims, company relationships, production caps, sales figures and accusations are preserved as source-attributed assertions. The printed G.O. references and numerical series are retained without outside adjudication.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3478](../../../chapters/3478-oozhale-un-peyarthaan-jayalalithaavaa.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 364](../../../pages/page-364.md)–[PDF 370](../../../pages/page-370.md)  
**Date:** 4 July 2010

**Udanpirappē,**

The Leader of the Opposition, who may miss a day but never misses issuing a statement, appears to have planned to run her party by holding an agitation in one town every day. In yesterday’s statement she made what the author calls a baseless accusation: reports were supposedly coming in that Deputy Chief Minister Stalin and Rajya Sabha member Kanimozhi had raised new liquor factories at lightning speed and that TASMAC orders were being given to these companies in large quantities.

There is a proverb: “One who steals herself does not trust others.” If new liquor factories exist in the names of Stalin and Kanimozhi, where are those factories? If large TASMAC orders were given to them, how much was ordered? Is Jayalalithaa prepared to state all these details? Even when we decide not to say certain things, the author says, Jayalalithaa’s own statements compel us to recall and say them.

Was the “Midas” factory not started during Jayalalithaa’s rule? Whose factory is it? Is its ownership not widely known? Although the author says it is a fact known to the world that it belongs to people close to Jayalalithaa, were TASMAC orders to it reduced during these four years of DMK rule? No. This Government, he says, does not possess such a petty mentality.

I shall once again recall how permission was given to the Midas factory during Jayalalithaa’s rule and what, according to the source, that Government did to encourage it.

In Tamil Nadu there were only five factories manufacturing Indian Made Foreign Liquor during the M.G.R. period and continuously through the DMK Government of 1996–2001:

1. Mohan Breweries
2. Siva Distilleries
3. Balaji Distilleries
4. M.P. Distilleries
5. Sable Distilleries

Apart from these five, one more factory was started during Jayalalithaa’s previous rule. That was Midas Distilleries. The author points out that this was the factory permitted by the Jayalalithaa Government that now alleged lightning-speed creation of new liquor factories in Stalin’s and Kanimozhi’s names.

The story of how this “Midas” Distilleries came into being must be set out in detail. When I was Chief Minister, the owners of the five liquor factories above sought permission to increase their annual production capacity. I did not grant that permission.

At that time a private company called Golden Distilleries applied to the Tamil Nadu Government, saying that because demand for liquor was high in Tamil Nadu, instead of increasing the capacity of the existing factories it should be separately licensed to manufacture liquor. Since the policy was not to grant permission to anyone new, that request was rejected — `(G.O. Ms. No. 55 P & E iii dated 18-2-1999)`.

The private company immediately went to court and obtained an order in its favour on 30-3-2001. Even so, the special `Privilege` that had to be granted by the Tamil Nadu Government was not given. Instead, the Government appealed against the court order.

After the Jayalalithaa Government assumed office in 2001, Golden Distilleries submitted a petition to the Government on 1-2-2002 asking it to reconsider the request and withdraw the court appeal. The source says the Government considered the petition immediately and, within fifty days — on 21-3-2002 — issued an order permitting the company to manufacture low-priced liquor. Indeed, the Government Order was prepared on 21-3-2002, the file was seen and signed by all officials on that very day, and the order was issued the same day.

Having obtained that permission, Golden Distilleries submitted another petition the very next month, on 15-4-2002. It said permission to make only low-priced liquor was insufficient and sought permission to manufacture Indian Made Foreign Liquor. The source says the Jayalalithaa Government considered that petition too and within fifty days, on 30-5-2002, granted permission — `(G.O. Ms. No. 115 dated 30-5-2002)`.

Ten days after that order, Golden Distilleries again submitted a petition, seeking permission to move its factory from Thaiyur village in Tiruporur taluk to Sirumathur village in Sriperumbudur taluk. That request too was accepted within a month and permission for the change of site was granted on 10-7-2002 — `(G.O. Ms. No. 131 dated 10-7-2002)`.

The company then wrote to the Government on 26-9-2002 saying that its name had been changed to “Midas Golden Distilleries,” and the Government accepted that too. The author argues that once this name change became public, the reasons for the Government’s extraordinary speed in granting permission became known outside.

The Government determines how much liquor each of the six factories may produce in a year:

- Mohan Breweries — 42 lakh cases
- Siva Distilleries — 66 lakh cases
- Balaji Distilleries — 57 lakh cases
- M.P. Distilleries — 30 lakh cases
- Sable Distilleries — 30 lakh cases

But Midas Distilleries, the source says, could produce however many lakh cases it wanted in a year. A committee should have been constituted, its recommendations obtained, and a production limit fixed. But the author states that no such committee was appointed for as long as the AIADMK Government remained in office.

During DMK rule, the Government would issue an annual order on supply of raw materials, granting in one order permission for the twelve-month requirement from April through March. Under Jayalalithaa’s Government, the author says, a major change was made. In 2001, permission for the whole year was given only for 2001–02. After “Midas” began, the raw-material allocation order for each company was changed to four instalments a year.

If permission had to be obtained once every three months to buy raw material, the companies would continually need the Government’s favour. The author alleges that if a company was not accommodating to the Government, the Government could delay raw-material permission, while delays affecting others would enable Midas production to grow. He says this device was used and succeeded greatly.

When I earlier issued a statement about what I called corruption involving Midas, I asked whether the Government was prepared to publish the company’s annual production and sales. The AIADMK Government did not publish those details. I therefore published them myself, and, the author says, those then in office did not deny any of the information.

Midas sold only **7,48,638 cases** in 2003–04. Until that year liquor retailing was conducted by private parties through auction, and private retailers did not come forward to buy Midas products in large quantities. Jayalalithaa’s Government then announced that the Government itself would take over retail sales to increase State revenue. The author alleges that the actual intention was to force Midas products through TASMAC retail shops and increase its profits.

As a result, he says, Midas sales rose from about **7.48 lakh cases in 2003–04** to **28,50,095 cases in 2004–05** — four times as much in one year — because retailing had been taken over by the Government.

Midas’s share of total sales rose from **4.68 per cent in 2003–04** to **14.79 per cent in 2004–05**.

And in 2005–06? Against about 7.48 lakh cases in 2003–04, Midas sold **51.22 lakh cases** in 2005–06. The author asks what could explain an eightfold increase in two years. In percentage terms, he says, its share rose from **4.68 per cent** to **22.49 per cent** under the Jayalalithaa Government.

For comparison, Balaji Distilleries’ share fell from **25.58 per cent in 2003–04** to **22.08 per cent in 2005–06**; Siva Distilleries fell from **25.34 per cent** to **19.40 per cent**; Mohan Distilleries fell from **25.43 per cent** to **13.06 per cent**. Only Midas, he argues, rose from **4.68 per cent** to **22.49 per cent**.

Why did the Jayalalithaa Government give Midas products such priority in sales? Was this why the Government itself took over retail trade? What kind of offence was it, he asks, to claim that State revenue was being increased while increasing the sales of what he calls a benami company? Was it not, he asks, a scheme to channel the liquor trade into the treasury of that benami? He says no reply was given by the Government to the statement in which he raised these allegations during its earlier term.

After, in the author’s account, doing corruption on such a scale and assuming the people have forgotten it, she now issues statements like the proverb: “An elegant hair-knot, a screw-pine flower — but inside, lice and nits.” I have explained the details of what happened during her rule. Is she prepared to publish the evidence for what she has now alleged in her statement?

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**4-7-2010**

பினாமி நிறுவனத்தின் விற்பனையை அதிகரித்தது எப்படிப்பட்ட
குற்றம்? இதற்கு ஜெயலலிதாவின் பதில் என்ன? மதுபான
வியாபாரமே ஒட்டுமொத்தமாக தமது பினாமியின் கஜானாவிற்குப்
போய்ச் சேரத் திட்டமிட்ட சதியா அல்லவா இது என்றெல்லாம்
கடந்த கால ஆட்சியில் நான் விடுத்த அறிக்கைக்கு அரசின் சார்பில்
எந்தப் பதிலும் தரப்படவில்லை.

இந்த அளவிற்கு ஊழல்களைச் செய்து விட்டு -
அவற்றையெல்லாம் மக்கள் மறந்திருப்பார்கள்’ என்று
எண்ணிக்கொண்டு - “ஒய்யாரக் கொண்டையாம் தாழம்பூவாம்;
உள்ளே இருக்குமாம் ஈரும் பேனும்” என்பதைப் போல அறிக்கை
விட்டிருக்கிறார்! அவர் ஆட்சியில் நடைபெற்ற விவரங்களை நான்
விளக்கியிருக்கிறேன். தற்போது அவர் அறிக்கையிலே தெரிவித்த
தகவலுக்கான ஆதாரங்களை வெளியிடத்தயாரா?
