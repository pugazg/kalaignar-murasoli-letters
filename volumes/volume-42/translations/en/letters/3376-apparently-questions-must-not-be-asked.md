# 3376. Apparently, Questions Must Not Be Asked!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This letter questions the conduct and perceived neutrality of election authorities, using the Thirumangalam by-election and the announcement of the 2009 Parliamentary election as examples. Its allegations, suspicions and rhetorical questions are retained as the author’s political argument. The complete audited Tamil is reproduced below and remains authoritative.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3376](../../../chapters/3376-kelvi-ketka-koodatham.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 85](../../../pages/page-085.md)–[PDF 91](../../../pages/page-091.md)  
**Date:** 5 March 2009

**Udanpirappē,**

I am not writing today in order to find fault with anyone. I am setting down some thoughts because it would be good if there were a broad reflection and discussion on them. If these thoughts help bring about greater clarity, that would be good for everyone who values democracy. Neutrality should not remain merely a word; it should become truth in public life.

The “Election Commission” is an autonomous institution. No one is supposed to have the right to question its actions. During elections it can transfer any official it chooses. Neither a State government nor the Union Government can question that, because the Election Commission is treated as all-powerful. It is the Election Commission that fixes the dates on which elections are to be held.

Yet when the head of that very Commission and its members disagree among themselves, and the person in the first position complains that the person in the second position should be removed, and newspapers comment on it, those too are unfortunate events.

When a seat in Parliament or a Legislative Assembly falls vacant, the by-election must be held within six months. Ordinarily, if a general election to Parliament or an Assembly is only a few months away, the by-election too is held along with that general election in order to avoid unnecessary expenditure.

But in the case of the Thirumangalam constituency in Madurai district, the by-election was hurriedly held before six months had elapsed from the death of its Member.

When that by-election was announced, AIADMK leader Jayalalithaa summoned in person the leader of the MDMK, even though the deceased Member had belonged to that party, and directed him to leave the constituency to her. There was no room even for allied sentiment. Like people who, in the source proverb, “lost the salted meat to the grand lady,” MDMK cadres and front-line functionaries kept their mouths shut out of fear of party discipline.

Just as there is a Central Election Commission, there is a Chief Electoral Officer in the State. No one is supposed to question his authority either. Did he come from the Central Election Commission to serve as Tamil Nadu’s election officer? No. He is one among Tamil Nadu’s IAS officers. He is appointed to that post for a defined period and, when that term ends, is expected to return to State government service. His monthly salary is paid by the Tamil Nadu Government. Even if officers working under him are to be transferred, that too can be done only with the approval of the Tamil Nadu Government.

Yet Naresh Gupta, the present Chief Electoral Officer of Tamil Nadu, has not met the Chief Minister even once in the name of a “courtesy call” from May 2006, when the DMK assumed office, until today. The reason, apparently, is that he is an election officer and that even meeting me out of courtesy in my capacity as Chief Minister would compromise his integrity.

But when the Chief Election Commissioner and members of the Election Commission are appointed, do they not call on the Prime Minister and the President as a courtesy? They do.

And did earlier Chief Electoral Officers of Tamil Nadu — Oza, IAS; Chandrasoodan, IAS; Sarangi, IAS; Mathew, IAS and others — never meet Chief Ministers who were then in office? They met them not merely out of courtesy, but many times.

It is natural that by-elections attract more importance and fiercer competition than general elections, and that some law-and-order issues may arise. Whichever party is in office, the ruling party also has to take steps to maintain law and order.

Reports said that some such incidents occurred in Thirumangalam. The author argues that the principal opposition AIADMK sowed the seeds of violence because it engaged in giving money to voters and because attempts were made to catch and stop that activity red-handed. Then, after itself sowing the seeds of violence, it issued daily statements blaming the ruling party and police — like a pickpocket who steals and then runs shouting, “There goes the thief! The thief!”

In one such statement, issued on 31.12.2008, Leader of the Opposition Jayalalithaa demanded that certain police officers be removed from their posts immediately.

On that very day, the Chief Electoral Officer of Tamil Nadu, in an interview to newspapers, said that there had been intimidation and attacks, that numerous violations had taken place and that he could not betray the Election Commission.

Did any major riot, attack, violence or murder actually take place during the Thirumangalam by-election to justify language of that scale? The author answers: no, no.

Yet in his interview, a senior official who is expected to conduct elections without bias or support for one side said things such as, “There have been violent incidents and many wrongdoings in the constituency. Elections were conducted smoothly even in Jammu and Kashmir. Thirumangalam has been stained. The Thirumangalam incident is unfortunate.”

Whether, under election rules, such remarks by an official amount to one-sidedly influencing voters is a question that lawyers and politicians must answer.

On the same day that the Leader of the Opposition and some of her allied parties issued statements asking for officers to be transferred, I, as Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu, issued a statement saying:

> “The Tamil Nadu Government is ready to cooperate with the Election Commission of India, which is taking steps with the strong desire that the Thirumangalam by-election should be conducted peacefully.
>
> “For example, if the Election Commission accepts demands made by opposition parties regarding which officers should be transferred and where, and takes action accordingly, this government has so far shown no hesitation in cooperating with such action and will show none in the future either. This government has no two views about the fact that what must win in Thirumangalam is peace and democracy.”

But what did the *Dinamalar* newspaper recall about what happened when Jayalalithaa was in office?

During the 2006 Assembly election, when the Central Election Commission ordered the transfer of then Chennai City Police Commissioner Nataraj, did Jayalalithaa not go to court opposing that transfer? *Dinamalar* asked this question.

That same Jayalalithaa later issued a statement not merely asking for officers to be transferred but demanding their immediate removal from office.

What did the Central Election Commission do in this matter? On the very day that the Leader of the Opposition named three police officers in Madurai and demanded that they be removed immediately, the Central Election Commission moved with extraordinary speed and sent an urgent communication to the Tamil Nadu Government that evening.

What was the urgent message? It directed that three police officers serving in the Thirumangalam constituency in Madurai be transferred immediately.

The communication did not stop there. It also asked the Tamil Nadu Government to send a panel of persons who could be appointed to those three posts and instructed that the names of officers specified by the Central Election Commission itself be included in that panel.

If so, what better example is needed, asks the source sarcastically, to show how “neutral” the Election Commission was?

How did the Central Election Commission come to know the officers in Tamil Nadu and their performance well enough to name three persons for inclusion in the panel? Who from here supplied the Commission with the names of the three officers it wanted appointed there? The source concludes: they themselves know.

When the Central Election Commission hurriedly recommended three names and asked that they be included in the panel, one of the officers recommended for a DIG post was already an IG. Can an IG be appointed as a DIG?

The Tamil Nadu Government therefore explained this to the Election Commission and, on the morning of 1.1.2009, sent a list of DIG-level officers and names for the other two posts, including the names recommended by the Central Election Commission.

Although that day was a holiday, the Commission issued orders the same day directing that the very officers it had already recommended be appointed there, and ordered them to join immediately.

During the Thirumangalam by-election, when a Central Election Commissioner came to Chennai on a private visit, he made several allegations to reporters about the by-election.

What did Leader of the Opposition Jayalalithaa say about the Tamil Nadu Chief Electoral Officer? Was it not reported in the newspapers that she described him as “an efficient officer and a good man”? Does that not create suspicion, asks the author, when the Chief Electoral Officer criticises the Tamil Nadu Government?

The Central Election Commission announced the dates for the Parliamentary general election only at about 3.30 p.m. on 2.3.2009. Only after that did the election code of conduct come into effect.

Could schemes announced by the Tamil Nadu Government before that — on February 28, March 1 or even on the morning of March 2 — not be published in newspapers?

For example, an announcement establishing a Vanniyar Public Property Welfare Board and appointing an officer for it was given to newspapers on 2.3.2009. But, the source says, if the Tamil Nadu Chief Electoral Officer telephoned certain journalists and stopped them from publishing that news, why such concern on his part?

Even if no one is supposed to question the actions of the Election Commission and election officials, are people not even to be informed that such things are happening?

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**5-3-2009**

## Letter-specific notes

1. The letter’s suspicions concerning election officials are translated as the author’s claims and rhetorical questions, not as independent findings.
2. The Thirumangalam allegations concerning money distribution and violence are likewise retained as source-framed political assertions.
3. Institutional titles are translated consistently, while the named officers and dates follow the audited Tamil.
4. The closing question preserves the source’s central theme: whether an institution’s autonomy also prevents public questioning of its conduct.

திருமங்கலம் இடைத் தேர்தல் நடைபெற்ற நேரத்தில் மத்திய தேர்தல் ஆணையத்தின் ஆணையர் இங்கே தனிப்பட்ட வருகையாக சென்னைக்கு வந்தபோது, திருமங்கலம் இடைத் தேர்தலைப் பற்றி செய்தியாளர்களிடம் பல்வேறு குற்றச்சாட்டுகளைக் கூறினார். தமிழகத் தேர்தல் அதிகாரியைப் பற்றி தமிழகத்திலே உள்ள எதிர்க் கட்சித் தலைவர் ஜெயலலிதா என்ன விமர்சனம் செய்தார்? “அவர் திறமையான அதிகாரி என்றும், நல்லவர் என்றும்” விமர்சனம் செய்து ஏடுகளில் வெளியாவில்லையா? அதனால்தான் தமிழக அரசை தமிழகத் தேர்தல் அதிகாரி விமர்சனம் செய்தாரா என்ற சந்தேகம் வருமா வராதா?

தற்போது 2.3.2009 அன்று மதியம் 3.30 மணி அளவில் தான் மத்திய தேர்தல் ஆணையர் நாடாளுமன்ற பொதுத் தேர்தல் நடைபெறும் தேதியை அறிவிக்கிறார். அதற்குப் பிறகுதான் தேர்தல் கட்டுப்பாடுகள் எல்லாம் நடைமுறைக்கு வருகின்றன. ஆனால் 2ஆம் தேதிக்கு முன்பு பிப்ரவரி 28ஆம் தேதியோ, மார்ச் முதல் தேதியோ ஏன் 2ஆம் தேதி காலையிலோ தமிழக அரசின் சார்பில் அறிவிக்கப்பட்ட சில திட்டங்கள் ஏடுகளில் வெளியே வரலாமா கூடாதா? உதாரணமாக வன்னியர் பொது சொத்து

நல வாரியம் என்ற ஒரு அமைப்பு ஏற்படுத்தப்பட்டு, அதற்கான அதிகாரி நியமனம் செய்யப்பட்டதற்கான அறிவிப்பு ஏடுகளுக்கு 2.3.2009 தரப்பட்டுள்ளது. ஆனால் தமிழகத் தலைமை தேர்தல் ஆணையம் சில பத்திரிகையாளர்களுடன் தொலைபேசியில் பேசி, அந்தச் செய்தியினை ஏடுகளிலே வெளியிடக் கூடாது என்று தடுத்திருக்கிறார் என்றால், அவருக்கு இதிலே ஏன் இப்படிப்பட்ட அக்கறை?

தேர்தல் ஆணையமும், தேர்தல் அதிகாரிகளும் செய்கின்ற செயல்கள் பற்றி யாரும் கேள்வி கேட்கக் கூடாது என்ற போதிலும் - இப்படியெல்லாம் செயல்கள் நடைபெறுகின்றன என்று மக்களுக்குத் தெரிவிக்கக் கூட கூடாதா என்ன?
