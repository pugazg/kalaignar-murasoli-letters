# 3557. Who Should Seek Public Forgiveness?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 24 April 2011

**Udanpirappē,**

**“Karunanidhi must seek public forgiveness for merely watching the ethnic massacre that took place in Sri Lanka.”** Do you know who has issued this command? None other than Jayalalithaa! Do you know from where she has scattered these pearls of words? Staying at the summer retreat of **Kodai Nadu Estate**, poor thing, unable even there to find rest because she is wondering whether she can find something with which to shower abuse on me, she grieves for the Sri Lankan Tamils and tells me to seek public forgiveness!

Whom is Jayalalithaa telling to seek forgiveness?

She is telling **me** to seek public forgiveness—the man who, in **1956**, in the DMK General Council at Chidambaram, in the presence of Arignar Anna, moved a resolution for the Sri Lankan Tamils.

I am the man who, on **24-8-1977**, organised in Chennai a procession of **five lakh people** over the Sri Lankan Tamil issue. In **1981**, on the very day news arrived that Tamils were being affected in Sri Lanka—yes, on **August 13 itself**—I sent a telegram to the Prime Minister saying:

> “Reports coming from Colombo indicate that riots are spreading in the east and south of Sri Lanka. It appears that the government there itself is encouraging the riots. Tamil homes in North Colombo are being attacked; Jaffna trains are being attacked; Tamil passengers travelling in them are being robbed and thrown out of the coaches on the way. I request assistance on humanitarian grounds to resolve this problem.”

And Karunanidhi, who sent that telegram, must now seek public forgiveness, it seems!

On **15 August 1981**, over the Sri Lankan Tamil issue, Karunanidhi was arrested by the then AIADMK government—and he must seek public forgiveness, it seems!

On **25-7-1983**, when Sinhalese entered Welikada prison and massacred **35 Tamils**, including Thangathurai, Kuttimani and Jegan, I gathered **eight lakh people** in the capital of Tamil Nadu within **seven hours** and organised a procession. I must seek public forgiveness, it seems!

On **10-8-1983**, urging the Union and State governments to pay sufficient attention to the Eelam Tamil issue, both I and the DMK general secretary, Perasiriyar, resigned even our positions as Members of the Legislative Assembly.

On **16-5-1985**, I took part in a blockade protest at Kanchipuram and was arrested.

On **23-8-1985**, after orders were issued to deport Chandrahason, Balasingham and Sathyendra, I led a procession in Chennai and announced that the struggle would continue unless the deportation was stopped; following that, the deportation order was withdrawn.

In support of the Sri Lankan Tamils, and in order that there should not be fratricidal war among them, in **May 1986** at Madurai, through the **TESO** organisation, I organised a Sri Lankan Tamil Protection Conference, bringing together leaders of various parties from across India. Among those who participated were the general secretary Perasiriyar, Tamil leader K. Veeramani, Thambi Murasoli Maran, Thambi Vaiko and Nedumaran. Karunanidhi, who did this, must seek public forgiveness, it seems!

On **3-6-1986**, my birthday celebration was cancelled, and I distributed among the militant movements the funds collected that day through donation boxes.

On **15 October 1987**, we organised a procession on behalf of the DMK.

On **16-10-1987**, I issued a statement condemning the arrest of Vaiko when he went to meet Commander Kittu.

I am also the one who sent telegrams on the Sri Lankan Tamil issue to the Chief Ministers of eight States and to opposition leaders across India.

On **6-11-1987**, in Chennai, on behalf of the Federation for the Protection of Eelam Tamil Rights, I organised a human chain in which people from various parties, Tamil scholars, poets, artists, writers, students and women participated.

On **15-3-1989**, in Delhi, I met Rajiv Gandhi, who was then Prime Minister, twice and discussed the Eelam Tamil issue.

On **15-6-1989**, I met Prime Minister Rajiv Gandhi at Chennai airport and spoke about the Sri Lankan issue.

In **January 1991**, when the DMK government itself was dismissed over the Sri Lankan Tamil issue, I was the Chief Minister! In **1997**, when the DMK was in power and the Sri Lankan Tamil issue arose, do you know what Jayalalithaa—who now tells me to seek public forgiveness—said?

She issued a statement saying: **“Karunanidhi is trying to disrupt law and order—the Liberation Tigers have raised their heads again in Tamil Nadu.”**

When Rajiv Gandhi spoke in Tamil Nadu on **5-11-1989** and **6-11-1989**, he said:

> “I thank the DMK government and Chief Minister M. Karunanidhi, who for the past several months have been extending cooperation in finding a solution to the Sri Lankan Tamil issue.”

And I, who was praised in this way by the young leader Rajiv Gandhi, must seek public forgiveness, it seems!

On **23-4-2008**, in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly, I moved the following resolution:

> “In order to establish peace in Sri Lanka, the Government of India must arrange talks between the two sides engaged in conflict there. This House urges the Union Government to come forward to arrange useful negotiations so that a proper political solution may be reached for establishing peace in Sri Lanka.”

And I must seek public forgiveness, it seems!

On the morning of **6-10-2008**, I contacted Indian Prime Minister Dr Manmohan Singh by telephone and asked that the Union Government immediately summon the Sri Lankan envoy in New Delhi and convey India's anguish over the killing of unarmed Sri Lankan Tamils, and that the Sri Lankan government's military action and ethnic massacre be brought to an immediate end. And I must seek public forgiveness, it seems! Do you know what kind of person is saying this?

It is Jayalalithaa who, in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly on **16-4-2002**, moved and passed the following resolution:

> “This Legislative Assembly urges the Union Government to take immediate action to ensure that the Sri Lankan government immediately arrests Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam leader Velupillai Prabhakaran and hands him over to the Government of India. Further, this House urges the Union Government not to permit any member of the banned Liberation Tigers organisation to enter the Indian nation.”

It is she who tells me to seek public forgiveness.

When Rajapaksa's Sinhalese forces in Sri Lanka were killing innocent Tamils in large numbers, and processions, public meetings and demonstrations were being held in Tamil Nadu in condemnation, Jayalalithaa issued a statement on **17-1-2009** saying:

> “The Sri Lankan military does not intend to kill Sri Lankan Tamils. When a war—a battle—is taking place, innocent people will be killed. No country is an exception to this. Wherever war or battle takes place, innocent people are killed. But what is happening in Sri Lanka today is that the Liberation Tigers are holding the Sri Lankan Tamils back from going to safe places and are forcibly using them as a shield in front of the military.”

If Jayalalithaa, who issued that statement, tells me to seek public forgiveness, could there be any greater disgrace than this?

Is that all? When, condemning the ethnic massacre of Tamils in Sri Lanka, an all-party meeting in Tamil Nadu unanimously passed a resolution demanding an immediate ceasefire, it was this same Jayalalithaa who said: **“The people of Tamil Nadu have begun to suspect whether Karunanidhi is acting in support of the Liberation Tigers. The Government of India has no authority to stop the civil war now taking place in Sri Lanka.”** She also issued a statement saying that the war in Sri Lanka was a war against the banned terrorist organisation called the Liberation Tigers, and that it was for that reason that Karunanidhi was writing elegies on the deaths of Liberation Tigers leaders. Even some of the newspapers that publish Jayalalithaa's statements today published that statement then!

For the rights of the Eelam Tamils living in Sri Lanka—for those people not to be pushed aside as second-class citizens, but to obtain all rights in Sri Lanka and live with equality and peace—the DMK has raised its voice from **1956** onward. Is Jayalalithaa qualified to find fault, on the Sri Lankan Tamil issue, with the DMK and with me as the leader of that DMK?

When all the parties in Tamil Nadu came together for consultations organised by the government on the Sri Lankan Tamil issue, Jayalalithaa described even that as a **“deceptive drama.”** Keeping one thing inside and saying another outside; abandoning those who trusted her in midstream; threatening people while in office and pleading when seeking office; behaving with an arrogant attitude that respects no one and thinks nothing exists above oneself—these are arts mastered by **“deceptive actors”** such as Jayalalithaa. But I, who value labour, righteous struggle, sacrifice, embrace, civility, culture and self-respect, will never come to a position in which I must seek public forgiveness!

In truth, the person who should seek public forgiveness over the Sri Lankan Tamils is Jayalalithaa.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**24-04-2011**

3-6-1986 அன்று என்னுடைய பிறந்த நாள் விழா ரத்து செய்யப்பட்டது, அன்று உண்டியல் மூலம் வசூலான நிதியினை போராளி இயக்கங்களுக்காகப் பகிர்ந்து கொடுத்தவன் நான்.
