# 3736. Do Not Sully Departed Leaders!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

A resolution was introduced in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly to name the second terminal of Chennai's domestic airport after my dear friend M.G.R., and it was passed unanimously with the support of all parties. Thambi Thalapathi M.K. Stalin, leader of the DMK Legislature Party, too said on behalf of the DMK that he welcomed the resolution.

At this moment my thoughts go back to an event in February 1990. After V.P. Singh became Prime Minister, he came at my invitation to take part in a public meeting on the Chennai beach. While speaking there, I requested that the airports at Meenambakkam be named after Anna and Kamaraj. Prime Minister V.P. Singh immediately used the special “Hot Line” telephone placed a short distance from the stage for the Prime Minister, contacted Delhi and spoke to the officials concerned. When he later came to the stage to speak, he announced that Chennai's domestic airport would bear Kamaraj's name and the international airport Anna's name. Those are the names by which they have continued to be known to this day.

It is as a continuation of that history that the AIADMK government has now sought to name the second terminal of the modernised Chennai domestic airport after M.G.R. Following a letter from a Union Minister asking the Tamil Nadu Chief Minister to have a resolution passed in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly and sent to the Centre, the Assembly passed the resolution yesterday.

Although we welcomed the resolution on behalf of the DMK, one cannot help thinking for a moment about what my dear friend M.G.R. thought in his final days about the Tamil Nadu Chief Minister who introduced this resolution, and about the way those now in power criticised him. That people who once spoke in that manner have changed today and have gone so far as to take the initiative to name an airport terminal after M.G.R. is a great consolation to the mind.

In the 1 January 1989 issue of *Makkal Kural*, its editor T.R. Ramasamy published at the time remarks attributed to M.G.R.: “I cannot bear Ammu's excesses. Thinking of something else, I brought Ammu into politics. I set out to make a Pillaiyar and it ended up becoming a monkey. This Ammu, who has learnt deceit, argument, treachery and intrigue, is now laying a trap even for me. My situation has become like inviting a harmless running lizard onto one's own face. This Ammu is dangerous. She will use anyone for herself. She will hesitate at nothing. She is very bad.”

The 22 January 1985 issue of *Anna* carried M.G.R.'s reported view: “It was my mistake to seat Jayalalithaa in a position of responsibility beyond her age and qualifications. I can no longer tolerate Jayalalithaa using my name in many places without my knowledge.”

It was my dear friend M.G.R. who brought Jayalalithaa into politics and gave her various responsibilities. It was M.G.R. who enabled her to rise to such a high position after him. It was he who made her a Rajya Sabha member and thereby made her known across India. Yet the reports published at the time in *Anna* and *Makkal Kural* showed how deeply distressed M.G.R. had become in his final period and what views he had expressed.

To say more: when a new film city was created in Chennai by the Tamil Nadu government, the film world of Tamil Nadu widely expected and requested that it be named either after M.G.R. or Nadigar Thilagam Sivaji. Chief Minister Jayalalithaa ignored those requests and gave the film city her own name. Later, after the DMK government came to office, the film city was named after my dear friend M.G.R., and it continues to bear that name to this day.

Speaking at a film-industry function held at the Madras University Centenary Hall on 16 April 1989, I announced: “Even if a thousand differences arose in politics in the final period, in memory of my dear friend M.G.R., with whom I lived and worked together for forty years in the world of art from the earliest days, an award in his name will be presented every year by the Government of Tamil Nadu to artistes.” That award continues to be presented every year even now.

On 31 August 1989, the inauguration of the Tamil Nadu Dr. M.G.R. Medical University and the foundation-stone ceremony for its buildings were held. But because the DMK government was dismissed the very next year after the foundation stone had been laid, the building work did not proceed quickly. After the DMK returned to office in 1996, the construction was expedited, and on 31 July 1998 the then Vice-President, Krishan Kant, was invited to inaugurate the M.G.R. University buildings constructed at a cost of ₹6 crore; I myself unveiled the statue of M.G.R. installed at the front of the building.

In the 1989 Budget, funds were allocated and I ordered that the place where M.G.R.'s body had been buried—where no memorial platform had been built and stones were merely lying piled up—be beautified immediately. To evoke how M.G.R. had helped many people and stood as their shade, I asked that the place be designed in the form of an umbrella. The M.G.R. memorial was inaugurated under DMK rule on 15 November 1989, and I myself opened it. When opposition to it was expressed from the AIADMK side, P.H. Pandian, speaking at that function, said words recorded in the old newspapers: “It is regrettable that Tamil Nadu politics has reached a stage where, however much good is done, it is belittled. Only one person opposes this function. I do not even wish to say her name. As one who served as Speaker, I know certain truths. She tried three times to remove Puratchi Thalaivar from office. She is the very person now trying to dismiss this government. Is such a way of making a living necessary?”

It is that very Jayalalithaa who has now changed herself and come forward to propose that a part of the airport be named after my dear friend M.G.R.

And that is not all. In a letter Jayalalithaa wrote in her own hand about my dear friend M.G.R. and sent through Salem advocate Kannan to be delivered to Prime Minister Rajiv Gandhi, she wrote:

> “The root cause of everything that is happening here is that the C.M. is terribly jealous of my popularity. He cannot stomach the fact that I have become so popular. So he is doing everything possible to eliminate me from the political scene and from public life.”

That is: “The Chief Minister (M.G.R.) is intensely jealous on seeing that I have become so influential and popular. This is the root cause of everything happening here. He cannot digest the fact that I have become so popular. Therefore he (M.G.R.) is doing everything he can to eliminate me from politics and public life,” she wrote about M.G.R.

Nor was that all. In the same letter she wrote:

> “MGR himself who does not want to give me due importance does not want to induct me into the Cabinet. No one here can really dare to oppose him for without him they are Zeros.”

That is: “M.G.R., who does not wish to give me the importance due to me, does not wish to include me in the Cabinet either. No one here has the courage to oppose him, because without him all the others are zeros.” Is it not, then, something to be welcomed that the person who wrote all this has today herself come forward to ask that Chennai airport be named after my dear friend M.G.R.?

But according to the information available to us, an airport can have a domestic terminal and an international terminal—those are the two terminals. The domestic terminal has already been named after Perunthalaivar Kamaraj and that remains in practice. What has now been done is only an expansion of the domestic terminal. Giving that another name therefore appears somewhat confusing. I am not alone in raising this point; those who cherish Perunthalaivar Kamaraj are raising it as well.

Further, today's (16 April 2013) *Deccan Chronicle* carried a report under the headline “Naming Wars - Passengers say airport already named”, which read:

> “What’s in a Name? This is what are passengers’ and airport officials asked when this newspaper tried to find out their reaction for the State Assembly’s Resolution to name the new domestic terminal after former Chief Minister M.G. Ramachandran. “Where is the question of naming the terminal again, when it is already named? The domestic terminal-1 at Chennai airport has been named after former C.M. Kamaraj and we have just added another terminal (II) to it. So, where does the question of renaming the extension crop up? It will only add to the confusion” said a senior airport official”

That is, when passengers and airport officials were asked for their reaction to the Tamil Nadu Assembly resolution to name Chennai airport's new domestic terminal after M.G.R., they responded, “What's in a name?” A senior airport official said: “Where is the question of naming the terminal again when the domestic terminal is already named? The first domestic terminal has already been named after former Chief Minister Kamaraj. Now only a second terminal has been newly added. Where does the question arise of naming this expansion? It will only add to the confusion.” That is what the English daily *Deccan Chronicle* reported.

When one intends to give the name of a leader whom all Tamil Nadu has held in the highest esteem and continues to honour and celebrate, it should be done in a way that creates no confusion or difference of opinion and that every section can wholeheartedly welcome. It is only to point this out that I write this letter!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**17 April 2013**
