# 3741. Strong Reasons Are Piling Up!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 23 April 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

Eelam Tamils who cross the sea trying to escape the Sri Lankan military and save themselves are being caught by the armed forces of various countries. Of 45 people who were caught in this way and put ashore in Dubai, the source says the U.N. has ordered that 19 be sent back to Sri Lanka. One of them, Ramanan, gave the following interview to the weekly *Junior Vikatan*:

> “In October last year, 102 Eelam refugees came to Sindalakarai in Thoothukudi district in order to travel to Australia. We gathered at the Kali temple there. My daughter, my wife and I were in that group. Forty-five of us who boarded the boat first managed to leave for Australia without being caught by the police. My wife and I got onto the boat. My child was supposed to come with the group behind us, but the child was caught by the police. My wife and I are now in Dubai, while my child is in Tiruchi.
>
> “The boat that left Sindalakarai on 9 October developed a hole while travelling in international waters. Exhausted from bailing water out of the boat, we informed the Australian Navy by satellite phone. After a little while a cargo ship came and rescued us. It was dark everywhere. We did not know the language. We believed we were being taken to Australia. But they told us to disembark at a port in Dubai called Jabar Ali. Some voluntary organisations also came. We refused to leave the ship and asked them to guarantee that, ‘We set out for Australia seeking asylum. You have brought us here. You must guarantee that you will not hand us over to Sri Lanka.’ We disembarked only after they assured us that ‘the Dubai government will not send you to Sri Lanka.’ But now the U.N. has abandoned us. The U.N. has decided to send 19 of us back to Sri Lanka.”

Logini Rathi Mohan, called Harini, who was part of this group, said:

> “I worked as a newsreader on the Tigers' national television. In 2009 we escaped and came to Tamil Nadu. We came ashore only because we were promised that we would not be sent back to Sri Lanka. But now the U.N. refugee organisation is telling us to go to Sri Lanka. They have confined us without allowing us to speak freely to the media or communicate with the outside world. If I am sent back to Sri Lanka, what happened to Isaipriya will happen to me too.
>
> “Besides, my husband is in one country, my parents and sisters in another—we are scattered in different directions. We want to escape to some country and from there bring our scattered families together. We cross the sea because we too desire a good life. Did they rescue us from the middle of the sea only to send us to the very country we do not wish to enter?”

Thus have they expressed the anguish in their hearts. Among the 45 put ashore in Dubai were, importantly, the wife and children of Ilanthirayan, who had been a military spokesman for the Tigers, as well as some other fighters. Sweden accepted eight of them. The United States promised asylum to 11. Seven had already been sent back to Colombo, and no country had come forward to give asylum to the remaining 19. Reports have reached us that the Dubai government is preparing to send them back to Colombo.

There is no need to search for any other example of how a Tamil without refuge suffers in different parts of the world. They are Tamils—Eelam Tamils. We have the first duty to help them. We have a country of our own. It must take immediate steps to relieve the distress of these 19 people. These 19 do not wish to return to Sri Lanka. They are prepared to seek refuge in any other country. Should the Government of India not help light a lamp in their lives?

Other information about the Sinhala majoritarian government of Sri Lanka continues to appear in newspapers. Just last week the British government issued a report stating that human-rights violations in Sri Lanka were in a very serious condition. Following that, the United States Department of State released its report on human-rights violations in Sri Lanka during 2012. According to the account quoted in this letter, people holding positions opposed to the government face attacks, disappearances, killings and various other cruelties; Sri Lankan security forces and the military are involved in such human-rights violations; attacks are especially frequent against social activists, supporters of the Liberation Tigers and journalists; the authorities threaten them; and people expressing opinions against the government suddenly disappear. The report is also described as saying that Social Welfare Minister and Eelam People's Democratic Party leader Douglas Devananda encourages intimidation, robbery and corruption in the Jaffna area.

It was with these concerns in mind that, in the resolution adopted at the TESO conference we held in Chennai on 12 August 2012, we pointed out:

> “Many Tamil youths have mysteriously disappeared. Many are undergoing indescribable suffering and anguish in torture camps. Tamil women are being subjected by military personnel to many cruelties, including sexual violence. Tamil children live looking at guns, affected by the violence unleashed by military personnel and in endless fear.”

We have continuously insisted that an independent, credible international commission of inquiry be established to investigate such human-rights violations and the genocidal killings that took place in Sri Lanka.

Reports say that 250 members of the Sri Lankan Navy have been trained on Indian naval vessels, and that three Indian Coast Guard ships—“Sujata, Tarangini, Varuna”—provided this training. A Sri Lankan Deputy Naval Commander is said to have stated that India itself trains 80 per cent of Sri Lankan naval personnel. All this news increases our anguish day by day. We, and everyone concerned about the welfare of Eelam Tamils, have continuously urged the Union Government that no member of any branch of the Sri Lankan military should be trained anywhere in India. That such training still continues is cruelty upon cruelty. On behalf of Tamil Nadu, there seems no course except to condemn this action of the Union Government.

Meanwhile, according to a report in an English newspaper today, a Commonwealth Law Conference was held in Cape Town, South Africa, from 14 April to 18 April. Twenty-seven Chief Justices from Commonwealth countries participated. In a resolution they unanimously adopted, they asked that the Commonwealth Ministerial Action Group meeting scheduled for 26 April 2013 in London principally consider the Sri Lankan issue and decide whether Sri Lanka should be removed from the Commonwealth; that removal had become important because of continuing human-rights violations in Sri Lanka and violations of the fundamental principles upheld by the Commonwealth; and that the decision to hold the Commonwealth conference in Sri Lanka should be reconsidered. They also condemned the dismissal of Sri Lankan Chief Justice Mrs. Shirani Bandaranayake by the President of Sri Lanka.

At the emergency meeting of the DMK Headquarters Executive Committee held in Chennai on 25 March 2013, a resolution was adopted stating:

> “It is neither just nor acceptable in any way for a Commonwealth conference to be held in Sri Lanka, which carried out genocide and sought to destroy the Tamil Eelam people. Therefore, this Executive Committee of the DMK calls upon the Commonwealth leadership not to hold that conference there under any circumstances.”

Further, in the statement I issued on 6 April 2013, I insisted that the Commonwealth Ministerial Action Group meeting in London on 26 April 2013 decide that the Commonwealth conference should not be held in Sri Lanka.

The very demand we pressed that day has today been echoed by the Chief Justices of Commonwealth countries. I therefore once again insist that all the countries represented in the Commonwealth Ministerial Action Group support a decision not to hold the Commonwealth conference in Sri Lanka, and that our Government of India apply the necessary pressure toward that end.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**23 April 2013**
