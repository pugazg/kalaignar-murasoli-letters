# 3807. Bearing the Sword of Righteous Struggle, Let Us Continue on Periyar’s and Anna’s Path!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 17 September 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

Today, 17 September 2013, is Mupperum Vizha: the 135th birthday of Periyar, the 105th birthday of Anna, and the sixty-fifth birthday of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam. Joined together, these three anniversaries form the great festival that raises and inspires us.

Speaking of his association with Thanthai Periyar, Arignar Anna said: “My association with Periyar began in 1935. I first met him at the Youth Conference held in Tiruppur. From that day he became my leader. In public life, I became his adopted son.” With overflowing enthusiasm he described the bond that grew between father and son. Nor was that all. Anna also said with pride: “In all these years, the only leader I have known, understood and seen is Periyar. I have never worked under the leadership of anyone else. I have never felt the desire to do so, and I never will. Periyar is the leader of our hearts—the leader who has made his home in our hearts.”

About that Periyar, when I was twenty-one years old in 1945, I wrote:

> Those who planted disorder within the race
> staggered as though thunder had struck.
> Entangled religions, gods and foolishness
> fled with the speed of lightning—behold!
> The old world, praised and worshipped,
> is collapsing in the Erode earthquake.
>
> The word “E.V.R.” is a storm
> shaking the very foundations of Aryanism in this land!
> It is enough if his white beard moves;
> enough if the signal of his eye is seen.
> There are those dressed in black—
> swords that return only after cutting through!

In the very first issue of *Kudi Arasu*, Thanthai Periyar explained the purpose of his public life: “Self-respect, equality and fraternity must flourish among the people. The feeling of high and low is the ghee feeding the fire of caste conflict that is spreading in our country. That feeling must disappear, and the understanding that all life is one must grow.” He travelled that path without the slightest deviation until his last breath. Today is the 135th birthday of that Periyar.

It is also the 105th birthday of our Anna, the commander who stood beside Periyar. The memory of that foremost son of Mother Tamil has never left our hearts even for a moment. We have carved his sacred image into our hearts and continue tirelessly along his path. About Anna I wrote:

> A sacred heart softer than flower petals—
> a loving heart,
> the heart of a mother who embraces all.
> His flower-petal lips speak Tamil;
> mango, jackfruit and banana—the three sweet fruits—are defeated.
> His flower-eyes become spears and swords
> if harm approaches Mother Tamil.
> Though the flowers of his feet may wither,
> his determined journey will not stop.
> The flowers of his hands hold together
> his younger brothers and the movement.
> Those very flowers forgive the enemy
> and make his head bow like a ripened stalk of grain.

Those lines I wrote in 1969 still stir me.

Today is also the sixty-fifth birthday of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam, founded under Anna's leadership in pouring rain at Robinson Park in Chennai on 17 September 1949.

When he launched this great movement, Arignar Anna declared: “Let me state this with absolute clarity. The Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam is in no way opposed to the Dravidar Kazhagam, nor does it have a contrary purpose. The policy is one: social reform in society, a socialist objective in economics, and liberation in politics from North Indian domination. These are the principles of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam.”

He further said:

> The Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam is a grafted mango sapling. The soil is rich; it is the same earth. Many are ready to water it, prepare the ground and build the beds. The day will certainly come when the grafted sapling flowers, bears fruit and bends under the weight of its yield.

Though Anna founded the DMK, it was because of the “Anna Resolution” at the Salem Conference of 1944 that the Dravidar Kazhagam, with Periyar as its leader, was formed. On that foundation, we combine Periyar's birthday, Anna's birthday and the birthday of the DMK and celebrate them every year as Mupperum Vizha.

If Thanthai Periyar was the sculptor of social reform, Arignar Anna was the teacher of political renaissance. Periyar and Anna were like the two sides of a single coin. As Karl Marx and Engels are the founding fathers of the communist movement, Periyar and Anna are the founding fathers of the Dravidian movement.

Anna gave us phrases that continue to renew our thought:

- The line rose; the mountain fell!
- If this army is defeated, what army can win?
- The man who has a younger brother fears no army!
- Even the jasmine in an opponent's garden has fragrance!
- Those without money in their hands, but without stain in their hearts!
- Some unemployed people by the roadside; and in the minds of the unemployed, dangerous thoughts!
- Nature caresses; deprivation lashes!
- He who conquers himself will conquer the world!

The DMK, the ideological training ground created by Anna, has sat both in government and in opposition and has achieved much by following his path and its ideals. Though the DMK is once again seated in the opposition benches, no one can doubt that it will responsibly fulfil its democratic duty and remain the protector of the Tamil people.

This year's Mupperum Vizha was originally planned for Vellore, with my approval, and preparations had moved quickly; even the work of erecting the pandal had begun. But the weather forecast warned of continuous rain until the 15th. We therefore decided that, rather than holding the event in an open ground beneath a temporary pavilion, it would be more appropriate to conduct it in Kalaignar Arangam at Anna Arivalayam in Chennai. Our Vellore district comrades agreed, and the DMK's Mupperum Vizha is being held today, 17 September 2013.

As always, to express Perarignar Anna's affection for students, the festival includes financial assistance from the DMK Trust. Students who secured high marks in the Higher Secondary examination will receive financial awards and certificates. College, high-school and higher-secondary students selected at the State level in competitions reciting the songs of Puratchi Kavignar Bharathidasan will receive financial assistance and certificates from the Murasoli Trust.

To express Anna's friendship with workers, the annual presentation of certificates of merit, cash awards and medals to outstanding auto drivers will also take place.

This will be followed by the presentation of the DMK's Mupperum Vizha awards under the chairmanship of our General Secretary, Perasiriyar. He is recovering after major surgery and had told us: “Do not print my name on the invitation. If my health permits, I shall come.” But I instructed them: “Print that the function will be chaired by Perasiriyar. If his health permits, let him attend; otherwise, that is all right.” The invitation was printed accordingly.

As in every year, this year's Thanthai Periyar Award will be presented to Mumbai D.M. Arya Sankaran, who spread the DMK's roots in Maharashtra; the Anna Award to language-war commander L. Ganesan; the Pavendhar Award to the master of letters K. Thirunavukkarasu; and the Kalaignar Award named after me to S.A.M. Hussain, whom I have praised as “the wondrous lamp of Thousand Lights.”

DMK Treasurer Thalapathi M.K. Stalin, Principal Secretary Arcot N. Veerasamy, and Deputy General Secretaries Duraimurugan, V.P. Duraisamy and Mrs Sarguna Pandian will offer felicitations. South Chennai district secretary J. Anbazhagan, MLA, will deliver the welcome address, and North Chennai district in-charge R.D. Sekar will propose the vote of thanks. Since the festival is being held in Chennai, the arrangements are being made jointly by the South and North Chennai district secretaries and leading functionaries under the guidance of Thalapathi.

News of the festival has already appeared in the newspapers and has been announced through posters. I know very well that you will already have begun your journey to attend this celebration for Anna, and that you will come with your family. Yet would not the thought arise in your heart that this elder brother, who invites you every year to the festival held for Anna, has failed to invite you this year? That is why I am writing this letter in haste.

A few may still be waiting to depart only after receiving my invitation. I shall expect them too, on seeing this letter, to leave immediately and come with their families.

Along the path shown by Periyar, on the road refined by Anna, let us carry the sword and shield of righteous struggle called the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam and continue our journey.

**With affection,**
**M.K.**
17 September 2013
