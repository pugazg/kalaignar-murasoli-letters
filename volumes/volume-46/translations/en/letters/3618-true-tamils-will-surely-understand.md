# 3618. True Tamils Will Surely Understand!

**Date:** 22 April 2012

**Udanpirappē,**

When neither the Speaker nor the ruling party gives an opportunity in the Assembly to raise legitimate issues or to give a proper reply when slander and unsupported accusations are made, the Speaker himself, as already planned, called on 19 April 2012 upon a member belonging to an allied party of the AIADMK to speak. He immediately rose and praised the Government to the skies. Thereupon Chief Minister Jayalalithaa rose, read a lengthy statement and also gave it to journalists. That long statement was nothing but a series of “adjustments” written and read in the name of an explanation to what I had written, with evidence piled one upon another, that the first day of Thai alone is Tamil New Year, and to my demonstration of how mistaken all Jayalalithaa’s replies were. At their foundation, none of them contains truth.

At the Government function Jayalalithaa said that during AIADMK rule they conducted two World Tamil Conferences in order to spread Tamil throughout the world. I therefore asked: did we not conduct two World Tamil Conferences during DMK rule too; were those not intended to spread Tamil throughout the world? Unable to give a direct answer, Jayalalithaa said in the Assembly that the conference held at Coimbatore was a “selfish self-trumpeting conference” conducted for Karunanidhi’s self-publicity.

Generally, when a statement is read under Rule 110 in the Assembly, or when some member raises a doubt and a reply is given, the tradition is that accusations should not be included. Are you asking what connection tradition has with the AIADMK Government? Tradition or mud-clod—what do they care? Still, when the Chief Minister herself has read it in the Assembly, can I remain without giving an explanation?

In the very benediction sung at the Thanjavur World Tamil Conference conducted by Ammaiyar Jayalalithaa, Jayalalithaa’s name was praised in many places. But at the Coimbatore conference conducted by the DMK Government, the Tamil Thai benediction was written by me, and there was no praise of me in that song. At the Thanjavur conference conducted by Ammaiyar Jayalalithaa, a number of Sri Lankan Tamil scholars who had come—including K. Sivathamby—were not even permitted to participate. Indeed, newspapers at the time reported that foreign Tamil scholars were driven away.

But at the conference conducted by the DMK Government at Coimbatore, overseas scholars including George Hart, Asko Parpola, Vacek, K. Sivathamby, Ulrike Niklas, Gregory James, Alexander Dubiansky, Andras Katholios, Kalaiyarasi from China and Thomas Lehmann came and offered their appreciation. They sat in the front row. Yet Jayalalithaa mocked that my family members and the DMK’s “eminent Tamil scholars” sat in the front row. Is my family sitting in the front row some sinful thing worthy of reproach? Are there no Tamil scholars in the DMK? Do all the eminent Tamil scholars exist only in the AIADMK?

Jayalalithaa asks whether my family should sit in the front row. But at an event held in the Legislative Assembly, did she not bring her non-born sister and, contrary to convention, seat her in the place where the Deputy Speaker should sit? In what way was that proper? Jayalalithaa herself sat in the Speaker’s chair; Jayalalithaa must explain what kind of tradition that was.

At the University of Madras Centenary Auditorium, Jayalalithaa had said that whichever star occurs on the full-moon day gives its name to the month. I asked in response: apart from Chithirai and Karthigai, are not all the other month names different from the names of stars? Replying in the Assembly, Chief Minister Jayalalithaa tried, as usual, to manage the matter by saying those names had changed in form and become month names. She said, for example, Visakam became Vaikasi; Anusham became Aani; Pooradam became Aadi; Thiruvonam became Aavani; Mrigasirsham became Margazhi; Poosam became Thai; Magam became Maasi; and Uthiram became Panguni. If Tamil scholars can accept this display of great intellect, very well! She says these words changed just as Chennapattinam became Chennai. She has made a fine attempt to put flowers in our ears!

I ask further: Jayalalithaa says that all these month names arose by alteration from the names of stars. If the words changed, why are the star names alone still pronounced in their old forms? Should not the star names also have changed in usage?

Tolkappiyar says, “திங்கள் முன்வரின் இக்கே சாரியை”—that month names ending in *i* (Aani, Aadi, Aavani, Purattasi, Aippasi, Margazhi, Maasi, Panguni) take the suffix *ikku*—and also says, “திங்களும் நாளும் முந்து கிளந்தன்ன,” that month names ending in *ai* (Chithirai, Karthigai, Thai) take *ikku* in the manner stated before. Does Jayalalithaa therefore say that all these names had already undergone such alteration in Tolkappiyar’s time?

Likewise, month names occur throughout Sangam literature. Does that mean, as Jayalalithaa says, that the names of the stars had already become these month names even before the Sangam works?

Because Ammaiyar Jayalalithaa had quoted in her speech an article by Siruvai Nachinarkkiniyan in the souvenir of the Fifth World Tamil Conference, I replied by reproducing, without changing a line, what the same Siruvai Nachinarkkiniyan wrote in the opening part of that very article about Thai—especially his sentence, “Let us reckon the month of Thai as the first month of the year and put it into practice.” Ammaiyar now turns her back completely, saying she did not speak about the view Siruvai Nachinarkkiniyan expressed on the earlier page. Did Chief Minister Jayalalithaa mention Nachinarkkiniyan at the Centenary Auditorium or not? Did that same Nachinarkkiniyan say that Thai could be put into practice as the first month of the year or not? The DMK Government did nothing concocted on its own; it said that it was bringing the law after accepting what Tamil scholars had said.

Jayalalithaa says that the book *Vaazhviyal Kalanjiyam* merely contains the saying “When Thai is born, a way is born,” and does not say “When Thai is born, the New Year is born”! What can one do? Perhaps the author of that book did not foresee that people such as Jayalalithaa would one day become Chief Minister and raise doubts of this kind! But when I wrote my reply, I pointed out in bold letters that the same book says that the Tamil year begins on the first day of Thai and that this is celebrated as a festival. Ignoring this meaning, Jayalalithaa has stated in the Assembly, contrary to truth, that the book contains only “When Thai is born, a way is born.”

Next, Jayalalithaa cited the *Senthamizh Sorpirappiyal Peragaramuthali* and read in the Assembly: “Mr. Karunanidhi says that the book states that Thai is the first month of the Tamil year. Conveniently, Mr. Karunanidhi concealed the note on page 113 saying, ‘This information was compiled from the book *Tamil Aandu*.’”

I had pointed out that the *Senthamizh Sorpirappiyal Peragaramuthali*, published by the Tamil Nadu Government at Government expense and with Chief Minister Jayalalithaa’s foreword, itself gives for Thai the meanings “first month of the Tamil year” and “first month of the Thiruvalluvar year.” Jayalalithaa says I concealed that the information was compiled from a book called *Tamil Aandu*. When a dictionary gives a meaning, it explains from what book the material has been drawn. What is wrong in that?

Indeed, Ammaiyar has quoted only half the sentence. I will give the complete sentence: “This information was compiled from *Thiruvalluvar Aandu or Tamil Aandu*, written by Thirukkural Mani Pulavar Iraikkuruvanar.” Does not Ammaiyar Jayalalithaa thereby herself accept another piece of evidence for what I said—that a scholar such as Iraikkuruvanar too accepted Thai as the first month of the Tamil year? Does this not confirm that I enacted the law only after accepting suggestions made by scholars such as Iraikkuruvanar? They included that meaning in the dictionary only because they accepted Iraikkuruvanar’s view. Did not Chief Minister Jayalalithaa read the book fully before contributing her foreword? Can she now change that very view?

Tamil scholars, poets and learned people of Tamil Nadu: weigh Ammaiyar’s argument and my explanation like an even balance and tell us yourselves—which is correct?

Chief Minister Jayalalithaa next raised what she considers a very clear doubt. Citing a speech I made in the Assembly in 1963, she asked why, if Thiruvalluvar was born in Thai, I did not at that time demand that the day be declared a holiday. Had Tamil scholars and poets placed this demand before me before 1963, I could have spoken of it then. If one asked them, they might reply: how were we to know that after Anna’s death in 1969 you would become Chief Minister?

I answered this very question clearly at the Mylapore meeting. People once said, “The sun rises in the east and sets in the west.” Only later did scientists say that this statement was wrong, that the sun remains in one place while the Earth rotates on its axis and revolves around the sun; after that we accepted it. Likewise, people once said the Earth was flat. After scientists discovered and explained otherwise, we accepted that the Earth is round. Can one ask, “Why did you not say this earlier?”

Nor is the issue which year a decision was taken. Mr. Sami Thyagarasan, who had written an article in the *Dinamani* daily, even wrote me a letter saying there was no evidence that a decision was taken at Pachaiyappa’s College in 1921 under the leadership of Maraimalai Adigal. More important than when a decision was taken is what decision was taken. More important is who took it. It was on that basis that the DMK Government acted.

The principal deficiency here is that Tamils do not possess a properly ordered history of their own. This shortcoming has been recognised not only by me but by many of our Tamil scholars and professors of history.

In engineer V. Varadarasan’s book *Tamilar Nagarigam*, it is stated that in 1921, at Pachaiyappa’s College in Chennai, Tamil scholars, learned men and poets assembled under the leadership of Tamil-kadal Maraimalai Adigal and, after deliberation, decided to follow a continuous year count in the name of Thiruvalluvar; that Thiruvalluvar’s period was 31 BCE; and that this was to be the beginning of the Thiruvalluvar Year, beginning on the first day of Thai, the Pongal festival.

At the anti-Hindi conference held in Chennai in September 1937, in an assembly attended by Thanthai Periyar, Umamaheswaranar, Professor Ka. Subramaniam, Te. Po. Meenakshisundaranar, Tamil Thendral Thiru. Vi. Ka., Maraimalai Adigal, P.T. Rajan, Arcot Ramasamy Mudaliar, Revolutionary Poet Bharathidasan, Pattukkottai Azhagiri and others, Navalar Somasundara Bharathiar stressed in his presidential address that the first day of Thai should be the beginning of Tamil New Year.

At the thirtieth meeting of the Tamil Nadu Pulavar Kuzhu held at Tiruchi on 9 May 1971 under the leadership of Muthamizh Kaavalar Ki. Aa. Pe. Viswanatham, it was decided that the first day of Thai would be Tamil New Year and Pongal festival.

Further, during the last two days our *Viduthalai* daily has carried a lengthy article by the noted scientist Dr. Na. Ganesan, working at NASA in the United States, giving detailed explanations on this subject and listing the poets and Tamil scholars who had stated in the past that the first day of Thai is Tamil New Year.

It was on the basis of suggestions made by all these scholars—without worrying over the particular year in which each suggestion was made, but because the truth that the first day of Thai is Tamil New Year had been accepted from every side—that in 2008, after learning and examining all this, I took the decision and announced it.

Yet Chief Minister Jayalalithaa’s Assembly statement says that I enacted the law without properly studying anything, arbitrarily and capriciously. It is Ammaiyar’s present act of changing Tamil New Year from the first day of Thai to Chithirai that was done without examination and out of animosity. It is she who has acted arbitrarily and capriciously, in a manner that diverts the Tamil people from their course. One day the history of Tamil Nadu will certainly establish that truth!

To attempt to erase the true identity of Tamils is injustice! To try to invent false justification for it, while a few mean men set out beating their shoulders in boastfulness, is lawlessness. True Tamils will soon, and surely, understand this!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**22 April 2012**
