**Udanpirappē,**

Thirteen thousand contract workers of Neyveli Lignite Corporation have been on strike since 21 April 2012, pressing ten demands. In my Udanpirappu letter in *Murasoli* dated 8 May 2012, I wrote that even if the AIADMK Government in the State remained indifferent, the Union Government and the minister concerned should immediately contact the Neyveli management, call the agitating workers for talks, seek an amicable settlement and that the Prime Minister should take the necessary steps.

During the Assembly session, after Question Hour on 3 May 2012, members of the Communist parties said Neyveli workers were agitating over several demands, including regularisation, and that the Union Government did not appear to be taking action to end the agitation. They therefore asked the State Government to intervene. Labour Welfare Minister C.T. Chellapandian immediately replied that the NLC workers’ agitation did not fall within the jurisdiction of the Tamil Nadu Government and that the State therefore could not intervene. Yet on 8 May, the same minister said in the Assembly that the Union Labour Commission should hold talks and resolve the issue, that the DMK—being part of the Union Government—had not pressed for a solution, and that the Tamil Nadu Government would urge the Union Government to settle the NLC workers’ agitation.

The contract-labour problem at Neyveli is one that recurs roughly once every two years. In 1994, through the efforts of the Labour Progressive Federation, the problem was partly resolved. An Industrial Co-operative Service Society—**Neyveli Lignite Corporation Industrial Co-operative Service Society**—was formed. Five thousand contract workers were enrolled in it; intermediaries—**Contractors**—were removed; and the co-operative society itself paid wages. Later, whenever permanent vacancies arose, contract workers were regularised, and around 4,000 now work as permanent employees. About 800 remain in the co-operative society without regularisation.

After 1994, the management avoided enrolling later contract workers in the co-operative society, allowing intermediaries to regain influence. This led to a strike in 2008 during DMK rule, again involving about 13,000 workers. The DMK Government did not say this was none of the State’s concern. As Chief Minister, I intervened, and on 16 June 2008 a tripartite agreement was reached before the Chief Labour Commissioner in Delhi between the unions and management. It provided for enrolling 5,000 contract workers in the co-operative society and for a monthly wage increase of ₹750. The strike was withdrawn and workers returned to duty.

When the management later delayed registration in the society, the contract workers again struck in 2010. I wrote to Prime Minister Manmohan Singh on 15 October 2010. The management was called for talks and agreed to a monthly increase of ₹1,040, but only LPF leaders accepted it; other workers continued the strike. I therefore called the union representatives to the Secretariat on 26 October 2010. The meeting included LPF leaders C. Kuppusamy and M. Shanmugam; MLA Velmurugan; contract-union general secretary Anbazhagan; representatives from AITUC and other unions; and, for the Government, Deputy Chief Minister M.K. Stalin, then Electricity Minister Arcot Veerasamy, then Health Minister M.R.K. Panneerselvam and Chief Secretary Malathi, IAS. After negotiations and obtaining management’s consent, the increase was raised from ₹1,040 to ₹1,560, along with several additional concessions. LPF, Pattali Thozhir Sangam, AITUC, CITU, AIUTUC, ATP and INTUC signed the agreement. The union representatives returned to work satisfied and thanked me. That happened in 2010 under DMK rule. What is the position now?

The management now cites pending court cases and delays enrolling contract workers in the co-operative service society, and the workers are therefore agitating. On 9 May 2012, Cuddalore MP K.S. Alagiri met the Union Coal Minister and requested that the company chairman himself hold talks with union functionaries. Though the present State Government did not intervene as I had during DMK rule, a tripartite meeting was held on 10 May at the NLC training complex under the Cuddalore Collector. Four rounds of talks produced no progress.

DMK Parliamentary Party leader T.R. Baalu raised the issue in the Lok Sabha through a calling-attention motion, urging Union intervention. The strike had crossed thirty days. More than 13,000 contract workers were participating. Baalu said neither NLC management nor the Union Government had so far acted; power generation, once 2,490 MW, had fallen to 1,690 MW. In the Rajya Sabha too, Tiruchi N. Siva pressed the issue. D. Raja of the CPI, Tapan Kumar Sen of the CPI(M), and DMK members Vasanthi Stanley and A.A. Jinnah associated themselves with his intervention.

After talks at Neyveli failed, tripartite talks began from the 15th at the office of the Chief Labour Commissioner, Ministry of Labour and Employment, Nungambakkam, Chennai; still no settlement emerged. It was then said that NLC management should take steps to expedite the Supreme Court case pending on regularisation.

I have already described what procedure we followed when Neyveli contract workers struck during DMK rule. But what is the position now? Is there even such a thing as a Tamil Nadu Government? Yet they complain as though there is no Government at the Centre! Talks drag on day after day only at official level. Why have the Chief Minister or the minister not intervened? Should they not have called the union leaders for talks? Why did they not? Full-page advertisements are poured out daily from the tax money paid by the people, apparently so that newspapers will not write about such matters.

I make an appeal to the workers. The Government now in office is not one that will tremble with concern because you are on strike. If the strike continues, it is your families who will suffer. Power generation too may suffer further. Thinking of all this, I ask you to come forward to end the agitation soon and return to work.

When the State itself is without an acting Government, I once again urge the United Progressive Alliance Government at the Centre—speaking as the leader of one of the parties in that Alliance—not to delay further or remain indifferent, but immediately call the management, arrange negotiations with the workers and strive for an amicable settlement.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**25 May 2012**

உடன்பிறப்பே,

நெய்வேலி பழுப்பு நிலக்கரி நிறுவனத்தின் ஒப்பந்தத் தொழிலாளர்கள் 13 ஆயிரம் பேர், பத்து அம்சக் கோரிக்கைகளை வலியுறுத்தி 21-4-2012 முதல் வேலை நிறுத்தத்தில் ஈடுபட்டிருக்கிறார்கள். இந்தப் பிரச்சினை குறித்து 8-5-2012 தேதிய “முரசொலி”யில், உடன்பிறப்பு மடலில் நான் எழுதும்போது, “மாநிலத்தில் உள்ள அ.தி.மு.க. அரசு இந்தப் பிரச்சினையிலே அக்கறையின்றி இருந்தாலும், மத்திய அரசும், இந்தத் துறையின் அமைச்சரும் உடனடியாக நெய்வேலி நிர்வாகத்தோடு தொடர்புகொண்டு போராடுகின்ற தொழிலாளர்களின் கோரிக்கைகளை; அழைத்துப்பேசி சுமுக முடிவுகாண முன்வர வேண்டுமென்றும், அதற்கு பிரதமர் அவர்கள் ஆவன செய்திட வேண்டும்” என்றும் கேட்டுக்கொண்டேன்.

நெய்வேலி நிலக்கரி நிறுவனத்தில் இந்த ஒப்பந்தத் தொழிலாளர்கள் பிரச்சினை என்பது இரண்டு ஆண்டுக்கு ஒருமுறை தொடர்கின்ற ஒன்றாகும். நெய்வேலி நிலக்கரி நிறுவனம் தொடங்கப்பட்ட காலந்தொட்டு பணிபுரிந்து ஒப்பந்தப் பிரச்சினை 1994ஆம் ஆண்டில் தொழிலாளர் முன்னேற்றச் சங்கப் பேரவையின் முயற்சியின் காரணமாக ஓரளவு தீர்த்து வைக்கப்பட்டது. அதன் அடிப்படையில் தொழில்கூட்டுறவு சேவை சங்கம் தொடங்கப்பட்டு (Neyveli Lignite Corporation Industrial Co-operative Service Society) அதில் 5000 ஒப்பந்தத் தொழிலாளர்கள் பதிவு செய்து, அதன் மூலம் இடைத்தரகர்களை (Contractors) நீக்கி, அவர்களுக்கு கூட்டுறவு சங்கமே ஊதியம் வழங்கி வந்தது. பின்னர் நிர்வாகத்தில் பணி காலியாகும் பொழுதெல்லாம், ஒப்பந்தத் தொழிலாளர்களை நிரந்தரப்படுத்தி சுமார் 4000 தொழிலாளர்கள் தற்போது நிரந்தரத் தொழிலாளர்களாகப் பணியாற்றி வருகின்றார்கள். மீதமுள்ள சுமார் 800 பேர் கூட்டுறவு சங்கத்தில் நிரந்தரப்படுத்தப்படாமல் பணியாற்றி வருகின்றார்கள்.

1994க்குப்பின், தொடர்ந்து ஒப்பந்தத் தொழிலாளர்களாக வந்தவர்களை கூட்டுறவு சங்கத்திலே உறுப்பினர்களாக நிர்வாகம் தவிர்த்துவிட்டதால், மீண்டும் இடைத்தரகர்களின் ஆதிக்கம் தொடர ஆரம்பித்தது. இதனால் 2008ஆம் ஆண்டில் தி.மு.கழக ஆட்சியின் போது போராட்டம் நடைபெற்றது. அப்போதும் 13 ஆயிரம் தொழிலாளர்கள் அந்தப் போராட்டத்தில் ஈடுபட்டனர்.
