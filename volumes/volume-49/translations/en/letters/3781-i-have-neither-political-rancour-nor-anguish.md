# 3781. I Have Neither Political Rancour nor Anguish!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

Chief Minister Jayalalithaa has issued a statement under the headline: “Victory for the Tamil Nadu Government in the NLC issue — newspapers praise it — Jayalalithaa replies to Karunanidhi’s accusation.” It surprises me that the Chief Minister has responded so quickly—within just four days—to the statement I issued on the nineteenth. I am pleased; I thank her. But that long reply reflects anger and haste rather than an argument supported by reason and explanation. I wish to offer my explanations once again so that at least neutral readers who study both her statement and mine may understand the truth.

At the very beginning of her statement, the Chief Minister says that I issued my statement “out of jealousy that Jayalalithaa has earned a good name” and “because of political rancour,” and that it represents “the height of anguish.”

In my statement of 22 June 2013, I referred to the Chief Minister’s own suggestion in an earlier letter to the Union Government: either amend the Securities Contracts law to exempt Neyveli Lignite Corporation, or remove NLC from the list of companies whose shares may be sold. I then asked the Union Cabinet to take that suggestion into account and withdraw its decision. Does citing the Chief Minister’s own view amount to political rancour?

Why, even earlier, at the very beginning of my *Murasoli* letter of 26 May 2013, I wrote that Chief Minister Jayalalithaa had informed the Prime Minister on 23 May that she understood the Union Government was planning to sell a further five per cent of its shares in Neyveli Lignite Corporation; that the proposal was undesirable; and that she had asked it to explore alternatives instead. I also wrote that her letter had appeared in every newspaper. Is that an example of political rancour and anguish?

I have not the slightest rancour or anguish about the Chief Minister’s actions in the NLC share-sale issue. Political rancour is seen instead in keeping the Secretariat complex built by the DMK Government at a cost of ₹600 crore closed for three years; preventing the Classical Tamil Institute from raising its head; blocking the Maduravoyal–Port Express Highway; putting the Sethusamudram Canal Project to sleep; and preventing the Anna Centenary Library from functioning properly. None of the questions I have raised about Neyveli Lignite Corporation arose from political rancour.

Jayalalithaa says in her statement that Karunanidhi has “beaten his own drum” by claiming that he alone stopped the Union Government when it decided in 2006 to sell ten per cent of NLC shares. She once again praises herself, saying that the Union Government abandoned the privatisation decision only because of the AIADMK’s severe opposition and the Neyveli workers’ hunger strike.

I ask one simple question. In 2006 the Union Government first decided to sell NLC shares and then withdrew that decision during the DMK Government. This time, the Union Government’s decision has not been withdrawn in the same way. Whose victory is this? Whose defeat?

Honourable Chief Minister, in your statement dated 29 June 2013 you signed and declared that the Union Government withdrew its NLC decision because the AIADMK had held a massive protest demonstration. Can you tell us the date on which that demonstration was held? The AIADMK held no demonstration over the Neyveli issue in 2006. May a Chief Minister provide information so false and contrary to fact?

What exactly happened at Neyveli in 2006?

On 23 June 2006, *Dinamani* reported: “Over the objections of parties in the ruling alliance from Tamil Nadu, the Union Government has decided to sell NLC shares.” On 24 June, the same newspaper reported that the Neyveli trade-union federation had decided on a one-day token strike against the sale, quoting DMK trade-union leader Rasavanniyan. The same issue reported opposition from the Communist Party of India (Marxist), the PMK and the MDMK. There was no opposition from the AIADMK.

On the following day, 25 June, the Communist Party of India’s opposition was reported, as was the workers’ decision to begin an indefinite strike on 4 July. The same *Dinamani* also carried DMDK leader Vijayakanth’s suggestion that the Tamil Nadu Government itself could purchase the NLC shares. Thus even the idea that the State Government might buy the shares was expressed in 2006 by Vijayakanth; it is not an original idea of today’s Chief Minister. Had our Chief Minister known that the DMDK leader had already advanced it, she might not have written the same suggestion to the Union Government now!

On 25 June 2006, as Chief Minister, I wrote to the Prime Minister asking that the NLC share-sale decision be reconsidered. On 4 July I told journalists that the DMK would participate in the workers’ strike. Jayalalithaa had still not opened her mouth on the Neyveli issue.

Only after all this—on 27 June, five days after the Union Government’s decision of 22 June—did the AIADMK General Secretary speak. Even then she said merely that the DMK Ministers should resign from the Union Cabinet over the NLC matter. She also said it was laughable for Karunanidhi to announce that the DMK trade union would join the workers’ struggle, and declared that the Anna trade union would participate. This was reported in *Dinamani* on 28 June.

On 26 June, trade-union leaders met me in Chennai and thanked me. They also visited the offices of the Congress, the CPI(M) and the CPI and thanked their leaders. In continuation, some union leaders met Jayalalithaa on 28 June and invited her to inaugurate their struggle.

At that time the Prime Minister indicated that the Union Government was unable to retreat from selling the shares to private buyers. On 2 July I wrote to him: “The trade unions have announced an indefinite strike. The sale of ten per cent of the shares to private parties must be reconsidered. If the Union Government remains determined to sell, the shares may instead be offered to the workers of the undertaking.” As now, the Prime Minister was inclined to accept my suggestion then; but the workers were not prepared to accept it.

Therefore, on 4 July I spoke by telephone with my friend, Union Finance Minister P. Chidambaram. He assured me that he would speak to the Prime Minister. In an urgent letter sent to the Prime Minister the same day, I explained that the workers did not accept priority in the purchase of shares and insisted that the NLC disinvestment itself be abandoned.

In a statement dated 3 July, Jayalalithaa said she could not come to Neyveli to participate in the protest, though she supported it. When no reply had arrived to my letter of 4 July and the workers’ agitation had begun, I met the press on 6 July in my capacity as Chief Minister and said:

> “If the Union Government is in a position where it cannot fulfil the workers’ demand in the Neyveli issue, must the DMK remain in that Government and accept responsibility for its decision? We are considering this. The DMK Ministers in Delhi will convey it to the Prime Minister.”

After my announcement, the Union Government declared that it was putting the NLC share-sale decision on hold. On 7 July, *Dinamani* reported on its front page that the decision was announced in Delhi within hours of Tamil Nadu Chief Minister and DMK President M. Karunanidhi’s stern warning in Chennai on the morning of 6 July that, if the Union Government could not accept the workers’ demand, the DMK might have to decide to leave the Union Government. It also reported that the Prime Minister’s Office had issued a statement.

None of these reports appeared only in *Murasoli*. They appeared in *Dinamani*. Yet, suppressing all this, a Chief Minister has said in her statement of 29 June 2013 that the Union Government withdrew the NLC sale in 2006 because of a protest she conducted. I have set out these details so that journalists and neutral observers may understand how greatly that claim departs from the truth.

The Chief Minister next says that, unable to tolerate political parties praising her efforts, DMK President Karunanidhi has tried to deceive and console himself.

After citing what I had written, one would expect the Chief Minister to list the political-party leaders who praised her effort. I searched and searched through her long statement; not one name appears.

Only her party newspaper reported, without giving a meaningful list, that leaders including the Samathuva Makkal Katchi leader and the Kongu Youth Federation leader had expressed praise and thanks to “Amma.” Are these, according to their dictionary, all the political-party leaders of Tamil Nadu?

The Chief Minister wrote all this, but why did she not answer my point that the ruling party’s paper alone carried fifty-five pages of advertisements on 16 July declaring an “Himalayan victory” for Jayalalithaa, while other papers too carried page after page of advertisements? Is it because that is true?

An agreement in the Neyveli issue was reached only on the evening of 15 July, and workers returned to duty at seven that night. The news appeared only in the morning papers of the sixteenth. Yet the ruling party newspaper published on its front page that very morning the Chief Minister’s statement calling it “a magnificent victory achieved through my personal effort.” How, in that same paper, did fifty-five pages of advertisements praising and thanking the Chief Minister already appear? With no time available, were those advertisements genuine expressions of praise?

Now let me remind you what leaders of all parties said when the Union Government first decided in 2006 to sell NLC shares and later withdrew the decision during the DMK Government.

CITU General Secretary Chittabrata Majumdar, MP, said in a statement on 6 July 2006: “We record and convey our appreciation to Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Kalaignar for intervening immediately and acting to make the Union Government take this decision.”

CPI(M) Tamil Nadu State Secretary Comrade N. Varadarajan met me in person at noon on 6 July and praised me. He said: “All six crore Tamil people are happy and appreciative on seeing Chief Minister Kalaignar’s announcement on the NLC share sale. This action by Kalaignar will be a driving force in protecting public-sector enterprises.” The party’s General Secretary, Prakash Karat, also said in a statement that day: “We convey our appreciation for the stand taken by Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Kalaignar that shares in public-sector enterprises must not be sold.”

Dravidar Kazhagam President and Tamilar Thalaivar K. Veeramani expressed happiness and praised the manner in which I had handled the Neyveli problem.

CPI State Secretary Comrade D. Pandian said: “The Communist Party of India welcomes Chief Minister Kalaignar’s decision expressing the feelings of the people of Tamil Nadu.”

In a statement on 7 July, PMK founder Dr Ramadoss said: “Chief Minister Kalaignar too made a major effort to change this decision. As a result of these efforts, the Union Government has changed its decision.”

Forward Bloc Secretary G. Devarajan issued a statement of praise. In addition, resolutions appreciating me were adopted at the CPI National Council meeting in Hyderabad and the CPI(M) State Executive Committee meeting in Chennai.

On 7 July 2006, thirty-six representatives of eighteen Neyveli workers’ unions met me at Anna Arivalayam and thanked me.

I did not then claim that it was “a victory achieved by my personal effort.” When journalists asked, I said with restraint: “This is a victory for sweat and truth.” Not even a single page of congratulatory advertising appeared in *Murasoli*, our party newspaper.

I say again that I certainly have no anguish, political rancour, jealousy or frustration, as the Chief Minister alleges. But she herself will accept, I trust, that she always has an eagerness to say everything happened because of her. Whatever the issue, she speaks of “the Government under my leadership,” “my personal effort,” and “the Chief Minister’s order.” That gives her pleasure and pride.

Even when the Assembly is in session, every announcement—whatever the department—is read by the Chief Minister under Rule 110. No other Minister receives that opportunity. Even when an AIADMK member wishes to display a poster, put up a hoarding or publish an advertisement, that person’s own photograph must not appear; only the Chief Minister’s photograph is displayed. I cannot object to this. It is their preference. But to say that I am jealous or anguished because of it is an unadulterated lie. Every newspaper that happily published her statement in full must understand this.

Had Chief Minister Jayalalithaa not written to the Prime Minister offering that Tamil Nadu public-sector undertakings were prepared to purchase the NLC shares, is it true or not that the Union Government might itself have withdrawn the sale, as in 2006 under the DMK Government, because of the united and total struggle of the Neyveli workers? Many believe that this opportunity was lost because of the Chief Minister’s hurried letter.

The Chief Minister concluded her long statement with a *Tirukkural*. I remind her of another couplet by Valluvar:

> “The king who does not daily inquire and govern by due order—
> his country will decline day by day.”

Its meaning is that a country will fall into ruin when its Government does not examine every day the benefits and harms caused by its rule and act accordingly.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**24 July 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. **NLC** refers to Neyveli Lignite Corporation, now NLC India Limited.
2. **Rule 110** refers to the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly procedure under which a Minister may make a statement without an immediate debate.
3. The quoted *Tirukkural* is `நாடொறும் நாடி முறை செய்யா மன்னவன் / நாடொறும் நாடு கெடும்`.
4. One erroneous internal space in `கட்சி` on PDF page 142 was corrected after visual comparison with the scan.
