# 3592. I State My Wish!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 5 October 2011

**Udanpirappē,**

Comrade Ramakrishnan, State Secretary of the Communist Party of India (Marxist), who had until now been speaking only in attacks on the DMK, has now sharply attacked the ruling AIADMK as well at a meeting in Chidambaram. *Dinamalar*, taking only one portion of his speech, reported him as saying:

> “We (CPI(M)) are the first front to contest the local-body elections through an alliance. Without us, the AIADMK could not have come to power now. She has kicked away the ladder that helped her climb. Jayalalithaa is acting as though she has forgotten that we won the Assembly election by forming a strong alliance.”

Why, even today's *Theekkathir* has written:

> “The movement asserting that governments must not kill their own citizens is gaining strength worldwide. But in Tamil Nadu, in complete contrast, state violence is running rampant. Instead of reforming the police, Jayalalithaa staged in the Assembly itself an act of sharpening their horns and letting them loose. As a continuing consequence, the police have begun behaving like an unbridled horse. In the five months since the AIADMK government assumed office, eight custodial deaths have occurred. This has caused great shock and outrage among human-rights activists. During the last one and a half years of the previous AIADMK government, eight custodial deaths occurred. But under the present AIADMK government, they have set a record by killing eight people in police stations within five months. There are widespread complaints that, ever since the AIADMK government assumed office, the police have continuously engaged in excesses. The media exaggerate and report that crime is lower whenever the AIADMK is in power. But the truth is the opposite.”

That is what has been written.

As in every year, Jayalalithaa today announced an increase in the procurement price of paddy. In a statement issued today, the State General Secretary of the Tamil Nadu Farmers' Association, affiliated with the Communist Party, said:

> “We strongly condemn the attitude of the Tamil Nadu government in failing to take into account the farmers' associations' continuing demand that ₹1,500 per quintal be paid for paddy. We point out that this incentive announced by the Tamil Nadu government will not help protect the interests of farmers.”

Yet Comrade Ramakrishnan, speaking at the Chidambaram meeting, unnecessarily lashed out at us too, saying, “It is our duty to ensure that the DMK suffers a crushing defeat in this election.” Why does Ramakrishnan say that the DMK must be made to suffer a crushing defeat? Is the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam ideologically opposed to the Communist Party of India (Marxist)? Even if not one hundred per cent, are these things unknown to Ramakrishnan—that, as an opposition party, the DMK campaigned for ninety-five per cent of the policies of the Marxist Communist Party, then fought for those policies to succeed, and, when in government, implemented those policies through legislation? Would the other Marxist comrades not know this?

In 1971, under the DMK government, for the first time in India, the “Occupants of Kudiyiruppu (Conferment of Ownership) Act” was enacted, making the house sites in which more than one lakh agricultural labourers lived their own property. At the function held in Thiruthuraipoondi to mark this, veteran Communist leader Comrade Manali Kandasamy said, “What the Communists could not accomplish even by shedding blood, Karunanidhi has accomplished with a single drop of ink.” Is this something Comrade Ramakrishnan does not know?

When Prime Minister Indira Gandhi came forward to nationalise the banks, the DMK supported her despite opposition from the Syndicate Congress leaders. From 1969 to 1971, when I was Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu, we nationalised bus services, created the transport corporations, established the Slum Clearance Board, abolished hand-pulled rickshaws—which had not even then been abolished in West Bengal—and provided free cycle rickshaws. Were these measures contrary to the policies of the Marxist Communist Party?

A government that wishes to create a socialist social order must not treat land reform as mere eyewash; it must be committed to introducing progressive laws step by step. On that basis, under the DMK government we brought in the land-ceiling law, identified and acquired 1 lakh 78 thousand 880 acres as surplus land, and distributed it to 1 lakh 37 thousand 236 eligible poor landless farmers. Of them, 61 thousand 985 were Adi Dravidars and 204 were tribal people. Is that a policy opposed to communism? Does Comrade Ramakrishnan say that all these measures are contrary to the policies of the Marxist Communist Party?

It was precisely as a consequence of the Marxist principles I followed that I rendered the Russian writer Maxim Gorky's epic *Mother* in poetic form, and veteran Communist leaders Comrade N. Sankaraiah and Comrade R. Nallakannu wrote forewords for it. If Comrade Ramakrishnan were simply to read those forewords once, he could easily understand my commitment to communism.

Why stop there? At many meetings in which Comrade Ramakrishnan himself participated with me, when I spoke I said that, had I not met Thanthai Periyar and Perarignar Anna in my life, I would have been a Communist. He could not have forgotten that!

Am I like today's Chief Minister Jayalalithaa—did I invite Gujarat Chief Minister Narendra Modi to an oath-taking ceremony? Or, when he recently undertook a three-day fast, did I, like Jayalalithaa, send two Members of Parliament on behalf of the AIADMK to express support? Yet even after seeing all this, was I the one who came forward seeking an understanding with that AIADMK for the local-body elections, wandered from place to place, was made to wait for three days without even being given drinking water—as the newspapers reported—and returned empty-handed when the bargaining failed?

When I was Chief Minister, leaders of this very Marxist Communist Party met me at Fort St. George and complained that Jayalalithaa and those close to her had taken over land reserved for Dalits in Siruthavur village and driven them away. As soon as they asked me to order a judicial inquiry, I accepted their demand and appointed the Justice Sivasubramaniam Commission of Inquiry. Did I instead ignore them by saying that this Marxist demand was contrary to the policies of our party? Why, then, has Comrade Ramakrishnan said that the DMK must be made to suffer a crushing defeat?

How many measures compatible with the Marxist Communist Party did I announce and implement one after another during DMK rule? With the aim of creating a Tamil Nadu without huts, we announced a housing programme and arrangements began for constructing three lakh houses. What has happened to all those houses now? Do the Communists not know?

A paid holiday for May Day; May Day Park in Chennai; abolition of the confidential-report system for government employees; free housing for fishermen; legislation giving women property rights; direct procurement from farmers; 33 per cent reservation for women in local-body elections; the Periyar Memorial Samathuvapuram scheme; various welfare boards for all unorganised workers; a welfare-board law for agricultural labourers; the Namakku Naame scheme; a pension scheme for transport workers; legislation enabling people of all castes to become temple priests; cancellation of farmers' loans; the Samacheer Kalvi law—the list can go on and on. Are these not all programmes compatible with Marxist principles? Why does Comrade Ramakrishnan wish for the DMK to suffer a crushing defeat?

Even though Comrade Ramakrishnan has spoken in this manner, I am certain that the other comrades in that party will not accept it. The Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam and the Communist Party of India (Marxist) are not parties that differ fundamentally in ideology. From time to time, electoral relationships and agreements may fail to materialise. In one election we may be in separate fronts; in another we may be in the same front. Therefore, my wish is that, at least from now on, Comrade Ramakrishnan should keep in mind that these are not parties that must fundamentally stand in opposing camps, and that winning one or two seats in an election is not what matters most, before expressing such views!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**5 October 2011**

எதிரான கொள்கையா? இவைகள் எல்லாம் மார்க்சிஸ்ட்
கம்யூனிஸ்ட் கட்சியின் கொள்கைகளுக்கு விரோதம் என்று தோழர்
ராமகிருஷ்ணன் கூறுகிறாரா?

நான் கடைப்பிடித்த மார்க்சிஸ்ட் கொள்கையின்
விளைவாகத்தான் - மாக்ஸிம் கார்க்கி என்ற ரஷ்ய படைப்பாளியின்
எழுத்தோவியமான “தாய்” காவியத்தை கவிதை வடிவில் எழுதி,
அதற்கு முதுபெரும் கம்யூனிஸ்ட் தலைவர்களான தோழர்கள்
என். சங்கரய்யா அவர்களும், ஆர். நல்லகண்ணு அவர்களும்
அணிந்துரைகள் வழங்கியிருக்கிறார்கள். அந்த அணிந்துரைகளை
தோழர் ராமகிருஷ்ணன் ஒருமுறை படித்துப் பார்த்தாலே
கம்யூனிசத்தில் எனக்குள்ள ஈடுபாட்டினை அவர் எளிதில் புரிந்து
கொள்ள முடியும்.

அவ்வளவு ஏன்? தோழர் ராமகிருஷ்ணன் என்னுடன் கலந்து
கொண்ட எத்தனையோ கூட்டங்களில், நான் பேசும்போது
தந்தை பெரியாரையும், பேரறிஞர் அண்ணாவையும் என்
வாழ்வில் சந்திக்காமல் இருந்திருப்பேனே யானால், நான் ஒரு
கம்யூனிஸ்டாகத்தான் இருந்திருப்பேன் என்று குறிப்பிட்டதை
அவர் மறந்திருக்க முடியாது!

நான் என்ன, இன்றைய முதலமைச்சர் ஜெயலலிதா மாதிரி,
குஜராத் முதல் அமைச்சர் நரேந்திர மோடி அவர்களை பதவியேற்பு
விழாவிற்கு அழைத்தவனா? அல்லது அவர் அண்மையில்
மேற்கொண்ட மூன்று நாள் உண்ணாவிரதத்திற்கு அ.தி.மு.க.
சார்பில் ஜெயலலிதா இரண்டு நாடாளுமன்ற உறுப்பினர்களை
அனுப்பி ஆதரவு தெரிவித்தாரே, அப்படி ஆதரவு கொடுத்தவனா?
ஆனால் இதையெல்லாம் கண்ட பிறகும், அந்த அ.தி.மு.க. வோடு
உள்ளாட்சி மன்றத் தேர்தல்களில் உடன்பாடு கண்டிட முன்
வந்து, அலையாய் அலைந்து மூன்று நாட்கள் பத்திரிகையில்
வந்ததுபோல குடிக்கத் தண்ணீர்கூடக் கொடுக்காமல் அலைக்
கழித்து பேரம் படியாமல் வெறுங்கையோடு திரும்பி வரும்
நிலையை ஏற்படுத்தியவனா?

நிறைவேற்றியிருப்பேன்? தமிழ்நாட்டில் குடிசைகளே இல்லாத
நிலையை உருவாக்க வேண்டும் என்பதற்காக வீடு வழங்கும்
திட்டத்தை அறிவித்து மூன்று இலட்சம் வீடுகள் கட்டுவதற்கான
ஏற்பாடுகள் எல்லாம் நடைபெறத் தொடங்கியதே, தற்போது
அந்த வீடுகள் எல்லாம் என்ன ஆயிற்று? கம்யூனிஸ்ட்களுக்குத்
தெரியாதா?

பல்வேறு நல வாரியங்கள்; விவசாயத் தொழிலாளர்களுக்கு
