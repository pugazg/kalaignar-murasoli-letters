# 3434. Who Will Know Our Silent Pain?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation preserves the source’s retrospective political argument about Sri Lanka, its named organisations and individuals, quoted interview, descriptions of intra-Tamil conflict, electoral figures and rhetorical grief. All historical and political claims remain explicitly source-framed; no outside account is used to reconcile or correct them.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3434](../../../chapters/3434-nam-mouna-vali-yaarukkuth-theriyap-pogirathu.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 70](../../../pages/page-070.md)–[PDF 75](../../../pages/page-075.md)  
**Date:** 18 November 2009

**Udanpirappē,**

Since 1956, together with the voice of Eelam Thanthai Selva, the voice raised by the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam in South India over the Sri Lankan issue —

our arguments that democracy must bloom in Sri Lanka —

our moral, non-violent struggles —

our acts of sacrifice that filled the prisons —

the occasions when Legislative Assembly memberships were thrown away —

why, even the historic events in which we lost power twice —

the untainted money collected from Tamils and given as funds on behalf of the DMK, only for those valid coins to become worthless —

and, on behalf of the “TESO” movement, the rallies led district after district by me, Tamil leader Veeramani, Pazha. Nedumaran and Ayyannan Ambalam, followed by the Eelam Tamil Support Conference held in Madurai on 4-5-1986, in which Amirthalingam participated on behalf of TULF, Chandrahassan on behalf of PROTEK, Rathinasabapathy on behalf of ERAS, Eelaventhan on behalf of TELF, Varadaraja Perumal on behalf of EPRLF, and Vasudevan on behalf of PLOT —

and the fact that, at the all-India level, N.T. Rama Rao, Vajpayee, Bahuguna, Ramwalia, Upendra, Unnikrishnan, Subramanian Swamy and many others took part in that conference —

and that leaders of various militant groups came to the conference, discussed the storms that frequently arose among the militants, and stressed that fraternal unity was needed to stop them —

and that only Thilakar came on behalf of the LTTE, while Prabhakaran did not —

and the occasion on which, while serving as Chief Minister, I refused to go and welcome the Indian Peace Keeping Force when it returned to India, thereby expressing the feeling of Tamil Nadu —

no neutral observer can forget that these were the hand of support extended from our motherland to the liberation struggle in Sri Lanka.

But to some commanders, military leaders and leaders who stood on the liberation front, the support we ourselves went out of our way to give appeared very slight. The views we put forward came to be treated as things that could be ignored. For whatever reason, they disregarded our repeated insistence that, in battlefronts such as these, wisdom had to be used as much as courage.

At a sorrowful time when everything had come to an end and one wondered whether even more would end, even when a democratic opportunity came right up to the doorstep, the mistaken act of kicking it away took place. As to what happened and why it happened, it is not I, nor people like me, who now say it. Here is what our dear friend — self-respecting comrade and one who stands tall among Tamil writers, brother Gopal — was told in an interview for the 18-11-2009 issue of *Nakkheeran* by Ranil Wickremesinghe, leader of Sri Lanka’s United National Party, current Leader of the Opposition and former Prime Minister of Sri Lanka, during his recent visit to Chennai:

> “In one sense, Prabhakaran too was responsible for the consequences of this war. Whenever attempts were made to find a political solution to the problems of the Tamils, Prabhakaran avoided them. He withdrew on his own from the peace talks held in 2003. In the talks held in Tokyo in 2005, he dragged matters out without even stating what the Tamils’ demands were. In the end, he avoided participating in them. Further, in the presidential election held in 2005, Prabhakaran made the Tamil people boycott the election and prevented Tamils from participating in the voting. If they had taken part in the election at that time, it would have been possible to know the state of mind of the Tamils. Prabhakaran failed to give the Tamil people that democratic opportunity.”

If Ranil’s words are examined closely, one can understand the argument that the reason was the LTTE’s failure to pursue its war strategy with a view to future consequences.

To think about the situation that resulted — among the consequences of this liberation war, because of fratricidal war —

the LTTE itself imposed and carried out the death sentence on the warrior Mahaththaya;

TELO’s Sri Sabaratnam was made a corpse;

Padmanabha and ten militants with him were killed;

and, at the highest point of these events, senior Amirthalingam, who had stood as a companion of the militants from the early days, and Jotheeswaran were sacrificed;

Mano Master, also known as Panchalingam of the TELO movement, that movement’s leader Kulasekaram Devasekaram, distinguished political scholar Neelan Tiruchelvam, Sivasanmuga Moorthy known as Sundaram, George, Sabalingam, Sam Thambimuttu, Kala Thambimuttu, and Yotheeswaran and Vasudeva of the PLOT movement were all pushed into the pit of death;

the history of that battlefront still speaks and laments that these became acts in which they themselves planned and destroyed their own companions.

I am not writing all this to accuse or find fault with anyone. In Sri Lanka in 2004, within a few days of the parliamentary election and the formation of a new government, a fratricidal war broke out in eastern Sri Lanka on 9-4-2004 between the forces of Prabhakaran and Karuna, in which 20 militants and two civilians were killed.

Thus, because of fratricidal war, it was not merely that we killed our own people. Because we failed to act with proper planning, because we did not accurately assess our own strength and the strength of the other side, how many of our Tamil people had to come under attack from the other side and lose their lives? How many young children were burned to death in their tender years? How much pain must their parents have suffered after losing them? How many people lost their property and comforts and had to move from country to country as paupers and wanderers? How many places did they have to roam simply to continue their lives? How many had to languish for years in refugee camps? What was the reason for all this? What, indeed, became the fate of Prabhakaran’s wife, children and family? How many families were there like this?

I have had to write this out of anguish that people who should have lived peacefully for many more years and worked for the advancement of Tamils brought their own lives to an end at a young age. I write in pain that thousands of young shoots who should have lived instead withered away.

On 9-2-1989, the then Prime Minister Rajiv Gandhi called me and brother Maran to Delhi and discussed the LTTE and the Eelam issue with us over two days. Minister of State for External Affairs Natwar Singh then explained the details to us for about two or three hours at Tamil Nadu House. That young leader, Rajiv Gandhi, told us: you, Maran and Vaiko should go to Sri Lanka and stay in Colombo or any place you prefer; speak to Prabhakaran in detail about this issue; set aside as many days as necessary; I will arrange from here whatever is required for you to meet them in Sri Lanka, or for them to come and meet you where you are staying; at the very least, find out in detail what their demands are; I am prepared to fulfil the rights of the Tamil people in Sri Lanka. The fact that Rajiv Gandhi, who gave us all these assurances, was assassinated on Indian soil — and on Tamil soil itself — was a great tragedy. The world has not forgotten that this incident too became like water poured upon the fire of the Eelam liberation struggle.

Here is another event. On 17-11-2005, the election to choose the President of Sri Lanka was held. Thirteen people contested. Mahinda Rajapaksa, who was then Prime Minister, and former Prime Minister Ranil Wickremesinghe were the principal contestants.

The LTTE instructed the minority Tamils to boycott that election. During the election, Ranil said that, if elected President, he would give priority to continuing peace talks with the LTTE. What Ranil said then is what he has now repeated in his interview to *Nakkheeran*:

> “In the presidential election held in 2005, Prabhakaran made the Tamil people boycott the election without taking part in the voting. If they had contributed through the election at that time, it would have been possible to know the state of mind of the Tamils. Prabhakaran failed to give the Tamil people that democratic opportunity.”

In that 2005 election, Rajapaksa, who had been Prime Minister, won as Sri Lanka’s fifth President. Rajapaksa received 48,87,152 votes (50.29 per cent) and Ranil received 47,06,366 votes (48.43 per cent). Ranil lost by about 1 lakh 81 thousand votes.

When one considers that nearly seven lakh Tamil voters did not vote because they boycotted the election, what became of the consequences of the political decision taken in haste on behalf of the LTTE that day? Where did they finally lead? Who is going to hear us when we silently weep while thinking about it? Who will know our silent pain?

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**18-11-2009**
