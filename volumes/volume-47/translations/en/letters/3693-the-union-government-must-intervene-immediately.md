# 3693. The Union Government Must Intervene—Immediately!

## English translation

Udanpirappē,

Lawyers have gone to the Governor of Tamil Nadu and submitted a petition asking that Tamil be recognised as a language of advocacy in the Madras High Court. When the DMK Government was in office, I met journalists on 22 November 2006 and announced:

> “Until now, English alone has been the language used in all proceedings of the Madras High Court. Article 348 of the Constitution states that all proceedings in the High Courts and the Supreme Court shall be conducted in English. Accordingly, English has been used in all proceedings of the Madras High Court. However, Article 348(2) of the Constitution provides that, with the consent of the President, the State Government may take appropriate legal steps for High Court proceedings to be conducted in the State language. Invoking this constitutional provision, the Tamil Nadu Government has decided to introduce our mother tongue Tamil as the language of proceedings in all matters before the Madras High Court.”

My announcement was also issued as an official press release of the Tamil Nadu Government. Both the High Court and the Bar Association welcomed it. The Chief Justice of the High Court supported the decision and himself indicated the steps that would need to be taken. I wrote to him immediately thanking him.

Using the provision made available by the Constitution of India and after obtaining the President's permission, Hindi has been used as the language of proceedings in the Uttar Pradesh High Court.

As soon as my announcement was made, Dr Ramadoss—at that time—issued a statement saying, “Who other than Kalaignar can realise the principle of Tamil everywhere and Tamil in everything? He has made the beginning.”

On behalf of the Tamil forum of High Court advocates, a writ petition on this very issue had been filed in September 2001. The High Court dismissed it at that time. The reason given was that under Article 348(2) of the Constitution, only the Governor had the authority to issue the relevant order; therefore, the Governor had to issue such an order and there was no use arguing the matter before the court. The AIADMK was in power then, but no follow-up action was taken.

Following my announcement of 22 November 2006, on 6 December 2006 I myself moved in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly the Government's resolution seeking Tamil as the language of proceedings in the High Court. That resolution had originally been placed last on the day's agenda. But because AIADMK legislators, the principal opposition at the time, were due to leave in the afternoon by special flight to Delhi for the unveiling of the statue of my dear friend M.G.R., the then Leader of the House, Professor, and I discussed the matter. We changed the order of business so that this important resolution on Tamil could be taken up in the morning, giving them an opportunity to remain in the House and support it.

Despite our doing so, perhaps because they had decided not even to be present when the resolution was passed, neither the AIADMK nor its then ally, the MDMK, was in the House or took part in the debate. Even in their absence, when this important resolution was debated, E.S.S. Raman spoke for the Congress, G.K. Mani for the PMK, Rajakannappan for the DMK and Ku. Selvam for the Viduthalai Chiruthaigal.

The historic resolution passed during the DMK Government on 6 December 2006 read as follows:

> “Until now, English alone has been the language used in all proceedings of the Madras High Court. Under Article 348(1) of the Constitution of India, all proceedings in the High Courts and the Supreme Court are to be conducted in English.
>
> However, under Section 7 of the Official Languages Act, 1963 read with Article 348(2) of the Constitution, provision has been made for the State Government, after obtaining the consent of the Hon'ble President of India, to take appropriate steps for all proceedings of the High Court to be conducted in the official language of the State.
>
> In keeping with the policy that all administrative proceedings in our State should be carried out in Tamil, the Tamil Nadu Government has decided, under Section 7 of the Official Languages Act, 1963 read with Article 348(2) of the Constitution, to introduce Tamil, the official language of our State, in all proceedings of the Madras High Court, including judgments and decrees. This Legislative Assembly unanimously resolves to request the Hon'ble President of India to grant approval at the earliest so that this decision—worthy of being inscribed in golden letters in the history of Tamil Nadu—may be implemented.”

Before I replied to the debate on that resolution, younger brother Duraimurugan, who was then Public Works Minister, congratulated me. He proudly said that his descendants would one day speak of the fact that he had been present in the Assembly when this important resolution was passed.

I had gone to Delhi for the unveiling, on 8 December 2006, of the statue of younger brother Murasoli Maran in the Parliament complex. There, I met President Abdul Kalam at his residence and personally handed over, along with the recommendation of the Governor of Tamil Nadu, a copy of the Government resolution passed on the 6th on “Tamil as the language of proceedings in the High Court.” I requested that he permit the resolution to be implemented in the Madras High Court. The President assured me that he would consider it.

However, in a letter dated 27 February 2007, the Department of Justice in the Union Ministry of Law and Justice stated that the matter had been considered in consultation with the Supreme Court and that the Chief Justice of India was of the view that it would not be appropriate at that time to introduce a regional language in High Court judgments, orders and other proceedings.

On 11 March 2007, I wrote detailed letters on the matter to Prime Minister Manmohan Singh, Home Minister Shivraj Patil and Law Minister Bharadwaj. In that letter I pointed to the precedents of Hindi being used as the regional language in the High Courts of Rajasthan, Bihar, Uttar Pradesh and Madhya Pradesh, and finally requested urgent action to give shape to the long-standing desire of the people of Tamil Nadu and to obtain the President's approval at the earliest.

In reply to my letter of the 11th, Union Law Minister H.R. Bharadwaj wrote back the very next day, the 12th, saying: “I have carefully read your letter and, keeping in mind the points raised by you in this matter, I have asked my Department to reconsider the issue afresh and obtain the approval of the President of India at the earliest.”

On 12 March 2007, Union Ministers P. Chidambaram, T.R. Baalu, A. Raja, S. Regupathy, K. Venkatapathy and Subbulakshmi Jagadeesan, along with Members of Parliament from Tamil Nadu, signed a petition and met the Prime Minister, requesting that the President's approval be obtained for Tamil to be used as the language of proceedings in the Madras High Court.

As a continuation of the steps taken by the DMK Government on this issue, the World Classical Tamil Conference held in Coimbatore in June 2010 passed a resolution stating:

> “For Tamil to be recognised as a language of use in the Madras High Court, the Tamil Nadu Government unanimously passed a resolution in the Legislative Assembly in 2006 and forwarded it to the Union Government together with the recommendation of the Hon'ble Governor of Tamil Nadu and the recommendation of the Hon'ble Chief Justice of the Madras High Court. Therefore, this Conference urges the Union Government to recognise Tamil as a language of use in the Madras High Court immediately and without delay.”

At the same conference, another resolution was passed stating:

> “Making Tamil an official language of the Union has been a long-standing demand of the people of Tamil Nadu. If there is to be delay in making all the State languages of India official languages of the Union, then as a first step the World Classical Tamil Conference urges the Union Government to make our classical Tamil language an official language of the Union.”

Even after all this, because the Supreme Court had expressed an objection, the Union Government at that time could not approve the Tamil Nadu Government's resolution. Even so, because the DMK Government was then in office in Tamil Nadu, hearings in a large number of cases in the Madras High Court were conducted in Tamil. During the DMK Government's tenure, in 2010, then Chief Justice M.Y. Eqbal also issued an order permitting arguments in Tamil in the High Court.

But now, on 3 January, a High Court judge refused to allow an advocate to argue in Tamil and also observed that “the Constitution does not permit argument in Tamil.” It is against this that lawyers have begun an agitation demanding that Tamil be declared a language of advocacy in the High Court. The Union and State Governments must understand that their struggle is entirely just.

In particular, I once again repeatedly urge the Union Government to intervene in this issue and, just as is already the practice in some northern States, obtain the approval of the Supreme Court and secure from the President consent for the legal change—pending for approval since the period of the DMK Government—so that Tamil can lawfully be used as the language of advocacy in the High Court in Tamil Nadu.

With affection,
M.K.
10-01-2013

எனும் அரசின் தீர்மான நகலை, தமிழக ஆளுநர் அவர்களின்
