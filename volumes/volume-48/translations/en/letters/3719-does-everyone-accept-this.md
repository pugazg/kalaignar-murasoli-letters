# 3719. Does Everyone Accept This?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 12 March 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

A felicitation ceremony has been held for Chief Minister Jayalalithaa in Thanjavur, and all the farmers came to the function in great satisfaction (?) and made it a success. They had received no Cauvery water for kuruvai and samba cultivation; their crops withered, their hearts withered with them, and seventeen farmers died by suicide. Farmers had listed those who had not received the relief announced by the Chief Minister. Eight ministers went from village to village gathering people with cries of **“Come, come, get into the vehicle—here, take ₹500 in your hand,”** bringing them to the Thanjavur felicitation function in the expectation that the Chief Minister would announce relief for all those left out. Yet no such relief was announced. Instead, Jayalalithaa gathered together the separate statements in which she had abused me in the past, read them out for hours at the meeting, and returned!

Her first accusation was that the Cauvery Tribunal's final award had been delivered on 5 February 2007, but that then Chief Minister Karunanidhi took no action to have it published in the Central Gazette. When the final award came on 5 February 2007, it contained both favourable and adverse features. Rather than taking a unilateral decision, we convened all-party leaders' meetings on 5 February 2007 and 15 April 2007 and acted in accordance with the view expressed by the majority on what decision should be taken and how we should proceed. It is therefore not true, as Jayalalithaa says, that we simply failed to act.

Speaking in Thanjavur, Chief Minister Jayalalithaa referred to the statement I had issued under the title **“Does Everything the Winner Says Become Scripture?”** She said that although I had written things contrary to the truth, through the title itself I had, without realising it, called her the “winner”; and she congratulated herself on that basis.

In that letter I set out one after another what I described as Jayalalithaa's false statements on the Cauvery issue. My title asked whether everything said by a person who had won an election—specifically everything she had said in her interview about Cauvery—automatically became **“scripture.”** Even the word “scripture” was used in the sense of **“truth.”** Such titles are framed with literary flavour. If Amma does not understand that, she can ask someone there who knows literature and obtain clarification. But simply reading aloud for hours from the stage everything that has been written is not right. Nowhere did I say that Jayalalithaa had “won” the Cauvery issue. Nor will I say so.

Jayalalithaa says she was responsible for publication of the Tribunal's final award in the Gazette. In my statement I asked, giving the date, whether she had or had not issued a statement on 6 February 2007 calling the final award delivered on 5 February an **“award adverse to Tamil Nadu.”** At the Thanjavur meeting she herself acknowledged that there were some adverse elements in the final award, although she said she had nevertheless called for its Gazette publication.

But is it not that very Tribunal final award, containing what Jayalalithaa herself then called “adverse elements,” that has now been published in the Gazette? Is a felicitation truly necessary for the publication of an award she herself described as containing such adverse features?

In her Thanjavur speech Jayalalithaa said:

> “On 6 July 1986, the Tamil Nadu government headed by then Chief Minister M.G.R. sent a letter to the Union Ministry of Water Resources seeking the constitution of a Tribunal. For many years the Union Government took no action on this letter and kept it pending.”

By saying that no action was taken for many years during M.G.R.'s period, she did not even spare M.G.R. from attack.

She then said that farmers' associations went to the Supreme Court seeking constitution of a Tribunal; that the Supreme Court ordered on 4 May 1990 that a Tribunal be constituted; that only thereafter, on the basis of M.G.R.'s 1986 letter, the Tribunal was constituted on 2 June 1990; and that Karunanidhi had no part whatsoever in its creation.

The people of Tamil Nadu know very well what role I had not only in constituting the Tribunal but in the Cauvery issue as a whole. The people of Thanjavur district know it particularly well. When Jayalalithaa went there and spoke about me in that way, those people must have been thinking about what kind of Chief Minister Tamil Nadu had received!

Jayalalithaa says Chief Minister M.G.R. wrote to the Union Government on 6 July 1986 asking for a Tribunal. But, Jayalalithaa, have you forgotten that much earlier, on 17 February 1970, when I was Chief Minister, the Tamil Nadu government wrote for the first time to the Union Government seeking constitution of a Tribunal?

On 8 July 1971, during the DMK government and while I was Chief Minister, the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly passed a resolution pressing the Union Government to constitute a Tribunal. Did these things happen before 1986 or not? Readers can understand for themselves and judge the truth of what the Chief Minister said.

At the Thanjavur meeting Jayalalithaa also said that the Tamil Nadu government filed a case in the Supreme Court on 4 August 1971, but that I, as Chief Minister, withdrew it at the insistence of the then Prime Minister, causing a major setback. She has repeated this accusation against me for many years, and I have already answered it on Thanjavur soil itself. Even so, I will remind you once more.

In 1971 a case was filed in the Supreme Court seeking to stop construction of the Hemavathi dam. Murasoli Maran, Ko. Si. Mani, G.K. Moopanar and various farmers' associations in Thanjavur district took the lead in pursuing it. Prime Minister Indira Gandhi then called me and said that the Cauvery issue could be resolved through talks; that with a case pending, the other side would not come forward to negotiate; and that I should trust her and withdraw the case.

I did not immediately withdraw it on my own. I invited all-party leaders to the Secretariat and discussed the matter with them. Only after that did the Tamil Nadu government decide to withdraw the case, while keeping open the possibility of reviving it whenever necessary. This is what Jayalalithaa went to Thanjavur and lamented about as though something I had done caused a setback in the Cauvery issue.

Jayalalithaa says the Supreme Court ordered constitution of the Tribunal, leading the Union Government to issue the notification establishing it on 2 June 1990, but that Karunanidhi merely happened to be Chief Minister at the time and had no role. Let me clarify this history too.

In 1990, during the DMK government, we contacted Prime Minister V.P. Singh and asked him to cooperate in constituting a Tribunal. Because a case filed by the Tamil Nadu government was then before the Supreme Court, the Union Government was asked to state its position in court. Prime Minister V.P. Singh telephoned me as Chief Minister and told me that the Supreme Court had sought the Union Government's view. I told him that Tamil Nadu was no longer prepared for negotiations and asked him to make that clear before the court on behalf of the Union Government, because only then would we get a Tribunal.

We did not stop with words. Since the Legislative Assembly was then in session, on 24 April 1990 we passed a resolution in the Tamil Nadu Assembly and immediately sent it to the Prime Minister. The Prime Minister formally conveyed the position to the Supreme Court through the Union Government's counsel. Only after that did the court say that if there were to be no more negotiations it would order constitution of a Tribunal.

Even after the Tribunal was constituted, when we asked it to issue an interim award, the Tribunal said on 5 January 1991 that it had no power to grant such interim relief. Under the DMK government, on 10 January 1991 itself we filed a petition in the Supreme Court; on 26 April 1991 the Court ruled that the Tribunal did have power to issue an interim award.

That is the truth. People of Thanjavur district, you yourselves say whether Karunanidhi had a role in constituting the Tribunal or not.

At Thanjavur Jayalalithaa said:

> “On 14 May 1992 an original suit was filed in the Supreme Court on behalf of the Tamil Nadu government under my leadership, seeking a direction to the Union Government to frame a scheme to implement the Cauvery Tribunal's interim order. For this, in 1993 I undertook a four-day fast.”

In the same speech she said that the determination to establish Tamil Nadu's rights was something soaked into her blood. Let me give just one example of how someone with such determination “soaked into the blood” acted when she was already in power.

Why did Jayalalithaa not answer at the Thanjavur meeting an accusation I had made a few days earlier about how many months she took to reply to a letter written by Union Minister Shukla on the Cauvery issue during her government? On 9 June 1992, Shukla, then Union Irrigation Minister, wrote that, keeping the Cauvery Tribunal's interim order in mind, it had been decided that responsibility for implementing it could be entrusted to the Central Water Commission with the cooperation of the States concerned, and that an immediate indication of Tamil Nadu's consent would help enable committees to be notified and orders issued. Jayalalithaa replied only nine months later, on 30 March 1993. That tells us what sort of determination had been “soaked into her blood”! The Thanjavur farmers might have awarded her a separate title of **“Ponniyin Selvi”** for that alone!

It was because the committee had not been constituted that Jayalalithaa began her fast on 18 July 1993. She said Prime Minister Narasimha Rao must order the Karnataka Chief Minister to release 205 TMC of water; only if such an order was issued would she end the fast, otherwise she would fast unto death. The Prime Minister issued no such order. Union Minister Shukla came to the fasting venue and merely said that a committee would be formed. Immediately, just as now, she proclaimed **“victory, victory”** and withdrew the fast.

I do not criticise her for withdrawing the fast. But even then it was celebrated as a victory function. Our Navalar announced: **“Kalaichelvi, who gave us Cauvery, has now recovered Cauvery water. To celebrate this victory she will go to the memorials of Anna and Puratchi Thalaivar before returning home.”** Jayalalithaa told journalists that a great victory had been achieved.

But the truth is that nothing happened even though the AIADMK government continued in office until 1996. Mannai Renganathan, who now presided over the Thanjavur felicitation ceremony for Jayalalithaa, said at the time: **“Even if this cannot be called a victory, a welcome change has occurred in the issue.”** The fact that Jayalalithaa herself later wrote to the Prime Minister on 27 July 1993 asking him to keep the promise made to her showed the people of Tamil Nadu even then how mistaken the cry of “victory” at the fasting venue had been.

At the Thanjavur meeting, Jayalalithaa described as a great betrayal the arrangement reached in 1998 under the DMK government, through the intensive efforts of Prime Minister Vajpayee and accepted by Karnataka, to implement the Tribunal's interim award, under which the Cauvery River Authority was constituted.

It was during the DMK government in 1998 that an agreement was reached to implement what the source here calls the Tribunal's **final award**. The Cauvery River Authority headed by the Prime Minister was constituted, and the Monitoring Committee was formed.

In 1998 Vajpayee was the BJP Prime Minister and I was Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu. Jayalalithaa was in opposition in Tamil Nadu but was part of the BJP alliance at the Centre. Prime Minister Vajpayee told me that the Supreme Court had asked for the Union Government's position by the 12th and invited me to come so that everyone could discuss the issue and reach a good decision before then.

Again I did not take a unilateral decision. I called more than forty party leaders and consulted them. Thirty said I should go to the Delhi meeting; ten said I should not. In a democracy, which view should one follow? Prime Minister Vajpayee himself sat through nine hours of uninterrupted negotiations and an agreement was reached. The Cauvery River Authority too was formed. Jayalalithaa now calls that my betrayal. Because a BJP Prime Minister reached such an agreement while the DMK was governing Tamil Nadu, Jayalalithaa withdrew from that alliance itself. I do not wish in this letter to describe how deeply that senior statesman Vajpayee was hurt when she withdrew or the statement he issued at the time.

Just as a felicitation ceremony has now been held for Chief Minister Jayalalithaa on behalf of Thanjavur district farmers, when the 1998 agreement was reached under Prime Minister Vajpayee, a thanksgiving meeting was held for me as Chief Minister by Cauvery-irrigation farmers in this same Thanjavur on 23 August 1998.

That morning I visited the Samathuvapuram at Kallakudi in Tiruchi, laid foundation stones for new houses, and set out by car at about one in the afternoon, travelling to Thanjavur through Keezhapazhur, Thirumanur and Thiruvaiyaru. Even now I remember the sight of farmers, mothers and children standing together to welcome me all along forty-five kilometres of the route.

It was not like Jayalalithaa's function, for which eight ministers camped for a week and went from village to village gathering a crowd. Thambi Ko. Si. Mani alone was a minister then. Thambi Palani Manickam and Thambi Ubayadullah made the arrangements. Do you know who presided over that meeting? The very same Mannai Renganathan, who was already a farmers' association leader then.

Do you know what Mannai Renganathan said at that function?

> “This is a meeting organised by the Thanjavur Farmers Association to express thanks. Even if it is called a felicitation meeting, thanks must come first. In the fifteen years since this association began, we are now seeing a fulfilment. The principal reason is that our Chief Minister Kalaignar has acted with political courage. Our Prime Minister Vajpayee possesses the same courage. Because of the political courage of both men, I say that this Cauvery problem has reached a conclusion. This meeting has been organised to felicitate the leader who initiated this beginning. We felicitate him wholeheartedly. We are forever obliged to thank you.”

Tha. Pandian, State Secretary of the Communist Party of India, took part in and praised Jayalalithaa at the recent Thanjavur felicitation. At the Thanjavur felicitation held for me then, Comrade R. Nallakannu participated on behalf of the CPI and praised me. Renganathan, who has now presented Jayalalithaa with a **Ponniyin Selvi** statue, presented me then with a **Mother Cauvery** statue.

Regarding the 1998 agreement reached while I was Chief Minister under the DMK government, the Tamil Nadu State Executive Committee of the Communist Party of India met on 7 August 1998 and passed a resolution saying:

> “It is a matter of happiness that an agreement has been reached to implement the Tribunal's interim award. Keeping Tamil Nadu's interests in mind, Chief Minister Kalaignar's concern in ascertaining the views of all sides and making continuous efforts on the extremely difficult Cauvery water-sharing issue, and the actions of the Tamil Nadu government, were major reasons for this agreement. The State Executive Committee of the Communist Party of India welcomes it.”

Not only they: apart from the AIADMK, every party in Tamil Nadu, all newspapers, and political leaders from C. Subramaniam and Moopanar onward praised the agreement.

At that Thanjavur meeting, did I say **“the government under my leadership alone caused this,”** **“I am everything,”** or **“all the praise belongs to me”?** No. Do you know what I said instead?

> “If today, through the talks held in Delhi, we have achieved a good solution that everyone praises and everyone welcomes, it is not because of my individual effort. When the question of whether I should go to Delhi was placed before the all-party leaders' meeting, this honour belongs to all the party leaders who told me that I must go and sent me on my way; the praise and congratulations belong to them. Likewise, the congratulations belong to the great farming people of the Thanjavur land, to the farmers of Tamil Nadu, and to leaders like our friend Renganathan who labour for farmers. This praise does not belong only to me as an individual. Nor is this thanks meant only for me. I thank all of you. O land of Thanjavur! Mother! I will always labour for you. I will go on labouring. You have written here that I should live for a hundred years. Not for a hundred years—even if I live only one year, I will live it for you.”

That speech is still in *Murasoli*. Fifteen years have passed since I spoke those words. Is not this very letter proof that I am still labouring?

It is now said that farmers who came to the Thanjavur meeting expecting a relief announcement are wondering how to conduct a protest!

At the Thanjavur meeting Jayalalithaa said that when the Chief Minister announced relief in the Assembly the Communists welcomed it. But if the Chief Minister had read *Janasakthi* and *Theekkathir*, she could have seen the reports saying that the relief had not been announced properly.

If a felicitation ceremony is held for Chief Minister Jayalalithaa in Thanjavur, let them felicitate her well. We bear no hostility toward that. But is it fair for her to go there, stand on that soil and launch attack after attack calling me a traitor and saying that from the beginning I acted like Karnataka's lawyer? Do the farmers of Tamil Nadu, political leaders and newspapers accept this?

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**12 March 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. The question mark in **“great satisfaction (?)”** is preserved because it is part of Kalaignar's irony in the source.
2. `பொன்னியின் செல்வி` is retained as **Ponniyin Selvi** in the passages where the source treats it as an awarded title/statue; the wordplay and local cultural resonance are not replaced by an invented English honorific.
3. On PDF 118 the audited source says that the 1998 agreement was to implement the Tribunal's **“final award”** even though other passages in the same letter speak of the interim award. The translation preserves the printed wording rather than silently reconciling it.
4. All dates, court chronology, quotations and political claims are translated from the audited source as presented, without external correction or adjudication.
