**Date:** 17 November 2010

**Udanpirappē,**

In an English statement issued on 15-11-2010, Jayalalithaa said:

> “Raja had been mocking at all constitutional institutions including the Office of the Prime Minister, the Comptroller and Auditor General of India and even the Supreme Court of India”

That is, when she says that former Union Minister A. Raja made a mockery of constitutional institutions such as the Prime Minister’s Office, the audit institution and the Supreme Court, can we forget what the Supreme Court itself asked this same Jayalalithaa in an income-tax case on 25-2-2006?

> “You are making a mockery of the Judicial Process. How long you can drag the proceedings”

The source immediately explains this as the Court asking how long she would continue dragging out judicial proceedings.

The basis on which Jaya and other opposition parties make allegations against brother Raja over spectrum allocation, the letter says, is the audit officer’s report alleging a loss to the government. Even before the report they cite was placed in Parliament, copies had appeared throughout the media.

One sentence in that report reads:

> “Any loss ascertained while attempting to value the spectrum can only be ‘presumptive’..........”

The letter therefore argues that the huge loss figures now being stated are, by the very report cited as evidence, based only on a presumption. Is it proper, it asks, to write a final verdict on the basis of a presumptive opinion?

It then describes the normal handling of audit reports: the Comptroller and Auditor General annually examines departmental receipts and expenditure; reports are given to government and placed before Parliament or the State Legislature; the observations are sent to the departments concerned; departmental explanations are obtained; and the matters are discussed in the Public Accounts Committee. Such audit reports, it says, were also placed before the Assembly during AIADMK rule in Tamil Nadu.

The letter recalls how the press, including *The Hindu*, reported one such audit report under AIADMK rule. It reproduces this source-English passage from the 3-8-2004 issue of *The Hindu*:

> “Short levy / audit report for 2003 – 2004” / Rs. 1033/- crore potential Revenue loss
>
> The State Government suffered a loss of Rs. 1033 /- crore potential Revenue in 2003 – 2004 as a result of short levy of Taxes. Besides, it incurred an “avoidable expenditure” of various companies and autonomous bodies under government control was to the tune of Rs. 3681/- crores in the last financial year.
>
> Giving these figures, T. Teethan, Accountant General (audit) for Tamil Nadu, told press persons here today that the aggregate of “Short levy” and “wasteful expenditure” was Rs. 2982 crores in 2002-2003; Rs. 3930 crores in 2001-2002; Rs. 2621 crores in 2000-2001 and Rs.1901 crores in 1999-2000. ........
>
> A Rs. 61 crore cash loss was incurred owing to an excess procurement of 1.75 lakh tonnes of paddy. Poor planning, lack of infrastructure and a defective pricing policy of the Tamil Nadu Housing Board led to blocking of Rs. 96 crores on 18,755 plots under 17 schemes completed.”

The source then prints a Tamil rendering of this passage, retaining the same figures: ₹1,033 crore potential revenue loss, ₹3,681 crore avoidable expenditure, aggregates of ₹2,982 crore, ₹3,930 crore, ₹2,621 crore and ₹1,901 crore in the stated years, ₹61 crore related to excess procurement of 1.75 lakh tonnes of paddy, and ₹96 crore blocked in 18,755 plots under 17 Housing Board schemes.

The letter says that the same day’s *Hindu* also reported an audit observation about the department held by Jayalalithaa as Chief Minister. It reproduces:

> “The Comptroller and Auditor General of India (CAG) has criticized the Tamil Nadu Home Department for not following open tender system in purchase of tents for the police. Though the rates quoted by ordinance factories were higher than open market prices, orders were still placed with them resulting in an extra expenditure, the CAG said in his report for the year ended March 2003 placed in the Legislature.”

The source again follows this with a Tamil rendering: the audit authority criticised the Home Department for not following an open-tender system for police tents and said orders were placed with ordnance factories even though their quoted rates exceeded open-market prices, resulting in additional expenditure.

Although all newspapers carried the audit news, the letter recalls one Tamil newspaper headline that described an Accountant General interview as showing a ₹11,000-crore loss under Jaya’s rule, administrative disruption due to frequently shifting ministers and officers, a ₹66-crore loss from not buying the Union Government’s cheaper rice, return of ₹3,500 crore because 46 schemes were not begun, increasing wasteful expenditure and complaints of irresponsibility.

What did Jayalalithaa, then Chief Minister, do? The letter says she immediately used government money for full-page advertisements in *Indian Express*, *Financial Express* and *Dinamani*. When those newspapers themselves cost only one, two or three rupees, it remarks, everyone knows how many lakhs a full-page advertisement would cost. The government advertisement appeared under a heading stating that there was a detailed reply to deficiencies connected with the audit officer’s report and that “revenue loss” was only a notional amount, not an actual loss.

The letter says Jayalalithaa issued this unprecedented full-page response to the official who had explained the audit report to journalists, not merely as a statement but as a government advertisement. It alleges that ₹1 crore 22 lakh 81 thousand 100 of Tamil Nadu taxpayers’ money was spent on those full-page advertisements in Tamil Nadu and North India.

It then reproduces the substance of the advertisement: the CAG report on Tamil Nadu government accounts for 2002–2003 had only recently, in July 2004, been placed before the Assembly; it would be discussed in due course by the Public Accounts Committee; the report pointed to no grave irregularity but contained observations on certain matters; the process would become complete only after the PAC had finished its work; and, in many instances, the report itself indicated that the stated loss was merely notional. It therefore said it was improper to magnify those matters and create the impression of a huge actual revenue loss.

The letter contrasts that earlier explanation with Jayalalithaa’s present insistence that the audit report itself is final and that Raja should be arrested and prosecuted, asking what kind of motive is revealed by comparing the two positions.

It next recalls an audit observation on TANSI from Jayalalithaa’s period which, the letter says, concerned an actual transaction and was accepted even by the Supreme Court. The heading is reproduced:

> “TANSI - Loss of revenue due to failure to follow uniform procedure in disposal of assets”

The source then prints:

> “........the sale of land in all the three defunct units had been effected almost at the same time, in the same locality, the sale price of land of Tansi Foundry to Jaya Publications Limited at Rs. 1350 per square metre was very low when compared to the sale price of Rs. 2080 and Rs, 2100 per square metre obtained in case of sale of land of Tansi Enamelled Wires and Tansi Forgings, respectively. Thus, the Company had not fully explored the possibility of getting a higher rate..........”

The accompanying Tamil rendering says that while land of TANSI Enamelled Wires and TANSI Forgings in the same area was sold at ₹2,080 and ₹2,100 per square metre, the TANSI Foundry land was sold to Jaya Publications at ₹1,350 per square metre and thus, according to the quoted audit passage, TANSI had not fully explored the possibility of obtaining a higher price.

The letter then contrasts this with Jayalalithaa’s criticism of land leased by the current government to a private enterprise for information-technology work. It says the allotment involved a private participant working jointly with the Tamil Nadu Industrial Development Corporation with the intention of launching IT operations and providing employment to many thousands, and that the private company itself had later petitioned the government saying it did not require the land. From this, it argues, the government cannot be said to have helped some individual person.

The letter then turns directly to Jayalalithaa’s statement that corruption was Karunanidhi’s way of life. Was it about me, it asks, that Supreme Court judges said in an income-tax case that judicial proceedings were being mocked? Was it about me that judges said government-owned TANSI land bought cheaply should be returned to government? Was I the one compelled to return land allegedly purchased at a deliberately reduced price and stamp-duty valuation? Am I the one who bought an estate at Kodanadu and spends months resting there? Was I accused even by Jaya’s allied party of taking land assigned to Dalits at Siruthavur, leading to a judicial inquiry which, according to the letter, found that assigned land had been occupied and should be recovered and that adjacent government poramboke land had also been occupied for a mansion?

Was it I or Jayalalithaa, the letter asks, who deposited in a personal bank account a cheque for ₹1 crore received from abroad and said the sender was unknown? It states that Jaya’s assets were worth ₹2 crore 2 lakh on 1-7-1991 before she became Chief Minister, and ₹66 crore 45 lakh on 30-4-1996 after five years in office. Who, then, had made corruption a daily way of life? It refers to the disproportionate-assets case concerning ₹64 crore and says it was still pending in the Bengaluru Special Court, rhetorically asking who had been obtaining adjournments year after year.

It also recalls her response when journalists had asked whether she could remain in office while corruption cases were pending: according to the letter, she had said there was scope and time for proceedings up to the Supreme Court and that she would become guilty only if the Supreme Court found her guilty; until then she was merely an accused person. Forgetting all this and accusing me of making corruption a way of life, the letter says, shows what sort of political deception Tamil Nadu faces from its Leader of the Opposition.

Believing that the people have forgotten all these truths, those who rejoice that a rule of Manu Dharma can be brought back are given, the letter says, one final warning and declaration. It recalls Bharati’s lines:

> “One justice for the Sudra —
> another for the Brahmin who eats without labour.”

And then counters:

> “One justice for oneself,
> another justice for the younger brothers” —

There will be no rebirth in Tamil Nadu for such Manu Dharma!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
17 November 2010

வாழ்க்கை முறையாக உள்ளதாகத் தெரிவித்திருக்கிறார். என்னைப்
பற்றியா உச்ச நீதிமன்ற நீதிபதிகள் வருமானவரி வழக்கில் நீதிமன்ற
நடவடிக்கைகளை கேலிக் கூத்தாக்குகிறேன் என்று சொன்னார்கள்?
அரசுக்குச் சொந்தமான டான்சி நிலத்தை குறைந்த விலைக்கு
வாங்கி, அதனைத் திரும்ப ஒப்படைக்க வேண்டுமென்று
என்னைப் பற்றியா உச்ச நீதிமன்ற நீதிபதிகள் கூறினார்கள்?
ஜெயாவுக்குத்தானே வேண்டுமென்றே விலையைக் குறைத்தும்,
முத்திரைக் கட்டணத்தைக் குறைத்தும் வாங்கிய நிலத்தை
அரசுக்குத் திரும்ப கொடுத்திட வேண்டிய நிர்ப்பந்தம் ஏற்பட்டது.
கொடநாட்டில் எஸ்டேட் வாங்கிக் கொண்டு, மாதக்கணக்கில்
ஓய் வெடுத்துக் கொண்டிருப்பவனா நான்? சிறு தாவூரில் தலித்
மக்களுக்காக ஒப்படைக்கப்பட்ட நிலத்தையெல்லாம் பறித்துக்
கொண்டதாக ஜெயாவின் தோழமைக் கட்சியாலேயே குற்றம்
கூறப்பட்டு, அதற்காக நீதிபதியைக் கொண்டு விசாரித்து,
தலித்துகளுக்காக ஒப்படைக்கப்பட்ட நிலம் கைப்பற்றப்பட்டது
உண்மைதான் என்றும், அந்த நிலங்களைத் திரும்பப் பெற
வேண்டும் என்றும், அந்த இடத்திற்குப் பக்கத்திலே உள்ள
அரசுக்குச் சொந்தமான புறம்போக்கு நிலத்தை இன்றளவும்
கைப்பற்றி மாளிகை கட்டியிருப்பதிலே அடிக்கடி சென்று

தன் பெயருக்கு வந்த ஒரு கோடி ரூபாய்க்கான காசோலையை
வங்கியிலே உள்ள தன் கணக்கிலே வரவு வைத்துக் கொண்டு,
யார் அனுப்பியது என்றே தெரியவில்லை என்று ஊரை
ஏமாற்ற நினைத்த ஜெயலலிதா ஊழல் பேர் வழியா? நானா?
முதலமைச்சராக ஜெயா பொறுப்பேற்பதற்கு முன்பு 1-7-

ரூபாய். அவர் முதலமைச்சராக ஐந்தாண்டுகள் பொறுப்பிலே

வாழ்க்கை முறையாகக் கொண்டிருப்பது நானா? ஜெயலலிதாவா?
வருமானத்திற்கு மீறி 64 கோடி ரூபாய் சொத்து சேர்த்ததாக ஊழல்

மீதா? அல்லது அந்த வழக்குக்கு ஆண்டுக் கணக்கில் வாய்தா
வாங்கிக் கொண்டிருப்பது ஊழல் மகாராணி ஜெயலலிதாவா?

வழக்குகள் நடைபெறுகின்றனவே, இந்நிலையில் நீங்கள்

மன்றம் வரையில் விசாரிக்க வாய்ப்பும் காலமும் இருக்கிறது.
உச்ச நீதிமன்றம் என்னைக் குற்றவாளி என்று தீர்ப்பளித்தால்
