# 3433. A Lesson the Lady of the Press Must Learn!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation follows the audited canonical Tamil in source order. The opening Bharathidasan poem is translated for sense while retaining its personification of the press. The letter’s press-freedom argument, political criticism, Nilgiris disaster-relief chronology, administrative figures and closing warning against sensational exaggeration are preserved without outside reconciliation.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3433](../../../chapters/3433-pathirigai-pen-payilavendiya-paadam.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 61](../../../pages/page-061.md)–[PDF 69](../../../pages/page-069.md)  
**Date:** 15 November 2009

**Udanpirappē,**

> “Within the heart of pitch darkness,
> you are the good ray of light;
> you are the awakening that darts
> into the eyes of those asleep in this world;
> to build up the town,
> to bring this world together,
> O lady of the press, born
> in the hearts of the greatly learned!
>
> Drawing the deep waters
> of the stream in scholars’ hearts,
> pouring and pouring them out
> so that the people’s thought may flourish,
> ending narrow deeds
> and making the world rise —
> fragrant-petalled lady,
> those who do not see your welfare do not see the world.”

Whenever I see the course taken by some newspapers, I remember this poem written about the press by the revolutionary poet Bharathidasan. It is because I still regard newspapers and those who run them on the basis of that high ideal and great respect he held for the press that, whenever governments have subjected them to any kind of repression, I have argued for them and fought for them.

During AIADMK rule, when my dear friend M.G.R. was Chief Minister, Mr Balasubramaniam — editor of *Ananda Vikatan* and son of Vasan, who was called the patriarch of weekly magazines — was arrested and imprisoned because he had written something in *Vikatan* about the government of the day. I was then Leader of the Opposition and travelling by car through Tindivanam. When I heard on the car radio that Balasubramaniam had been arrested, I asked for the car to be stopped there itself, immediately issued a statement condemning the arrest, and continued thereafter to raise my voice on his behalf.

Mr Saavi, who was called another patriarch of the press world, published a cover in his weekly magazine. Over an illustration on that cover, the AIADMK government of the day became angry in a way that neither the woman depicted in the illustration nor her parents or relatives had become angry. When I heard that Saavi, editor of the magazine, had been arrested and taken to the police station, my friends and I rushed to the police station itself and secured his release on bail.

They were people who conducted journalism in keeping with Bharathidasan’s song addressing the “lady of the press.” Yet they had to face arrest.

At the same time, if anyone imagines that in the name of press freedom anything at all may be written, any kind of picture may be published, and anyone may be written or spoken about in a manner that stains that person’s honour and dignity — and, saying “this is my way,” crosses the bounds — then when those affected make a complaint, no government can simply watch and enjoy it.

Even in such situations, no one can say that the actions taken so far by this government against newspapers have been harsh or rough. I am not unaware that those who deliberately wish to find fault with me severely criticise this gentle approach — an approach that at the same time handles matters cautiously with the broader aim that hostility should not spread between the two sides.

It is because I am a friend of journalists that I have fulfilled the demands they have raised. Near Thiruvanmiyur in Chennai, I set apart an entire area for journalists and made it possible for them to build their homes there. Likewise, land has been given to journalists to build houses in Tiruchi, Madurai, Salem, Vellore and Dindigul. Applications have recently come even from Coimbatore; they too will be fulfilled.

Thus, because I too have been a journalist from the beginning until today, I have shown them affection. I will continue to show affection and attachment. It is a feeling that has naturally become mingled with my blood. On that basis, whether today’s journalists are taken as my friends, my brothers or my comrades, the appeal I have made from time to time — and make now — is this: “Argue for rights; publish the truths.” And also: “Do not exaggerate anything! Do not sow hostility between anyone!”

This is a government that has never asked, “Who are you to point it out?” when faults or shortcomings are brought to its notice. Such arrogance has never approached this government. Many journalists whom I hold dear know that I begin reading newspaper reports at dawn, immediately discuss them with officials and those connected with the report, and act at once to remove shortcomings if they are found.

Yet I also know those who have learned the art of enlarging even a mustard-seed-sized report to the size of an ocean and publishing it — whether or not they realise that, not against me but among the people, they may unknowingly create tension.

I also know the good intention of those who point out what a government ought to have done but has not yet done. It is because I care about how the effects produced by these two kinds of journalism reach the people that I have had to write this. For example, can deliberately magnifying the suffering caused by nature’s fury not sometimes lead to dangerous consequences? That is why I must appeal: do not exaggerate anything!

Because of the recent cyclone, rain and severe flooding, a few districts in Tamil Nadu were badly affected — especially Nilgiris district, which lost its beauty, was thrown into disorder and saw its people suffer. The people living in those hills know very clearly how this government did not stand watching even for a moment, but moved swiftly to carry out relief work.

As soon as that cruel event which claimed more than forty lives began, I was shaken. I immediately asked my younger-brother ministers Veerapandi Arumugam and Pongalur Palanisamy to go there. They tried to travel by helicopter, but were told that the helicopter could not fly in that rain. Without even worrying about what danger might occur on the road in that region, those ministers — my beloved Udanpirappus — travelled by road and joined Ministers Ramachandran and Vellakoil Saminathan, who were already camping there, along with brother A. Raja, the Member of Parliament for the constituency and a Union Minister, and DMK Udanpirappus Gundan, Mubarak and MLA Soundirapandian. They toured all the damaged areas, set relief work in motion, went to console the families of those who had died, provided the appropriate government relief there, and comforted all those affected.

Look at the photograph attached to this letter. You can see how my younger brothers stand without concern for themselves in such a dangerous place — a place that could collapse at any moment.

But some people, without knowing any truth, issue statements in the manner of “whether the mouth is sour or the mango is sour,” saying that no one other than two ministers went there and that no one visited those affected. Journalists who know the truth then publish even that in banner headlines. Should those newspapers not publish the truth? Can anything be published merely because it is news against the government? If newspapers firmly decide that they will not knowingly publish untrue news, those who issue statements will themselves stop issuing untrue reports.

Did you see that scene of them visiting the affected area without the slightest fear, with concern that their affection for those people mattered more? When I saw newspapers such as *The Hindu* and *Dina Thanthi* publish it on their very front pages, it was a comfort to me amid the tragedy on the Nilgiri hills to see my younger brothers standing high in an area where landslides were continuing, doing their duty without the slightest fear.

The Fire and Rescue Services, rescue teams, Electricity Board personnel, Highways Department personnel and police are engaged in removing the effects of the disaster. Rescue work is proceeding on a war footing. According to the initial information received, 700 houses in the district have been damaged by landslides.

The National Disaster Management team from the paramilitary disaster-relief division, together with 76 personnel under National Disaster Response Force Inspectors Arun Kumar and Bootmange Prashanth, are engaged in restoration work.

I sent Revenue Commissioner Sundara Devan, Fire and Rescue Services Director Nataraj and Additional DGP for Law and Order Radhakrishnan to Nilgiris district to console those affected and encourage relief work. Even before them, I sent senior IAS officer Govindan to Nilgiris district and instructed him to remain with the District Collector and take part in relief work. I ordered that ₹1 lakh each be immediately given through the District Collector to the families of those who had died.

At about 10 a.m. on 12-11-2009, a meeting was held on flood damage and relief work in Nilgiris district under the leadership of Union Minister A. Raja, in the presence of State Ministers K. Ramachandran and Vellakoil Saminathan, with the District Collector and officials of all departments participating.

At that meeting, the Udhagai Divisional Manager representing the Transport Department said that 50 per cent of buses were currently operating and that the remaining buses would be operated soon. Instructions were given to take immediate steps needed to operate all buses quickly.

The Udhagai Electricity Board Superintending Engineer, who attended on behalf of the Electricity Board, said that 95 per cent of electricity supply in Nilgiris district had been restored and that the rest would be restored within three days. The Electricity Board was asked to take steps to bring replacement electricity poles immediately from other districts, and where roads had been cut off, even to carry them in by head-load where necessary.

As the Mettupalayam–Udhagai road had been severed, instructions were given to ensure that petrol, diesel, kerosene and cooking gas were brought without interruption through Mysuru or Palakkad. Arrangements were made accordingly, and these essential commodities are now available.

The Managing Director of Aavin was instructed to ensure that milk supply was not interrupted. The District Supply Officer and Aavin Managing Director were asked to coordinate and ensure milk distribution to all areas. It was also stressed that appropriate action be taken so that milk products needed by children would be available without interruption.

Because drinking-water pipelines in most areas had suffered heavy flood damage, the concerned Municipal Commissioners in municipal areas, and the Assistant Director of Town Panchayats and Assistant Director of Village Panchayats in town-panchayat and village areas, were asked to take immediate steps to prevent interruption of drinking-water supply to the public. Damaged pipes were to be replaced at once with new pipes. If water tanks had been damaged, it was firmly directed that Sintex tanks be placed in those areas and drinking water supplied. The District Collector’s Personal Assistant (Panchayat Development) was asked to supervise drinking-water supply.

The Deputy Director of Public Health reported that no infectious disease had arisen because of the flood. As affected people were staying in flood-relief camps, arrangements were made to appoint appropriate officers to conduct medical examinations, ensure medical facilities and immediately provide necessary medicines.

Instructions were given to identify at once trees that had to be removed from roadsides and residential areas and to take action to clear them.

Large areas of crops have been damaged by the flood. Officials were asked to collect details and submit a report to the government. Likewise, the Udhagai Assistant Director of Animal Husbandry was told to collect a detailed report on livestock losses.

Alongside these government measures, party members were also told to engage in relief work. In just the past few days, the DMK has distributed to affected people in the district 2,000 woollen blankets, food items, milk, bread, dhotis and sarees.

According to figures collected so far, 55 houses have been completely damaged and 909 partially damaged. So far, ₹9 lakh 52 thousand has been given as relief assistance to 476 people. Work is continuing to give another ₹9 lakh 76 thousand to 488 people. A total of 1,808 people have been moved from affected places to safe areas. These 1,808 people belonging to 610 families are being cared for in 22 relief camps. Thirty livestock have died. Roads extending 105 kilometres have been damaged.

Reports have come that roads destroyed by the rain have been repaired, transport has resumed and normal life has returned. Yet I see that some newspapers still prominently publish statements by some people as though no relief work at all has taken place there.

In the same way, when sensational reports appeared in some television channels and newspapers saying that seven hundred fishermen were missing, I was shocked and ordered an inquiry. When information came that only eight persons in all were missing, and that four of those had already returned, I felt distressed that newspapers were in such haste to sensationalise and exaggerate false news. We must put all those sorrows aside and continue our journey on our path.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**15-11-2009**

## Letter-specific notes

1. The opening Bharathidasan poem is translated for sense while preserving the source’s personification of the press as a woman.
2. `வாய் புளித்ததோ, மாங்காய் புளித்ததோ` retains its “mouth / mango sour” image instead of being flattened into a generic accusation.
3. Disaster-response percentages, house counts, relief figures, camp counts, livestock losses and road-length figures remain in source order.
4. The source’s contrast between the reported **700 missing fishermen** and the later inquiry result of **8 missing, 4 returned** is preserved exactly as the letter presents it.
