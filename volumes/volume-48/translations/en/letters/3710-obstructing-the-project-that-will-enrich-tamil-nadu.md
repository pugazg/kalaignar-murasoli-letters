# 3710. Should a Project That Would Enrich Tamil Nadu Be Blocked?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 25 February 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

On 1 September 2007, the Supreme Court of India imposed an interim stay on implementation of the Sethu Samudram project and advised the Union Government to consider an alternative route. Accordingly, on 20 July 2008 the Union Government constituted an expert committee headed by Dr. R.K. Pachauri. The expert committee submitted its report to the Union Government on 12 February 2012.

Eleven months later, the Union Government has now stated its views on the Pachauri Committee's report and filed a reply in the Supreme Court on 23 February 2013. In that reply, the Union Government informed the Supreme Court that the committee headed by noted environmental expert R.K. Pachauri had reported that the Sethu Samudram project could be implemented only through Adam's Bridge and that implementing the project along an alternative route was not feasible.

The Union Government's Solicitor General, Rohinton Nariman, submitted the Pachauri Committee's report to the Supreme Court.

The report says that choosing an alternative route for implementing the Sethu Samudram project is not feasible either economically or geographically. At the same time, the Union Government told the Supreme Court that since the Union Cabinet had not yet examined the Pachauri Committee's report, no decision had been taken on it.

Speaking to reporters about this reply filed by the Union Government in the Supreme Court, BJP spokesperson Ravi Shankar Prasad said, **“Under no circumstances can a decision to destroy Ram Setu for the Sethu Samudram project be accepted.”** The RSS and various Hindu religious organisations too have opposed implementation of the Sethu Samudram project. The Tamil Nadu government headed by Jayalalithaa has followed the BJP and pressed the same position before the Supreme Court.

As long ago as 8 May 2002, in a letter I wrote to then Prime Minister Vajpayee of the National Democratic Alliance government, I set out all the details concerning the Sethu Samudram project.

On 23 July 1967, Arignar Anna announced an **Ezhuchi Naal**—a Day of Uprising—demanding implementation of the Tuticorin Port project, which formed one part of the Sethu Samudram proposal, and the Salem Steel Plant project.

It was understood that this project could attain significance on a world scale like the Suez Canal and the Panama Canal. Many committees were constituted to study it, yet the project remained shelved for a long time. When the National Democratic Alliance government was formed at the Centre, funds were allocated to examine the feasibility of the Sethu Samudram project.

The route through which the Sethu Samudram Canal should pass and the related details were considered by the National Democratic Alliance government led by BJP Prime Minister Vajpayee, and **Route No. 6**—through Adam's Bridge, which the BJP now calls Ram Setu and says cannot be accepted—was selected. Having forgotten that the BJP-led National Democratic Alliance government itself approved the route on which the project should be implemented, the BJP now says that this is the place where Ram Setu stands and that the project must not be implemented there. One must think about the contradiction involved.

It is not only the BJP. In Tamil Nadu, the AIADMK, which is now in government, stated in its 2001 election manifesto:

> “There is at present no route suitable for continuous shipping around the Indian peninsula. Ships travelling by sea from west to east have to go around Sri Lanka. The solution to this is the Sethu Samudram project. Under this project, the principal objective of the Sethu Samudram scheme is to remove the sandbanks and rocks obstructing shipping in the Adam's Bridge area between Rameswaram and Talaimannar in Sri Lanka, deepen the channel and form a canal.”

The very people who said that the principal objective was to remove the sandbanks and rocks in the Adam's Bridge area—the same place that they now call “Ram Setu”—and construct a canal there, today say Ram Setu must be declared a national monument. One must think about what a contradiction that is. Is the underlying motive simply that this project must not come into being through the efforts of the DMK?

Not only did the AIADMK say this in its election manifesto; in a long statement issued on 26 June 2005, AIADMK leader Jayalalithaa said:

> “It was only at my insistence that in 1998 this project was taken up as a priority project, and the preliminary environmental impact assessment work was entrusted to the National Environmental Engineering Research Institute in March 1998. That institution's report was also submitted in August 1998. In this way, I have been a principal reason for ensuring that the Sethu Samudram Canal Project becomes a reality.”

One must remember that the same Jayalalithaa then spoke as though she herself was responsible for the project.

If the Sethu project is implemented, industry and trade in Tamil Nadu will grow; the country's foreign-exchange earnings will increase; the distance and time travelled by ships will be greatly reduced; cargo-handling capacity at the ports of Tamil Nadu and neighbouring States will increase; a new minor port will emerge at Rameswaram or Mandapam; because fishing harbours will be improved under the Sethu Canal project, marine-products trade will grow and the economy and standard of living of fishermen will rise; and the canal will enable fishermen to travel between the Gulf of Mannar and the Palk Bay.

Trans-shipment of Indian cargo at ports in other countries, including Sri Lanka, will be avoided; the country's coastal security will be strengthened; the project will continuously provide direct and indirect employment to more than 50,000 people; the Sethu Samudram project will generate eight crore person-days of employment every year; many direct and indirect jobs will be created; development of the Rameswaram and Nagapattinam ports will provide direct employment to about 10,000 people; use of the Sethu Samudram Canal will reduce the shipping distance between India's eastern and western sea regions by about 424 miles; this may reduce ship travel time by about 30 hours; substantial fuel and foreign-exchange savings will result; there will be considerable savings in freight charges; because travel time will be shorter, ships will be able to make additional voyages and handle more cargo; one of the east-coast ports of South India will be developed as a hub port for container traffic; Tuticorin Port will develop on a large scale; Navy and Coast Guard vessels will be able to move very quickly from the eastern region to the western region, substantially enhancing India's defence capability; and infrastructure development will grow in Ramanathapuram, Nagapattinam and Tuticorin districts.

It was precisely in view of these many benefits, which would greatly contribute to Tamil Nadu's development, that when the United Progressive Alliance government was formed at the Centre, the Sethu Samudram project was included—through the DMK's efforts—not only in its Common Minimum Programme but also in the UPA government's Budget. Prime Minister Dr. Manmohan Singh, UPA guiding leader Mrs. Sonia Gandhi, I and leaders of the other allied parties all participated in the grand inaugural function held in Madurai.

At that time, some people tried through the courts to obtain a stay preventing the inaugural function of the Sethu Samudram project. In the case relating to that effort, the Bench headed by the Chief Justice of the Madras High Court ruled:

> “The petitioner has rushed to the Court and filed this case with the object of stopping the Sethu Samudram project, which is being brought forward in the national interest. It is known to everyone that the Sethu Samudram project will bring good benefits to the country. At present ships have to sail around Sri Lanka to enter the Bay of Bengal. If a shipping canal is created across the Palk Strait, it will make possible substantial savings of money and time. This canal project will produce good results. Therefore, there can be no stay on the function.”

Thus the Sethu Samudram project was inaugurated. While the project was progressing rapidly because of the good efforts of Thambi T.R. Baalu, who was then the minister, communal forces took this issue to court with the intention of somehow creating obstacles to the project. I urge the Union Government to take the necessary steps to secure an early and favourable decision from the Supreme Court on this matter, obtain a good judgment, complete the Sethu Samudram project and thereby create the conditions for the livelihoods of the people of southern Tamil Nadu to improve.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**25 February 2013**
