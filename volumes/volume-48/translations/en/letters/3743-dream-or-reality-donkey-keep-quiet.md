# 3743. Dream or Reality? Is This the Tale of “Donkey, Keep Quiet”?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 28 April 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

At the public meeting held in Tiruvannamalai on 24 April 2013 under the title **“The Plight of Democracy in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly”**, the DMK General Secretary, Perasiriyar, began his speech with the words, **“Tamil Nadu and the farce now taking place.”** Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Jayalalithaa is proving Perasiriyar's observation true every single day. From newspaper reports of the Assembly proceedings, one can see that matters have reached the point where nobody has the right to express a view against this government. One must praise Amma; one must thank her for every announcement; everyone must bow their heads and listen to whatever she reads out in the House. The Assembly is increasingly being transformed into a forum for singing her praises.

Tamil Nadu has had many Chief Ministers. None of them behaved as the present Chief Minister does—day after day reading statements under Rule 110 even on subjects that do not belong to the Chief Minister's own departments but to other ministers. Because the Chief Minister herself reads every announcement, leaders of the other parties and the Speaker then rise to welcome and praise them. If anyone wants to point out a flaw or shortcoming in those announcements, they are told that no criticism of a statement read under Rule 110 is permitted. If they still try to speak, the Speaker immediately expels them from the House.

After being expelled in this way four times, they are then told not to attend for the rest of the session. Such is the democracy that flies its flag every day in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly.

On 25 April 2013 alone, the Chief Minister herself read out everything that the Electricity Minister ought to have said about the power department. Then what is the Electricity Minister for? Has some other special “work” been assigned to him? Is that the only “work” he is to attend to? How many announcements! And all those announcements involve projects worth many thousands of crores of rupees. Why were these announcements not made in the Budget? Does spending under these announcements not require the approval of the Assembly? Of course, that would matter only if they were actually going to be implemented! Were these announcements made merely so that they would appear in the newspapers every day? Do these announcements by the Chief Minister not diminish the value of the Budget and the demands for grants?

Let me ask just one question by way of example. On 3 May 2012, Chief Minister Jayalalithaa made certain announcements relating to the Police Department under Assembly Rule 110. Was there not a debate on the Police Department in 2012? Did the Chief Minister not reply to it? She did. On 24 April 2012, she read out a lengthy reply to the debate on the Police Department and made announcements. Yet barely ten days later, on 3 May 2012, she made one very large announcement. Could she not have made that announcement while replying to the Police grant itself? She did not. Why not? If it had been made then, it would merely have become the eleventh announcement among ten others. Only if it was read separately under Rule 110 would all the newspapers waiting to put it on the front page publish it prominently—and all the party leaders in the Assembly would praise “Amma”. Is that the reason? I do not know.

What was the statement the Chief Minister read under Rule 110 on 3 May 2012? Here it is:

> “Under the ‘Your Own Home’ scheme, the Tamil Nadu Police Housing Corporation will take steps to build houses for personnel in the Police Department from constables up to Deputy Superintendents of Police, and for personnel in the Fire and Rescue Services Department from firefighters up to Divisional Officers. Around 36,000 residential units will be constructed under this scheme. Ten acres of land will be allotted in every district headquarters, and 100 acres will be allotted in Melakottaiyur village in Kanchipuram district.”

The moment the Chief Minister made this announcement in the Assembly under Rule 110, Honourable Member Se.Ku. Tamilarasan began the praise. He was followed by U. Thaniyarasu of the Kongu Tamilar Katchi, Dr Krishnasamy of Puthiya Tamilagam, Dr M.H. Jawahirullah of the Manithaneya Makkal Katchi, M. Kalaiarasu of the Pattali Makkal Katchi, A. Narayanan of the Samathuva Makkal Katchi, S. Vijayadharani of the Congress, M. Arumugam of the Communist Party of India, and A. Soundararajan of the Communist Party of India (Marxist), all of whom praised and thanked the Chief Minister.

Going beyond all of them, my dear friend Jayakumar, who was Speaker on that day, even recited a poem in the House:

> “Life guards the body; our Mother guards our possessions —  
> light guards the world; our Mother guards life —  
> the shore guards the sea; our Mother guards against theft...”

And he continued in that vein. It was not the present Speaker who sang it—the Speaker at that time did.

It is now almost a year since the Chief Minister made that announcement. Of those 36,000 residential units, how many have been completed by the Police Housing Corporation during this year? Our friends in the police paid all the money required for that scheme, did they not? Have those houses been built and opened? Did the Chief Minister go there in person and inaugurate them? Or have they already been inaugurated by video conference?

It was another such enormous announcement that the Chief Minister read in the Assembly on 25 April 2013. And what huge front-page headlines appeared in all the newspapers!

- A hydroelectric project in the Nilgiris at a cost of ₹7,000 crore!
- A ₹5,000 crore scheme to strengthen power infrastructure and the transmission system!
- Nineteen new 400-kV substations at a cost of ₹3,572 crore!
- A ₹1,000 crore substation and transmission-line scheme in the future!
- Projects worth ₹30,602 crore to make Tamil Nadu a power-surplus State once again!

The Tamil Nadu Chief Minister has read out power projects worth about ₹30,000 crore as a statement under Rule 110. Did the leaders of the various parties in the Assembly ask whether these projects were feasible? Were all these announcements discussed in the Cabinet? Were they at least discussed among the officials? Nobody asked any question, because under the rules they are not permitted to ask.

Do you know how the Tamil Nadu newspapers celebrated this announcement by the Chief Minister? **“New hydel project for ₹7,000 crore”** — *Dina Thanthi*; **“₹20,000 for fitting solar-power equipment in homes”** — *Dinamalar*; **“Substations for ₹8,000 crore, hydel project for ₹7,000 crore”** — *Dinamani*; **“Jayalalithaa schemes worth ₹16,350 crore”** — *Maalai Murasu*; **“₹30,602 crore scheme”** — *Namadhu M.G.R.*. That is how prominently they were published.

Even today, villages are suffering power cuts of more than fifteen hours. Summer has begun. Children cannot sleep at night without air. Parents carry their children and wander through the streets. In Coimbatore, Tiruppur and Erode districts, factories have shut down, workers have lost their livelihoods and are struggling for food. Do these rulers care about any of that? Yes, of course they do! Out of that very concern, our Chief Minister has thought and thought and devised and announced all these schemes.

The Tamil Nadu ministers can print the Chief Minister's Rule 110 announcements as handbills and take them to all the mothers suffering from power cuts in the villages. They should particularly underline the lines saying that these projects will take about eight to ten years to complete. They could even hold meetings and campaign like this: **“These schemes announced by the Chief Minister will be completed within ten years. Then the power cuts will ease and your children will get plenty of air. You can put them to sleep then.”**

Will power generation begin at the already-planned Kudankulam nuclear power station? Will electricity become available? If it does, how much will Tamil Nadu get? On one side there is an agitation demanding that the plant itself must not be started. On another side is the episode of first stirring up those protesters and then later refusing even to meet them. In an interview given this week to *Ananda Vikatan*, the protest committee leader Udayakumar has openly spoken about the promise Chief Minister Jayalalithaa first gave them and how she later abandoned them.

In such a situation, do you know what kinds of projects the Chief Minister has announced in the Assembly? We are told that a 2,000-MW hydroelectric project costing ₹7,000 crore will be created in the Nilgiris; that it will be implemented in two stages; that it will take around eight to ten years to complete; that a ₹5,000 crore scheme has been prepared to strengthen power infrastructure and the transmission system; that, in addition, works will be undertaken to establish fourteen new 230-kV substations at a cost of ₹822 crore; that this year work will begin on a new 400-kV substation in Tiruvallur at a cost of about ₹1,000 crore, together with its transmission lines. (No one should ask in which year all the other projects will begin, because what the Chief Minister said was a statement under Rule 110!) We are told that work is under way on a ₹2,300 crore transmission line from Kayathar to Chennai; that works to establish substations at a cost of ₹1,230 crore will begin; that a substation will be set up at Kanarpatti in Tirunelveli district at a cost of ₹150 crore; that work on eleven new 33-kV substations will also begin this year; and that altogether fifty-six substations will be created at a cost of ₹8,000 crore.

We are also told that household electricity consumers who install the equipment mentioned above and generate power will be given a capital subsidy of ₹20,000. And nobody should laugh on seeing the final announcement. Yes—we are told that eleven lakh new electricity connections will be provided this year! What announcements! When the Budget for an entire year itself is around one lakh crore rupees, if the Chief Minister announces projects worth ₹30,000 crore in a single day under Rule 110, it brings to mind the village saying: **“I am going to marry the mountain; donkey, keep quiet.”**

There is a story in children's textbooks. A glassware merchant named Claudius went from street to street selling glass articles. It was scorching hot. Unable to bear the heat, he put down the basket containing his glass vessels under a tree, lay down comfortably and fell asleep.

In his sleep he had a dream. The glass merchant sold his vessels, made a large profit and little by little became a rich man. He rose in status until he became a great industrialist. Then one of his servants came and said something to him. The master immediately became angry and kicked out fiercely at the servant. Thinking he was kicking the servant in his dream, the merchant in reality kicked hard at the basket of glass vessels beside his feet. All the glassware shattered to pieces. That is the story.

Everyone has the right to daydream like that, do they not?

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**28 April 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. **Perasiriyar** is retained as the established personal honorific used in the source rather than flattened into a generic “Professor”.
2. **Rule 110** is retained as the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly procedural reference around which the letter's satire is built.
3. The village saying `மலைக்கே வாழ்க்கைப்படப் போகிறேன்; கழுதையே சும்மா இரு` is kept close to its source image as **“I am going to marry the mountain; donkey, keep quiet.”** In context, Kalaignar uses it to mock an extravagant future promise invoked while present hardship remains unresolved.
4. The source names the glassware merchant **Claudius**; that name is retained as printed without attempting to identify or correct the textbook story from outside sources.
