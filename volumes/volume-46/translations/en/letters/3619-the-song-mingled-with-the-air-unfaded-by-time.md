# 3619. The Song Mingled with the Air, Unfaded by Time!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This draft follows the locked Volume 46 conventions. Kalaignar’s follow-up argument on Separate Tamil Eelam, the history and political claims he invokes, his discussion of repeated Sri Lankan promises, the 1976–1977 electoral sequence, the Cuba comparison, Erode Tamilanban’s verse and the closing democratic-referendum argument are preserved in source order. Historical and political assertions are represented as the source presents them and are not independently adjudicated. The complete audited Tamil remains authoritative. This draft awaits separate bilingual source-alignment review.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3619](../../../chapters/3619-kaalaththaal-maraiyaatha-kaatril-kalantha-geetham.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 214](../../../pages/page-214.md)–[PDF 220](../../../pages/page-220.md)  
**Date:** 25 April 2012

**Udanpirappē,**

In the letter I wrote on 20 April 2012 under the title “Separate Tamil Eelam Will Be a Lamp Set Atop a Hill!”, I described Kosovo, South Sudan, East Timor and Montenegro, which were proclaimed separate countries after referendums conducted with the intervention of the United Nations. I also wrote that the liberation song called “Separate Tamil Eelam” is sounding without interruption in the ears of Tamils living throughout the world; that Separate Tamil Eelam will certainly come into being; that the incomparable identity of the Eelam Tamil people will shine across the world like a lamp set atop a hill; and that, in the present circumstances, following precedents already found in history, the United Nations should conduct a referendum among Eelam Tamils and help Separate Eelam come into being.

After my appeal, Gotabaya Rajapaksa, brother of Sri Lankan President Rajapaksa, spoke sharply criticising me, and all the newspapers carried the report. Following that, I saw a strong editorial in an English daily dated 23 April 2012 written against Separate Tamil Eelam. Further, Mrs. Sushma Swaraj, leader of the Indian parliamentary delegation that visited Sri Lanka, and Mr. T.K. Rangarajan, a leader of the Communist Party of India (Marxist), appear to have said that those living in Sri Lanka are not now demanding Tamil Eelam.

In my earlier letter itself, I wrote about when the demand for “Tamil Eelam” arose and from when the DMK has been raising its voice in support of it. If one pauses for even a moment to consider why the demand for Tamil Eelam arose, one can understand the justice within that demand.

The 35 lakh minority Eelam Tamils living over an area of 8,000 square miles were oppressed and suppressed by 110 lakh majority Sinhalese. Though Tamils and Sinhalese had for a very long time possessed different languages, religions, cultures and geographical boundaries, British imperialism imposed a single administration without giving even the slightest consideration to the fundamental differences between these two distinct national peoples. Under that unitary system, the majority Sinhala community made itself the new master over the Tamil people and sought to treat Tamils only as second-class citizens.

The civic rights of ten lakh Tamils who worked as labourers on tea and rubber estates were taken away and all of them were rendered stateless. In 1956 Sinhala alone was made the official language throughout Sri Lanka, denying Tamils rights in education and employment and compelling them to migrate and settle in foreign countries. In 1960–61, through the nationalisation of educational institutions, discriminatory and inhumane policies against Tamil education were followed, and Tamils were compelled to study through the Sinhala medium. If one reads history fully, one can understand that it was because of all this that the demand for “Separate Tamil Eelam” arose naturally among Tamils some fifty years ago, and that as a consequence various militant groups emerged and were compelled to wage armed struggle.

Thereafter, the “Colombo” agreement reached on 29 July 1987 was expected, in the difficult circumstances of that time, to provide at least a small ray of light for Eelam Tamils. But as early as August 1987, under the heading “Karikalan Answers,” I had said that the agreement was “not merely full of defects; it was also not an agreement made with a pure heart.”

Writing further about the consequences of that agreement, I recalled what Anna had said: a wife asked her husband for a string of jasmine for her hair; that ill-natured husband thought, “As long as she has hair, she will keep troubling me by asking for flowers. So, to spare ourselves the difficulty of searching for flowers, let us cut off her hair itself!”

On the basis of the 1987 Rajiv Gandhi–Jayewardene agreement, a Thirteenth Amendment was brought into the Sri Lankan Constitution. It is through that amendment that devolution of power can be provided to the areas where Tamils live. But the Sri Lankan Government, which accepted that amendment only half-heartedly, has made it clear to this day that it has no intention of implementing it. That is why the present Sri Lankan President Mahinda Rajapaksa has asked whether it is possible in Sri Lanka to unite the traditional Tamil-inhabited areas into Northern and Eastern Provinces and grant those provinces powers of the kind enjoyed by States in India.

What Tamil National Alliance spokesperson Mr. Suresh Premachandran has said on this matter deserves attention. He explained that in the 1990s a Parliamentary Select Committee headed by Mangala Moonesinghe was formed to consider devolution of power to Tamils; that after meeting and debating more than a hundred times, none of that committee’s recommendations has been implemented even to this day; that another parliamentary committee formed thereafter prepared and submitted a draft Constitution providing for devolution, but opposition members burned the draft inside Parliament itself and that effort too was abandoned; and that after Rajapaksa came to office, an All-Party Representatives Committee working with the assistance of an expert group was created to find a solution on devolution, yet its proposals too were not implemented.

He further said that the Lessons Learnt and Reconciliation Commission was formed in 2010, but Sri Lankan ministers Peiris and Nimal Silva openly declared that the Commission’s report could not be implemented in full; that when Indian External Affairs Minister Mr. S.M. Krishna visited Sri Lanka last January, President Rajapaksa assured him that power would be devolved to Tamils, only for Rajapaksa to deny that he had given any such assurance before Mr. Krishna had even returned to India; and that although Rajapaksa had said that, after comprehensive talks with the Tamil National Alliance, a parliamentary committee could be formed and consent obtained, the Sri Lankan Government, contrary to what he had said, unilaterally withdrew from the talks with the Tamil National Alliance.

Thus Suresh Premachandran has described the history of the Sri Lankan Government making a habit of breaking promises.

Viewed against the background of these historical events hostile to Tamils, which Mr. Suresh Premachandran has laid bare, one can easily understand that all the assurances now given by President Rajapaksa to the Indian parliamentary delegation led by Mrs. Sushma Swaraj are merely deceptive tactics.

From published reports it is also clear that thousands upon thousands of Tamils are still languishing in barbed-wire camps and prison enclosures; that there is not even information about the thousands of Tamils who are missing or were abducted; that even after two years rehabilitation and resettlement work has not been completed; and that in Tamil-inhabited areas—even in temples—the Sri Lankan Government has concentrated troops and kept Tamils at all times within the tight grip of fear, contrary to human rights.

Another piece of information released by Comrade T.K. Rangarajan shakes our hearts. He said: “When we went to the Batticaloa areas, we encountered a painful situation. I truly shed tears. We saw that 35,000 Tamil women had become widows in the Sri Lankan war. Of these, 13,000 widows are under twenty-three years of age.”

I wish to remind Mr. T.K. Rangarajan, who has said that no one in Sri Lanka is now insisting on Separate Eelam, of one event. Before the election held in 1977, at the conference held at Vaddukottai in 1976, the liberation alliance of all the Tamil people, the organisation of the hill-country people and others all came together and framed as a resolution the decision that “Separate Tamil Eelam” alone was the goal; they contested the election on that basis. In the election held in the Tamil areas, they won eighteen constituencies, losing only one. “Separate Tamil Eelam” was the verdict that the Tamil people themselves gave as early as 1977. But thirty-five years later, the present feeling in the hearts of Tamils spread throughout the world has become even firmer in looking toward the single direction of Separate Tamil Eelam.

On 14 November 2007, writing in *Murasoli* about Cuba and that country’s liberation hero Fidel Castro, I said:

> “Cuba is a very small country. Its population is sixty lakhs. One cannot judge that country merely by its borders or its population. Fidel Castro, who was—and remains—the reason it found a place as a country capable of thinking on a world scale, sowed the seed of that country’s victory while he was president of the university students’ council. Cuba, a tiny country beside the great superpower America, has raised its voice declaring that it will not bow to imperialism, and for the past fifty years has functioned successfully to such an extent that the peoples of the world look upon it in wonder.”

I hope Mr. T.K. Rangarajan will compare what I wrote then about Cuba and Fidel Castro with today’s demand for Separate Tamil Eelam and recognise the truth.

Because “political solution” and “devolution of power” have remained nothing more than words that never enter practice; because the Sri Lankan Government lacks even the most basic sense of honesty required to honour any assurance it gives before the world; because lakhs of lives have been sacrificed in pursuit of the ideal of Separate Tamil Eelam; and because lakhs of Tamils have paid an enormous price—losing their possessions in their own homeland, parting from relatives, wandering the streets of countries across the world as destitute people and refugees, bewildered and in tears—Sri Lankan Tamils will never agree to prepare themselves for, or reconcile themselves to, anything less than Separate Tamil Eelam!

Every effort undertaken by people such as Amirthalingam and by foreigners to find a peaceful solution to Sri Lanka’s ethnic problem was broken at the very point of advance. Because agreements that were made were not made wholeheartedly, they were turned into heaps of wastepaper and allowed to fly away in the wind. The armed struggle of the liberation fighters too was crushed through the extraordinary use of military force. Sinhala majoritarianism did not allow even the slightest room in its mind for equal rights for Tamils.

The Sri Lankan Tamil question cannot be solved through repressive excesses, abuses of authority or the dance of dictatorship. It is the command of time that a solution to the Sri Lankan Tamil question be found without further delay or hesitation. It cannot be postponed indefinitely.

Even if those who raised the song of Eelam liberation are killed and buried, that song has mingled with the air. The song cannot be separated from the air. Through the air, that song has become inseparably mingled with the feelings of Tamils throughout the world. Poet Erode Tamilanban’s lines, carrying this meaning, flash now in my memory:

> “The veena  
> may be smashed and thrown away—  
> the song once plucked  
> is in the air;”

In the liberation struggle of Bangladesh, called “Sonar Bangla,” did I demand that, in the Sri Lankan Tamil question, India adopt an approach like the one it used to help Mujibur Rahman? What I wrote was that, democratically, following the procedure accepted by the United Nations and the international community, a referendum should be held among Tamils and a decision taken on Separate Eelam. I wrote that as one who stands in the path of Arignar Anna and forever respects and honours the dignity of democracy!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**25 April 2012**
