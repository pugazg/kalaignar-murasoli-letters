# 3593. Alas! Government Land Under AIADMK Rule—Arokara!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3593](../../../chapters/3593-ayyakko-admk-aatchiyil-arasu-nilam-arokara.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 30](../../../pages/page-030.md)–[PDF 36](../../../pages/page-036.md)  
**Date:** 10 October 2011

**Udanpirappē,**

In an area that is like the heart of Chennai city, adjoining the Anna Flyover, 13 kani, 4 grounds and 1,262 square feet of government-owned land was being used for private gain by Horticulture V. Krishnamurthy, an AIADMK functionary, in the name of an organisation called the “Horticultural Society”; from time to time he was also said to be using the building there for his own political activities. When the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam came to power in 1989, action was taken on the complaints received. With the aim of bringing this priceless land into public use, the government decided to resume the site so that horticulture could be developed without causing any harm to the environment and horticultural research could be conducted and encouraged. Notice was accordingly issued to the Horticultural Society.

Opposing this action of the DMK government, Horticulture Krishnamurthy approached the Madras High Court. Dismissing that petition, the High Court directed the Secretary of the Revenue Department of the Tamil Nadu government to give the Horticultural Society an opportunity for a personal hearing. Accordingly, the Revenue Secretary personally conducted the inquiry, decided that the government should take back the entire land, and a Government Order was issued on 5 August 1989 on that basis. In accordance with the Government Order, the Chennai District Collector also sent a report to the government stating that the land had been resumed.

Against this action taken by the DMK government for a public purpose, Horticulture Krishnamurthy once again filed a writ petition in the Madras High Court on behalf of the Horticultural Society. While this petition, filed in 1989, was still pending, there was a change of government in 1991, and no action was taken during the AIADMK government that remained in office until 1996. In an order dated 19 June 1998, the High Court set aside the DMK government's order resuming the land. The Tamil Nadu government—the DMK government—immediately appealed in the High Court against that order. In its ruling of 20 August 1998, the High Court temporarily directed that the existing position should continue: “Status quo as on date shall be maintained.”

There was another change of government in 2001 and the AIADMK came to power. While the appeal initiated in the Madras High Court under the DMK government remained pending without a final judgment, on 21 January 2004, during AIADMK rule—and without written permission from the government—the government advocate told the High Court: “As per instructions, I may be permitted to withdraw appeals,” and withdrew the case that had been pursued under the DMK government. The procedure followed by the government advocate in this matter was not in the least consistent with, or appropriate to, the government's official procedures.

After the government advocate himself made such a request during AIADMK rule, the High Court, having no other course, permitted withdrawal of the case pursued under the DMK government and ordered the appeal dismissed. Immediately thereafter, on 3 March 2006, a Government Order was issued to hand the land back to the Horticultural Society. In the meantime, because the Model Code of Conduct for the 2006 Assembly election came into force, the Government Order issued on 3 March 2006 was not implemented.

After the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam came to power in the 2006 Assembly election, the issue of the Horticultural Society was discussed at the DMK government's Cabinet meeting on 7 July 2006. It was decided to cancel the AIADMK government's decision that supported an individual's pursuit of private gain and to file an appeal once again in the High Court and continue the case. On that petition, the Madras High Court, in an order dated 4 April 2007, permitted the appeal to proceed. The case filed by the Horticultural Society in the Supreme Court of India against this was dismissed on 16 May 2007.

In the proceedings on the appeals filed by the DMK government in the Madras High Court, the High Court, in its judgment dated 11 April 2008, allowed the appeals filed by the DMK government and set aside the judgment of the single judge dated 19 June 1998, which had quashed the DMK government's order resuming the land. In its judgment of 11 April 2008, the High Court stated:

> “Without obtaining the government's prior permission, the Agricultural Horticultural Society had already subleased a portion of this land to ‘Woodlands Drive-In Restaurant.’ It was brought to the notice of the Court that, after Mr. Krishnamurthy became the honorary secretary of the Society, he was dealing with this land as though it were his own property. Many allegations have been made concerning financial irregularities in this Society. Following this, the District Registrar initiated action under the Tamil Nadu Societies Registration Act. When such serious allegations exist against the Society, the single judge, disregarding the reasons given by the government for resuming the land for public purposes, allowed the writ petitions filed by them on the basis of an unproved allegation that the action was motivated by ‘political vendetta.’ During the DMK government, the Chief Secretary stated in his petition that huge advertising hoardings had been erected without government permission and cement works had been carried out on the ground. Apart from conducting a flower show at an annual expenditure of five thousand rupees, the Society has not been shown to have made any development during the past twenty years. Further, because large sums were collected in connection with the flower show while only a small amount was spent, resulting in financial irregularities, the appropriate officer must inquire into the Society and its secretary.
>
> “On one side stand the norms under the Revenue Standing Orders and government guidelines requiring accommodation to be provided to poor and ordinary people who have long had no place to live; on the other, these lands remain with this Society, which uses only a portion of them for horticultural development. Documents show that Mr. V. Krishnamurthy, honorary secretary of the Society, has been using government-leased lands for his own purposes—that is, for a rental-car business and for an advertising company called Meena Advertisers. Therefore, no fault can be found with the government's action to resume the said lands. Mr. V. Krishnamurthy has stated in his own petition that he conducts car and advertising-company businesses.
>
> “Considering that the land is already in the government's possession, and on the basis of the petition filed by the Chief Secretary to the government, these lands must be used only for public purposes. The sublessee must vacate the premises on his own.”

Udanpirappē, following this order of the Madras High Court, Horticulture Krishnamurthy appealed to the Supreme Court. In its interim order dated 13 November 2009, the Supreme Court stated that, since the land was already in the government's possession, there was nothing wrong in the government continuing to use it for the development of horticulture. The Chennai District Collector therefore took the land from the Horticultural Society and handed it over to the government's Horticulture Department.

A large park was planned on this land—to protect the environment, to enable the public to understand the importance of flowers, medicinal herbs and plants, and to encourage horticultural research—and it was decided to implement the project in stages. First, an excellent park was created in the area where “Drive-In Woodlands” had stood, and I inaugurated it. Even today, thousands of people visit and enjoy that park every day.

Before expanding the park into the site opposite it, where the office of the Horticultural Society had been functioning, a notice was sent to the Society on 15 December 2010, during the DMK government, asking it to vacate the site and hand it over to the government. Before issuing the notice, encumbrances going back one hundred years were checked and it was confirmed that the land was indeed government land. Horticulture Krishnamurthy challenged this notice in the Madras High Court. The judgment directed that the Society be given an opportunity for a personal hearing. Accordingly, he was given opportunities for a personal hearing both before and after the 2011 Assembly election. But until the general election was held in May, Horticulture Krishnamurthy did not appear for the personal hearing. After the AIADMK won the general election, he suddenly appeared before the Chennai District Collector on 15 July 2011 and submitted certain documents.

The Chennai District Collector, yielding to influence exerted from somewhere, to pressure and to political dominance—and after obtaining the government advocate's opinion merely for form's sake—issued an order on 22 August 2011, under the AIADMK government, declaring that the 4 kani, 18 grounds and 1,683 square feet of land on which the Horticultural Society stands belonged to that Society itself.

In the permanent land register, this land is recorded as government land. A check of encumbrances extending over more than one hundred years confirmed that the land belonged to the government. At no stage in the cases concerning this land that had continued over many years was it stated that the land belonged to the Horticultural Society. It was precisely because this was government land that, under the DMK government, it was taken from the Horticultural Society and handed over on 8 May 2008 to the Horticulture Department, one of the departments of the Tamil Nadu government.

When this is the position, there is no basis whatsoever for deciding that this land belongs to Horticulture Krishnamurthy's Society. It was the District Collector who had earlier decided that the site belonged to the government. Now, through the present District Collector, it has been made to be said that the site does not belong to the government, and orders have been issued to hand it over to Horticulture Krishnamurthy—within just five months of this government coming to power. And these are the people who say they are going to provide corruption-free administration!

At this time, when local-body elections are about to take place, I have exposed this major corruption committed by those in the AIADMK government. I ask the people of the State to remember that, if they are given another opportunity in these elections, a path will be opened for corruption to continue.

At a time when the AIADMK government is fabricating numerous false cases against opposition parties in the name of “land grabbing,” should not good people and impartial newspapers come forward to question the injustice of government land in the heart of Chennai city, worth no less than ₹200 crore, being grabbed with the blessings of the AIADMK rulers and the assistance of officials? I remind everyone in this State today of the question Arignar Anna asked then: “What will become of the country if good people simply remain indifferent, saying, ‘What is it to us?’”

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**10 October 2011**

## Letter-specific notes

1. **Udanpirappē** is retained as Kalaignar's characteristic political-familial address.
2. The title's **`அரோகரா`** is retained as *Arokara* rather than flattened into a literal English equivalent, preserving the source's sarcastic exclamatory force.
3. Land measurements (`காணி`, `கிரவுண்ட்`) are retained as **kani** and **ground(s)** rather than converted, because the source states them as legal/land-record measurements and supplies no conversion.
4. The source's English court phrases **“Status quo as on date shall be maintained”** and **“As per instructions, I may be permitted to withdraw appeals”** are preserved verbatim.
5. The long passage attributed by Kalaignar to the High Court judgment is translated as quoted material; no outside judgment text has been substituted for the Tamil source.
