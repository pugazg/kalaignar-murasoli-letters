# 3705. In a State Ruled by a Woman, Women's Lives Turn to Dust!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This thought-preserving translation retains the source's list of reported sexual-violence cases, argument on punishment and the death penalty, legal and judicial references, names, dates, quotations and political framing. Tamil remains authoritative. `Udanpirappē` is retained as Kalaignar's distinctive address.

**Date:** 19 February 2013

## English translation

Udanpirappē,

At a time when a woman Chief Minister sits on the seat of power and rules Tamil Nadu, look at the list of women subjected to sexual violence in recent months:

- In Andiyappan village near Tirupattur in Vellore district, nine-year-old Gayathri was sexually assaulted and murdered.
- In Sivagiri, a woman named Nandini was raped and murdered.
- In Saidapet, the body of a woman named Vijaya was found.
- At Srivaikuntam in Thoothukudi district, thirteen-year-old schoolgirl Punitha was raped and murdered.
- In Nagapattinam district, an eleven-year-old Dalit girl was raped by two men.
- At Virudhachalam, a young woman named Suganthi was raped on the bank of the Manimuthar river.
- At Manjakuzhi village near Chidambaram, a young woman named Sandhya was raped and thrown onto the street from the third floor.
- At Thatharavalasai village in Harur taluk, Dharmapuri district, a married young woman named Menaka was raped and murdered.
- In Matha Nagar Second Street, Thoothukudi city, Mariammal, a young woman separated from her husband and living with her father and three children, was raped after an intruder entered the house before dawn.
- At Rukmanipuram in Vanur, Villupuram district, schoolgirl Kalki was raped and found hanging dead in a casuarina grove.
- At Udumalpet, a twelve-year-old girl was sexually assaulted; unable to bear the humiliation, she set herself on fire.
- In Namakkal, an eighteen-year-old young woman was raped by a four-member gang near the District Collector's office.
- At Timiri near Arcot in Vellore district, seventeen-year-old Abinaya, facing threats and intimidation, died by self-immolation in an attempt to protect herself from sexual assault.
- At Pozhichalur in Chennai, Premalatha was raped and murdered and her body thrown into a forested area.
- Near Thottiyam in Tiruchirappalli district, a married young woman named Meena, who had gone to a relative's wedding, was abducted in a car and raped by a four-member gang.
- At Pudur in Orathanadu, an eleven-year-old sixth-standard girl was raped while grazing goats.
- Near Ranipet, a young girl's body was found burned in a cremation ground.

This continuing list shows how atrocities against women have been occurring without restraint. When the medical-college student in Delhi died after sexual violence, I said in my condolence statement that such horrific incidents were being reported not merely in one place but across India, particularly in Tamil Nadu; that students and poor women were increasingly becoming victims; and that the harsh punishment for such offenders should be solitary confinement for the rest of their lives. I urged the Union and State Governments to take a firm decision on this.

The Verma Commission's recommendations broadly followed the direction of this view by recommending life imprisonment in cases involving rape and murder. But in Tamil Nadu, action in sexual-violence cases has largely remained at the level of formally registering a police case. After the Delhi incident, this Government too, wishing to sound severe, announced that sexual offenders should be detained under the Goondas Act and even spoke of chemical castration. Yet sexual assaults have not stopped.

In Coimbatore, five people, including a thirteen-year-old schoolgirl's maternal uncle, were arrested for allegedly injecting her with a sedative and committing a sexual offence. Women's organisations and others condemned the incident. Newspapers carried the report on 17 February 2013.

The same day's newspapers reported that in Dharmapuri district, near Bommidi, a sixteen-year-old girl had allegedly been abducted and sexually assaulted on the 14th. As such offences increase, questions naturally arise about what punishment should be imposed on people committing crimes completely contrary to culture and civilisation. The Union Government has gone a step beyond the Verma Commission's recommendations and announced that the death penalty should be provided in certain cases.

The death penalty has now become a major question in Indian public life.

When the issue arose regarding the death sentences of Perarivalan and two others, I said that my general view was that there should be no death penalty at all, and that the DMK had already appealed regarding those three death sentences.

Meanwhile, in the case against four associates of Veerappan—Gnanaprakasam, Simon, Meesai Madayan and Bilavendran—the Mysore TADA court sentenced them to life imprisonment. Only because an appeal was taken to the Supreme Court did the Supreme Court alter the life sentences into death sentences in 2004. Nine years passed without those death sentences being carried out. The four submitted mercy petitions to the President of India. After remaining pending for a long time, those petitions were rejected on the 11th of this month. Thereafter hurried steps appeared to be under way to execute them, and newspapers began reporting on the matter.

On behalf of the four, a petition was filed in the Supreme Court on 16 February 2013 opposing the executions and seeking a stay. The petition also asked that their death sentences be commuted to life imprisonment. Advocates Colin Gonsalves and Samik Narain went to the residence of Chief Justice Altamas Kabir, filed the petition and sought an urgent hearing.

The Chief Justice said there was no basis for the claim that the four might be hanged on Sunday and that the petition would be heard at the appropriate time. Following this, views on the death penalty have been expressed vigorously across the country from many quarters. Meanwhile, the Supreme Court imposed an interim stay protecting them until Wednesday. Veerappan's wife herself has said the four condemned men had no connection with Veerappan. Their families too say they are innocent. Their allegation that a case was deliberately foisted on them during Jayalalithaa's Government in 1993, and their demand for a CBI reinvestigation, cannot simply be ignored. Those in authority must remember that even if a guilty person escapes, an innocent person must not be punished.

Attention is now being drawn to an earlier Constitution Bench judgment of the Supreme Court in *Triveniben v. State of Gujarat*, which stated that where there is excessive and unexplained delay in carrying out a death sentence, a condemned prisoner may approach the Supreme Court for relief under Article 32 of the Constitution. Because there has been a nine-year delay here, it is argued that the four have the right to ask the Supreme Court to commute their death sentences.

Another judgment now being cited is the Supreme Court's decision in *Kehar Singh v. Union of India*, which held that an order of the President under Article 72 of the Constitution is subject to judicial review.

The views expressed by former Supreme Court Justice V.R. Krishna Iyer about the death sentences of these four also deserve deep consideration. He appealed to those in power to remove the death penalty from the statute book. He said an execution carried out by the Government is nothing other than a killing by the Government itself; that the people of India should mobilise against such grave acts committed by the State; that the Government should not arbitrarily permit the constitutional “right to life” to be sent to the gallows; that ninety per cent of the world's countries had already abolished the death penalty; and that India too should abolish it and demonstrate to the world that this is the land of Gandhi.

These are the same principles I have been expressing for many years. In today's circumstances, I strongly urge the Union Government, legal experts and the courts to weigh these views carefully and seriously consider removing the death penalty from the statute book in a manner that honours human rights and humanitarian values. Had this long-standing view already been properly considered and accepted, the executions now being carried out one after another might have been prevented altogether.

With affection,
M.K.
19-2-2013
