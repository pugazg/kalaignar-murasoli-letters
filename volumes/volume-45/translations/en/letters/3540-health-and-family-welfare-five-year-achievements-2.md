# 3540. Five-Year Achievements of the Health and Family Welfare Department! (2)

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

I continue to write about the achievements and schemes carried out by the DMK government in the Health and Family Welfare Department during the past five years.

### Primary Health Centres being upgraded

On the principle that every block must have at least one 30-bed hospital, during the past five years 165 Primary Health Centres have been upgraded as 30-bed Primary Health Centres at a cost of ₹104.53 crore, with additional doctors appointed and modern medical equipment provided.

To encourage and support the functioning of Primary Health Centres, 213 Primary Health Centres have been repaired through District Health Societies at a cost of ₹16.08 crore. Wards in which pregnant women and mothers who have delivered can stay and receive treatment, labour rooms and operating theatres have been renovated and expanded. Existing infrastructure in Primary Health Centres has been improved at a cost of ₹20.38 crore. In the current financial year, construction works have been completed in a further 300 Primary Health Centres at a cost of ₹18 crore. Work has also been undertaken to construct their own buildings for 20 Primary Health Centres that do not have buildings of their own, at a cost of ₹4 crore.

Computers have been supplied to all Primary Health Centres at a cost of ₹5.74 crore. Information is received every day through the internet, and the functioning of the Primary Health Centres is monitored. Telephone connections have been provided to all Primary Health Centres and cellphones to all Village Health Nurses, enabling rural people to contact them easily in emergencies and receive appropriate treatment. Departmental activities are also reviewed through direct contact with district officers by means of the video-conference hall, followed in the source by the visibly garbled parenthetical `எனைநடி ஊடிகேநசநடேந`.

### Mobile Medical Teams

At the rate of one Mobile Medical Team for each Panchayat Union, 385 teams have been established at a cost of ₹37.18 crore. Each team has been provided with a vehicle, and a doctor, nurse, assistant and driver have been appointed.

These teams travel to remote villages and provide free medical treatment to the people living there. Up to 28 February 2011, 3,98,800 camps had been conducted and 2,74,08,896 people had benefited.

### Medicines

Over five years, quality medicines worth ₹1,282 crore have been purchased and supplied to government hospitals and Primary Health Centres for the benefit of the public.

### Modern medical equipment

At a cost of more than ₹600 crore, government hospitals and Primary Health Centres have been provided with many advanced medical devices comparable to those in leading private hospitals, including MRI scanners, 64-slice CT scanners, lithotripsy equipment, colour Doppler, semi-auto analysers, cobalt therapy equipment, linear accelerators, PCR equipment, dialysis machines and cath labs.

### New government medical colleges

In accordance with this government's policy that government medical colleges should be opened in districts that do not have them, medical colleges have been opened in Dharmapuri, Villupuram and Tiruvarur districts. Three more medical colleges—in Sivaganga, Perambalur and Tiruvannamalai—are to be opened in the current academic year. In the next academic year, new medical colleges are to be opened in Cuddalore, Virudhunagar, Dindigul, Pudukkottai and Ramanathapuram districts.

With ₹100 crore from the Union Government and ₹54 crore from the State Government, Salem Government Mohan Kumaramangalam Medical College Hospital has been upgraded, at a total cost of ₹154 crore, into a high-standard hospital with several specialty departments. A nursing college has also been started in this hospital during the current year.

With Union Government financial assistance, steps are being taken to upgrade Madurai Government Rajaji Hospital into a high-standard hospital with several specialty departments at a cost of ₹150 crore. The Department of Anaesthesiology in this hospital is one of the largest anaesthesiology departments in India. In the current financial year, an order has been issued to upgrade it as a higher centre at a cost of ₹2.60 crore, and further action is being taken. Construction of an accident-treatment unit costing ₹28.64 crore has been completed there, and the unit will begin functioning shortly.

### Development of medical-college hospitals

A ₹100-crore development programme has been planned for Stanley Hospital, Chennai, and additional buildings are under construction.

Additional buildings are being constructed at an estimated cost of ₹395 crore in Kilpauk Government Hospital, Chengalpattu Government Hospital, Chennai Government Dental Hospital, the Institute of Child Health at Egmore, Royapettah Government Hospital, Government Kasturba Gandhi Hospital at Triplicane and Government Maternity Hospital at Egmore.

Steps are being taken to construct a new building, auditorium and hostels for medical-college students at Chennai Medical College at a cost of ₹76 crore.

To mark the centenary of Coimbatore Government Medical College Hospital, orders have been issued for a multi-storeyed building at a cost of ₹50 crore, and construction is to begin shortly. A cancer unit is to be started at Kanyakumari Government Medical College Hospital at a cost of ₹12.82 crore; a special government mental-health hospital in Theni town at a cost of ₹10 crore; and a new nursing school at Chengalpattu Government Hospital. Steps are also being taken to start nursing schools and urban health centres during the current year in the buildings where Villupuram and Tiruvarur district hospitals had already been functioning.

### Dental-care facilities

Facilities for treating dental diseases among rural people have been established in 148 Primary Health Centres.

Dental-treatment units have also been newly started in 52 government taluk hospitals. Dentists and dental technicians have been appointed, and new equipment has been supplied.

### Accident and emergency-treatment facilities

To provide immediate treatment to people affected in accidents on national highways, accident and emergency-treatment units have so far been opened and are functioning in nine hospitals at Namakkal, Cuddalore, Padmanabhapuram, Walajapet, Melur, Tenkasi, Tambaram, Perambalur and Omalur. Such units are being established in the district headquarters hospitals at Krishnagiri, Dindigul, Kovilpatti and Karur; the government medical-college hospitals at Vellore and Kilpauk; Madurai Rajaji Hospital; Salem Mohan Kumaramangalam Hospital; and the government medical-college hospitals at Kanyakumari and Tirunelveli.

### Improvement of infrastructure in government hospitals

Under the World Bank-assisted Tamil Nadu Health Project, new buildings have been constructed in 198 government hospitals at an estimated cost of ₹285 crore. Infrastructure in taluk and non-taluk hospitals has been strengthened with ₹75 crore from National Health Mission funds. Because Tamil Nadu alone implemented the World Bank-assisted Tamil Nadu Health Project and completed its target within the stipulated period, the World Bank agreed to extend the project for another three years with financial assistance of ₹627 crore, and various works have begun.

### Neonatal Intensive Care Units

In 15 districts with high infant mortality, Neonatal Intensive Care Units have been created at a cost of ₹11.48 crore, with additional doctors and trained nurses appointed and the necessary medical equipment supplied. In the current year, ₹26.84 crore has been allocated and action begun to establish such units in a further ten districts.

### Emergency life-saving treatment scheme for infants

The emergency life-saving treatment scheme for infants from birth to one year of age applies to all families holding identity cards under the Kalaignar Insurance Scheme. An explanatory card for the scheme was issued on 26 February 2011. ₹10 crore has been allocated in the 2010–11 financial year and ₹15 crore in 2011–12.

### Dental-protection scheme for school students

The Dental Protection Scheme for School Students was launched on 26 February 2011 at a cost of ₹5.66 crore. It is implemented for students from Classes 3 to 8 in government and government-aided schools. Through this scheme, 55 lakh students will benefit.

### Upgraded School Health Scheme

This scheme was implemented in ten districts on 26 February 2011 at a cost of ₹6.18 crore. The printed source states that **32.06 students** benefit. Health teams have been formed in each school and new health cards have been issued.

### Indian systems of medicine

Indian-system medical units were already functioning in 479 Primary Health Centres. Since 2006, such units have been started in another 444 Primary Health Centres, and infrastructure is being improved with financial assistance of ₹54.90 crore from the Union and State Governments.

In 31 Primary Health Centres with Siddha units, yoga and naturopathy maternity units have been started at a cost of ₹59.19 lakh. Chennai Government Yoga and Naturopathy College has been upgraded at a cost of ₹90 lakh, and Chennai Government Unani Medical College at a cost of ₹92.50 lakh. At the Government Siddha Medical College, Palayamkottai, degree courses described in the source as **“Nasing Therapy”** and **Pharmacist** have been started at a cost of ₹3 crore each. At Arignar Anna Government Hospital of Indian Medicine in Chennai, diploma courses in **“Nasing Therapy”** and **Pharmacist** have likewise been started at ₹3 crore each.

**M.K.**

மருத்துவப் பிரிவுகள் தொடங்கப்பட்டு 54 கோடியே 90 இலட்சம்

ரூபாய் மத்திய மற்றும் மாநில அரசின் நிதியுதவியோடு அடிப்படை
வசதிகள் மேம்படுத்தப்படுகின்றன. சித்தா பிரிவு உள்ள 31 ஆரம்ப
சுகாதார நிலையங்களில் யோகா மற்றும் இயற்கை மருத்துவ முறை
மகப்பேறு பிரிவுகள் 59 இலட்சத்து 19 ஆயிரம் ரூபாய்ச் செலவில்
தொடங்கப்பட்டுள்ளன. சென்னை அரசு யோகா மற்றும் இயற்கைக்
கல்லூரி 90 இலட்சம் ரூபாய்ச் செலவிலும், சென்னை அரசு
யுனானி மருத்துவக் கல்லூரி 92.50 இலட்சம் ரூபாய்ச் செலவிலும்
மேம்படுத்தப்பட்டுள்ளது. பாளையங்கோட்டை அரசினர் சித்த
மருத்துவக் கல்லூரியில் நாஸிங் தெரபி ஒரு மருந்தாளுநர் என்ற
பட்டப் படிப்புக்கு தலா 3 கோடி ரூபாய்ச் செலவிலும், சென்னை
அறிஞர் அண்ணா அரசினர் இந்திய மருத்துவ மனையில் நாஸிங்
தெரபி, மருந்தாளுநர் பட்டயப் படிப்புகள் தலா 3 கோடி ரூபாய்ச்
செலவிலும் தொடங்கப்பட்டுள்ளன.
(தொடர்ச்சி நாளை)
