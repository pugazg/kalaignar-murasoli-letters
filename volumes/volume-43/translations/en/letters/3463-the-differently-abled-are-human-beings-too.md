# 3463. The Differently Abled Are Human Beings Too!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> The actual letter-start title on PDF 274 uses `மானிடரே!`; the printed contents separately uses `மனிதரே!`. The English title follows the actual record start while the source-layer difference is preserved below. This letter deliberately contrasts older `ஊனமுற்றோர்` terminology with the rights-based term `மாற்றுத் திறனாளிகள்`; English therefore uses **differently abled persons** when carrying that explicit terminological argument, while source-supplied statutory English is preserved exactly as printed.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3463](../../../chapters/3463-maatru-thiranaaligalum-maanidare.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 274](../../../pages/page-274.md)–[PDF 278](../../../pages/page-278.md)  
**Date:** 2 March 2010

**Udanpirappē,**

At the thanksgiving function held by the film industry on 6-2-2010, younger brother Lawrence Raghavendra, choreographer and actor, brought more than twenty children and had them stand on the stage. He explained the medical-treatment assistance that the DMK government had provided to all of them through the “Young Children’s Heart Protection Scheme,” and some of those children themselves movingly described their condition. From the time I witnessed that, and as a continuation of it, as usual this morning I had the opportunity to watch and listen on “Kalaignar Television” to younger brother Suba. Veerapandian’s word-picture programme “Oru Sol Kelir.” As soon as I understood that he was going to say something about people with physical disabilities, I sat down intending to hear the entire talk.

Even though many voluntary organisations have come forward to provide assistance for people with physical disabilities, and governments too have taken up that work, this collection of thoughts by younger brother Suba. Veerapandian greatly attracted me.

He said:

“Work for differently abled persons is now being carried out on a **Right Based** basis. Hereafter, neither governments nor non-governmental organisations should confine themselves merely to such work as giving them free bicycles or keeping and caring for them in charitable homes. Going beyond that, we must work to establish their rights, ensure their participation in every field and create opportunities for such participation. They do not expect any freebies from the government. The laws enacted for them must be implemented. They ask that their rights be won under the rule of those laws.”

After he finished speaking, I obtained and read the details of the **United Nations Convention Rights for Person with Disabilities Act, 2007** that he had referred to in that talk. Yes, it became clear that when such a law was adopted at the United Nations, India was the seventh country among those that accepted and signed it.

From the figures concerning persons with disabilities shown in connection with that law, I was able to learn that they constitute two per cent of India’s total population and that their number in Tamil Nadu is 18 lakh.

Within the category of people with physical disabilities are all those in human society who cannot hear, cannot see, cannot speak, cannot use their hands or legs, and those with mental illness. Rather than setting them aside as people unable to use particular organs, or placing them in a separate list, and rather than calling them by terms that today sound degrading — blind, deaf, lame, disabled, crippled — the practice is to call them generally **differently abled persons** instead of `ஊனமுற்றோர்`, on the basis that a reduction in the functioning of one organ enhances another of their abilities by an additional measure. This approach was discussed at the United Nations some years earlier, in 2007 itself, and it was in that connection that the law was enacted.

Even now, the following remain their demands. They do not stretch out their hands in the manner of beggars asking for these demands to be granted; my own view too is that they should not have to stretch out their hands.

1. The United Nations charter for differently abled persons must be fully implemented in India.

2. Along with reservation in employment in all sectors, **3 per cent** of the resources of every sector must be spent for the development of differently abled persons.

3. To establish the political rights of differently abled persons, they must immediately be given **3 per cent reservation** in the State Legislative Assembly and in Parliament — the Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha.

4. A ministry for differently abled persons must be created both in the State and in the Union Government. There must also be a separate minister. (In Tamil Nadu, instead of keeping “Welfare of the Disabled” linked with the midday-meal scheme, a separate ministry must be created for differently abled persons.)

5. At a time when government schools and colleges have declined in number, differently abled male and female students studying in private schools and colleges must be given educational assistance.

6. A house-to-house census of differently abled persons must immediately be conducted across the country. This information must be properly computerised and preserved.

7. The national identity card issued to differently abled persons must serve as a complete document of acceptance. Separate medical certificates, residence certificates and the like must not be demanded each time they seek an assistance measure, a job or an assistive device.

8. A fifteen-digit electronic identity card must be issued. All services and welfare schemes must be provided through it.

9. Government assistance and welfare schemes are denied by taking the family income of differently abled persons into account. For a differently abled person who has completed eighteen years of age, government assistance must be given by calculating that individual’s own income rather than the family income. At present, many receive no assistance at all because family income is cited as the reason.

10. Under the **Persons with Disabilities (Equal Opportunities, Protection of Rights and Full Participation) Act 1995** — the 1995 law for persons with disabilities (`சம வாய்ப்பு, பங்கேற்பு`) — the Union and State governments must immediately provide the total employment reservation due for fifteen years.

All people must extend their support to differently abled persons in securing their social, economic and political rights. Educationists, politicians, social activists, voluntary organisations, trade unions and the media must carry this forward.

These are all described as their demands. But, as I said at the beginning, let us understand them not as demands but as an **assertion of rights**. To give them the opportunities due to them, I believe that through this government we can contact the Union Government, appropriately wipe away the sufferings of differently abled persons and raise them equally with everyone else. Through our government, we can place this idea before the Union Government and make a request to it.

Under the law enacted by the United Nations forum, certain important concessions — particularly the demand that they be given their due percentage in every sphere — should first be considered by us. Instead of approximately giving assistance on behalf of the government out of a sense of humaneness, we must contact the Union Government so that specified percentage-based opportunities can be provided to establish the rights they demand. I expect that the Union Government too, remembering that India signed the 2007 United Nations charter as the seventh country to do so, will agree, take the necessary action and arrive at a favourable decision.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**2-3-2010**

7. மாற்றுத் திறன் உடையோருக்கு வழங்கப்
பட்டிருக்கும் தேசிய அடையாள அட்டை
முழுமையான ஏற்பு ஆவணமாக இருக்க வேண்டும்.

வேண்டும்.

9. மாற்றுத்திறன் உடையோரின் குடும்ப
வருமானத்தை கணக்கில் எடுத்துக் கொண்டு
அரசின் உதவிகள், நலத்திட்டங்கள்
மறுக்கப்படுகின்றன. பதினெட்டு வயது
பூர்த்தியான மாற்றுத்திறன் உடையோரின்
குடும்ப வருமானத்தை கணக்கில் எடுக்காமல்

எடுத்துக் கொண்டு அரசின் உதவிகள் வழங்கப்பட
வேண்டும். குடும்ப வருமானத்தைக் காரணமாகக்

உதவிகளும் கிடைப்பதில்லை.

10. Persons with Disabilities (Equal Opportunities,
Protection of Rights and Full Participation) Act 1995
ஊனமுற்றோருக்கான சட்டம் 1995 (சம வாய்ப்பு,
பங்கேற்பு)ன்படி பதினைந்து ஆண்டுகளுக்கான
மொத்தமான வேலை வாய்ப்பு இட ஒதுக்கீட்டை
உடனடியாக மத்திய மாநில அரசுகள் வழங்கிட
வேண்டும்.

மாற்றுத்திறன் உடையோருக்கான சமூக, பொருளாதார,
அரசியல் உரிமைகளைப் பெறுவதில் அனைத்து மக்களும்
தங்களது ஆதரவினை வழங்க வேண்டும். கல்வியாளர்கள்,
