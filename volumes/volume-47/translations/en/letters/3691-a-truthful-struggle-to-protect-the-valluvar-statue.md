# 3691. A Truthful Struggle to Protect the Valluvar Statue!

## English translation

Udanpirappē,

Yesterday, 1 January 2013—and indeed throughout the previous night—I could not sleep. At Kanyakumari, where the three seas meet, the majestic 133-foot statue of Ayya Valluvar has stood unlike anything elsewhere. For the past year and a half, under a Government in which Tamil feeling seems dead and which does not know how to honour the greatness of the classical language, the statue has reportedly been left without maintenance and is gradually being damaged by the salt-laden sea air. Around four thousand people a day, moved by Tamil and by Valluvar's words, travel by boat to marvel at it. Visitor fees alone bring the Government about ₹60 lakh a year, yet the Government has not found the heart to care for the statue. Even Dinamani, a newspaper supportive of the Government, carried a large half-page report on this.

Though Dinamani's public-opinion pieces, editorials and cartoons often attack the DMK and me as its leader, I have never underestimated editor Vaithiyanathan's love of Tamil. Moved by Tamil itself, he published that report.

I also remember an editorial Dinamani published on 4 January 2000, just after the statue's inauguration. It said, in substance, that representatives of Tamil communities across the world had taken part; that the celebration led by Chief Minister Karunanidhi had been an all-party event; that the same Karunanidhi who created Valluvar Kottam in Chennai had conceived the Valluvar statue; and that the statue would stand as a lasting testimony not only to sculptor Ganapathi Sthapathi's artistry but also to Chief Minister Karunanidhi's Tamil heart. Can that be forgotten?

To protect the statue from rain and salty air, a chemical coating must periodically be applied. During the DMK Government, more than ₹65 lakh was spent on such treatment. The report said that after the change of government in 2011 this essential coating had not been applied for a year and a half and the statue was deteriorating. Reading that placed a heavy burden on my mind. Yesterday I could not even smile and converse normally with some who came affectionately to greet me, because the Valluvar statue at Kanyakumari has a long history for me.

The DMK Government returned to office in January 1989. Presenting the Budget on 17 March 1990, I announced that a 133-foot statue of Tiruvalluvar would be installed at Kanyakumari. The work was entrusted to Ganapathi Sthapathi, and on 6 September 1990 I personally began the sculpting work by taking up the chisel.

The Government later decided that the historic, world-renowned 133-foot statue would be inaugurated in a grand two-day State function on 31 December 1999 and 1 January 2000, with Tamil scholars from many countries invited.

On 1 January 2000, after events throughout the morning, the principal inauguration began around 4.30 p.m. Education Minister Professor Anbazhagan presided. Sirkazhi Sivachidambaram and T.K.S. Kalaivanan sang the Tamil Thai Vazhthu, and Information Minister V. Mullai Vendhan welcomed the gathering. Amid thunderous cheers from lakhs of Tamil people gathered at Kanyakumari, I unveiled the sky-reaching statue. Those who witnessed it felt they had received a great blessing in life.

In stories and legends, those who receive boons generally use them to strengthen themselves and widen their domination. My boon was different: to see my Ayya Tiruvalluvar in stone, and to raise him at the southern sea of Kumari, counterpart to the Himalayas in the north, to a height of 133 feet—one foot for each of the 133 chapters of his Kural.

When I pressed the button to unveil the statue, my finger became motionless without my knowing it, and I forgot myself. From youth I had never forgotten that philosopher for even the blink of an eye; I invoked him in writing, speech and the movement of my blood. I built Valluvar Kottam for him in Chennai; declared the day after Pongal each year as Valluvar Day; placed moral couplets with his portrait in buses and travellers' rest houses; created Kuraloviyam and later wrote commentary on all 1,330 Kurals. After twenty-five years of longing, the statue had finally risen, and I had lived long enough to unveil it. Tamil Mother herself seemed to say she lacked words to describe my state of mind—what then could I do?

At the inauguration, leaders from many political parties and public life—including G.K. Moopanar, Il. Ganesan, N. Sankaraiah, Vaiko, Vazhapadi Ramamurthy, R. Nallakannu, S. Thirunavukkarasu, R.M. Veerappan, Kumari Ananthan and Sivaji Ganesan—joined Tamil scholars and overseas guests including Malaysian minister Datuk Seri Samy Vellu, Sri Lankan minister Arumugam Thondaman, Mauritius minister Ramasamy Chidambaram Pillai, Singapore's Vai. Thirunavukkarasu, Scotland's R.E. Asher, Germany's Dr Thomas Malten, Professor Arogyasamy, Perur Santhalinga Adigalar, Kundrakudi Ponnambala Adigalar, Radha Thiagarajan, Ezra Sargunam and editor Saavi in praising the occasion.

Perhaps this Government cannot bear that I received such praise. That is why, I say, it has allowed Ayya Valluvar's statue to reach this condition without maintenance. Poets, scholars and Tamils of Kanyakumari have already held protests and fasts once or twice, yet the Government has ignored them and allowed time to pass.

Yesterday I spoke about this to all the DMK MPs who met me and asked them to raise it in Parliament. If the AIADMK Government does not allocate the necessary funds and quickly undertake maintenance, we are considering whether the DMK itself should undertake the work or organise a major agitation in Kanyakumari district to draw the rulers' attention. Poet Vairamuthu and district secretary Suresh Rajan, who met me yesterday, even suggested a major seminar in Kanyakumari.

We shall wait a few days to see whether the Tamil Nadu Government itself comes forward and announces action, without forcing us to these steps. But if, as usual, the AIADMK Government remains silent, we shall decide and announce what further action must be taken. Let us rally to protect Ayya Valluvar's statue! Let us prepare for a truthful struggle to protect the Valluvar statue!

With affection,
M.K.
03-01-2013
