# 3620. When Will the Thirst of Eelam Tamils Be Quenched!

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3620](../../../chapters/3620-endru-thaniyum-eezhaththamizhar-thaagam.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 221](../../../pages/page-221.md)–[PDF 232](../../../pages/page-232.md)  
**Date:** 28 April 2012

**Udanpirappē,**

When the war against Eelam Tamils was taking place in Sri Lanka, could we not have shown then itself the same heartfelt concern that we show for Eelam Tamils today? Could we not have stopped that final war in Sri Lanka, in which thousands upon thousands of Eelam Tamils were killed? I can understand that such a thought, such anguish, exists not only among some Tamils here, but also among some of the Tamils scattered across the world!

But they themselves know! Leaving aside the fact that one reason the liberation fighters fell without reaching the summit of victory in this war was that they did not stand and fight as one force, the fighters who were in different formations also clashed among themselves and became the very cause of Tamil lives being taken by Tamils. Though we pleaded with each formation, almost to the point of falling at their feet, clasping their hands and begging, saying that there must be no fratricidal war, we could neither end the internal enmity among those formations nor prevent the collapse of the liberation struggle that followed from it.

The leaders of the formations destroyed one another and pushed back the very period in which a people ought to have attained victory. If they are true Tamils, who can think of that tragic event without shedding tears!

Amid these waves of sorrow, it is my duty to recall what efforts we undertook in 2008–2009, when the final war was taking place in Sri Lanka and the Eelam Tamil people were shedding blood and life, to prevent those dreadful conditions from arising there and to bring about a ceasefire, and also the ways in which the Union Government—the Government of India—extended as much cooperation as it could.

On behalf of the DMK Government, an all-party leaders' meeting was convened on 14 October 2008 on the Sri Lankan Tamil issue, and a resolution itself was adopted stating that if the Government of India did not come forward to secure a ceasefire in Sri Lanka within two weeks, all Members of Parliament from Tamil Nadu would have to resign. Following that, all Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha members belonging to the DMK brought their resignation letters and handed them to me. Thambis L. Ganesan and Gingee Ramachandran, who were then MDMK Members of Parliament, and Professor Kader Mohideen of the Indian Union Muslim League also handed their resignation letters to me. On the very day the resolution was passed, it was sent to the Prime Minister through Thambi T.R. Baalu. After examining it, the Prime Minister assured Baalu that the Union Government would quickly take every step to find a political solution, and this appeared in all the newspapers at the time. In an interview that same day, the Prime Minister said:

> “The ethnic problem that has arisen in Sri Lanka is a matter of great concern. A solution through military means would not be proper. A political solution must be found through talks in a manner that respects Sri Lanka's sovereignty and the human rights of the Tamils living there. The Union Government will make every effort, with full involvement, to find a solution to Sri Lanka's ethnic problem.”

On 18 October 2008, Prime Minister Dr. Manmohan Singh himself spoke directly by telephone with Sri Lankan President Rajapaksa. On 22 October 2008, Union Minister Pranab Mukherjee stated in Parliament that the Sri Lankan Government had been pressed to provide proper protection to Sri Lankan Tamils. On 24 October 2008, we conducted a massive “human chain” in Chennai. On 26 October 2008, Mr. Pranab Mukherjee came to Chennai and spoke with me for more than an hour about the Sri Lankan Tamil question. Speaking to journalists in Chennai, he said:

> “Kalaignar insisted that the ceasefire brought about through Norway's efforts should be ensured to continue. The Sri Lankan Government has given an assurance that humanitarian assistance will be provided, through the International Red Cross, the United Nations and the Government of India, to more than two lakh Tamils suffering in the war zones.”

On 12 November 2008, I moved a resolution in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly for Sri Lankan Tamils, and it was adopted. On 4 December 2008, taking all the party leaders in Tamil Nadu with me, I went to Delhi, met the Prime Minister and requested that Union Minister Pranab Mukherjee be sent to Sri Lanka regarding the Sri Lankan Tamil issue. Thereafter, Pranab Mukherjee himself went to Sri Lanka and held talks with Rajapaksa.

At the DMK General Council meeting held on 27 December 2008, we passed a resolution on the Sri Lankan Tamil issue.

On 12 January 2009, after Tamil leader K. Veeramani, PMK leader Dr. Ramadoss and Viduthalai Chiruthaigal leader Thol. Thirumavalavan met me, I had then Union Minister T.R. Baalu and Rajya Sabha member Kanimozhi explain the situation to the Prime Minister and to Sonia Gandhi. But in an interview published in Jayalalithaa's name in the newspapers dated 18 January 2009, she had said:

> “Sri Lanka is another country, and therefore there is a limit to intervention in that country's problem. A country called Eelam has not yet been formed in Sri Lanka. The Sinhala army does not intend to kill Sri Lankan Tamils. When a war takes place, innocent people being killed is natural. No country is an exception to this.”

On 28 March 2009, I wrote to the Prime Minister and Mrs. Sonia Gandhi requesting India's help to bring about an immediate ceasefire in Sri Lanka. In the long reply Mrs. Sonia Gandhi sent me on 31 March 2009, she wrote:

> “I too wish to assure you, on behalf of everyone in the Congress Party, regarding the various measures taken by the United Progressive Alliance Government in relation to the recent developments in Sri Lanka, which our Prime Minister has described in the letter he wrote to you......... The war now taking place in Sri Lanka, and the great sufferings being endured because of it by innocent Tamils there, including mothers and children, have caused us great concern. We expect the Sri Lankan authorities to undertake all necessary efforts to protect them and safeguard their welfare. In fact, as the first step towards that, a ceasefire must be brought about. Our Government is in contact with the Sri Lankan Government on various matters. In particular, contact is being maintained regarding the ceasefire. Only if there is a ceasefire can innocent people move to safe areas.”

Then, when journalists met me on 5 April 2009 and asked, “Reports say that the army has captured all the positions of the Liberation Tigers in Sri Lanka, that their military bases have been destroyed, and that Prabhakaran and his son are hiding in an area where Tamils live,” I said:

> “For the past six months we have been continuously saying that there must be a ceasefire. Respecting our voice, governments abroad, the United Nations and foreign diplomats have been telling Sri Lanka this. The Government of India has been asking them to stop the war. Even so, Rajapaksa has not stopped the war. We strongly condemn that.”

In the telegram I sent on 7 April 2009 to Prime Minister Manmohan Singh, Mrs. Sonia Gandhi, External Affairs Minister Pranab Mukherjee and Home Minister P. Chidambaram, I requested that “the entire Tamil people of Sri Lanka be saved from being annihilated wholesale, that a ceasefire there be ensured, and that immediate steps be taken towards peace talks.”

To save Sri Lankan Tamils in the final war, it was not I alone, nor the Government of India alone. In statements issued by major countries such as the United States, Britain, Japan and Norway, they too said that Sri Lankan Tamils must be saved and that Sri Lankan warplanes must stop bombing the safe zone. Beyond even that, United Nations Secretary-General Ban Ki-moon himself contacted Sri Lankan President Rajapaksa by telephone and asked that Sri Lankan Tamils be protected and that humanitarian action be taken. A senior United Nations official too spoke with Rajapaksa at that time.

On 9 April 2009, under my leadership in Chennai, a massive procession was held raising the slogan, “Sri Lankan Government, stop the war!” Sri Lankan President Rajapaksa announced a two-day ceasefire in Sri Lanka. The Government of India invited MPs of parties supporting the Liberation Tigers to come to India for talks. In an interview given on 12 April 2009, Chandrahason, son of Eelam Father Chelva, said, “It was Chief Minister Kalaignar's statesmanlike request that became the reason for the Sri Lankan ceasefire.” In a statement issued on 14 April 2009, Union Home Minister and friend P. Chidambaram said India would continue pressing the Sri Lankan Government for the ceasefire in Sri Lanka to continue. Tamil leader Ilaval K. Veeramani stated:

> “Because of pressure from the countries of the world, the Rajapaksa Government has announced a 48-hour ceasefire. The unceasing pressure applied by Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Kalaignar is an important reason for this. The Government of India too has not merely been watching.”

In the telegram I sent on 21 April 2009 to Prime Minister Dr. Manmohan Singh, United Progressive Alliance Chairperson Mrs. Sonia Gandhi and then External Affairs Minister Pranab Mukherjee, I requested:

> “The war situation in Sri Lanka is dangerous. The hardships being experienced by the Tamils have reached an extremely perilous stage. All countries, including the United Nations, have exerted pressure on the Sri Lankan Government to announce an immediate ceasefire. I urge that a final warning be issued to the Sri Lankan Government to bring about an immediate and permanent ceasefire in Sri Lanka in order to save the lives of lakhs of Tamils there.”

On 21 April 2009, I also wrote an emotional Udanpirappu letter under the title “A Plea to the Union Government to Protect the Helpless Eelam Tamils,” and issued a statement calling for a one-day strike on 23 April 2009, asking the Union Government to take immediate steps for a ceasefire to save Sri Lankan Tamils. Requesting cooperation from all parties for that strike, I wrote to the leaders of the Congress, AIADMK, CPM, CPI, Dravidar Kazhagam, BJP, MDMK, DMDK, Viduthalai Chiruthaigal, Indian Union Muslim League, MGR Kazhagam, PMK, Puthiya Tamilagam and other parties, as well as to the newspapers. That strike was held peacefully and successfully.

On the basis of the telegram I had sent, on the night of 22 April 2009 the Prime Minister convened an emergency meeting in Delhi of Union Ministers and officials, and they called upon Sri Lanka to immediately stop the war on innocent Tamils. To press this directly upon the Sri Lankan Government, a high-level group of the Prime Minister's special envoys went to Colombo. On the same day, relief materials weighing 936 metric tonnes, worth ₹6 crore 46 lakh 16 thousand, packed in 40,000 parcels, were sent to the Indian High Commission in Sri Lanka for the Tamils affected there. The high-level team sent from India met Rajapaksa directly in Colombo and conveyed India's request that steps be taken immediately to stop the war. On the night of 24 April 2009, then Home Minister P. Chidambaram came to Anna Arivalayam, met me and explained all the details. On the same day, the Prime Minister too spoke to me by telephone and told me of all the steps taken by the Union Government.

In a statement issued from the White House on behalf of newly elected U.S. President Obama on 25 April 2009, it was stated that the Sri Lankan Government should immediately stop military attacks and that the actions being taken by the Government of India in the Sri Lankan matter were supported.

When journalists asked me on 26 April 2009 about a ceasefire in Sri Lanka, I said, “Let us wait one night and see.” In a statement issued that same day, 26 April 2009, on behalf of the Liberation Tigers, they said:

> “Accepting the request of India, the United States, the European countries and the United Nations, we are declaring a ceasefire. This indefinite ceasefire comes into force immediately. The suffering of the Tamil people because of the war being waged by the Sri Lankan army has reached its peak. The Sri Lankan Government too must announce a ceasefire.”

But, in a situation no one expected, the Sri Lankan Government mocked that announcement as a “joke.” After receiving these reports on the 26th, I did not sleep the whole night. I kept contacting Delhi and asking whether the Sri Lankan Government had announced anything about a ceasefire. I contacted Home Minister P. Chidambaram many times. The Prime Minister too contacted me. Large numbers of Tamils from abroad kept speaking to me by telephone. Until four in the morning I watched television hoping that good news would come. No news came. The only news was that the Sri Lankan Government had not stopped the war.

Only after that, at about five o'clock, I told my family that I was going to Arivalayam and went to Anna's memorial. In January of that same year, at this age, I had undergone a major operation on my back and was travelling in a wheelchair, unable to walk. Without worrying about my health or anything else, I went on my own decision, telling no one—because if I had told them, they would not have permitted me—with the resolve to fast until death.

That morning at the fasting venue, I told the journalists who met me:

> “Since the Sinhala Government has not come forward to stop the war, I have undertaken this fast as a symbolic act on behalf of all Tamils—to dedicate myself, to give my very life for our Tamils, and to become one more sacrifice among the sacrifices taken by the Sri Lankan Government.”

On the very day I began the fast, at about 11 a.m., the Sri Lankan Defence Council met for consultations. It was reported that various matters were discussed at the meeting, including the war being conducted in Mullaitivu and, in connection with it, my being on hunger strike. At the end of that meeting, a decision was taken to bring the war being conducted in northern Sri Lanka to an end, and at about 12 noon the Sri Lankan Government issued a statement. It said:

> “The war that had been taking place in northern Sri Lanka has ended. The army has been ordered not to use heavy weapons in the northern region hereafter. Bombing from aircraft is also being stopped. Sri Lankan soldiers will engage in operations to safely rescue the civilians trapped in the north. Priority will henceforth be given to protecting innocent people.”

On the basis of the statement issued by the Sri Lankan Government, then Union External Affairs Minister Mr. Pranab Mukherjee stated on 27 April 2009:

> “The Sri Lankan Government has announced that combat operations have concluded and that orders have been issued to the Sri Lankan security forces not to use heavy guns and weapons of war because they would kill civilians in large numbers. The Sri Lankan security forces will henceforth concentrate on making every effort necessary to rescue and protect civilians.”

A copy of that statement was sent to the very place where I was fasting, and the Prime Minister and Sonia Gandhi Ammaiyar asked me to end the fast.

Dravidar Kazhagam leader K. Veeramani; G.K. Vasan, E.V.K.S. Elangovan, literary figure Kumari Ananthan, C. Gnanadesikan MP, D. Sudarsanam MLA, D. Yasodha MLA, Gopanna, S.M. Hidayathullah, Royapuram Mano and Karate Thiagarajan of the Congress; Viduthalai Chiruthaigal leader Thol. Thirumavalavan; Puratchi Bharatham leader Poovai Jagan Moorthy MLA; MGR Kazhagam leader R.M. Veerappan; Minorities Commission Chairman Vincent Chinnadurai; Agricultural Labourers Party leader Ponkumar; former MP P.D. Elangovan; Indian Union Muslim League General Secretary Syed Sardar, M. Zainul Abideen, Kamuthi Basheer, Tiruppur Sattar and Quaid-e-Millath's grandson Dawood Miankhan; Bishop Ezra Sargunam; Secular Janata Dal leader G.A. Vadivelu; Liaquat Ali Khan; former minister Aranganayagam; Thiruvavaduthurai Aadheenam Muthu Kumaraswami Swamigal; Kaviperarasu Vairamuthu; Kundrakudi Ponnambala Adigalar; Kavikko Abdul Rahman; poet Mu. Metha; Nakkheeran Gopal; writer Kandasamy; railway trade-union leader M. Kannaiyan; Sun Television director Kalanithi Maran; Nalli Kuppusamy Chettiar; Gandhi Kannadasan; our Dravida Kazhagam leader Mrs. Oviyam; former television director Natarajan; C.K. Perumal; Anna's son C.N.A. Ilangovan; Minorities Commission member Inigo Irudayaraj; epic poet Vaali; director-sikaram K. Balachander; Chandrahason, son of Eelam Father Chelva; Maravanpulavu Sri Lankan scholar K. Sachithananthan; Secretariat Association leader Ganesan; small-screen artists' federation leader Viduthalai; Ilavenil; advocate Gandhi; Suba. Veerapandian; actors S.S. Rajendran, Sathyaraj, S.V. Shekher MLA and Radharavi; actress Vijayakumari; actors Selva and Prashanth; and people belonging to all parties came in person and pressed me to give up the fast. After that, at about one in the afternoon, I ended the fast.

But even after that, because of the Sinhala chauvinist obstinacy of Rajapaksa's Sinhala Government in Sri Lanka, the fighters who stood in the field were killed in heaps. We undertook so many efforts to stop the fighting, and although the Union Government, the Prime Minister and the Union Ministers stood in support, the Sinhala rulers repeatedly violated all the assurances they had given, continued the war and, taking advantage of the collapse caused by fratricidal conflict, wore the garland of victory. What fell was the uprising of the Tamil people in Eelam Tamil land!

In world history there are incomparable examples of the rise of a people, its fall and decline, and then its climbing to the summit of victory. Even if the Tamil people have continued to suffer falls because of lack of unity and because of destroying one another, what has now happened to that people in Tamil Eelam is temporary; it is not permanent; and it must not be allowed to become permanent.

Holding that unshakable faith in our minds, paying our final respects to all the great warriors who disappeared in the field, and with the resolve that we shall not hesitate to give our body, wealth and life itself so that the Tamil people may hereafter live with their heads held high—

on the path shown by Annal Gandhi;

in the way laid down by Arignar Anna;

on the path taught by Thanthai Periyar, who awakened the feeling of self-respect;

in the spirit instilled by Eelam Father Chelva;

let us array ourselves to continue the righteous struggle!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**28 April 2012**
