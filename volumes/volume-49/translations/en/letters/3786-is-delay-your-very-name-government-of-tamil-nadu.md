# 3786. Is Delay Your Very Name, Government of Tamil Nadu?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 31 July 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

As examples of just how swiftly the AIADMK Government acts—(?)—I wish to point to two actions taken by the Chief Minister and reported on the 28th and 29th of this month.

On 28 July 2013, Chief Minister Jayalalithaa wrote to Prime Minister Manmohan Singh. She said that sixteen fishermen from Ramanathapuram, Kanyakumari and Nagapattinam districts were languishing in an Iranian prison, and that she had learnt of the matter only after their families submitted a petition on the 24th asking for her intervention. The fishermen had allegedly entered Iranian waters while fishing, had been arrested by that country’s authorities in December of the previous year and imprisoned, and had then been tried without access to legal assistance. An Iranian court sentenced them to six months’ imprisonment and imposed a fine of 5,750 US dollars. Though their prison terms had ended, the poor fishermen remained in jail because they could not pay the fine. They had not even been able to contact the Indian Embassy there. Jayalalithaa asked the Prime Minister to intervene immediately, direct the Indian Embassy in Saudi Arabia to arrange payment of the fine, and take steps to secure their release. Whether or not the letter had yet reached the Prime Minister’s Office, it appeared in large type in the Tamil Nadu newspapers.

I welcome and appreciate the concern shown by the State Government in taking this step. I urge the Union Government and the Prime Minister to act immediately to rescue the sixteen Tamil Nadu fishermen imprisoned in Iran. Even if the Union Government does not come forward to pay the fine, the Tamil Nadu Government should not wait. The fishing community expects the State itself to pay the amount and bring all sixteen men home without delay, and I too press that demand.

The Chief Minister says that she learnt of the problem only after the fishermen’s families submitted their petition on the 24th. Is there, or is there not, a Minister for Fisheries? Are there, or are there not, departmental officials? Why did they fail for six months to bring the matter to the Chief Minister’s attention? Even the 27 July issue of *Dinamalar* carried the headline, “Six Tamil Nadu Fishermen Suffering in Iranian Prison—Families Petition Minister Seeking Rescue.” That report said the families had already petitioned the District Collectors of Kanyakumari and Ramanathapuram, the fisheries officials there, and the Union and State Governments; it was only because no action followed that they met the Minister the previous day.

Even if the State officials did nothing, the DMK Member of Parliament for the constituency, Mrs Helen Davidson, acted as soon as she learnt of the fishermen’s plight. On 28 July 2013 she wrote to Union Ministers Vayalar Ravi and Salman Khurshid and to the Indian Ambassador in Iran, reminding them that she had already raised the matter with the Union Ministers on 13 March 2013. She also contacted the Ministers by telephone.

In her letter, our Member of Parliament wrote in English that she was representing the Indian fishermen from her constituency who had been in prison since 14 December 2012; that she had already informed the authorities by email on 13 March 2013 and had received an acknowledgement from the Embassy on 23 May; that the prisoners had been unable to contact their families for four months and that she had received no report of progress; that, as they were the sole earners in their families, their relatives were deeply worried; and that immediate action should be taken for their repatriation. These events show both how actively the DMK works in such cases and how late the AIADMK Government’s actions come.

In the petition submitted to the Minister, the fishermen’s families asked the Tamil Nadu Government to bear the cost of obtaining their release and returning them home because the prisoners had no income. If the State Government voluntarily bears these expenses and helps them return quickly, the Minister concerned and others will then have an opportunity to place page after page of newspaper advertisements proclaiming, “Thanks, thanks, thanks to Amma, who brought light into the fishermen’s lives.”

Speaking of AIADMK advertisements, one point must be made. Whenever the Chief Minister is in Chennai, rows of boards praising her are erected near her residence, leaving hardly any space even to walk. Yet she has been away from Chennai for the past month, and although she has issued one announcement after another every day, not even one such board has appeared on that road. Does this not show the depth of the heartfelt affection with which all those boards are normally erected?

At this point I recall a similar matter at the beginning of this year. On 9 January 2013 I wrote to the Prime Minister about twenty-nine fishermen from Kanyakumari in Tamil Nadu and from Kerala who had been suffering for more than fifty days in a prison on Iran’s Kish Island. I said that they were reported to be enduring severe hardship; that Member of Parliament J. Helen Davidson had described their painful experience to me; that food, drinking water and medical facilities had been denied to them; that the Iranian prison authorities were treating them inhumanely; and that their dependent families were suffering indescribable distress in their daily lives. Although the Union Government had acted, its efforts to release the prisoners did not appear to have succeeded. I therefore requested that all concerned be instructed to make unremitting efforts to secure the release of the twenty-nine fishermen and bring them back to India as quickly as possible.

Because of the action then taken by the Prime Minister’s Office on the basis of my letter, and because Mrs Helen Davidson personally pressed the matter there, all twenty-nine fishermen were promptly released. When they arrived at Thiruvananthapuram by air, she went in person to receive them. Later, on their behalf, she met me at Anna Arivalayam and thanked me; a photograph of that meeting was published at the time in *Murasoli*. Following that precedent, I once again urge the Union and State Governments to act swiftly to release the present group of sixteen fishermen.

The Chief Minister wrote another letter to the Prime Minister on 29 July 2013. She said that, accepting Tamil Nadu’s request, the Supreme Court had on the 18th struck down the entrance examination for medical studies, but Union Health Minister Ghulam Nabi Azad had said that the Union Government would approach the Court again seeking review of that judgment. She called this a wrong decision and asked the Prime Minister to abandon any move to seek review of the Supreme Court’s ruling on the common entrance examination.

That letter too is welcome. But the Supreme Court delivered its judgment against the entrance examination on the 18th. Two days later, on the 21st, Union Health Minister Ghulam Nabi Azad told journalists that filing a review petition would be appropriate. As soon as I read that report, I wrote in my *Udanpirappu* letter on the 22nd, setting out in detail the efforts made over many years concerning entrance examinations. I reproduced the reply that Mr Azad himself had sent on 27 August 2010, while the DMK was in office, supporting the position I had expressed to him. I concluded that the Supreme Court judgment was welcome, that the Union Minister’s announcement of a review was unacceptable, and that the DMK would oppose an entrance examination in whatever form it came. My letter of the 22nd appeared in the newspapers on the 23rd.

It was precisely that position—that the Supreme Court judgment should not be challenged—that the AIADMK Government conveyed to the Prime Minister by letter only on the 29th. I offer this as an example of the speed with which the Tamil Nadu Government acts on matters of urgent importance.

There is also a small doubt. If the State Government is so firmly opposed to entrance examinations, why did it attempt to conduct one this year at Annamalai University, even after taking over the university the previous year, appointing an IAS officer as Special Officer and assuming control of its administration?

At any rate, although its letters came late, the State Government has expressed the correct position both on the Supreme Court judgment and on releasing the fishermen suffering in Iran. I therefore welcome these two decisions.

**Delay—are you the very name of the Government of Tamil Nadu?**

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**31 July 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. The English passage from Helen Davidson’s letter is retained exactly in the Tamil source section; the translation body conveys its meaning without silently editing the quoted original.
2. The source uses both the ideas of a **review petition** and an **appeal** while describing opposition to reopening the Supreme Court judgment; the translation follows each passage’s immediate meaning.
3. `Murasoli` is retained as the newspaper’s proper name.
