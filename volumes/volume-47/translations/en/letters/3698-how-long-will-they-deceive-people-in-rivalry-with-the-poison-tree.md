# 3698. How Long Will She Deceive the People, Competing Even with the Poison Tree?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This thought-preserving translation retains the source's criticism of price rises, power shortage, Cauvery politics, allegations against the Union Government, figures, rhetorical questions and idioms. Tamil remains authoritative. `Udanpirappē` is retained as Kalaignar's distinctive address.

## English translation

Udanpirappē,

Most parties in India have condemned the Union Government for raising diesel prices and reducing the number of subsidised LPG cylinders. The DMK too, though part of the Union Government, has strongly condemned the move. Whenever Governments impose such price increases, opposition parties condemn them; and when those same opposition parties come to power, they often forget—or conceal—the condemnations they voiced earlier and impose similar increases themselves. The country has come to watch such practices with astonishment.

When the Union Government follows policies or raises prices against public wishes, opposition parties discharge their democratic duty by condemning them and holding demonstrations. It is less usual for those who themselves run a State Government to stage such protests. One may recall that even when Perarignar Anna was Chief Minister, an “Uprising Day” was observed for major projects needed by Tamil Nadu such as the Salem Steel Plant, the Sethusamudram project and the second mine at Neyveli. But the present AIADMK Government has made it a habit to blame the Union Government for everything, behaving as though it alone is the direct opponent of the Centre. We know the motive behind this.

At the National Development Council meeting, more than thirty Chief Ministers had to speak. Was it some strange new practice to ask each to finish within ten minutes, and to follow that rule? Though the Tamil Nadu Chief Minister was due to speak later according to the order, was she not allowed to speak first when she requested it? If she had asked for another five or ten minutes because she had important matters to raise, she might have obtained that too. Instead, without saying anything in the meeting, she came out, told reporters she had staged a walkout, and created an all-India “stunt” as though she had launched some new revolution. We know why.

It is in the same series of political stunts that Jayalalithaa has now announced an AIADMK demonstration on the 24th condemning the Union Government. What is the protest for? She says it is against the Union Government—of which the DMK is a part—for failing to control prices that are “rising like poison,” and for increases in petrol, diesel, cooking gas and railway fares.

But what is the record of the AIADMK Government that announces this protest? Did it not impose about ₹4,000 crore in new taxes immediately after coming to power? Did it not raise bus fares beyond what poor and middle-class people could bear? Did it not raise the price of milk? Did it not sharply raise electricity tariffs even when electricity itself was unavailable? After imposing all these burdens on the public, is it not amusing and strange for the AIADMK Government in Tamil Nadu to hold a demonstration against tariff increases by the Union Government? Does it not look like a double role and a deceptive drama? Why this sudden protest? Is it with the 2014 Lok Sabha election in mind? Do they imagine the people know nothing?

Jayalalithaa's statement contains many more allegations against the Union Government. One is that the Centre refuses to give Tamil Nadu power surrendered by the Delhi Government as surplus. The Union Government has already answered clearly: even if it was ready to supply that power, the transmission “corridor”—the infrastructure needed to bring it to Tamil Nadu—was insufficient, and this was stated even in court. Yet Jayalalithaa repeats the same allegation. To say something false, receive a factual explanation, and then repeat the same falsehood to divert and confuse—is that Jayalalithaa's “style”? No, it is Goebbels's style!

In paragraph 70 of the AIADMK Government's 2012–13 Budget, on 26 March 2012, it was stated that because of congestion in transmission facilities for bringing power from other States, there was no way to import electricity from outside. Yet at the end of 2012 they entered into an agreement to purchase electricity from Gujarat and simultaneously demanded that Delhi's surplus power be given to Tamil Nadu. Would educated people not ask whether these very people prepared the Budget they read in March—or at least listened when it was read? Why such contradictions between what the Budget said and what they later did?

Finance Minister O. Panneerselvam, while reading the 2011–12 Budget, stated in paragraph 67 that power cuts would gradually be reduced and completely eliminated across the State by August 2012. August 2012 has passed and 2013 has arrived. Have power cuts disappeared across Tamil Nadu? Who will demonstrate against the AIADMK Government for failing to fulfil the promise it made in the Assembly and protect the people?

Likewise, while replying to the debate on the Governor's Address in 2012, did not Chief Minister Jayalalithaa say the existing three-hour power cut would be reduced to two hours and that the electricity shortage would be completely solved from July? What is the situation now? Who will demonstrate because Jayalalithaa failed to keep her word?

There is also the allegation that the Union Government did not grant a digital licence to the Tamil Nadu Arasu Cable TV Corporation. The concerned Union minister has already given a detailed reply, yet Jayalalithaa repeated that charge too in yesterday's statement.

Next she blamed the Union Government over Cauvery water. The DMK's demand is that the final award of the Cauvery Water Disputes Tribunal be published in the Central Gazette. I have written to the Prime Minister asking for that. I also asked DMK Members of Parliament to meet the Prime Minister personally and press the request. Yet after all this, Jayalalithaa writes that the people of Tamil Nadu believe Karunanidhi, for selfish reasons, asked the Union Government not to publish the final award. No one in Tamil Nadu believes that. Jayalalithaa alone deliberately imagines it and makes the accusation against the DMK.

In Karnataka, the Government repeatedly convenes all parties on Cauvery, speaks with one voice and approaches the Union Government. Even yesterday the Karnataka Cabinet met and decided that the final Tribunal award should not be published. What is the situation in Tamil Nadu?

Since the AIADMK came to power, despite repeated requests by many parties, has it ever convened an all-party meeting? Even if it did not want all party leaders, should it not at least have convened its allied parties, taken a collective decision and shown that Tamil Nadu stood together on the issue? Why was no such effort made? Is it the arrogance that no one else knows anything beyond what she knows? Not only is no effort made, but baseless allegations are hurled at the opposition.

What will people in Karnataka and in the Union Government think of this? Jayalalithaa, as Chief Minister, says the DMK asked the Union Government not to publish the final award. Is she prepared to prove that accusation?

Another allegation is that the Union Government has not supplied Tamil Nadu the kerosene it needs. The child that cries is the one that gets milk. When the DMK Government was in office, Food Minister E.V. Velu would obtain my permission and repeatedly go to Delhi. He would meet the Union minister then in charge, Murli Deora, present Tamil Nadu's demands and request additional kerosene and more LPG cylinders. He would meet the Union secretary from our State, Mr Srinivasan, and explain the kerosene shortage. Because we kept asking, they would help as far as possible. But how many times has the minister holding that portfolio in Jayalalithaa's present Cabinet gone to meet the Union minister and demand kerosene? Can Jayalalithaa answer?

It is neither good nor becoming of a Chief Minister to find fault with everyone over everything and pile allegation upon allegation. Villages face eighteen hours without electricity. Farmers are burning in anguish. Even yesterday, in Kottur Union of Tiruvarur district, a farmer named Ramesh died after consuming poison. Farmer suicides are becoming a tragic serial. In Coimbatore and Tiruppur districts, factories are closed, workers have lost livelihoods and are in tears. Weavers ask whether at least gruel centres will be opened. Instead of finding ways to relieve their suffering and help the people of the State live, the Tamil Nadu AIADMK Government says it will hold a protest condemning the Union Government!

If the AIADMK wants to hold a political demonstration on the 24th against the Union Government, let it do so. Why needlessly drag in the DMK, as though “whatever the issue, it is the DMK; in my dreams and waking life, only the DMK”? In yesterday's statement Jayalalithaa says she strongly condemns DMK leader Karunanidhi, who, by supporting the Union Government, continues to betray Tamil Nadu. Not an ordinary condemnation—a strong condemnation.

As an ally of the Congress, the DMK supported the Union Government without betraying the alliance. But whenever issues concerning Tamil Nadu arose, did the DMK ever abandon them? Did it ever fail to raise its voice? Yet Jayalalithaa, and some parties that indirectly support her because they hope to ally with her in the parliamentary election, have an inner desire: somehow the DMK must leave the Congress alliance, confusion must arise at the Centre, and they must fish in troubled waters. The DMK understands that well. The people of the country know it too.

How long will she deceive the people, competing even with the poison tree?

With affection,
M.K.
22-1-2013

வேதனையில் வெந்து தவித்துக் கொண்டிருக்கிறார்கள். திருவாரூர்
மாவட்டத்தில், கோட்டூர் ஒன்றியத்தில் நேற்றையதினம்கூட
ரமேஷ் என்ற விவசாயி விஷமருந்தி மடிந்திருக்கிறார். விவசாயிகள்
தற்கொலை என்பது சோகத் தொடராகி வருகிறது. கோவை,
திருப்பூர் மாவட்டங்களில் தொழிற்சாலைகள் எல்லாம் மூடப்பட்டு,
தொழிலாளர்கள் வேலை இல்லாமல் வாழ்வாதாரத்தை இழந்து
கண்ணீர் விட்டுக் கொண்டிருக்கிறார்கள். நெசவாளர்களோ
கஞ்சித் தொட்டிகளாவது திறக்கப்படுமா என்கிறார்கள். இவர்களின்
வேதனைகளைத் தீர்க்க வழிகண்டு மாநில மக்களை வாழ
வைத்திட வேண்டிய தமிழக அ.தி.மு.க. அரசு, மத்திய அரசைக்
கண்டித்துக் கண்டன ஆர்ப்பாட்டம் நடத்தப் போகிறார்களாம்!

24ஆம் தேதி அ.தி.மு.க. மத்திய அரசைக் கண்டித்து
கண்டன ஆர்ப்பாட்டம் அரசியல் ரீதியாக நடத்துகிறது என்றால்
நடத்தட்டும்; அதிலே தேவையில்லாமல், “எதற்கெடுத்தாலும்
தி.மு.க., என் கனவிலும் நனவிலும் தி.மு.க. தான்” என்பதைப்
போல எதற்காகத் தி.மு.க. மீது குறை கூற வேண்டும்? ஜெயலலிதா
நேற்றைய அறிக்கையில், மத்திய அரசுக்கு உறுதுணையாக
இருந்துகொண்டு, தமிழகத்திற்குத் தொடர்ந்து துரோகம்
இழைத்துவரும் தி.மு.க. தலைவர் கருணாநிதிக்கு எனது
கடும் கண்டனத்தைத் தெரிவித்துக் கொள்கிறேன் என்கிறார்.
சாதாரணக் கண்டனம் கூட அல்ல; கடும் கண்டனத்தைத்
தெரிவித்திருக்கிறார். காங்கிரஸ் கட்சியின் தோழமைக் கட்சி என்ற
முறையில், கூட்டணிக்குத் துரோகம் செய்யாமல், மத்திய அரசுக்கு
உறுதுணையாக தி.மு.கழகம் இருந்த போதிலும், தமிழகத்திற்கான
பிரச்சினைகள் என்று வரும்போது, தி.மு. கழகம் அவற்றைக்
கைவிட்டது உண்டா? அதற்காக ஓங்கிக் குரல் கொடுக்காமல்
இருந்தது உண்டா? ஆனால் ஜெயலலிதாவுக்கும், அவரோடு
நாடாளுமன்றத் தேர்தலில் கூட்டணி அமைக்க வேண்டும் எனும்
நோக்கில் அவரை மறைமுகமாக ஆதரிக்கும் சில கட்சிகளுக்கும்
தி.மு.க., காங்கிரஸ் கூட்டணியிலிருந்து எப்படியாவது விலகிவிட
வேண்டும், மத்திய அரசில் குழப்பம் ஏற்பட வேண்டும்,
அதை வைத்துக் குழம்பிய குட்டையில் மீன் பிடிக்க முயல
வேண்டும் என்பதுதான் உள்ளார்ந்த எண்ணம். அதைத் தி.மு.க.
நன்றாகவே உணர்ந்துதான் உள்ளது. நாட்டு மக்களும் அறிந்தே
வைத்திருக்கிறார்கள். எட்டிக்குப் போட்டியென எத்தனை காலம்
