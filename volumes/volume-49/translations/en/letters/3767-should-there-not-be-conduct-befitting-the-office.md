# 3767. Should There Not Be Conduct Befitting the Office?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

After Jayalalithaa assumed office as Chief Minister, drought has run rampant in Tamil Nadu. When I pointed this out and asked whether water would be released from the Mettur Dam on 12 June this year, as it ordinarily is every year, Jayalalithaa replied that in June 2011 the Mettur Dam had been opened as early as the 6th for kuruvai cultivation.

The Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam governed Tamil Nadu until 13 May 2011. That year, because the DMK Government had held cordial discussions with Karnataka and maintained good relations, the Mettur Dam was full. Jayalalithaa, who opened it within twenty days of coming to office, now speaks boastfully as though it had been made possible by the efforts of her government. Neutral observers should consider what else this can mean except that Jayalalithaa is now trying to claim as her own an achievement made possible by the precautionary measures taken by the DMK Government in the interests of farmers.

The years that truly fell under Jayalalithaa's administration were 2012 and 2013. Should she not have said when the Mettur Dam was opened in those two years? What was the record of the AIADMK Government during those two years?

From the time Madam came to power, the Karnataka Government—whichever government has held office there—has not supplied water to the Mettur Dam. Even nature, apparently afraid of her, has gone into hiding and refuses to send rain. Yet she seems to believe that she can spend her time blaming the Union Government for everything, or blaming the Opposition parties here, particularly the DMK.

The statement I issued—“The AIADMK Government must at least now wake up and clearly state whether there will or will not be kuruvai cultivation this year”—is, according to the Chief Minister, a farcical, laughable and unclear statement. Yes, her office makes her speak in that manner.

What is farcical in the statement I issued to remove the confusion and anxiety of the farmers of the Delta districts? What is unclear in my insistence that the livelihoods of farmers and agricultural labourers be protected? Until I issued my statement—that is, until 12 June, the date on which the Mettur Dam should have been opened—had the Tamil Nadu Government issued any announcement about kuruvai cultivation? Was it not only after I issued the statement that an urgent consultation meeting was held, followed by the announcement of concessions? Was my statement not the underlying reason for all this? What is there to laugh at? Does being Chief Minister permit one to issue statements in any manner one pleases?

Jayalalithaa has also pointed in her statement to the funds allocated by the Tamil Nadu Government for drought-relief work. Did the Government announce that drought relief immediately? How loudly did the political parties and farmers' associations in Tamil Nadu have to raise their voices for it? How many protests did farmers conduct? How many statements were issued? Was it not only after all this, and after considerable delay, that relief was announced? And how many irregularities have there been in distributing that drought relief?

If Chief Minister Jayalalithaa would stop listening only to what her ministers tell her, come down a little, and patiently lend an ear to the anguish and voices of Tamil Nadu's farmers, she could easily understand the real situation. Or she should at least read the reports—even in some of the newspapers that benefit by publishing the full-page advertisements she gives—about how drought relief is actually being distributed. Or she should watch the interviews on certain television channels in which affected farmers directly pour out their anger. Leaving all that aside, what purpose is served by sitting aloft and boasting, “I have given so many crore rupees as drought relief”?

Did not all the newspapers publish the names of seventeen farmers who died by suicide last year because cultivation had failed in Tamil Nadu? Even among those seventeen, did the Government not announce relief only for a few families and declare that the others were not farmers at all?

Have newspapers not carried complaints that relief was being paid for uncultivated fallow land while land that had actually been cultivated received none? Did the Chief Minister not see the report that confusion prevailed over who should receive the relief because the landowner and the person who cultivated the land were different people? Farmers say that in some places they are receiving only ₹3,000 an acre, far below the amount announced. Has that not reached the Government's ears? It is still being said that village administrative officers and cooperative-bank officials are deducting between ₹500 and ₹1,000 from the relief. Does the Chief Minister know this? Although the Government ordered that crop loans should not be recovered from drought-relief payments, is it true or false that some cooperative banks are recovering crop loans at the very moment the relief is paid? Let her investigate and find out.

Instead of doing all this, she skims the surface without understanding the depth of the problem, leaps upon me, and issues a statement mixing vulgarity into her words. That may appear becoming to her; it is not becoming of the office she holds.

Jayalalithaa has said: “Karunanidhi looks at my Government, which protects farmers like the eyelid protects the eye, and says, ‘At least now, wake up.’ This only recalls the proverb that one can wake a person who is asleep, but cannot wake one who pretends to be asleep.” Does she protect farmers as the eyelid protects the eye? Or does she keep the eyelid open only to throw dust into the farmers' eyes? The farmers of Tamil Nadu know this very well. She says one cannot wake a person who is “acting”. That is true: one cannot wake the one who is “acting”—or the one who has acted.

Jayalalithaa says: “Karunanidhi's reference to the all-party meeting held in Karnataka is laughable and astonishing.” What is there to laugh at? Is it false that the Chief Minister of Karnataka immediately convened a meeting of all party leaders over the Cauvery issue? Did newspapers not publish a photograph showing the former Chief Ministers participating in it? Did Communist Party of India (Marxist) member K. Balakrishnan and Dravidar Kazhagam President and Tamilar Thalaivar Veeramani not issue statements asking that an all-party meeting be convened in Tamil Nadu?

During these two and a half years, many issues of great importance have arisen. Has the Tamil Nadu Chief Minister ever convened an all-party meeting and consulted others on even one of them? Does having a “majority” government mean that she may do whatever she pleases? Is this the respect given to people's rule? Is this how democracy is honoured?

“A person who, for political gain, selfish interest and family interest, can pawn the welfare of the people of Tamil Nadu; a person capable of playing a double role—that person is DMK leader Karunanidhi.” These are the “tributes” Chief Minister Jayalalithaa offered me in her statement. Does she imagine that the people of Tamil Nadu do not know the double roles Jayalalithaa herself has played on the Sri Lankan Tamil issue, the Katchatheevu issue, the Sethusamudram Project and even the Cauvery issue?

Jayalalithaa has also announced an extraordinary discovery in her statement: that I did not oppose the Union Government over its training of Sri Lankan military personnel at Wellington only because I wanted the support of Congress members for the election of my daughter Kanimozhi to the Rajya Sabha. Such “peculiar” thoughts can occur only to Jayalalithaa.

I issued my statement about the training of Sri Lankan military personnel at Wellington on the 10th of this month. At that time, no decision had even been taken on Kanimozhi contesting the Rajya Sabha election. The decision was made and the nomination filed only on the 15th. Who other than Jayalalithaa could think in such a distorted way and claim that a statement made on the 10th had been issued to obtain the support of Congress members?

That is not all. Even in that statement, I did not write anything in support of the Union Government. I wrote with particular clarity:

> “The DMK respects the feelings expressed in Jayalalithaa's letter to the Prime Minister. The feeling of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam regarding the training of Sri Lankan military personnel in India is the same. We have no different view on that. This statement is only a reply to Jayalalithaa's allegation that the Union Government is playing a double role. It must not be understood as justifying the training of Sri Lankan military personnel in India.”

The people of Tamil Nadu must decide whether this is a view in support of the Union Government.

Jayalalithaa says that my statement—“Because of the practice of appealing to the Supreme Court over everything and trying to escape by placing the blame on the Union Government, those who are actually harmed in many ways are the farmers of Tamil Nadu”—is entirely contrary to fact. Let the farmers of Tamil Nadu themselves decide whether what I said is contrary to fact.

Jayalalithaa repeatedly claims that the final award of the Cauvery Water Disputes Tribunal was published in the Union Gazette only because the Government under her leadership pressed the matter in the Supreme Court. The truth is that the award was published in the Union Gazette because the Supreme Court judges themselves pressed the Union Government. Jayalalithaa's effort to claim the credit for herself is what is contrary to fact.

She says that, anticipating that the Tamil Nadu Government under her leadership would issue an announcement on kuruvai cultivation after the meeting of the Cauvery Supervisory Committee on 12 June 2013, I issued the question “Will there be kuruvai cultivation this year?” as a perverse, spiteful and contradictory statement. If I anticipated that the Government might issue such an announcement and issued a statement beforehand, was that not the duty of the Opposition? What perversity is there in it? What spite? What contradiction? What empty disputation?

Farmers are confused about whether water will be available for kuruvai cultivation even this year. If I issue a statement with the good intention of asking whether the Government should not provide guidance that protects and reassures them, is that perversity? Is that spite?

At last, the Chief Minister has given the farmers of Tamil Nadu an assurance. Do you know what the assurance is? She has said: “I firmly state that, once rainfall is received in the Cauvery catchment area, the water due to us will be obtained.” Once it rains, the water due to us will apparently be obtained. Have you seen what kind of assurance the Chief Minister has given the farmers?

Fortunately, she stopped short of giving a full scientific explanation: clouds must first gather in the sky; if clouds gather, rain will come; and if rain comes, water will come. What has happened now to all those words of hope used to deceive the people—that the very day after the Cauvery Tribunal's final award was published in the Union Gazette, water would overflow in the Cauvery, farmers of the Delta districts would dance in joy, and three crops would flourish?

Reading the Chief Minister's statement as a whole leaves us only with sorrow. Let the Chief Minister read her own statement once again. Then it will be clear whether what I say is true or Madam's statement is true. At least now, give up resentment, set aside hostility and try to govern. Should there not be conduct befitting the office?

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**17 June 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. **Kuruvai** is the short-duration paddy crop cultivated in the Cauvery Delta, traditionally dependent on the timely release of water from the Mettur Dam.
2. The phrase about one who is “acting” also carries an evident reference to Jayalalithaa's career as an actor. The English preserves Kalaignar's repetition and wordplay without adding an explanatory phrase to the body.
3. **Three crops** translates `முப்போகம்`, the expectation of three agricultural seasons or harvests.
