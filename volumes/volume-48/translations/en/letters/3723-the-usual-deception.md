# 3723. The Deception Has Become Routine!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 28 March 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

Yesterday, 26 March 2013, while replying in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly to a calling-attention motion on the continuing attacks on Tamil Nadu fishermen by the Sri Lankan Navy, Chief Minister Jayalalithaa unnecessarily referred to me and said that Katchatheevu had been handed over to Sri Lanka in 1974 and that Karunanidhi was Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu at the time. Although I have rejected this accusation and explained the matter many times, for some reason Jayalalithaa keeps repeating the same thing she has already said.

When the Union Government decided in 1974 to give Katchatheevu to Sri Lanka, the then State government in Tamil Nadu—the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam government—strongly opposed it. In spite of that opposition, the Government of India, citing certain reasons, transferred Katchatheevu to Sri Lanka. Even when that was done, the then DMK government of Tamil Nadu insisted that clauses preserving the rights of fishermen to fish around Katchatheevu, to make pilgrimages there and to dry their nets there must be included, and those clauses were incorporated. But during the Emergency, when Tamil Nadu was under Governor's Rule, those provisions somehow disappeared from the agreement to such an extent that one cannot even understand how they were removed. Does that mean we failed to register our opposition?

In 1974 itself, while the DMK was in office, the DMK General Council adopted the following resolution:

> “The DMK General Council strongly condemns the agreement entered into by the Government of India granting Sri Lanka sovereign rights over Katchatheevu, which belongs to India and has close links with Tamil Nadu. The Tamil Nadu DMK government repeatedly approached the Union Government with evidence and sought to prevent an agreement contrary to the feelings of the people of Tamil Nadu; the Union Government ignored those efforts and gave Katchatheevu to Sri Lanka, causing great pain. This General Council urges Prime Minister Indira Gandhi not to treat the Katchatheevu issue as a closed matter, but to reconsider it and find a way to amend the agreement so that sovereignty over the island remains with the Government of India.”

We did not stop with passing a resolution. On 14 July 1974, throughout Tamil Nadu, the DMK observed a **“Katchatheevu Agreement Condemnation Day.”** On 6 January 1974, as Chief Minister, I wrote to Prime Minister Indira Gandhi setting out various pieces of evidence that the Katchatheevu agreement should not be made and that Katchatheevu was part of India.

It was while Mrs. Indira Gandhi was Prime Minister that the situation arose in which Katchatheevu was to be given to Sri Lanka. At the time she sent the External Affairs Minister and officials to discuss the matter with me. Even after we registered the Tamil Nadu government's strong opposition, the Union Government gave Katchatheevu to Sri Lanka.

When the Katchatheevu issue was discussed in Parliament on 23 July 1974, R. Sezhiyan in the Lok Sabha and S.S. Marisamy in the Rajya Sabha stated on behalf of the DMK: **“We will not accept the surrender of Katchatheevu to Sri Lanka.”**

Later, in one of her own statements, Jayalalithaa said:

> “The Katchatheevu issue is an issue with another country. The authority to retrieve it rests only with the Union Government. If a State Chief Minister had possessed that authority, Katchatheevu would have been retrieved long ago.”

Thus Jayalalithaa herself, forgetting her present position, acknowledged that authority over Katchatheevu rests with the Union Government. Yet she repeatedly says that **“I handed Katchatheevu away.”** Whenever she says that, does she not remember her own statement that this power belongs to the Union Government?

Jayalalithaa says she has repeatedly written to successive Prime Ministers urging action on Katchatheevu. But when I write to the Prime Minister, she asks: is merely writing a letter enough—why did you not go in person?

Jayalalithaa has frequently criticised us over Katchatheevu. During the DMK government we not only passed a resolution in the Tamil Nadu Assembly condemning the Union Government's transfer of Katchatheevu to Sri Lanka without the State government's consent; we also urged the Union Government that, if the agreement could not be cancelled, it should at least be amended so that the rights of Tamil Nadu fishermen were not affected.

When Jayalalithaa came to power in Tamil Nadu in 1991, she hoisted the flag at Fort St. George and thundered, **“I will retrieve Katchatheevu.”** During the five years that she governed after taking that vow, did she move even a tiny straw toward fulfilling it? During her rule, she did not even create a way for pilgrims to visit St. Antony's Church on Katchatheevu. It was during the DMK government that a way was found for that.

Speaking in the Legislative Assembly on 20 April 1992, Jayalalithaa said:

> “We passed a resolution here seeking the retrieval of Katchatheevu and sent it to the Union Government. But retrieving Katchatheevu does not appear to be something that can happen quickly or easily.”

The DMK—and DMK governments—have repeatedly raised the Katchatheevu issue and asked for the island to be retrieved. The DMK government has also repeatedly pointed out the erosion of fishermen's rights around Katchatheevu and asked that those injuries be remedied.

There is one thing worth recalling. On 30 September 1994, when Jayalalithaa was Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu, she wrote to then Prime Minister Narasimha Rao. In that letter she wrote these exact words:

> “The ceding of this tiny island to the Island Nation had been done by the Government of India in the interest of better bilateral relations”

That is to say, Jayalalithaa herself wrote that the Government of India had ceded this tiny island—Katchatheevu—to the island nation of Sri Lanka in the interest of maintaining good relations between India and Sri Lanka. That is the same lesson Union ministers continue to recite even now.

For many years before India actually transferred Katchatheevu to Sri Lanka, the possibility existed only at the level of reports. To give a further documented example, the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly itself took up a question on Katchatheevu on 29 March 1972, and I answered it. I said: **“We have communicated to the Union Government our legitimate arguments regarding Katchatheevu. The Katchatheevu issue is one that should be settled amicably through the intervention of the Government of India.”**

Later, speaking in Madurai, I said that I had placed before the Prime Minister all the evidence showing that Katchatheevu belonged to Tamil Nadu and to India, but that without giving any importance to it, and without even calling me and speaking a word about it, the Prime Minister had gifted Katchatheevu to Sri Lanka. Speaking in Tiruchi, I said:

> “Katchatheevu is Tamil Nadu's right. On the Katchatheevu issue the people of the country are united. Without listening to them, they hand it over to Sri Lanka and announce to the world that an agreement has been reached. We learn of it by reading the newspapers.”

It is also worth noting that during a DMK government, when the ruling DMK brought a resolution seeking the return of Katchatheevu, the opposition AIADMK did not support it and instead walked out.

On 27 June 1974 the Union Government suddenly issued its announcement. Two days later, on 29 June, I convened all-party leaders at the Chennai Secretariat, consulted them and moved a resolution based on the views they expressed. The AIADMK refused to support or sign that resolution.

After that meeting, I wrote to the then Prime Minister in these exact words:

> “On behalf of the Government of Tamil Nadu and on behalf of the people of Tamil Nadu, I am constrained to express our deep sense of disappointment over the recent Indo-Sri Lanka Agreement, according to which, Sri Lanka’s claim to Kachativu has been conceded by the Government of India. May I express the hope that you will take into consideration the decision contained in the Resolution and take appropriate action?”

In a letter Jayalalithaa wrote to then Prime Minister Vajpayee on 23 July 2003, she said that the only way both to preserve good relations between India and Sri Lanka and to protect the traditional rights enjoyed by Tamil Nadu fishermen was as follows:

> “The best possible solution is to get the island of Katcha Theevu and adjacent seas on lease in perpetuity solely for fishing, drying of nets and pilgrimage. Sri Lanka’s Sovereignty over Katcha Theeve could
> be upheld at the same time.”

In other words, she proposed obtaining Katchatheevu and the adjacent sea permanently on lease for Tamil Nadu fishermen to fish, dry their nets and make pilgrimages, while at the same time accepting Sri Lanka's sovereignty over Katchatheevu. The same person who wrote all this then now fires questions at us.

Chief Minister Jayalalithaa has spoken in the Assembly about the Berubari area in West Bengal. She made the same argument in a statement dated 21 June 2009, when the DMK was in government, and asked then too what I had done while in power to retrieve Katchatheevu. She asks me that question now as well. But Jayalalithaa herself governed from 1991 to 1996, from 2001 to 2006, and again from 2011 to the present. What exactly has she accomplished? Has she retrieved Katchatheevu? Yet in the Assembly, forgetting all dignity, she asked whether I had done anything to atone for the “sin” I had committed. That is why I have explained what I did while in office.

Jayalalithaa has herself said that **“only the Union Government has the authority to retrieve Katchatheevu”;** that **“Katchatheevu was given to Sri Lanka in the interest of good relations between India and Sri Lanka”;** and that **“Sri Lanka's sovereignty over Katchatheevu could be accepted.”** Yet she says that I handed it away and that she will retrieve it. Do the people of Tamil Nadu not recognise what kind of **“deceptive drama”** this is?

Jayalalithaa filed a case in the Supreme Court on Katchatheevu in 2008. Even earlier, Tamilar Thalaivar K. Veeramani had filed a case on the same Katchatheevu issue in 1997. The truth is that even after many years, no final outcome has been reached in that case.

Whenever Tamil fishermen are attacked by the Sri Lankan Navy, whether Jayalalithaa is in government or out of government, she speaks as though I personally handed Katchatheevu over to Sri Lanka. That has become a routine deception!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**28 March 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. The letter presents Kalaignar's political and legal account of the 1974 Katchatheevu settlement and later actions. The translation does not independently adjudicate sovereignty, treaty law or the legal status of fishermen's rights.
2. All three English passages printed in the Tamil source are reproduced exactly as printed. In particular, the 2003 quotation preserves the source's spellings **“Katcha Theevu”** and **“Katcha Theeve”** and its line break.
3. `பித்தலாட்டம்` is rendered **deception** here to preserve the title's accusation of recurring political misrepresentation without adding a stronger allegation than the source itself makes.
4. The Tamil source's reference to Berubari is retained without adding outside constitutional history.

1974ஆம் ஆண்டு தி.மு.கழகம் ஆட்சியிலே இருந்தபோதே கழகப் பொதுக் குழுவில் பின்வரும் தீர்மானம் நிறைவேற்றப்பட்டது.

திருமதி இந்திராகாந்தி பிரதமராக இருந்த நேரத்திலேதான் கச்சத் தீவினை இலங்கைக்குத் தர வேண்டிய ஒரு சூழ்நிலை ஏற்பட்டது. அப்போது அவர்கள் வெளியுறவுத் துறை அமைச்சரையும், அதிகாரிகளையும் என்னிடம் அனுப்பி விவாதிக்கச் செய்தபோது, தமிழக அரசின் சார்பில் நாங்கள் எங்கள் கடுமையான எதிர்ப்பினைப் பதிவு செய்த பிறகும், கச்சத் தீவினை மத்திய அரசு இலங்கைக்கு வழங்கியது.

1991இல் தமிழகத்தில் ஆட்சிக்கு வந்த ஜெயலலிதா கோட்டை கொத்தளத்தில் கொடியேற்றி வைத்து முழங்கியபோது “கச்சத் தீவை மீட்பேன்” எனச் சபதமிட்டாரே அந்த சபதத்தை நிறைவேற்றிட அதன்பின் ஆட்சி செய்த ஐந்தாண்டு காலமும் அதற்காக ஒரு சிறு துரும்பையேனும் கிள்ளிப் போட்டிருப்பாரா? கச்சத் தீவில் உள்ள அந்தோணியார் கோவிலுக்குச் செல்வதற்குக்கூட அவர் ஆட்சியில் வழிவகை செய்யவில்லை. தி.மு. கழக ஆட்சியிலேதான் அதற்கொரு வழி பிறந்தது.

மேற்கு வங்காளத்தில் உள்ள பெருபாரி பகுதியைப் பற்றி முதலமைச்சர் ஜெயலலிதா பேரவையில் பேசியிருக்கிறார். இதே கருத்தைத் தான் தி.மு. கழகம் ஆட்சியிலே இருந்த போதும் 21-6-2009 அன்று விடுத்த அறிக்கையிலே கூறிவிட்டு, கச்சத் தீவை மீட்க நான் ஆட்சியிலே இருந்தபோது என்ன செய்தேன் என்று அப்போதும் கேட்டார், இப்போதும் கேட்டிருக்கிறார். என்னைக் கேள்வி கேட்கும் ஜெயலலிதா 1991 முதல் 1996 வரையிலும், 2001 முதல் 2006ஆம் ஆண்டு வரையிலும், 2011 முதல் இதுவரையிலும் ஆட்சியிலே இருக்கிறாரே, இவர் என்ன செய்து கிழித்து விட்டார்? கச்சத் தீவை வாங்கித் தந்து விட்டாரா? ஆனால் நான் செய்த பாவத்திற்கு விமோசனமாக ஏதாவது செய்திருக்கிறேனா என்று கண்ணியத்தை மறந்து பேரவையில் ஜெயலலிதா கேட்டிருக்கிறார். அதனால்தான் நான் ஆட்சியிலே இருந்தபோது என்ன செய்தேன் என்பதை விளக்கியிருக்கிறேன்.

“கச்சத் தீவை மீட்கும் அதிகாரம் மத்திய அரசுக்குத்தான் இருக்கிறது” என்றும்; “இந்தியாவிற்கும் இலங்கைக்கும் இடையே நல்லுறவு வேண்டும் என்பதற்காகத்தான் கச்சத் தீவு இலங்கைக்குக் கொடுக்கப்பட்டது” என்றும் “கச்சத் தீவில் இலங்கைக்கு உள்ள இறையாண்மையை ஏற்றுக்கொள்ள வேண்டும்” என்றும் பேசிய, அறிக்கை விடுத்த ஜெயலலிதா அதை நான் தாரைவார்த்து விட்டேன் என்றும், அவர் அதை மீட்பேன் என்பதும் எப்படிப்பட்ட “கபட நாடகம்” என்பது தமிழ்நாட்டு மக்களுக்குத் தெரியாதா என்ன?

கச்சத் தீவு பற்றி ஜெயலலிதா உச்ச நீதி மன்றத்தில் வழக்கு தாக்கல் செய்தது 2008ஆம் ஆண்டு. அதற்கு முன்பே தமிழர் தலைவர் கி.வீரமணி இதே கச்சத் தீவுக்காக 1997ஆம் ஆண்டே ஒரு வழக்கினைத் தாக்கல் செய்திருக்கிறார். ஆனால் பல ஆண்டுகள் ஆகியும் இந்த வழக்கில் ஒரு முடிவு காணப்படவில்லை
