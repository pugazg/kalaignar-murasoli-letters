# 3766. Will the Government of India Continue to Sleep?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

The hunting of Tamil Nadu fishermen by the Sri Lankan Navy shows no sign of stopping. The Government of Tamil Nadu and the political parties in Tamil Nadu have appealed to the Union Government countless times. Even on the few occasions when the Union Government has brought the matter to the attention of the Sri Lankan Government, the Sri Lankan Navy has paid no heed.

Every year, mechanised boats are prohibited from fishing for forty-five days so that fish stocks may reproduce in the sea. Once the ban ended, fishermen began going to sea happily from the 1st of this month. Mechanised-boat fishermen generally go out on alternate days in rotation. They said with happiness that when they went fishing on 1 and 3 June, they caught large quantities of fish and returned without any threat from the Sri Lankan Navy.

But on 5 June 2013, more than 2,400 Tamil Nadu fishermen went out from Rameswaram in more than 600 mechanised boats. At around three in the afternoon, while one group was fishing near Neduntheevu, members of the Sri Lankan Navy arrived and surrounded them.

On the pretext that they had crossed the Indian maritime boundary to fish, the Sri Lankan Navy seized five boats and detained the twenty-four fishermen aboard them: Arulseelan, Maria Asrin, Vilvathurai, Ramakrishnan, Vasanthakumar, Poopandi, Vadivel, Lourdes, Ayodin, Muneeskumar, Kaatthanan, Selvan, Jayaraman, Pandi, Mathiyazhagan, Dasan, Stephen Raj, Kalimuthu, Bakkiyaraj, Sesathai, Babu, Rayappan, Kannan and Joseph. Claiming that they were being taken for questioning, the Navy took them to the Talaimannar naval camp. They were produced before the Mannar court. The judge ordered that they be held in custody until the 20th. They are now confined in Anuradhapura prison.

Before the Rameswaram fishermen could recover from this shock, news received yesterday, 6 June 2013, said that Sri Lankan naval personnel had camped in the Katchatheevu area with patrol vessels and had detained another twenty-five Tamil Nadu fishermen, including Sridhar, Anton, Greenson, Rayappan, another Rayappan, Rajadurai, Muruganandam, Bevinesh, Chithiraipandi and Kamatchi. They attached the fishing boats to their own vessel and towed them away. These twenty-five fishermen were produced before the Kayts court in Jaffna, ordered to be held until 19 June, and confined in Jaffna prison.

Apart from arresting these forty-nine fishermen, the Sri Lankan naval personnel are reported to have threatened other fishermen in the area at gunpoint, beaten them and driven them away. As a result, two boats struck rocks and met with accidents. Reports also say that a fisherman named Muniyasamy fell into the sea and died.

Speaking about the incident, Fishermen's Association President Jesuraj said that Tamil Nadu fishermen had been fishing in the Mannar Sea for the past seven hundred years, and that such problems had arisen only during the past thirty years. He said that the Governments of India and Sri Lanka must arrive at an amicable solution.

In addition to these forty-nine fishermen, the day before yesterday the Sri Lankan Coast Guard police detained Bose, Babu and Regan, three fishermen who had travelled in a boat belonging to Shanmugam of Rameswaram. The police questioned them aboard their patrol vessel and severely threatened them. They kept the three men on the vessel for about ten hours before releasing them.

The Rameswaram fishermen have now announced a peaceful protest: if the forty-nine fishermen held in Sri Lankan prisons are not released, they will sail towards Sri Lanka on 22 June in all their mechanised boats, flying white flags.

Further, eleven fishermen—including Jayaseelan of Colachel in Kanyakumari district and Antony Suresh of Arokiyapuram—were fishing in the Saudi Arabian sea region on 16 December last year when they were arrested by the Iranian Coast Guard and confined in Bandar Abbas prison. Repeated appeals have been made for action to secure their release, but no action appears to have been taken so far.

Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Jayalalithaa continues to act as though her duty in such matters ends once she immediately writes a letter to the Prime Minister. This time too, as soon as yesterday's news arrived, she wrote asking that action be taken to release the forty-nine Tamil Nadu fishermen, and hurriedly ensured that the letter appeared in every newspaper.

At the Chief Ministers' Conference on Internal Security, a speech on behalf of Tamil Nadu was read by some minister and distributed to all the newspapers. It stated that the Tamil Nadu Chief Minister had written several letters to the Prime Minister condemning the Sri Lankan Navy's indiscriminate and murderous attacks on Tamil Nadu fishermen; that the Union Government's failure to take proper action, and the continuation of the attacks, had angered the entire fishing community of Tamil Nadu; and that the Union Government should take immediate steps to recover Katchatheevu.

But it was this same Chief Minister Jayalalithaa who, while hoisting the flag from the ramparts of Fort St George during the Independence Day celebration on 15 August 1991, issued the challenge: “I will recover Katchatheevu at any cost.” Later, on 20 April 1992, she said, “Recovering Katchatheevu does not appear to be something that can happen soon.”

In a letter she wrote to the Prime Minister on 30 September 1994, Jayalalithaa herself offered a justification for the Government of India's action: “India gave Katchatheevu only so that friendly relations might prevail between India and Sri Lanka.”

The reality now is that a Sri Lankan warship has been stationed near Katchatheevu. It is said that Chinese military personnel are on board. The Sri Lankan Navy threatens Tamil Nadu fishermen. This is a challenge to India's sovereignty itself. When opposition to this grew, the Sri Lankan Government removed the warships; but it has now stationed them near Katchatheevu once again. Reports say that one large warship and ten patrol vessels have been placed there. Rameswaram fishermen have consequently been gripped by fear once more.

Because the Katchatheevu arrangement did not receive the approval of the Indian Parliament, it is not valid under the Constitution. Pointing this out, the DMK has filed a case in the Supreme Court.

Indian fishermen have continued to suffer for many years. Unless the Government of India acts firmly without any further delay, no light will appear in the lives of Tamil Nadu's fishermen.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**8 June 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. **Katchatheevu** and **Neduntheevu** are retained in their established Tamil forms. The court referred to in the source as the `ஊர்க்காவல்படை` court is rendered as the **Kayts court**, the established English name for the location in the Jaffna region.
2. The places of detention, numbers of boats and fishermen, dates, names and attributed allegations are preserved as stated in Kalaignar's source text.

இந்தப் பிரச்சினையில் மத்திய அரசு உரிய நடவடிக்கை எடுக்காமல்
இருப்பதும், தமிழக மீனவர்கள் மீதான தாக்குதல் தொடர்வதும்,

கூறியிருக்கிறார்.

ஆனால் இதே முதலமைச்சர் ஜெயலலிதா 15-8-1991 அன்று
