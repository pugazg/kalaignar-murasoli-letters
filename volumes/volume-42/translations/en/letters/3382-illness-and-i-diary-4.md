# 3382. Illness and I — Diary (4)

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This fourth diary instalment covers 11–17 February 2009: the spinal operation, intensive-care recovery, a brief post-operative health scare, and preparation and presentation of the Tamil Nadu Financial Statement. Medical and political descriptions remain those of the source. The complete audited Tamil is reproduced below and remains authoritative.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3382](../../../chapters/3382-nalivum-naanum-naatkurippu-4.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 131](../../../pages/page-131.md)–[PDF 140](../../../pages/page-140.md)  
**Date:** 13 March 2009

*(Through this serial article I am bringing together various events from the time I was admitted to Ramachandra Hospital, through the surgery and the continuous treatments that followed.)*

★★

**Udanpirappē,**

## 11-2-2009

Today was the day of my operation.

The doctors had told me the previous day that I should be ready by 6 a.m. The newspapers that usually reached me by 5 a.m. did not arrive that morning. Perhaps they were afraid that reading them would make me emotional.

When they came at six and asked whether we could leave, I said, “Let the newspapers come first. I will take a quick look and then go.” Only after reading them did I leave for the operating theatre.

At around 7 a.m., all along the way from my room to the operating theatre on the seventh floor, close relatives and Kazhagam leaders stood on both sides with troubled eyes. Looking at all of them, I entered the theatre.

Anaesthesia was administered in the proper manner and the operation was successfully completed.

More than a hundred journalists gathered at the hospital awaiting the outcome. I was told that, apart from them, thousands of Kazhagam comrades and leaders were waiting at the hospital entrance to hear the result.

After the operation, Dr. Marthandam and Dr. Jaiswal met the journalists and explained all the details of my condition.

I was taken directly to an ICU room and was told that I had to remain there for forty-eight hours before returning to my room on the seventh floor.

When it was said that I would have to remain in the ICU for forty-eight hours, hospital owner brother Venkatachalam said I should not stay in the common ICU with other patients. Overnight, he had a separate ICU prepared for me on the sixth floor.

It was a large room arranged for me alone.

When I regained awareness there, it was 1 p.m.

My mouth was extremely dry and I asked for water. A nurse who was helping me gave me so little that even my lips barely became wet.

I asked her, “Is your name Kaveri?”

“No, Ayya. Why do you ask?” she replied.

I said, “You are giving me water just like Karnataka gives us the Kaveri!”

Forgetting that it was an ICU, even the doctors around me laughed.

When the doctors later met journalists, Kazhagam General Secretary Professor also joined them and, on behalf of the party and the people of Tamil Nadu, thanked and praised the doctors and everyone who had helped. He also requested visitors to reduce their visits and cooperate for a few more days.

Sonia Gandhi, Prime Minister Manmohan Singh and Union Minister Pranab Mukherjee contacted former Minister Dayanidhi Maran and Rajya Sabha Member Kanimozhi by telephone, enquired about the surgery and my condition, and conveyed wishes for my speedy recovery.

That afternoon the doctors examined me to see whether my nervous system was functioning normally after surgery and said they were satisfied.

They did not give me the evening newspapers until 5 p.m. Then I myself called the doctor, asked for the evening papers and read them.

## 12-2-2009

I remained in the ICU today as well.

There were strict restrictions. No visitors were permitted. Only my wives, daughters and sons were allowed.

Fortunately there was a television set.

I listened to the President’s Address in Parliament. I was happy to hear that the Sri Lankan Tamil issue had found a place in the speech.

I immediately called an assistant and dictated a statement saying that, while I was in hospital under medical observation after surgery, the President of India’s statement to the joint session of Parliament — that there should be an immediate ceasefire in Sri Lanka and that both sides should find a peaceful solution through talks — had given me great comfort and happiness.

That statement was released to the newspapers.

After surgery I was today given solid food in measured quantities.

An echocardiogram was taken and confirmed to be normal.

At around 5 p.m. the operated area on my back was cleaned and fresh medication was applied.

## 13-2-2009

Forty-eight hours after surgery, I was shifted back to Ward D7.

Dr. Marthandam, head of the medical team, issued a statement explaining the operation and my recovery afterward.

Delhi doctor Jaiswal returned to Delhi that day, saying that he would come back to Chennai on the twenty-first.

Because I had to remain lying down continuously, they alternated my position from side to side to prevent sores from developing on my back.

The attendant helping me turn from side to side was himself named “Mari”!

All the tubes fitted to my body during surgery were removed today.

## 14-2-2009

I called the Finance Secretary and discussed the Financial Statement that was to be presented on the seventeenth.

Though senior doctors had frequently come to see me during preparations for the surgery, I must specifically mention the work done by Dr. Jambu, Dr. Sameer and Dr. Boovesh Karthik, both during my hospital stay and after I returned home.

They took turns and also worked together in caring for me. Even if I merely needed to roll over in bed, they came to my side each time and made sure I did not experience any difficulty.

Once Dr. Jambu stood beside me and asked me to turn toward his side.

I laughed and told him: “I keep trying to make everyone else turn to my side — but you are asking me to turn to yours!”

Among the nurses, especially Senthamarai, Jacqueline, Flora, Chitra, Shanthi and Ameena, each took turns over the full twenty-four hours and cared for me as if I were a close relative, giving medicines on time and providing whatever help I needed.

Likewise, ward boy Marimuthu and my personal assistant Nithya had stayed close to me for the past month without moving away. Their service too must be recorded in this series.

Even if, unable to bear the pain, I became angry at times, none of the doctors mentioned here showed irritation.

I must especially record with gratitude that my family doctor Gopal, though himself a heart patient, coordinated all the doctors and took care of me in consultation with them.

## 15-2-2009

For the first time since the operation I suddenly developed a burning pain in my legs.

I told the doctors. A pain-relief injection was given immediately.

The pain eased somewhat, but at around 8 p.m. I began sweating heavily all over and needed to urinate repeatedly. My body temperature also dropped.

All the senior doctors rushed in.

I do not know why, but I felt that I wanted to see senior Ministers and Kazhagam leaders including Professor.

I called an assistant and asked him to contact them by telephone and tell them to come to the hospital.

Ponmudy, Velu and others who had been sleeping in another room became alarmed.

I called Stalin and said: “Something is happening to me. I do not know how to explain it. My thoughts keep going to the Financial Statement. Every paragraph of it was written after I thought through it again and again. If something happens, treat it as my final testament.”

He cried out, “Appa! Appa!”

He said, “Do not talk like this, Appa. Nothing will happen.”

Oxygen was administered immediately.

An ECG was taken.

All the doctors kept me under intensive observation.

They brought a blanket called a **Forced Air Warmer**, which helps keep the body warm, and covered me with it.

I was in a condition where I could not even explain what was happening.

My daughter Selvi sat alone and cried.

I called her close. She gripped my hand tightly.

Of all the days I spent in hospital, this was the day I became most emotional.

The doctors kept trying one measure after another to bring me back to a normal condition.

Dr. Marthandam, Dr. Thanikachalam, Dr. Mahesh and others did not go home at all. They stayed awake in my room through the entire night watching me.

Dayalu, who normally cannot stand continuously even for a few minutes, stood for two and a half hours holding my head.

Raji entered in tears after hearing the news over the telephone. Instead of her consoling me, I had to console her.

After 4.30 a.m. I fell asleep.

When I woke after a few hours, I realised that I had returned to a normal condition.

Only then did everyone feel relief. I too felt as though I had taken “another birth.”

Only then did the doctors breathe freely.

Had something happened to me that very day, a few people today would have been left without anyone to abuse!

## 16-2-2009

Today I underwent blood tests and a heart X-ray, and all were found to be normal.

On behalf of the United Progressive Alliance government at the Centre, Union Minister Pranab Mukherjee presented and spoke on the Financial Statement today.

I listened to the entire speech on television, issued my views on the Financial Statement and conveyed my appreciation to the Union Finance Minister.

Kazhagam Treasurer and Youth Wing Secretary brother M. K. Stalin sought permission from Professor and me to organise, on behalf of the Youth Wing, a “Youth Chain” in Chennai and district headquarters on February 21.

We gave permission.

Deputy General Secretary brother Duraimurugan met Professor and me and sought permission for the subcommittee of the Sri Lankan Tamils’ Welfare Rights Council to go to Delhi on the seventeenth, meet the President and Sonia Gandhi, and ask for help in securing an immediate and permanent solution to the Sri Lankan Tamil issue.

We gave permission for that too.

Likewise, Government Chief Whip brother R. Sakkarapani met us and sought permission to convene a meeting of DMK legislators on the evening of the seventeenth.

We granted that permission as well.

## 17-2-2009

It has been six days since the operation.

Because of coughing I could not sleep continuously. Lung exercises were given.

Pain in the leg reappeared today. An injection was given immediately and the pain was reduced.

A scan was taken of the leg, and the doctors found blood circulation to be normal.

Today was the Tamil Nadu Government’s Financial Statement.

Every year, whether it is the Financial Statement or the Governor’s Address, whenever the DMK has been in government it has been my practice to spend around fifteen days sitting with officials of the relevant departments for long discussions and working to bring each scheme into implementation.

Officials of those departments know that I am not someone accustomed merely to signing reports prepared by officials and giving approval.

I have them read repeatedly, think about how they may be changed, and work with the idea that whatever is done should be done better.

Indeed, if the Financial Statement was to be presented the next day, it was my usual practice even at midnight on the previous day to go to the Secretariat and check the proofs.

This year, because I was in hospital, I did not go to the Secretariat the day before the Financial Statement.

But although I did not go in person, I repeatedly spoke with the Finance Secretary by telephone, asking that this be changed and that be changed.

The department’s officers too cooperated tirelessly in accordance with my wishes, and they deserve appreciation.

By 7 a.m., Finance Minister and Leader of the House Professor, Ministers M. K. Stalin and Arcot Veerasamy, and the Finance Secretary came to the hospital and received the Financial Statement from my hands.

I returned the shawl Professor had placed on me by placing it on him, and handed the Financial Statement to him to take to the House.

Today I saw the result of the effort I had taken from the hospital in preparing it.

The reading of the Financial Statement in the House was broadcast live on television. So I was able to see not only the statement being read but also how warmly Members of the House welcomed it.

Finance Minister Professor read the statement beautifully, firmly and with his characteristic dignity for more than two hours.

At the outset he thanked me for the difficulties I had undertaken in preparing it while suffering from back pain and receiving surgical treatment in hospital, and expressed the wish that I recover quickly and return to work.

Among the announcements was one that no one expected: cooperative loans to farmers, already subject to waiver measures, would henceforth carry no interest — an announcement the source presents as unmatched by any other State in India.

Usually, when such a major government announcement is coming, news about it leaks widely beforehand.

But even Ministers had not known in advance that farmers’ loans would carry no interest.

Likewise, the Financial Statement contained, among many other measures:

- ₹2,000 crore in new crop loans for farmers;
- reduction of land tax to a nominal level;
- cancellation of that tax as well in twelve flood-affected districts;
- Kalaignar’s insurance scheme for life-saving high-cost treatment for more than one crore families;
- a Metro Rail project costing ₹14,600 crore;
- 1.35 lakh concrete houses for poor people;
- one lakh new women’s self-help groups;
- and a series of tax concessions.

It was, in the source’s words, a Financial Statement full of achievements upon achievements.

After it was read, senior Ministers came to the hospital and conveyed appreciation and thanks.

In my life I am never continuously happy for long.

If I spend a few hours happily, some problem almost immediately arrives.

So it was today.

While we were happily discussing the Financial Statement, news reached me in hospital that Janata Party leader Subramanian Swamy had gone to the Madras High Court seeking to implead himself in an appeal concerning the Chidambaram Nataraja Temple in support of the Dikshitars; that some lawyers, angered by his criticism a few days earlier of their protest for the protection of Sri Lankan Tamils, entered the courtroom where he was seated and attacked him with rotten eggs.

After that, I did not sleep for the rest of the day.

I will continue this diary tomorrow.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**13-3-2009**

இந்தியாவிலேயே எந்த மாநிலத்திலும் இல்லாத அளவிற்கு விவசாயிகளுக்குத் தள்ளுபடும் கூட்டுறவு கடன்களுக்கு இனிமேல் வட்டியே கிடையாது என்ற அறிவிப்பு - யாருமே எதிர்பார்க்காதது - பொதுவாக இது போன்ற பெரிய அறிவிப்புகள் அரசின் சார்பில் வருகிறது என்றால் - அதைப்பற்றி பரவலாக வெளியிலே செய்தி கசிந்திடும். ஆனால் விவசாயிகளின் கடன்களுக்கு வட்டி கிடையாது என்பது அமைச்சர்களுக்கே புதிய செய்தி.

அது போலவே, விவசாயிகளுக்கு 2000 கோடி ரூபாய் புதிய பயிர்க் கடன், பெயருக்கு மட்டுமே நிலவரி என்ற அளவிற்கு குறைப்பு, வெள்ளம் பாதித்த 12 மாவட்டங்களில் அதுவும் ரத்து, ஒரு கோடிக்கு மேற்பட்ட குடும்பங்களுக்கு உயிர் காக்கும் உயர் சிகிச்சைக்கான கலைஞரின் காப்பீட்டுத் திட்டம், 14,600 கோடி ரூபாயில் மெட்ரோ ரெயில் திட்டம், ஏழையெளிய மக்களுக்கு 1 லட்சத்து 35 ஆயிரம் காங்கிரீட் வீடுகள், ஒரு லட்சம் புதிய மகளிர் சுய உதவிக் குழுக்கள், அடுக்கடுக்கான வரிச் சலுகைகள் என்பன

போன்ற எண்ணிலடங்கா சாதனைகள், சாதனைகள் கொண்ட நிதி நிலை அறிக்கை.

நிதி நிலை அறிக்கை படிக்கப்பட்டதும், மூத்த அமைச்சர்கள் எல்லாம் மருத்துவ மனைக்கு வந்து பாராட்டினையும் நன்றியையும் தெரிவித்துக் கொண்டார்கள். என்னுடைய வாழ்க்கையில் நான் எப்போதும் தொடர்ந்து மகிழ்ச்சியாக இருப்பதில்லை. மகிழ்ச்சியாக சில மணி நேரங்களை கழித்தேன் என்றால் உடனே ஏதோவொரு பிரச்சினை வராமல் இருக்காது. அதைப் போலத் தான் நிதி நிலை அறிக்கை குறித்து மகிழ்ச்சியாக பேசிக் கொண்டிருக்கும்போதே, சென்னை உயர் நீதி மன்றத்திற்கு, சிதம்பரம் நடராஜர் கோவில் பிரச்சினையில் தீட்சிதர்களுக்கு ஆதரவாக ஜனதா கட்சித் தலைவர் சுப்பிரமணியன் சுவாமி மேல் முறையீட்டு வழக்கில் தன்னையும் இணைத்துக் கொள்வதற்காக சென்றார் என்றும் - அவர் அதற்கு சில நாட்களுக்கு முன்பு இலங்கைத் தமிழர் பாதுகாப்புக்காக வழக்கறிஞர்கள் நடத்திய போராட்டம் குறித்து கண்டித்துப் பேசியதற்காக - சில வழக்கறிஞர்கள் சுப்பிரமணியம் சுவாமி அமர்ந்திருந்த நீதி மன்றத்திற்குள் சென்று, அழுகிய முட்டையால் அவரைத் தாக்கினார்கள் என்றும் மருத்துவ மனைக்கு எனக்கு செய்தி வந்தது. அதற்குப் பிறகு அன்று முழுவதும் நான் தூங்கவில்லை.

இந்த நாட்குறிப்பை தொடர்ந்து நாளையும் எழுதுகிறேன்.
