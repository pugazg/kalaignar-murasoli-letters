# 3441. An Appeal from One Among the Udanpirappugal!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> The English title follows the actual letter-start source on PDF 119, where `உடன் பிறப்புகளில்` is printed as two words. The printed contents independently uses joined `உடன்பிறப்புகளில்`. The letter is a by-election appeal built around a long source list of government measures; every listed item, figure and date is retained in source order without outside reconciliation.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3441](../../../chapters/3441-udan-pirappugalil-oruvanaaga-vidukkum-vendukol.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 119](../../../pages/page-119.md)–[PDF 130](../../../pages/page-130.md)  
**Date:** 13 December 2009

**Udanpirappē,**

Polling for the by-elections in the Tiruchendur and Vandavasi constituencies is to take place on the 19th. The campaign is proceeding intensely. Younger brother M.K. Alagiri, the Kazhagam’s South Zone Organising Secretary, the district secretaries of that region and DMK ministers are camped in Tiruchendur and are working tirelessly day and night. Likewise, in Vandavasi, Tiruvannamalai district secretary E.V. Velu, district secretaries of that region and DMK ministers are camped there, spinning like tops as they carry out election work.

The General Secretary, Professor; Deputy Chief Minister and Kazhagam Treasurer M.K. Stalin; Rajya Sabha member and poet Kanimozhi; and other leading members of the Kazhagam are undertaking whirlwind tours in the two constituencies and participating in election campaigning.

Because this is a by-election, and also considering my health, I may not come directly to those constituencies. But you should not have occasion to ask, “Did our elder brother not ask us even once for our vote?” So I make this appeal at least through a letter.

That is not the only reason. The opposing side is confused, not knowing what to say while asking for votes or what accusation to make against the government, and so they are speaking all manner of things. For example, only three days after the Arundhathiyar community — having benefited greatly from the 3 per cent internal reservation we granted to fulfil a demand denied to them for many years — held a meeting to thank me that was too large even for Valluvar Kottam, the State Secretary of the Marxist Communist Party goes to an election meeting in Tiruchendur and says nothing has been done for the Arundhathiyar community. I can only feel sorry when I think what the people of that constituency will think of him.

When we go before the voters and ask for votes, we can list what was accomplished during our period in office and say: “You placed us in power; we did not sit idle; here are the things we have done,” and ask for votes on that basis. The principal opposition, the AIADMK, can ask for votes only by listing the cruelties committed during its rule, or the injustices done under its government to government employees, workers and farmers.

To remind those who are going to vote of the achievements of the DMK government during the past four years, I wish to list them once. Here are only some of them:

- Rice at ₹1 per kilogram, 20 kilograms a month; 1 crore 85 lakh families benefiting.
- To reduce the burden of rising prices on poor people, palm oil, toor dal, urad dal, rava, maida and fortified wheat flour supplied every month at low prices under the Special Public Distribution Scheme.
- Cooperative loans of ₹7,000 crore waived for 22 lakh 40 thousand 739 farming families.
- Interest on crop loans for farmers reduced from 9 per cent to 7 per cent in 2006-2007, 5 per cent in 2007-2008 and 4 per cent in 2008-2009; in 2009-2010 interest abolished on crop loans of farmers who repay their loans.
- The Union Government’s previous-year procurement prices of ₹850 per quintal for ordinary paddy and ₹880 for fine-variety paddy raised to ₹1,000 and ₹1,050 respectively.
- 117 Uzhavar Sandhais reopened across the State with renewed vitality for the benefit of farmers and consumers.
- Against the Union Government price of ₹1,077.60 per tonne for sugarcane with 9.5 per cent recovery, a State-advised price addition of ₹359.80 raised it to ₹1,437.40 per tonne; with ₹90 transport charge and ₹22.60 sugar-construction incentive added, an order dated 24.9.2009 provided ₹1,550 per tonne to sugarcane farmers — ₹330 more per tonne than the previous year.
- With the government subsidising 50 per cent of the premium under the crop-insurance scheme, 8 lakh 50 thousand farmers took crop insurance with State assistance in 2008-2009. For crop damage from heavy rain in December 2008, compensation of ₹656 crore 66 lakh was fixed for 5 lakh 36 thousand 352 farmers, and the State share of ₹306 crore 48 lakh was released on 4.9.2009.
- ₹40 crore allocated as subsidy for 25 lakh farmers to take crop insurance in 2009-2010.
- ₹5 crore 25 lakh, including interest, waived on loans obtained through THADCO by Scheduled-Caste farmers up to 31.8.2006.
- For non-farm loans granted against land mortgages, interest and penal interest waived; if the principal borrowed is paid, the debt is cancelled.
- As the first stage of a revolutionary plan to link rivers within the State and carry surplus flood water to dry districts for expanded irrigation, the Cauvery–Gundar linking project at an estimated ₹165 crore.
- The Thamirabarani–Karumeniyar–Nambiyar linking project at an estimated ₹369 crore.
- Because of concessions given to farmers by the DMK government, foodgrain production rising from 61 lakh tonnes in 2005-2006 to 91 lakh tonnes in 2008-2009.
- 2 crore 77 thousand 817 members enrolled in 30 unorganised-worker welfare boards, including the Agricultural Labourers Welfare Board.
- Assistance of ₹544 crore 8 lakh 28 thousand 87 to 11 lakh 96 thousand 705 unorganised-worker families.
- Under the free colour-television scheme planned at ₹2,678 crore 37 lakh 50 thousand for 1 crore 12 lakh 80 thousand families, televisions already distributed to 89 lakh 78 thousand 496 families at a cost of ₹2,144 crore 19 lakh 59 thousand 216; distribution continuing, with ₹500 crore allocated to buy another 40 lakh sets for those still requiring them.
- Free gas stoves with gas connections provided to 20 lakh 680 families at a cost of ₹360 crore.
- 2 lakh 10 thousand 747 acres of free land provided to 1 lakh 75 thousand 798 landless poor agricultural-labourer and farmer families.
- Free house-site pattas given to 6 lakh 85 thousand 976 poor families.
- Kamaraj’s birthday made “Education Development Day” by law and observed as an education festival in schools.
- For 73 lakh children and students aged 2 to 15, three eggs a week with the nutritious meal, along with bananas.
- Special fees abolished in government and government-aided schools for more than 50 lakh students studying through Tamil medium; State examination fees for Standards 10 and 12 abolished for 11 lakh students.
- Tuition fees abolished for more than 3 lakh 75 thousand arts-and-science college students studying for degrees.
- Among poor students who had discontinued studies, up to 10,000 a year enabled to obtain employment-oriented vocational training through community colleges, with assistance of ₹1,000 each through the Open University at a cost of ₹1 crore.
- Free bus passes each year for 24 lakh 82 thousand school students and 2 lakh 99 thousand college students.
- Free education for poor women extended from undergraduate degree level to postgraduate degree level.
- Entrance examination for professional courses abolished.
- Three new Anna Universities of Technology at Coimbatore, Tiruchi and Tirunelveli.
- After 2006, seven new government arts and science colleges at Orathanadu, Perambalur, Valparai, Surandai, Kulithalai, Lalgudi and Mettur.
- Under the principle of one medical college per district, five new medical colleges at Villupuram, Tiruvarur, Dharmapuri, Sivaganga and Perambalur.
- Under the plan to establish government engineering colleges in districts without them, new engineering colleges at Tindivanam, Villupuram, Panruti, Ariyalur, Thirukkuvalai, Ramanathapuram, Tiruvannamalai, Thanjavur, Dindigul, Thoothukudi and Kanyakumari.
- A law making Tamil a compulsory subject in schools through Standard 10.
- Fulfilment of a century-old dream by establishing the Central Institute of Classical Tamil in Chennai.
- The works of 81 distinguished Tamil scholars nationalised and ₹5 crore 33 lakh paid as compensation.
- Renovation work completed and consecration ceremonies held in 3,072 temples.
- Moovalur Ramamirtham Ammaiyar marriage-assistance raised to ₹20,000; ₹315 crore 93 lakh 90 thousand provided to 2 lakh 14 thousand 189 poor women; a further ₹150 crore allocated for 75,000 poor women.
- ₹6,000 each in nutrition assistance for poor pregnant women; ₹774 crore 48 lakh provided to 15 lakh 47 thousand 205 poor women; a further ₹250 crore allocated for 4 lakh mothers.
- Monthly assistance of ₹400 to 9 thousand 158 unmarried poor women over 50 living in poverty.
- `Varumun Kappom` revived: 91 lakh poor people benefited in 10 thousand 394 medical camps; 4,500 more camps planned for the year, with 21 lakh 24 thousand 922 people already benefiting in 2,361 camps held so far.
- Three nurses appointed in each of Tamil Nadu’s 1,421 Primary Health Centres to provide 24-hour service; deliveries there rising from 80 thousand 766 in 2006 to 2 lakh 41 thousand 78 in 2008, bringing benefit to rural women.
- Young-child heart-surgery scheme begun on 21.11.2007 with government assistance of ₹20,000 for closed surgery, ₹50,000 for ordinary open surgery and ₹1 lakh for difficult open surgery; 653 children treated. Schoolchildren’s heart-surgery scheme begun on 3.6.2008; 1,663 schoolchildren treated. Through 23 renowned private hospitals, heart surgeries carried out at a cost of ₹13 crore 65 lakh, protecting children’s lives.
- In partnership with EMRI, a modern Emergency Medical Ambulance Service implemented across Tamil Nadu at a cost of ₹32 crore so rural poor people can receive immediate medical care.
- A new medical-insurance scheme for government employees providing up to ₹2 lakh assistance over four years.
- Kalaignar Insurance Scheme for Life-Saving High Treatment implemented at a cost of ₹517 crore 30 lakh for one crore families; 1 crore 31 lakh 53 thousand 161 families registered so far; life-saving surgeries performed for 16 thousand 639 poor people at a cost of ₹57 crore 5 lakh 69 thousand.
- Free electricity to 1 lakh 5 thousand 494 handloom weavers, 90 thousand 547 powerloom weavers and 2 lakh 39 thousand 511 farmers who had obtained electricity connections by paying the special charge.
- Memoranda of Understanding and 11 government orders for 25 new factories involving investment of ₹45 thousand 891 crore and expected employment for about 2 lakh 20 thousand 292 people; action taken for 36 factories, of which nine had opened so far.
- ₹156 crore 15 lakh 30 thousand 604 in assistance to 3 lakh 53 thousand 488 educated unemployed youth.
- New employment in government offices for about 3 lakh 44 thousand youth.
- Monthly assistance for the elderly, destitute widows and persons with disabilities raised from ₹200 to ₹400; 7 lakh 39 thousand 541 elderly people and 7 lakh 90 thousand 41 destitute persons benefiting.
- Assistance for persons with severe disabilities raised from ₹200 to ₹500 a month; 10,000 people benefiting.
- Through the Mahalir Thittam begun by the DMK government in Dharmapuri district in 1989, 4,12,104 women’s self-help groups created, with 65 lakh 72 thousand women members and total loans of ₹5,041 crore.
- After 2006, 72 thousand 346 new women’s self-help groups with 10 lakh 48 thousand women members formed and ₹3,276 crore in bank credit provided.
- Revolving fund of ₹175 crore 67 lakh 70 thousand provided to 1,75,677 women’s groups.
- Infrastructure improvement works in 10 thousand 96 village panchayats under the Anaithu Grama Anna Marumalarchi Thittam at a cost of ₹2,033 crore.
- Likewise, infrastructure works in 420 town panchayats under the Anaithu Peruratchi Anna Marumalarchi Thittam at a cost of ₹210 crore.
- ₹793 crore in debts owed to the government by corporations and municipalities waived, reducing their financial burden and enabling public amenities.
- Improvement works on 45 thousand 943 kilometres of roads; 4 thousand 730 kilometres widened into two-lane roads.
- 551 small and large bridges built on Tamil Nadu roads at a cost of ₹557 crore.
- Projects worth ₹55 thousand crore secured for Tamil Nadu through the Shipping and Highways Department over five years from 2004.
- Head tax, surcharge on head tax and water cess abolished; land tax reduced nominally from ₹15 to ₹2 per acre for dry land and from ₹50 to ₹5 for wet land.
- Erode, Tiruppur, Vellore and Thoothukudi municipalities upgraded as corporations.
- Ariyalur and Tiruppur created as new districts.
- In addition to 9,248 new buses already introduced without raising fares, a further 3,500 new buses.
- Separate 3.5 per cent internal reservation for the Muslim community.
- Separate 3 per cent internal reservation for the Arundhathiyar community.
- A law enacted so persons of all castes can become archakas, with priestly training provided to 216 persons from various castes.
- In addition to 145 existing Periyar Memorial Samathuvapurams where people of all castes live together, a plan to create 95 more and make all 240 Samathuvapurams with statues of Thanthai Periyar; 19 of the 95 new Samathuvapurams opened so far.
- A world-class State library in Chennai at an estimated ₹166 crore.
- A new Legislative Assembly–Secretariat complex at Omandurar Government Estate at a cost of ₹450 crore.
- Adyar Park project at a cost of ₹100 crore.
- Metro Rail project estimated at ₹14 thousand 600 crore with assistance from the Japan Bank for International Cooperation.
- Hogenakkal Combined Drinking Water Project estimated at ₹1,330 crore.
- Ramanathapuram–Paramakudi Combined Drinking Water Project completed and inaugurated at a cost of ₹630 crore.
- Elevated-road project from Maduravoyal to Chennai Port at an estimated ₹1,655 crore.
- Compulsory Religious Conversion Prohibition Act repealed to protect religious freedom.
- Third Police Commission constituted under senior IAS officer R. Poornalingam; 278 of its 444 recommendations accepted so far.
- Time-scale pay and pension for 2 lakh 12 thousand 981 nutritious-meal workers.
- TESMA and ESMA repealed; benefits taken away from government employees and teachers restored; as interim relief while implementing the Sixth Pay Commission report, three months’ salary arrears of ₹4,247 crore paid.
- From 1.1.2006, Sixth Pay Commission recommendations implemented in Tamil Nadu with an additional annual expenditure of ₹5 thousand 155 crore 79 lakh.
- As a result, ₹11 thousand 93 crore sanctioned as arrears to government employees and teachers, creating what the source calls a new history.

I have listed only some of the achievements.

Every person in Tamil Nadu benefits from one or another of these achievements. In the name of these achievements — and as the head of a government that has created such a record of accomplishment — I ask for the votes of all the people and dear mothers, with the right of one who is one among the Udanpirappugal of their own families and with the aim of working further for their social advancement. Dear Udanpirappē, through you I ask you to tell them this.

Work with the confidence that victory in this election will be a victory for our feeling of community and our commitment to our ideals. I wait in the belief that the great people who come to vote in these by-elections will fulfil this confidence of mine with cries of greeting.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**13-12-2009**
