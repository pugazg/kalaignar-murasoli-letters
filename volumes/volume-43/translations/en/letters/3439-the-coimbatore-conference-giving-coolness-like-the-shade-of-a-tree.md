# 3439. The Coimbatore Conference — Giving Coolness, Like the Shade of a Tree!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation preserves the letter’s World Classical Tamil Conference planning, Anna’s long 1968 speech, Tamil literary quotations, committee appointments, international scholar list, correspondence extracts, Tamil Internet Conference material and the title’s cooling-tree-shade image. Institutional and historical claims remain source-framed.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3439](../../../chapters/3439-kulir-tharuvena-tharu-nizhalena-kovaiyil-maanaadu.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 104](../../../pages/page-104.md)–[PDF 111](../../../pages/page-111.md)  
**Date:** 5 December 2009

**Udanpirappē,**

It has already been announced that the World Classical Tamil Conference will begin in Coimbatore toward the end of June, from the 23rd to the 27th, and the work for it is proceeding systematically. Only six months remain. If our attention is taken up by the by-election work, the Legislative Assembly session, the opening of the Secretariat building and the Anna Centenary Library, the conference will arrive very quickly. We are engaged in efforts to conduct the World Classical Tamil Conference with distinction in every respect.

Before beginning today’s letter, I wish to recall what Perarignar Anna said at the opening ceremony of the Second World Tamil Conference held in Chennai in 1968:

> “From tomorrow, university professors who have come from different parts of the world are going to conduct research on Tamil at the World Tamil Conference. President Zakir Husain, a person of great culture and balanced outlook, a scholar and an innovator in education, has come to inaugurate this conference. Our Presidents have successively been learned persons associated with the field of culture. Dr Radhakrishnan, a learned intellectual, was President first. Now Zakir Husain, a scholar like him, has become President. It is a matter of pride for us that such a person inaugurates the World Tamil Conference.
>
> “The World Tamil Conference was first held in Malaysia. It is now being held in the sacred city of Chennai. Kamaraj, who spoke here, said that we ought to have conducted the very first conference, but for some reason they got ahead of us. Even if the elder brother is sometimes a little tired, the younger brother acts with great concern and gets the work done; in that fashion they conducted the first conference. But this conference is a continuation of that one, not something new.
>
> “Representatives of 20 countries attended that conference. Now representatives have come from 30 countries, including Russia. Tamil has become known in all these countries. But within India it has not been properly made known.
>
> “People of Russia and Czechoslovakia have recognised the greatness of the Tamil language. But here it has not been properly recognised. When we learn that Tamil is being researched at a university in England, and when we say that we are the owners of such a Tamil, our thoughts travel somewhere. But when we think of the position we are in today, our thoughts return to our present condition. I said, ‘Our thoughts travel somewhere’; we will certainly reach that exalted place. When Tamils understand their Tamil culture, understand their history, understand their strength and work in unity, we can certainly reach that place.
>
> “We Tamils respect every language! Wherever in the world knowledge exists, we accept it! From whichever corner of the world people may come, we respect their knowledge!
>
> “But Tamils will never agree to lose what is their own. People from across the whole world have come here to say, ‘What they possess is excellent’; when they say so, no one can imagine that Tamils will fail to cherish it!
>
> “When a Norwegian, an Englishman, an American or an Australian comes here and says, ‘What a wonderful language is the Tamil language you possess,’ we rejoice in thinking, ‘We are the owners of that language!’
>
> “If, at any stage, for any purpose, whoever it may be refuses to give that language the respect it deserves, the Tamil people will not tolerate it! If the deep sea is raging, it can be crossed by a ship. In the same way, if Tamils are embraced and carried along, they will be friends to everyone.
>
> “They have also sung, ‘Good and evil do not come from others’; whether it is evil or good, it is something we ourselves seek, not something others give us! If we do not want any evil, it will not come! This good principle was shown to the Tamil people two thousand years ago!
>
> “In 1967, even though the flags of many countries are flown together at the United Nations and unity is spoken of in order to unite all nations, once people come outside after speaking, the fear arises: ‘Which country will invade which country, and when?’
>
> “But when we think how elevated the culture of the Tamil was who, two thousand years ago, declared ‘Every place is our town; everyone is our kin,’ the heart swells! The poet who sang ‘Every place is our town; everyone is our kin’ sang in the very next line that good and evil do not come from others. Do we want good to come? We ourselves must bring it about. Do we want evil to come? We ourselves must invite it. Therefore do not fear that evil may come. It comes only if we ourselves seek it. Good and evil are not given by others!
>
> “Research is being conducted on the Tamil language. Through the seminar of this conference, by making many people know the distinctions of that Tamil language and allowing others to see its richness and excellence, I have great confidence that a time will come when others too will desire Tamil and everyone will accept it, saying, ‘Is this not the great language? Is this not our language? Is this not the link language? Is this not the language of government?’
>
> “This will not happen through haste or anger! Just as one casts a fishing line and waits until the fish bites, we must learn to wait until that stage arrives! As Tamil literature teaches, we must act knowing the time, place and circumstance!
>
> “Let us resolve that, just as we have gained the distinction of seeing our Tamil receive its rightful elevation, the people of India too shall gain it and see the good day when they place it upon the throne! Let us greet and welcome the scholars and researchers who have come here to give us the ideas needed for that!”

As that bronze voice rang out, when Perarignar Anna concluded his conch-call in the presidential address of the World Tamil Conference on the Chennai seafront, more than three lakh Tamil hearts gathered there rose to their feet and applauded.

Carrying this opening address of Anna in my heart, I have personally visited the places where the conference and seminars we are now going to conduct in Coimbatore will be held, and I have returned with satisfaction. Suitable places have been found for the conference, for the separate seminars and for the procession. In particular, the Coimbatore “CODISSIA” hall where the conference is to be held is very grand. Under the leadership of the District Collector, officials are working on arrangements to accommodate the guests who will gather from countries around the world, from other States of India and from every district of Tamil Nadu.

For the research-seminar work connected with the World Classical Tamil Conference, an organising committee has been ordered to be formed with Professor Dr K. Sivathamby as chairman; Dr Avvai Natarajan and Dr Porko, also known as Pon. Kothandaraman, as co-chairmen; Rajya Sabha Member and poet Kanimozhi as secretary; and Dr M. Rajendran, Vice-Chancellor of Thanjavur Tamil University, as coordinator.

The Tamil scholar Professor Karthigesu Sivathamby is Head of the Faculty of Fine Arts at the University of Jaffna. I am duty-bound to request that under his leadership this committee function with distinction, and that Tamil scholars from around the world come well in advance and help conduct this conference splendidly.

I myself wrote to the leaders of all parties represented in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly asking them to participate in the World Classical Tamil Conference. The letters in which some party leaders agreed to come and a few declined have already appeared in the press.

The longing that, after 1995, for the past fourteen years no conference for Tamil has been conducted anywhere in the world is going to be answered through this conference. A special emblem — a logo — has been created for the World Classical Tamil Conference and released. Distinguished Tamil scholars have praised it as beautiful and full of meaning.

Just as the statue of Ayyan Valluvar occupies the central place in the emblem, Ayyan Valluvar’s words `பிறப்பொக்கும் எல்லா உயிர்க்கும்` — “All living beings are equal by birth” — have been chosen as the conference motto and included in the emblem.

Already, letters consenting to participate in the conference have come from 47 scholars. The long list includes George Hart from the University of California in the United States; Asha from England; Alexander Ruduvyaski from Russia; Vacek from Czechoslovakia; Thomas Malten from Germany; Arangavasu from the United States; S. Sreekandarasa from Australia; and Andiyappan, president of the Tamil Writers’ Association in Singapore.

Although many letters have come from Tamils living abroad, I think one or two may be included in this letter. S. Sreekandarasa, General Secretary of the Australian Tamil Research Forum, wrote to me through brother Suba. Veerapandian:

> “From the continent of Australia, where thousands among the lakhs upon lakhs of Tamil people who developed attachment to Tamil by listening to your speeches and learned to write the Tamil language by reading your writings live, we send you this letter with love and humility. We have learned the news of the Classical Language Conference that you, who live as Tamil itself for the Tamil people and the Tamil language, have decided to conduct next June, and we are very happy.
>
> “People like us who migrated from Sri Lanka and live in this country, together with Tamils whose motherland is Tamil Nadu, conduct an organisation called the Australian Tamil Research Forum. Many Tamil scholars, enthusiasts and professors from different fields are members. Some among us are eager to come as speakers at the Classical Language Conference you are going to conduct, to submit research papers and to participate as observers. We therefore request with love and humility that such opportunities be given to us.”

On behalf of the Singapore Tamil Writers’ Association, its president N. Andiyappan wrote on 26-10-2009:

> “Welcoming the announcement that the World Classical Tamil Conference will be held in Coimbatore, our Executive Committee meeting held on 19-10-2009 passed a resolution thanking you. I humbly request that the Singapore Tamil Writers’ Association be appointed as the Singapore delegate body and liaison for the World Classical Tamil Conference. That will help us gather delegates from here and bring them to participate in the conference, and to maintain contact with the conference organisers.”

He has made that request. I believe the conference coordination committee will attend to it. So do I!

As part of the World Classical Tamil Conference, steps have also been taken to conduct a Tamil Internet Conference in association with the World Tamil Information Technology Forum. After the Tamil Nadu government conducted the World Tamil Internet seminar-conference titled “TamilNet 1999” in Chennai on 7 and 8 February 1999 through the efforts of brother Murasoli Maran, another opportunity has now arisen in connection with this conference. Tamil information-technology specialists and computer-Tamil scholars from various countries will participate in the seminars and research sessions connected with it.

To plan and implement the preparatory work required for the World Classical Tamil Conference, supervise it and coordinate the work of various departments, a conference coordination committee has been constituted under Tamil Nadu Chief Secretary Mr K.S. Sripathi, I.A.S., and its first meeting has also taken place.

Senior I.A.S. officer Mr K. Alauddin has been appointed to coordinate all conference work as a whole and carry forward the necessary action.

As an enduring memorial to this conference, it has also been decided to create a world-class, grand botanical garden in the central part of Coimbatore city and to beautify it with plants, flowering plants, creepers and trees.

Work is also underway to constitute several more committees in connection with the conference. Announcements concerning three more committees have appeared in the newspapers today. I will continue to write from time to time about all these matters. One thing alone is certain: the Coimbatore conference, which is to shine with unfading fame in every respect, will be for Tamil enthusiasts and scholars throughout the world like something that gives coolness, like the shade given by a tree!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**5-12-2009**

ஆனால் தமிழர்கள் தமக்கு என்று இருப்பதை இழக்க
