# 3739. Two Unforgettable Gems! Kalaignar’s Condolence Letter!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 21 April 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

Last month, the General Council meeting of the Labour Progressive Federation was held on 23 March 2013 at Kalaignar Arangam in Anna Arivalayam. Around that meeting, when Federation president C. Kuppusamy, Shanmugam, Rethinasabapathy and other trade-union leaders met me to obtain a date for another function and asked me, I agreed immediately. I also took part in the special meeting of the General Council. The figure who sat beside me then—C. Kuppusamy—is, they say, no longer with us today! When the news was conveyed to me last night, I was shaken. How many, how many memories I have of him.

In those days it was very difficult to obtain a job in the Railways under the Union Government. Yet Kuppusamy was serving as a station master in the Central Railway. Out of his attachment to the DMK, he gave up that government job. At a time when no one could have expected that he would later become president of the Labour Progressive Federation or a Member of Parliament, he came to hold those responsibilities. I knew him well from the days when he was a station master. Whenever a DMK meeting was held in Panruti, Kuppusamy would bring food from his own home for the special speakers participating in it.

When I spoke recently at the Labour Progressive Federation General Council, never imagining that Kuppusamy would leave us so soon, I spoke at length about him at the beginning of the meeting. Even while he served as a station master under the Union Government, and afterwards, he was committed within the Dravidian movement to Thanthai Periyar's principles; he remained attached to and affectionate toward Arignar Anna, and along the same path worked with affection toward me as well. There could have been only a year or two of difference between his age and mine.

When the DMK headquarters functioned at the Omandurar complex, Kuppusamy was one of the headquarters secretaries. Perhaps because he had served as a government officer, he would arrive at the office by nine every morning. The place where he sat and the place where we sat were a long distance apart. Yet he would walk back and forth between the two perhaps twenty times a day. However sharply I might scold him over official matters, the very next moment he would patiently answer as though nothing at all had happened.

Kuppusamy faced so many trials in his life—in his personal life, public life and political life. He turned all those trials into achievements and, without even the slightest trace of what people call concern or anxiety about them, continued carrying out his work.

Even within the trade-union front that he himself built in this movement and in which he continued to labour and serve to this day, differences of view and disagreements about him would sometimes arise. When I think about how he handled them, how he endured them, and how he continued working until now without giving them importance, I think: was he not my comrade? Was he not my friend? So when I weigh whether I should worry about my own trials or about the trials faced by people like Kuppusamy, there is no room even for the question of whether that trial is greater or this one. He too regarded both as the common trials that arise for those who work in this movement, and he protected the movement.

Comrade C. Kuppusamy served continuously as president of the Labour Progressive Federation from 1977. Though the Federation had functioned as a central trade union from 1970, it had been unable to obtain recognition from the Union Government as a central trade-union organisation. Through the sustained efforts made by Kuppusamy, that “recognition” was secured for the Federation in 2008. It was because of this that the Federation obtained opportunities to participate in the Indian Labour Standing Committee, the Indian Labour Conference and international labour conferences.

It is painful to hear that dear Udanpirappu Kuppusamy—who did invaluable work in enabling the Federation to establish affiliated unions in 13 States and to raise its membership to more than 15 lakh—has left us. It makes the tears in our eyes overflow!

Labour is the noblest capital of the world. Labour is not a commodity for sale. The person who gives labour is a human being; the person who receives it is also a human being. All human beings stand on an equal plane. No one is inferior to anyone else. Kuppusamy was a man who worked throughout his life with these lofty ideas and instilled them in others too.

Because the North Chennai parliamentary constituency was a constituency filled with workers, Kuppusamy was fielded there as the DMK candidate and won a great victory. The next time, when his health made it necessary to leave that constituency to someone else, he accepted it magnanimously and without the slightest bitterness, and took part even in the introduction meeting for the DMK's North Chennai parliamentary candidate. Every task given to him by the DMK was carried out for the growth of the DMK. Until the day he died, he laboured with “DMK, DMK, DMK” as his constant concern. The late friend C. Kuppusamy was an unforgettable great human being. Though he has passed away, as long as the DMK exists and as long as the Labour Progressive Federation exists, the memories of C. Kuppusamy will endure. Long live Kuppusamy.

★ ★ ★

Next, as people say, “sorrow comes with company.” Another piece of painful news that reached us yesterday was that Dr. B. Sivanthi Adithan, proprietor of the *Dina Thanthi* daily and former president of the Indian Olympic Association, passed away at about 10 o'clock last night.)

Friend Sivanthi Adithan was the younger son of S.P. Adithanar, who founded the “Naam Tamilar” movement in Tamil Nadu and laboured greatly for Tamil and Tamils; who created among ordinary people in Tamil Nadu the desire to read newspapers and learn the news of the country; who served as Speaker of the Assembly when Perarignar Anna formed the government in Tamil Nadu in 1967; and who, after Anna's death, served with distinction as Minister for Cooperation and Agriculture in the Cabinet headed by me in 1969. Sivanthi was one of the dearest friends life gave me.

To say more: whether I was in office or out of office, once every fifteen days he would meet me, exchange views about the political situation in Tamil Nadu and in India, and speak intimately with me about his personal life and family. When I learnt that for the past few weeks his health had deteriorated and that he had been admitted to hospital in an unconscious state, I contacted the well-known doctor Devaraj whom I knew and asked him to see Sivanthi immediately and tell me whether I could come and visit. I also sent DMK Treasurer Thambi Thalapathi Stalin in person to see him. But I was told that Sivanthi remained unconscious and that no one was being permitted to see him, and so I could not go to the hospital and see him in person.

I had hoped that somehow his health would improve to some extent, that he would regain consciousness and that I could go and see him. In that state of mind, the news of his death came and shocked me.

To his dear son, my beloved younger brother Balasubramaniam, who grieves at this loss; to his family and friends; to the dear comrades working in his institutions including *Dina Thanthi*; and especially to the respected members of the community who placed such great faith in him, I convey my deepest condolences.

My tearful tribute to the passing of these two unforgettable gems!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**21 April 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. `தொ.மு.ச. பேரவை` is rendered **Labour Progressive Federation**, with the source's trade-union identity preserved rather than expanded into commentary.
2. The source-visible closing phrase on PDF 241 is `கண்ணீர் அஞ்சலி!`—**tearful tribute**—as restored by the completed textual-fidelity audit.
3. The closing parenthesis after the first Sivanthi Adithan death sentence is visibly present in the source and is preserved in the authoritative Tamil rather than silently corrected.
4. `நாம் தமிழர் இயக்கம்` is retained as **“Naam Tamilar” movement** so the translation does not silently identify it with a later organisation.
