# 3704. Who Took Proper Action—Me or Her?

## English translation

Udanpirappē,

I have already given detailed explanations regarding the relief for delta farmers, the Eelam Tamil issue and power generation referred to by Chief Minister Jayalalithaa in her reply to the Governor's Address on 8 February 2013. The remaining part of her speech concerns the Cauvery river-water dispute. Instead of explaining why, during her nineteen months in office, she had been unable to secure from Karnataka the water required by the people of Tamil Nadu, she followed her usual practice of making allegation after allegation against me in an attempt to escape responsibility. She said Karunanidhi took no action either to publish the final award of the Cauvery Tribunal in the Central Gazette or to obtain Cauvery water. She periodically makes this accusation, and each time I explain the facts; it has become a continuing story.

In the Assembly Jayalalithaa spoke as though the interim award of the Cauvery Tribunal had been obtained through her own unceasing efforts. But it was during DMK rule that, as early as 17 February 1970, we first wrote to the Union Government asking that a Cauvery Tribunal be constituted. It was under a DMK Government that the Assembly passed a resolution on 8 July 1971 demanding such a Tribunal.

Again, while the DMK was in office, an all-party leaders' meeting was held on 27 July 1989 and unanimously resolved to ask the Union Government to constitute the Tribunal. Every party attended except the AIADMK led by Jayalalithaa. Her degree of interest in securing a Tribunal can be understood from the fact that her party boycotted that all-party meeting.

After V.P. Singh became Prime Minister on 2 December 1989, I wrote asking him to constitute the Tribunal. He asked me to send a resolution from the Assembly, and accordingly on 24 April 1990 we again passed a resolution declaring that a Tribunal was necessary. As a result, the Tribunal was constituted on 2 June 1990—during DMK rule, when I was Chief Minister.

On 28 July 1990, the DMK Government petitioned the Tribunal for an interim award. On 5 January 1991, the Tribunal said it had no power to grant one. On 10 January 1991, while the DMK Government was still in office, we went to the Supreme Court seeking a ruling on whether the Tribunal possessed that power. The Supreme Court ruled on 26 April 1991 that it did. It was because of that judgment secured through action initiated by the DMK Government that the Tribunal delivered its interim award on 25 June 1991. In between, on 31 January 1991, the DMK Government had been dismissed and Jayalalithaa came to power. Though the interim award was then published in the Central Gazette during her rule, it produced no effective result. From 1991 to 1996, her Government could not implement it.

When the DMK returned to power in 1996, to prevent delay in implementing the Tribunal's order, I repeatedly asked that the implementation scheme be finalised and Gazetted. I wrote to the Prime Minister on 19 July, 23 July, 29 September, 1 November and 6 November 1997, and met him personally on 27 July and 29 September 1997. On 10 November 1997, the Tamil Nadu Government also filed a petition in the Supreme Court seeking an order directing the Union Government to act. Again I wrote on 28 March, 6 April and 31 May 1998 and met the Prime Minister personally on 7 April, pressing for the draft scheme to be finalised and published.

On 21 July 1998, the Supreme Court observed that fifteen months had passed since the Union Government assured it that the dispute would be settled amicably, yet no agreed scheme had emerged, and that this situation could not be allowed to continue indefinitely.

On 28 July 1998, Prime Minister Vajpayee invited me to a meeting at the Prime Minister's Office on 6 August to discuss Cauvery. On 3 August, I convened an all-party meeting in Tamil Nadu to decide whether I should attend. The meeting decided that I should. Jayalalithaa, however, issued a statement saying I should not go.

Only at the end of the two days of meetings conducted by Prime Minister Vajpayee did an agreement emerge, on 7 August 1998 during DMK rule, for implementation of the interim award. It provided that the 205 TMC specified in the interim order should be released and that a Cauvery Authority comprising the Chief Ministers of the four Cauvery-basin States would function under the chairmanship of the Prime Minister.

That agreement was welcomed by leaders including C. Subramaniam, G.K. Moopanar, Sankaraiah, R. Nallakannu, Abdul Samad, M.A. Latheef, Kumari Ananthan, Tindivanam Ramamurthy, R.M. Veerappan, G.A. Vadivelu, L. Ganesan, S. Thirunavukkarasu and Bishop Ezra Sargunam. Newspapers such as *The Hindu*, *Indian Express*, *Dinamani*, *Viduthalai*, *Theekkathir*, *The Times of India* and *The Hindustan Times*, and magazines including *Kalki*, *Kumudam*, *Saavi*, *Thuglak*, *Ananda Vikatan* and *Kungumam*, also praised it. That is the actual history.

For example, *The Hindustan Times*, under the headline “Karunanidhi Scores Diplomatic Victory,” described the outcome of the Cauvery talks as a major political victory for Tamil Nadu Chief Minister M. Karunanidhi. It said Karnataka's agreement to implement the interim award was itself a major achievement; that Karnataka Governments had resisted the 205-TMC interim award from the time of the Tribunal's decision; that Karunanidhi had secured Karnataka's acceptance of it and thereby ensured that 205 TMC would form the baseline when the Tribunal eventually gave its final award; that he had broken the AIADMK and its allies' position that there should be no negotiations with Karnataka; and that he had demonstrated political maturity by keeping the door open to talks and by placing the Prime Minister at the head of the Authority, thereby creating a Union-level guarantee for implementation.

When all-party leaders and newspapers welcomed this historic agreement, did Jayalalithaa welcome it? No. She said the solution was rejected and would not be accepted.

The person who enabled that settlement was BJP Prime Minister Vajpayee, who at the time was allied with the AIADMK. Yet he invited me, the leader of the DMK which was not part of that alliance, and arrived at an amicable solution. What did the AIADMK leader, whose party was then represented in the Union Government, tell reporters? She said: “On the Cauvery issue, Vajpayee has fallen victim to Karunanidhi's strategy. We reject the agreement the two have made together.”

Jayalalithaa always seems to assume that people will have forgotten earlier events. Concealing all this, she now praises herself in the Assembly as though it was through her relentless efforts that the interim award was resolved and the Cauvery River Authority and Cauvery Monitoring Committee were established.

The final award of the Cauvery Tribunal was issued on 5 February 2007 during DMK rule. It was welcomed at the time by Tamil Nadu Congress president M. Krishnasamy, Dravidar Kazhagam leader K. Veeramani, VCK general secretary Thol. Thirumavalavan, CPM State secretary N. Varadarajan, CPI Tamil Nadu secretary D. Pandian, Cauvery Delta Irrigation Farmers Association general secretary Renganathan and K.S. Natarajan of the Uzhavar Uzhaippalar Katchi.

Jayalalithaa's AIADMK statement, however, called the final award “unacceptable” and said Karunanidhi had failed to obtain through the Tribunal the justice Tamil Nadu was entitled to, that the award was harmful to Tamil Nadu, and that the DMK Government should accept moral responsibility and resign. As usual, she demanded my resignation.

It is the same Jayalalithaa who now claims that she alone is struggling to have the Tribunal's final award published in the Central Gazette and says the DMK made no effort. She goes so far as to allege that DMK MPs did not ask the Prime Minister for Gazette publication but instead obstructed it, thereby casting aspersions even on the Prime Minister's Office.

Even when the final award came, the DMK Government did not decide unilaterally as Jayalalithaa does. We convened all-party leaders on 19 February and 15 April 2007 before deciding our course. The present Chief Minister, by contrast, does not convene such all-party meetings in keeping with democratic tradition, but acts as though whatever she thinks is itself the decision.

The truth, therefore, is that Jayalalithaa did not accept either the Tribunal's interim award or its final award. Did she at least accept the Cauvery River Authority?

The Cauvery River Authority (CauveryAuthority) was constituted and notified on 11 August 1998. In a statement dated 21 August, Jayalalithaa said that an organisation deserved to be called an Authority only if it had adequate powers to issue and implement orders; that the Authority chaired by the Prime Minister possessed no such power over Cauvery; that it was merely a debating body incapable of resolving the dispute or taking decisions; that Karnataka would not suffer but Tamil Nadu would.

On 2 April 2002, Chief Minister Jayalalithaa told the Assembly that the Cauvery Authority was “non-functioning,” a “toothless authority,” and that the AIADMK had left the BJP alliance because it refused to accept it.

At a Cabinet meeting chaired by Jayalalithaa on 21 June 2002, it was declared that the Cauvery River Authority had no effective power and could not even minimally implement the Tribunal's award, and that Tamil Nadu would therefore no longer participate in its meetings. When asked about this decision, I said boycotting the Authority was not wise; that all parties should act together to obtain Union intervention and assistance for water needed for the kuruvai crop; that the AIADMK Government's dominant tendency was to reverse whatever had been decided during DMK rule; that DMK rule sought timely water for the Cauvery delta whereas the AIADMK declared it would not even attend Authority meetings.

In July 2002 Jayalalithaa said she would boycott the Authority; the next day she said she would see if invited. Even while I was out of office, on 5 July 2002 I wrote to Prime Minister Vajpayee asking him to convene the Cauvery River Authority immediately. I reminded him that the Authority under his chairmanship, comprising the Chief Ministers of Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Kerala and Pondicherry, had been formed to monitor Karnataka's release of 205 TMC under the interim award; that delta farmers were suffering because water was not being released; that I thanked him for asking Karnataka to release 3 TMC to Tamil Nadu to alleviate the distress; that Karnataka had rejected even that request; and that the Authority should therefore be convened quickly to enforce the Tribunal's order.

In his reply dated 10 July 2002, the Prime Minister said the matter would be considered. The Cauvery River Authority was accordingly convened.

When Jayalalithaa attended the Authority meeting in Delhi with the Prime Minister on 27 August 2002, instead of calmly discussing ways to obtain water she allowed her characteristic anger to overcome the urgency of the issue, announced a walkout midway and left. Another Authority meeting was scheduled for 29 November 2002. Karnataka's Chief Minister went to Delhi the previous day. Jayalalithaa said until the last moment that she would attend, then cancelled the Delhi trip altogether. Who lost? Tamil Nadu's farmers.

Meanwhile, Supreme Court judges told the Tamil Nadu Government's counsel that Jayalalithaa's letter to the Prime Minister saying she had no confidence in the Cauvery River Authority chaired by Vajpayee was highly objectionable and should be withdrawn within four days. State counsel K.K. Venugopal told the judges that Tamil Nadu had full confidence in both the Prime Minister and the Authority and that Jayalalithaa would soon write again expressing that confidence and withdrawing the earlier communication.

Accordingly, in a letter to the Prime Minister on 16 November 2002, Jayalalithaa wrote that she had great personal regard for the Prime Minister and, forgetting everything she had earlier said, that she had confidence in the Cauvery Authority too.

It is perhaps because she believes no one will remember all these historical facts that Chief Minister Jayalalithaa now proclaims in the Assembly that Karunanidhi did nothing on the Cauvery issue—like trying to hide a whole pumpkin in a plate of rice.

People of Tamil Nadu! I have written what happened openly, with a clear conscience and without concealment. You yourselves say: on the Cauvery issue, did Karunanidhi take proper action—or did he not?

With affection,
M.K.
12-02-2013

தி.மு.கழக ஆட்சியில் இடைக்காலத் தீர்ப்பை நிறைவேற்று

இடைக்கால ஆணையில் கூறப்பட்ட 205 டி.எம்.சி. தண்ணீர்
விடுவிக்கப்பட வேண்டும் என்றும், பிரதமர் தலைமையில் காவிரி
பாயும் நான்கு மாநில முதல்வர்கள் அடங்கிய காவிரி ஆணையம்
அமைக்கப்படும் என்றும் முடிவாயிற்று. அந்த ஒப்பந்தத்தை சி.
சுப்பிரமணியம், ஜி.கே. மூப்பனார், சங்கரய்யா, ஆர். நல்லகண்ணு
, அப்துல் சமது, எம்.ஏ. லத்தீப், குமரி அனந்தன், திண்டிவனம்

மற்றும் ஏடுகள் இந்து, எக்ஸ்பிரஸ், தினமணி, விடுதலை, தீக்கதிர்,

நாளேடுகளும் கல்கி, குமுதம், சாவி, துக்ளக், ஆனந்தவிகடன்,
குங்குமம், ஆகிய இதழ்களும் பாராட்டின. இதுதான் உண்மையில்
நடந்த வரலாறு.

உதாரணத்திற்கு ஒன்றைக் குறிப்பிட
வேண்டுமென்றால், “தி இந்துஸ்தான் டைம்ஸ்” ஏடு,
“Karunanidhi Scores Diplomatic Victory” என்ற தலைப்பில்,
“காவிரிப் பிரச்சினை பற்றிய பேச்சு வார்த்தையின்
முடிவு தமிழக முதலமைச்சர் மு.கருணாநிதி
அவர்களுக்குக் கிடைத்துள்ள மிகப் பெரிய அரசியல்
வெற்றியாகும். நடுவர் மன்றத்தின் இடைக்காலத்
தீர்ப்பை அமல்படுத்த கர்நாடகம் ஒத்துக் கொண்டதே
மிகப் பெரிய வெற்றியாகும். ஜூன் 1990 முதல்,
அதாவது நடுவர் மன்றம் 205 டி.எம்.சி. தண்ணீரைத்

தீர்ப்பை ஒத்துக் கொள்ளவில்லை. கர்நாடகத்தை
இடைக்காலத் தீர்ப்பை ஏற்றுக் கொள்ளச் செய்து,
நடுவர் மன்றம் தமிழகத்திற்கு வழங்கிட வேண்டிய
தண்ணீரின் அளவு பற்றிய இறுதித் தீர்ப்பை
வெளியிடும் போது 205 டி.எம்.சி. தண்ணீர்
என்பது அடிப்படை அளவுகோலாக இருக்கும்
என்பதையும் திரு. கருணாநிதி உறுதி செய்து
கொண்டுள்ளார். அ.தி.மு.க.வும் அதன் தோழமைக்

விட்டார். அதன் மூலம் பிரச்சினைகளை வழக்குகளின்

நிரூபித்துக் காட்டி விட்டார். பேச்சுவார்த்தைக்குக்
கதவடைக்க மறுத்ததன் மூலம், திரு. கருணாநிதி
தன்னுடைய மிகப் பெரிய அரசியல் முதிர்ச்சியை
வெளிக்காட்டியுள்ளார். ஆணையத்திற்குப் பிரதம
ரைத் தலைவராக அமைத்துக் கொண்டதன் மூலம்,
மத்திய அரசில் நடுவர் மன்றத் தீர்ப்பினை கர்நாடகம்

அது மாத்திரமல்ல; வரலாற்றுச் சிறப்பு மிக்க இந்த
ஒப்பந்தத்தை அனைத்துக் கட்சித் தலைவர்களும், ஏடுகளும்
பாராட்டியபோது ஜெயலலிதா அதனை வரவேற்றாரா என்றால்
இல்லை. மாறாக அந்தத் தீர்வினை நிராகரிக்கிறோம் என்றும்,
அதனை ஏற்க மாட்டோம் என்றும் கூறினார்.

அந்த முடிவு காண வழி வகுத்தவர், பா.ஜ.க. பிரதமராக
இருந்த திரு.வாஜ்பாய். அப்போது அவர் அ.தி.மு.க.வுடன்
கூட்டணியில் இருந்தவர். இருந்தாலும், அந்தக் கூட்டணியிலே
இல்லாத தி.மு.க.வின் தலைவனான என்னை அழைத்து சுமுக
முடிவு கண்டார். ஆனால் மத்திய அரசில் இடம் பெற்ற அ.தி.மு.க.
தலைவி அந்த ஒப்பந்தம் பற்றி பத்திரிகையாளர்களிடம் என்ன
கூறினார் தெரியுமா?

“காவிரி பிரச்சினையில் வாஜ்பாய், கருணாநிதியின்
தந்திரத்திற்கு ஆளாகி விட்டார். இரண்டு பேரும் சேர்ந்து
செய்து கொண்டிருக்கின்ற உடன்பாட்டை நாங்கள்
நிராகரிக்கிறோம்” என்றார்.

மக்கள் கடந்த கால நிகழ்வுகளை மறந்திருப்பார்கள் என்று

ஆட்சியிலேதான் 5-2-2007 அன்று வெளியிடப்பட்டது. இந்தத்

எம். கிருஷ்ணசாமி, திராவிடர் கழகத் தலைவர், தமிழர் தலைவர்

உழைப்பாளர் கட்சியின் சார்பில் கே.எஸ். நடராசன் ஆகியோர்
அப்போது வரவேற்று அறிக்கை கொடுத்தனர்.

ஆனால் அ.தி.மு.க.வின் சார்பில் ஜெயலலிதா விடுத்த

முதலமைச்சர் ஜெயலலிதா எழுதிய கடிதத்தில், “பிரதமர் மீது
தனிப்பட்ட முறையில் தான் மிகுந்த மதிப்பு வைத்திருப்பதாகவும்,
ஏற்கனவே தான் சொன்ன அத்தனையையும் மறந்துவிட்டு காவேரி
ஆணையத்தின் மீதும் நம்பிக்கை வைத்திருப்பதாகவும்” எழுதி
யிருந்தார்.

வரலாற்று ரீதியிலான இத்தனை உண்மை நிகழ்வுகளையும்
யார் நினைவிலே வைத்துக் கொண்டிருக்கப் போகிறார்கள்
என்ற நம்பிக்கையால்தான் முதலமைச்சர் ஜெயலலிதா
காவிரிப் பிரச்சினையில் கருணாநிதி எவ்வித நடவடிக்கையும்
எடுக்கவில்லை என்று முழுப் பூசணிக்காயை இலைச் சோற்றிலே
மறைப்பதைப்போல, பேரவையில் முழங்கியிருக்கிறார்.

தமிழ்நாட்டு மக்களே! நடந்தவைகளை மனசாட்சித்
தெளிவுடன் ஒளிவு மறைவில்லாமல் எழுதியிருக்கிறேன், நீங்களே
கூறுங்கள்; காவிரிப் பிரச்சினையில் கருணாநிதி முறையாக
நடவடிக்கை எடுத்தாரா? இல்லையா?
