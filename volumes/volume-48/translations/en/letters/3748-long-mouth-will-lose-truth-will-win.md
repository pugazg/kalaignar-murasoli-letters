# 3748. “Long Mouth” Will Lose; Truth Alone Will Triumph!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 5 May 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

For some reason, on 3 May 2013 the Chief Minister set aside Rule 110 in the Assembly. The Chief Minister herself moved a resolution urging the Union Government to take steps to retrieve Katchatheevu, spoke at length on that resolution herself, and had it adopted. When a resolution is to be brought on behalf of the government, if members are informed beforehand they can come prepared to speak on the subject. But the members were informed only the night before. The next day the Chief Minister came with a speech prepared for herself, gave the others in the House hardly any room to speak at length on the subject, and finished reading her own speech.

I ask the people of Tamil Nadu to look once at the speech the Chief Minister read in the Assembly. From beginning to end, it was an **archanai** directed at me! If Katchatheevu is to be recovered, let it be recovered. If the Union Government must act, let a request be made to it. But should there be an Assembly resolution merely to sing my “praises”(?)?

How did the Chief Minister suddenly remember Katchatheevu? It appears that the resolution adopted at the TESO meeting held at Anna Arivalayam on 15 April 2013 stirred Ammaiyar into bringing a Katchatheevu resolution in the Assembly!

The final part of the TESO resolution said that if any part of India is to be ceded to another country, a law must be enacted after consideration by Parliament under Article 368 of the Constitution of India. Since no such law was ever enacted in respect of Katchatheevu, the ceding of Katchatheevu to Sri Lanka through the agreement was constitutionally invalid. Therefore it was decided that TESO would approach the Supreme Court seeking cancellation of the 1974 agreement and a declaration that Katchatheevu is part of India.

That resolution said nothing about Jayalalithaa or her party. Then why, while passing her resolution in the Assembly, did Jayalalithaa need to spend so much time reciting a “purana” about me?

Whether or not I have worked during my lifetime for Sri Lankan Tamils and for Tamil Nadu fishermen is known to them and to the people of Tamil Nadu. Did I ask the Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu for a certificate about it?

Was it I who, like Ammaiyar Jayalalithaa, swore in 1991 that “I will definitely retrieve Katchatheevu”? Twenty-two years have passed since she took that vow. Has she accomplished it? Who said on 15 August 1991, “This government is prepared to argue with the Union Government and, if necessary, to struggle to retrieve Katchatheevu”—was it I, or Jayalalithaa? After issuing such a challenge, is it fair now to look at me and accuse me of not having fought and of not having lifted even a straw?

On 25 December 1990, when then Prime Minister Chandra Shekhar came to meet the Shankaracharya, Jayalalithaa invited him to her own house and gave him a tea reception. She then went to Delhi and personally submitted petitions to the Prime Minister and to then President R. Venkataraman alleging that the DMK government was acting in support of the Liberation Tigers and asking that the government be dismissed. In the same way, how many times did Jayalalithaa go to Delhi over the Katchatheevu issue and meet the Prime Minister and the President? Can she list what protests she conducted and on what dates?

Speaking in this very Legislative Assembly on 20 April 1992, Jayalalithaa said: “We passed a resolution here seeking the retrieval of Katchatheevu and sent it to the Union Government. But retrieving Katchatheevu does not appear to be something that can happen quickly or easily.” Having herself said in the Assembly that “retrieving Katchatheevu does not appear to be something that can happen quickly,” what a spectacle it is for her now to ask why I did not retrieve it and what exactly I was retrieving instead!

Jayalalithaa further said on that occasion that fishermen cross the international boundary and fish in the waters near Katchatheevu because that is where they obtain sufficient quantities of better-quality fish; that within Indian waters they do not get the quantity and quality they need; and that when they cross the international boundary, knowingly or unknowingly, the Sri Lankan Navy has on many occasions fired on their boats, seized the fish they had caught and burned their boats. Did she or did she not say all this in the Assembly?

The DMK never at any time agreed to hand over Katchatheevu, nor did it consent to such a handover. Not merely the DMK as a party: during DMK governments too, the Katchatheevu issue was repeatedly raised and requests were made to restore Katchatheevu. Nor did the DMK government fail to point out infringements of fishermen’s rights at Katchatheevu and ask that those injuries be remedied.

Let me cite one thing. On 30 September 1994, Jayalalithaa, then Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu, wrote to Prime Minister P.V. Narasimha Rao. In that letter she wrote: “The ceding of this tiny island to the Island Nation had been done by the Government of India in the interest of better bilateral relations”. In other words, as Chief Minister she herself stated that the Government of India had ceded the small island of Katchatheevu to the island nation of Sri Lanka in the interest of maintaining good relations between India and Sri Lanka. Did she write that or not?

In her speech, Chief Minister Jayalalithaa asked why Karunanidhi took no immediate action even though he knew, before the agreement was signed, that India was going to give away Katchatheevu.

Immediately after the agreement was signed on 26/28 June 1974, I convened an all-party leaders’ meeting at the Secretariat in Chennai on 29 June 1974. Ponnappa Nadar, A.R. Marimuthu, Arumugasamy, Tiruppur Moideen, Abdul Wahab, Aranganayagam, Venkatasamy, G. Saminathan, E.S. Thyagarajan, A.R. Perumal, Sakthi Mohan, Ma.Po.Si. and A.R. Damodaran participated, and we adopted a resolution on Katchatheevu. The AIADMK, however, refused even then to sign in support of that resolution.

On that same 29th, I wrote this letter to the Prime Minister:

> Dear Prime Minister,
>
> On behalf of the Government of Tamil Nadu and on behalf
> of the people of Tamil Nadu, I am constrained to express our deep
> sense of disappointment over the recent Indo-Sri Lanka Agreement,
> according to which, Sri Lanka’s claim to Kachativu has been conceded
> by the Government of India. May I express the hope that you will take
> into consideration the decision contained in the Resolution and take
> appropriate action?

Is this the evidence that I “gave away” Katchatheevu? Jayalalithaa has said that I “did not even lift a straw”; here is evidence that the straw was indeed lifted!

Nor was that all. On 24 July 1974 the DMK held protest demonstrations across Tamil Nadu over the Katchatheevu issue. As Chief Minister I myself participated in meetings held in Thanjavur district. When the issue came before Parliament on 23 July 1974, friend Era. Sezhiyan, then a DMK member, said: “We will not accept the ceding to Sri Lanka of Katchatheevu, which belongs to Tamil Nadu.” In the Rajya Sabha, DMK member friend S.S. Marisamy said: “The Union Government’s unilateral conclusion of this agreement without consulting the Tamil Nadu government is an anti-democratic approach.”

At the Legislative Assembly session that followed, on 21 August 1974, I moved on behalf of the government a resolution which expressed the House’s deep regret at the Union Government’s decision on Katchatheevu—an island belonging to India and one over which Tamil Nadu had close rights—and urged the Union Government to reconsider its decision, make efforts to revise the agreement with Sri Lanka so that India’s sovereignty over Katchatheevu would remain, and respect the feelings of the people of Tamil Nadu.

But Jayalalithaa now calls that a resolution passed merely “for namesake.” If we pass a resolution, is it only “for namesake,” while only Ammaiyar’s resolution is passed “wholeheartedly”?

The next argument in Jayalalithaa’s long speech was this: “In 1974 Mr Karunanidhi says that he learned only from the newspapers that Katchatheevu had been ceded to Sri Lanka and that he was shocked. But in 2013 he says that even before the agreement was concluded, certain clauses were included only because he said so. What kind of fraud is this?” These were not the words of some ordinary member. The Chief Minister herself used the word `பித்தலாட்டம்`—“fraudulent trickery”—in the Assembly, and it entered the proceedings.

The TESO resolution of 15 April 2013 says: “When the 1974 agreement was concluded, the DMK government strongly opposed it from the very first stage. Even after that opposition, when the agreement was signed, the DMK government insisted that, at the minimum, clauses protecting Tamil Nadu fishermen’s right to fish in the Katchatheevu area and their right to dry their nets there should be included in the agreement; those clauses were accordingly included.”

Yet Jayalalithaa said in the Assembly yesterday: “He says that even before the agreement was concluded, certain clauses were inserted only because Karunanidhi said so; what kind of fraud is this?” The resolution says **“when the agreement was signed”**; it does not say “before the agreement was concluded.” From this, the people of Tamil Nadu must understand who committed the trickery and that the Chief Minister herself concealed the truth and supplied incorrect information in the Assembly.

Jayalalithaa next said that because of “Mr Karunanidhi’s inaction on the Katchatheevu issue, because of his double position, because of the deceitful role natural to him, the livelihood of Tamil Nadu fishermen has now been severely affected.” The people of Tamil Nadu must decide whether it is proper for a Chief Minister herself to attack me with such severity in the Legislative Assembly.

What is a double position? Jayalalithaa moved a resolution in the Assembly on 16 April 2002 demanding that Liberation Tigers leader Prabhakaran be arrested and brought to India, and now she passes resolutions in the Assembly in support of Sri Lankan Tamils. Is that not the double position?

When innocent Tamils were being killed in Sri Lanka, Jayalalithaa declared in the Assembly that “in war it is natural for innocent people to be killed.” What should that be called—a double position or a deceitful drama? Is it acceptable to speak in this manner merely because one happens somehow to be in office, thinks there is nobody to oppose one, believes one can say anything anywhere, and knows there are people ready to clap and nod after the speech is over?

Again and again Jayalalithaa speaks as though I knew, before the India–Sri Lanka agreement was signed, that the agreement was going to be concluded. Rather than my offering an explanation, consider what *Dinamani* itself wrote in an editorial as early as 17 August 1991: “In the 1974 agreement entered into by then Prime Minister Indira Gandhi with Mrs Bandaranaike, it was announced that Katchatheevu would be ceded in order to establish good relations between the two countries. Before this agreement was signed, the Union Government did not consult the people of Tamil Nadu.”

The same editorial wrote that “during the Emergency in India in 1976, through another agreement entered into with the Sri Lankan government without consulting the people, the traditional right of Indian fishermen to fish near Katchatheevu was also surrendered.” That itself shows that the claim that fishing rights were surrendered while the DMK was in office is an outright lie and a distortion.

Jayalalithaa asks: the DMK was in office from 1996 to 2001 and again from 2006 to 2011; why did Karunanidhi not lift even a straw to retrieve Katchatheevu then? If I ask in return: Jayalalithaa herself was in office from 1991 to 1996, from 2001 to 2006, and again from 2011—why did she not retrieve Katchatheevu? Where would she hide her face?

Jayalalithaa has also said that Mr M. Karunanidhi became Chief Minister in 2006 but still took no action to retrieve Katchatheevu.

Even after the DMK returned to office in 2006, I reminded Prime Minister Dr Manmohan Singh, in a letter dated 22 September 2006, of the 1991 Assembly resolution passed during AIADMK rule on the Katchatheevu issue. The source reproduces my letter as follows:

> “I am sure you are also aware
> of the fact that the Legislative Assembly of Tamil Nadu had passed a
> Resolution in 1991 on this issue wherein the Tamil Nadu Legislative
> Assembly called upon the Government of India to take all urgent
> action and efforts necessary to get back Katchathivu and restore it to
> the Indian territory apart from moving the Sri Lankan Government to
> pay compensation for families of fishermen killed by the Sri Lankan
> Navy as well as those who have lost their belongings in the attacks by
> them. Government of India was also requested to move the Sri Lankan
> Government to create “Congenial conditions for Tamil Nadu fishermen
> to lead a peaceful life” -

Christians and others travel every year to Katchatheevu to participate in the St Antony festival. After 1983 that festival itself had not been held. It was in 2010, during DMK rule when I was Chief Minister, that I raised the matter with the Union Government and the church festival was held again.

In a letter dated 23 July 2003 to then Prime Minister Vajpayee, Chief Minister Jayalalithaa wrote that the only way to maintain good relations between India and Sri Lanka and protect the traditional rights enjoyed by Tamil Nadu fishermen was this:

> “The best possible solution is to get the
> island of Katcha Theevu and adjacent seas on lease in perpetuity solely
> for fishing, drying of nets and pilgrimage. Sri Lanka’s Sovereignty
> over Katcha Theevu could be upheld at the same time.”

That is, she proposed that Katchatheevu and the adjoining sea area could be obtained on permanent lease for Tamil Nadu fishermen to fish, dry their nets and undertake pilgrimage, while at the same time Sri Lanka’s sovereignty over Katchatheevu could be accepted. The person who wrote all this then now fires questions at us—poor thing!

Jayalalithaa herself once said in a statement: “The Katchatheevu issue is an issue involving another country. The authority to retrieve it rests only with the Union Government. If the Chief Minister of a State had possessed that authority, Katchatheevu would have been recovered that very day.”

But hiding all this, acting as though she alone has taken an “avatar” for Sri Lankan Tamils and Tamil Nadu fishermen and as though nobody else has done anything, she may go to the Legislative Assembly and unleash a storm of words because she has the opportunity. But if she shows her **“long mouth”** by speaking about me in that fashion, she will lose in that too. We shall establish that **truth alone will triumph**.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**5 May 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. The title’s `வாய் நீளம்` is a deliberately mocking expression about excessive or insolent speech; it is kept close to the source as **“long mouth”** so that the contrast with `வாய்மையே வெல்லும்`—**“truth alone will triumph”**—remains visible.
2. The source’s English Katchatheevu passages and spellings (`Kachativu`, `Katchathivu`, `Katcha Theevu`) are retained exactly inside the quoted correspondence rather than harmonised.
3. `அர்ச்சனை`, `புராணம்`, `அவதாரம்`, `பித்தலாட்டம்` and similar religious/theatrical imagery are part of Kalaignar’s satire; the English preserves their rhetorical role without treating them as factual descriptions.
4. The two corrected initials on PDF 298—`ஈ.எஸ். தியாகராசன்` and `ம.பொ.சி.`—follow the scan-verified canonical Tamil.
