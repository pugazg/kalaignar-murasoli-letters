# 3454. Alas! On This Day, the Memory of That Day!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This memorial letter recalls C. N. Annadurai’s illness from September 1968, treatment in the United States, return to public life, relapse and death at 1:22 a.m. on 3 February 1969. The author’s personal recollections, quoted speeches, elegiac imagery and source punctuation are preserved as memoir testimony rather than externally reconstructed biography.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3454](../../../chapters/3454-ammavo-indha-naalil-andha-naal-ninaivu.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 235](../../../pages/page-235.md)–[PDF 240](../../../pages/page-240.md)  
**Date:** 3 February 2010

**Udanpirappē,**

Today is 3 February 2010!

My memory travels to the month of February 1969. Even before that, from the beginning of September 1968, Anna had been suffering frequent stomach pain.

There is no flower that never falls;  
there is no dew that never dries;  
in this puzzling life  
there is nothing that is never buried;  
there is no chain that never breaks;  
there is no breath that never ceases;  
is not death too a part of nature’s law?

But I often think how good it would be if death came easily and carried a person away. If one sheds tears throughout the days of life, should one not at least be able to close one’s eyes peacefully at death? Even now, when I think of the disease that tormented Anna, tears well in my eyes!

At first it was only stomach pain. Dr Rathinavel Subramaniam was attending to Anna and giving him medicines. At that time Annamalai University had announced that it was going to confer a doctorate on Anna. Anna was in severe pain, but he thought it was customary to go in person and receive the doctorate. The doctor said that travelling with such pain was not good. One day Anniyar called me and said, “However much I tell your elder brother, he stubbornly says he must go. He will listen only if you tell him.” I went straight to Anna.

“Anna! Please do not go to Annamalai Nagar now, Anna! They say the pain will increase if you travel. Someone else can go on your behalf and receive the degree,” I said.

“So that is what you say! Hmm,” Anna murmured, looking at me with a smile. A sigh of relief escaped me because Anna had accepted my request. Anna continued: “All right — but how can all of us stay in Chennai? Can I avoid touring and you too avoid touring? No! You immediately go on the tour for which you first gave dates and come back!”

Accepting his command, I went to Tenkasi constituency in Tirunelveli district and participated in the opening of the auditorium built at Courtallam.

On 3.9.1968 I named that auditorium after Kalaivanar and opened it. After participating in a few other programmes, I was travelling toward Karaikudi for my next engagement. As we were nearing Madurai, Dr Rama Murthi telephoned from Chennai. “We have examined Anna. Signs of cancer are visible,” he said. The whole world went dark before my eyes. I could not continue speaking on the telephone. I sat alone and wept aloud as much as I could. I immediately cancelled all the other programmes, and that very night Madurai Muthu, Arangannal and I returned to Chennai.

When I saw Anna, who had been admitted to the Madras General Hospital, I could not keep from crying. The doctors said that tissue had grown in Anna’s food pipe and that it had to be removed through surgery. It was decided that it would be best for Anna to go to the United States for surgery. Anna left for America on 10.9.1968. Tens of thousands gathered at the airport and wished that Anna would regain his health and return. Along with Governor Ujjal Singh, Ministers and leaders of all parties, the sight of Thanthai Periyar himself coming there in a wheelchair and sitting with tearful eyes moved everyone.

A few of us travelled with Anna on the same aircraft as far as Mumbai and saw him off. Anniyar, Dr Parimalam, K. Rajaram, R. Sezhiyan and Dr Sadasivam travelled to America with Anna.

On the day after Anna’s birthday, September 15, surgery was performed on him at New York Memorial Hospital. On that same day, in Tanjavur city, I unveiled Anna’s statue at a function presided over by Navalar. In my speech I said:

> “...........In America Abraham Lincoln was born — Kennedy lived — but it seems Americans yearned because an Anna had not been born there. To satisfy their yearning, Mother Tamil has today given birth there once again to Anna, who has undergone surgery.”

Because of the skilful treatment of the world-renowned Dr Miller, Anna recovered and returned, and a welcome was given to him at Chennai airport on 6.11.1968. We had prepared an open jeep fitted with a canopy. Anna climbed into it. I, Navalar, N.V.N., Mathiazhagan and others were with him as the vehicle set out in procession. When the procession reached home, Anna went upstairs.

A few minutes later Chidambaram Pon. Sokkalingam came downstairs and said Anna had asked me to come up. When I went upstairs, Anna asked me to sit, lifted his shirt and showed me the place where the surgery had been performed. I embraced Anna and sobbed. Anna too could not control his tears. Afterwards we spoke about the condition of the country and the party.

Following Parliament in Delhi accepting the resolution passed by the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly naming the State “Tamil Nadu,” it was decided to hold a celebration in Chennai on 2.12.1968. Although Anna had not participated in any function after his treatment in America, he refused to listen to the doctors who tried to stop him and participated in that celebration. Silambuch Selvar M. P. Sivagnanam, C. P. Adithanar and I took part with Anna.

Speaking at that function, Anna said:

> “.........When I decided to participate in this joyful celebration, doctors and friends imposed a restriction on me: ‘You must not speak at this meeting; if you speak for a long time it will harm your health.’ I told the doctors and friends, ‘If speaking today, on the day of the joyful celebration of the change of name to “Tamil Nadu,” would itself harm this body, then there is no use in this body existing,’ and asked them to accept my request. Such an opportunity comes only once in a person’s life; it does not come many times.”

When he said this, the eyes of everyone gathered there became pools of tears.

Likewise, in January 1969, Anna participated in the unveiling of Kalaivanar’s statue during the Pongal festival. Our happiness did not last many days. Anna again suffered unbearable pain. The doctors called it “secondary.” We were making arrangements to take him to the hospital at Vellore. At that time Anna went to the bathroom, fell there and lost consciousness. We took him to the Cancer Institute at Adyar, Chennai. A team of medical specialists was formed under Dr C. S. Sadasivam. We decided to bring Miller from America.

He came to Chennai on 21.1.1969 and undertook the treatment.

It was midnight on 2 February 1969. Though the city of Chennai was sleeping quietly, tens of thousands of people had gathered around the Adyar hospital. Midnight passed and 3 February began.

1:22 a.m. — suddenly, a great shock — not a shock, a deluge.

Where is death ever going to find compassion?

A great ship of mercy that had set out to carry countless poor and simple lives to shore capsized that midnight!

An inextinguishable lamp that had raised rays of light in countless darkened minds went out that night!

The moment they heard the words “Anna has died,” some sent their own lives away too.

Among the lakhs who set out to have a final glimpse of Anna, unable to find room inside the trains and travelling seated on their roofs, more than thirty sacrificed their lives near Chidambaram.

We came to the conclusion that it was our duty to raise for Anna a permanent memorial that time could not erase. For that reason we decided that the right course was to build a tomb for him.

A site was selected on the shore of the Bay of Bengal.

Permission was obtained from Anniyar and the family.

Today that memorial stands in such a way that people who come to Chennai do not return without seeing Anna’s resting place. Is that all? From Anna Arivalayam to the Anna Centenary Memorial Library — bridges, roads, universities, statues in city after city and town after town — we continue singing Anna’s fame. Union Minister Y. B. Chavan had come on behalf of the Union Government to pay final respects to Anna. Coins bearing Anna’s image, postage stamps — so many countless memorials!

To make my grief my own and bear it, I wrote a poetic tribute to Anna and paid homage through the radio.

You will not have forgotten the poetic line, “Lend me your heart, Anna.”

From that year until today — even last year, when I was admitted as an inpatient at Ramachandra Hospital — I have never failed, every year, to come to Anna’s memorial and pay homage.

In the same way, this year too, on this day, I come to that place and shower my homage at Anna’s flower-like feet.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**3-2-2010**
