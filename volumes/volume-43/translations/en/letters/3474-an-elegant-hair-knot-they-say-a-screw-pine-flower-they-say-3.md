# 3474. An Elegant Hair-Knot, They Say; a Screw-Pine Flower, They Say... 3

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This third letter closes the `ஒய்யாரக் கொண்டையாம், தாழம்பூவாம்...` sequence. Actual PDF 340 uses short `ஒய்யாரக்...`, while the printed contents uses long `ஓய்யாரக்...`; both source layers remain independent. The author answers Jayalalithaa’s rebuttal and then reproduces or summarizes court observations concerning asset figures, bail, frozen funds, special courts and the 2003 government-employees dispute. All such statements below are translations of the source’s attributed political and judicial record, not independent factual findings.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3474](../../../chapters/3474-oyyaarak-kondaiyaam-thaazhampoovaam-3.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 340](../../../pages/page-340.md)–[PDF 347](../../../pages/page-347.md)  
**Date:** 2 June 2010

**Udanpirappē,**

Although I wrote, with dates, what judges of the Special Court, High Court and Supreme Court had said in cases concerning former Chief Minister Jayalalithaa, she has stated that I am repeatedly telling a falsehood. I do not know whether, by saying this, she is denying that those judgments exist or calling the judges’ own rulings false.

I wrote that Jayalalithaa was trying to delay the trial. She replies that I am the one delaying it. The accusation in the case is against her; if a punishment is imposed, it is she who will be affected. Even an ordinary person can therefore understand who would have reason to delay the trial. Yet she tries to establish that she never attempted any delay.

If that is true, why, thirteen years after the trial began, did she file a petition asking to be discharged from the case? If she is innocent, should she not cooperate with a speedy hearing and face the judgment? The source argues that her repeated petitions before different courts themselves show who sought delay. What use is there, it asks, in lamenting that I repeatedly tell a falsehood?

I had not intended to search out all these court records and remind the world of them. It is because she repeatedly speaks and writes as though she had done no wrong and as though false cases had been imposed upon her that I am compelled, says the author, to place the recorded material before the public.

Jayalalithaa next says that on 25-5-2010 in the Bengaluru Special Court her lawyer did not ask for a copy of the Supreme Court order, but only for copies of the case documents. I had referred in my letter to reports in newspapers such as *Dinamani*. But, the source argues, something was raised in court and the trial was delayed on that account. Since Jayalalithaa herself says her lawyer asked for time to read the case documents, the author says it is easy to see who sought additional time.

She also laments, according to the source, that a lawyer who once argued for her has now joined hands with me. The author turns that into a rhetorical question: if her own lawyer and others who trusted her leave her and come to me, what does that suggest about where justice lies?

Jayalalithaa has made other accusations against me as well. Referring particularly to a case involving my son, she asks why there was no appeal after the judgment. The source states that, when the Government lawyer in Andhra Pradesh was asked for an opinion, he advised that the case was not fit for appeal. It then asks whether, when Jayalalithaa was in office and witnesses changed their evidence in cases against her before the Special Court and she was later acquitted, she herself appealed those judgments.

On the spectrum issue, Jayalalithaa says that I had a Central Bureau of Investigation officer transferred. Can she prove it? Is she prepared to state, with evidence, whom I asked to transfer an officer? The source counters by referring to newspaper reports about requests for transfers said to have been written by Jayalalithaa herself.

She has also referred to attacks on lawyers who expressed criticism. The source responds by recalling the assaults on senior lawyers Vijayan and Shanmugasundaram during her period in office, which it associates with their willingness to argue against her.

I have been Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu five times; Jayalalithaa has been Chief Minister twice. Have Supreme Court or High Court judges, even once, made comparable adverse observations against me?

The letter then sets out what it describes as observations and judgments concerning Jayalalithaa and her Government.

The source gives the value of Jayalalithaa’s assets on 1-7-1991, before she became Chief Minister, as ₹2 crore 1 lakh 83 thousand 957, and the value on 12-5-1996, after five years in office, as ₹66 crore 65 lakh 20 thousand 395. The author says these were publicly known figures, not figures invented by him.

It recounts that a case was investigated; that a High Court stay obtained for a period was later lifted; and that Jayalalithaa later sought anticipatory bail. According to the source’s rendering, the High Court rejected that application while stating that prima facie material existed for the corruption allegations and that the contention that the case arose from political hostility could not be accepted.

The source says the evidentiary material included jewellery, silver articles and other assets found during a search of Jayalalithaa’s residence, together with documents relating to bungalows purchased between Thiruvanmiyur and Mahabalipuram.

It also recounts that three lakh dollars were credited to Jayalalithaa’s bank account, that a case was initiated under foreign-exchange law, and that Jayalalithaa said she did not know who had sent the dollars. When she sought bail in that matter, the source attributes the following substance to Justice Sivappa’s ruling of 6-12-1996: the argument that deliberately false cases had been filed out of political hostility could not be accepted; prima facie material existed; investigating the alleged corruption was important; and examination of the prosecution documents did not suggest that the cases had been filed with an ulterior motive.

The source further recounts a challenge to the freezing of ₹3 crore 58 lakh held in Jayalalithaa’s name in Mylapore Canara Bank and Shriram finance company. It attributes to Justice Ramamurthi the observation that the assets case was at an important stage and that the assertion that the money in her bank accounts had not been improperly earned could not then be accepted.

The letter then recalls Jayalalithaa’s challenge to the establishment of Special Courts, which she had described as politically motivated and vindictive. In the source’s rendering, Madras High Court Chief Justice Liberhan and Justice Padmanabhan held that establishing Special Courts to inquire into corruption during her rule was legally proper; that no political motive was established; that the Government order of 30-4-97 was not contrary to law; that day-to-day trials were the purpose of the arrangement; and that, because prima facie material existed, the cases had to be faced.

The letter next turns to the 2003 government-employees dispute. A petition was filed in the Madras High Court seeking cancellation of dismissals and release of arrested employees. The source renders Justice P.D. Dinakaran’s ruling of 6-7-2003 as saying that the Government had been given two days to reach a proper decision but had delayed; that employees had voluntarily offered to withdraw the strike; that dismissing employees without a fair inquiry was wrong and illegal; that those arrested under Sections 4 and 5 of ESMA should be released immediately; that suspension and dismissal orders should be stayed; that employees should return to work; that new appointments should not be made; and that the Government should hold talks with the employees.

When Government counsel asked that the order itself be stayed for two days, the source quotes the judge as refusing and saying, in substance, that just as people who had done nothing were awakened from sleep and arrested at midnight, they should be released at midnight that day.

The source says the Jayalalithaa Government then appealed during the night at the residence of the Chief Justice of the Madras High Court and obtained an interim stay of Justice Dinakaran’s order.

It further records that lawyer Nalini Chidambaram filed a public-interest petition in the Supreme Court seeking repeal of the Tamil Nadu Government’s emergency dismissal law. During the hearing, the source attributes to Supreme Court Judges M.B. Shah and A.R. Lakshmanan a recommendation that government employees who had not engaged in violence be reinstated and that the State Government should act magnanimously without waiting for a formal Supreme Court order.

The source gives the figures as 2,200 employees imprisoned in connection with the strike and another one lakh 70 thousand employees whom the Court said should be reinstated, with action to be reported on 24-7-2003. It says the State Government expressed a wish to put forward an alternative view, and that the judges responded that the welfare of government employees was what mattered to the Supreme Court and that the alternative view could not be accepted.

All these, the author says sarcastically, are “certificates” given by various judges to Jayalalithaa and her Government. If she had accepted moral responsibility and resigned over them, one could say she was qualified now to demand that Union Minister Raja resign. Since she did not, the letter ends by asking with what face she accuses others.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**02-06-2010**

தலைமை நீதிபதியின் இல்லத்திற்கே சென்று மேல் முறையீடு
