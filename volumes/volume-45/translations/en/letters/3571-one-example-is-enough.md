# 3571. One Example Is Enough!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 30 June 2011

**Udanpirappē,**

Newspapers have reported that **“the Tamil Nadu government has constituted a one-man Commission of Inquiry headed by Justice Thangaraj to investigate complaints that have arisen regarding construction of the new Secretariat.”** Until now, no newspaper has reported that anyone had submitted complaints about construction of the new Secretariat. The Tamil Nadu government has itself artificially claimed that complaints have reached it and has created a Commission of Inquiry to investigate those complaints.

Jayalalithaa knows well that the public may ridicule her for refusing to move into the new Secretariat created at the Omandurar Government Estate and for refusing to use a building constructed by the Tamil Nadu government. The people of Tamil Nadu will understand very well that she has deliberately planned and constituted such an inquiry commission merely to escape that charge. For this purpose, they themselves have prepared a list of complaints, and on that basis this inquiry commission has been appointed.

The terms of reference have been fabricated as: defects in construction quality; unnecessary delay in completing construction; many irregularities during construction; and unnecessary expenditure. An inquiry into unnecessary expenditure! Do you know who has ordered that inquiry?

Because Chief Minister Jayalalithaa went to Delhi to pay a one-day courtesy call on the Prime Minister, the Chief Minister's room itself was altered and even the road was newly laid. How much was separately spent on that? How much was spent to remove the Classical Tamil Research Library and recreate the Legislative Assembly there because she refused to use the Secretariat constructed under DMK rule? How much has been spent in order to continue functioning in the old Secretariat itself? How much extra expenditure is incurred because the Chief Minister uses a private aircraft instead of an aircraft used by ordinary passengers? Are no inquiries needed into all these wasteful expenditures?

Who is to head the Commission of Inquiry? **Justice Thangaraj.** Tamil Nadu may have forgotten his history. So I shall remind you of it again.

A foundry belonging to the **Tamil Nadu Small Industries Corporation—TANSI**, an undertaking of the State Industries Department, was closed in **1985**. In June **1991**, when Jayalalithaa became Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu, she also kept the Industries portfolio under her own charge. In November 1991, Jayalalithaa and her close companion Sasikala, as partners in **Jaya Publications**, applied to TANSI's Managing Director to purchase that valuable TANSI-owned land at Guindy.

The land measured **3.0786 acres or 12.462 square metres** [source figure as printed]. The building constructed there measured **30,196 square feet**. According to the government valuation, the land was worth **₹4.43 crore**. According to the Public Works Department valuation, the total value including the building and machinery was **₹4.93 crore**. Jaya Publications bid **₹1.82 crore** for it and subsequently purchased it.

In her capacity as the person responsible for the Industries Department, Jayalalithaa was the seller of the land; in her capacity as a partner in Jaya Publications, Jayalalithaa was the purchaser of the land. This, the letter states, was an offence under **Section 169 of the Indian Penal Code**. The sale was finalised after an advertisement appeared only once in a Tamil newspaper of very limited circulation and with very little explanatory detail.

According to the government's calculation, **₹57.58 lakh** should have been collected as registration charges for the land. But because it was purchased at a very low price, only **₹24.36 lakh** was collected as registration charges.

The Special Court that examined the evidence in the case accepted it for trial. A charge sheet was subsequently filed. Instead of answering the case, Jayalalithaa appealed to the Madras High Court. The High Court judge who admitted that appeal said there was no sufficient basic evidence to continue the case before the Special Court.

The High Court judge who delivered that ruling was **Justice S. Thangaraj**. Even then, that ruling created major controversy. Most newspapers and legal experts expressed the view that the judgment was not correct. The weekly **Kalki** wrote: **“This is a judgment that plunged every Indian except Jayalalithaa into astonishment.”** It is this very person whom the Tamil Nadu government has now searched out and appointed as head of the Commission of Inquiry into the Secretariat building.

Later, this case went all the way to the Supreme Court, and it is a fact known to everyone that a judgment there directed Jayalalithaa to return the land to the government. Therefore I do not wish to describe all that again.

Here is another incident. A well-known industrialist named **Velusamy** belonged to Thirumangalam, Chennai. **S.K. Krishnamoorthy**, a real-estate businessman from Anna Nagar, mortgaged his house to Velusamy for **₹10 lakh**. Krishnamoorthy later sold that house to a businessman named **Jayapal** in Malaysia. Velusamy therefore demanded repayment. Krishnamoorthy kept avoiding repayment. Velusamy then arranged to take legal action against Krishnamoorthy and bring the mortgaged house to auction.

On learning this, Krishnamoorthy went directly to Velusamy's office and quarrelled with him, demanding the mortgage deed. He then threatened him with a gun. Velusamy immediately shouted. Hearing the noise, people nearby ran there. By then, Krishnamoorthy had escaped in an autorickshaw.

Velusamy lodged a complaint with Thirumangalam police. The police registered a case and arrested Krishnamoorthy. They searched his video shop and seized pornographic CDs and cassettes there. When they searched his house, a fake gun and important documents were found. Krishnamoorthy was therefore produced before court and taken into police custody for four days for questioning.

The source recounts Krishnamoorthy as having told the police:

> “I run a real-estate business and a video shop. Because I was involved in real estate, I became acquainted with many people, including political-party figures, judges and police officers. I also have a friend in Malaysia. On **16-10-1999**, I, High Court Justice Thangaraj, advocate Selvaraj and retired Police Superintendent Selvarathinam travelled to Malaysia on an Air India flight. We stayed for ten days at a star hotel arranged by businessman Jayapal and returned on **26-10-99**.”

At that time, newspapers prominently reported that he had said this. Justice Thangaraj was thus reported as having travelled to Malaysia and stayed there for ten days with Krishnamoorthy, whom the source describes as **“such a criminal.”** He is now going to investigate the Secretariat building. Photographs taken by the judge in Malaysia were also published at that time.

Is this one example not enough to show how suitable a person the AIADMK government has searched out and appointed as chairman of the Commission of Inquiry?

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**30-6-2011**
