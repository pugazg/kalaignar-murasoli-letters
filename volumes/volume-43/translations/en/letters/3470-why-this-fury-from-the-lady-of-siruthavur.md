# 3470. Why This Fury from the Lady of Siruthavur?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This letter rebuts remarks attributed to Jayalalithaa by combining an autobiographical childhood story about exposing an astrologer, a long fate-versus-reason passage from the author’s *Silappathikaram — Dramatic Epic*, and accusations concerning astrology, the Secretariat and the Kannagi statue. The political allegations remain source-attributed. The English dialogue printed in the source from the Macmillan publication is preserved verbatim, including its printed line break inside `deter / mined`.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3470](../../../chapters/3470-siruthaavoor-seemaattikku-seetram-eno.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 317](../../../pages/page-317.md)–[PDF 322](../../../pages/page-322.md)  
**Date:** 18 May 2010

**Udanpirappē,**

Yesterday, when our Leader of the Opposition and former Chief Minister Jayalalithaa conducted a few marriages at the homes of members of her party and then spoke, she spent more time abusing me than blessing the bridegrooms and brides. Whether it is a wedding celebration, a demonstration or any other function, I alone seem to be the centre of her speech! So many people have already finished placing curses upon me. Why, even the many curses this lady herself has pronounced against me have not had any effect.

When I was a little boy studying in Tiruvarur while staying at the house of my relative Mr Krishnan — father of Murasoli Sornam — elder brother Krishnan approached me and said: “At this young age itself you keep propagating that there is no astrology and no scripture. I know an astrologer who says everything exactly as it is. He has said he will come to the house tomorrow. You too must be there then and listen to what he says.” I said I would see.

That evening, a woman met me. She asked, “Boy, are you the son of the owner of this house?” I understood who she was. In reply to her question, I said yes. When she asked how many siblings there were, I gave a rough answer: “three boys, two girls.” In the same way, she asked me some other questions, obtained the answers from me and went away.

The next morning, the astrologer and the same woman who had met me the previous day arrived at elder brother Krishnan’s house. Elder brother Krishnan, his wife, I and others sat around them. After doing some things peculiar to his calling, the astrologer pointed at me and asked Krishnan, “This is your son, is he not?” Since I had already told elder brother Krishnan what had happened, he said nothing and sat as though agreeing.

Then the astrologer asked, “Shall I tell you how many sons you have?” The astrologer’s wife, seated opposite him, placed a hand upon her cheek and extended three fingers. Watching her, he asked, “You have three sons, do you not?” Krishnan again said nothing. Then he asked, “Shall I tell you how many daughters?” and looked towards his wife. Without making it obvious, that woman extended two fingers. The astrologer immediately said, “Two daughters, is that not so?”

Elder brother Krishnan became angry. When anger came to him, there was no telling what words would come from his mouth. He began abusing the astrologer in a torrent. The astrologer’s wife pointed me out and said that it was this boy who had given her all those details the previous day. The astrologer turned towards me and delivered a great curse: “At this young age itself, are you doing such things? You will never prosper.” He then left. The whole world is witness to whether that curse came true!

When even that astrologer’s curse did not come true, is Jayalalithaa’s curse going to come true?

Many years ago, in the dramatic work I wrote titled *Silappathikaram Kaappiyam*, I wrote an entire section on this matter of astrology. In it, when an astrologer tells King Nedun Cheralathan that his younger son Ilango will next ascend the throne, the younger son Ilango intervenes. It goes like this:

“Ilango: No, no. My elder brother is the one born to rule!

Cheralathan: Ilango! The word of the soothsayer will not prove false!

Ilango: The soothsayer’s word will not prove false to those created for falsehood! This is the ear of truth! It will not tremble on hearing his divination!

Soothsayer: Fate cannot be changed, Ilango!

Ilango: Why can it not? I shall make true the teaching of the Kural — ‘Those who labour without faltering or slackening will see even fate turn its back.’ I shall defeat the fate you speak of through intellect!

Soothsayer: Was anyone able to change the fate that the elder Rama must rule the forest and the younger Bharata must rule the country?

Ilango: Bharata did not rule; Rama did not rule; for fourteen years, was it not the sandal that ruled the country! Soothsayer, do not display old epics and seek to establish fate! Here... I create a new epic! I defeat your divination with reason... I make my elder brother the rightful ruler! Accept my challenge, soothsayer! Your prediction will become dust! Dust! Dust — watch!...”

The source notes that this dialogue, written by me under the title *Silappathikaram — Dramatic Epic*, was translated by Mr T. G. Narayanasamy and Mr M. D. Jayabalan, edited by Mr P. Marudhanayagam and Mr V. Murugan, printed by Macmillan, and would appear in the book to be released in Chennai on 31-5-2010 by the Vice-President of India. The following English is printed in the source and is reproduced verbatim:

> Ilango :- No, No. It is my brother who is born to rule and nothing  
> shall change that position.  
>  
> Neduncheraladhan :- Who can defy Destiny, Ilango? Our  
> Astrologer’s prediction is bound to come true.  
>  
> Ilango :- That might be so for those with ears of superstition but  
> not for me, Sire, since my ears are tuned into the gospel of truth and  
> truth alone. The forecast is bound to fail and I accept the challenge of  
> the so-called Fate which has got to turn tail when confronted with deter  
> mined endeavour, as Valluvar has inspired us to believe.  
>  
> Astrologer :- Haven’t you read the epics, Ilango? Could anyone  
> alter the decree of Destiny that Rama the eldest brother should go into  
> exile and Bharata rule in his place?  
>  
> Ilango :- The epic I read says authoritatively that neither Bharata  
> nor Rama ascended the throne but it was the sandal that reigned supreme.  
> Revered Master - Astrologer, please do not dangle the epics before us  
> in order to prop up a false case. Here and now I swear to create an epic  
> new that shall smash your prediction to smithereens. My brother and  
> brother alone shall rule this land and the miracle that brings this about  
> will not originate from your chart-caged planets but from the effulgent  
> sun of honest intellect.)

I am the one who wrote that dialogue. And yet, it is said that I announced I was going to hand over my house to a charitable trust because of the words of some astrologer! It is said that I handed the house to the trust because some astrologer told me that my life would be in danger if I continued to stay there. Lady Jayalalithaa proclaimed this from a wedding stage. Is Jayalalithaa prepared to prove which astrologer said it?

I say this too: after my birthday, it is the deed of the house in which I am now living that I intend to hand over so that, after my death, the house may be used as a hospital.

Knowing none of this, Jayalalithaa has listened to whatever some astrologer told her and gone to a wedding function and lamented.

Not only that, it is said that even the Secretariat building was opened quickly by me because I listened to an astrologer! Which astrologer? Is she prepared to name him? It is said that an astrologer told me that if I remained seated in the old Fort St George after March I would never return to power, and that it was only after hearing this that I moved the Secretariat. March is over; April is over; May is now under way. The work on the new Secretariat will take another three or four months to be fully completed. Until then, I am going to remain seated at Fort St George. What becomes of Jayalalithaa’s falsehood now?

Why, when Jayalalithaa was Chief Minister, did the newspapers not carry page after page of reports that she said a new Secretariat would be built at Kotturpuram, brought astrologers there, conducted *puja* and consecration rituals, and laid a foundation stone? What happened? Did that astrology come true?

Not only that: when Perarignar Anna was Chief Minister, the Kannagi statue had been placed on the beach road. On hearing the words of some astrologer that if she looked at that statue every day on her way to the Secretariat her rule would not last, did they not send a lorry in the dead of night, have the statue knocked down, remove it and dump it in a corner of the museum? Did what that astrologer said come true and make her government last? I restored that Kannagi statue to the same place and see it every day on my way to the Secretariat. Has any obstacle therefore come to my government?

Why, the newspapers that arrived today report that Jayalalithaa is going to Kalahasti to perform Rahu–Ketu *puja* and that 300 police personnel are being arranged for security. Astrology, *yagam*, *puja* and consecration rituals are arts she herself has mastered. Yet, if she climbs onto a wedding stage and abuses me — one who has believed in none of these things from youth — saying that I donate my house and moved the Secretariat only after listening to astrologers, one naturally feels like asking: why this fury from the Lady of Siruthavur? Can anger that did not arise against those who submitted the complaint petition about Siruthavur be directed at me?

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**18-5-2010**

3. The printed Macmillan passage preserves the source line break `deter` / `mined`, its punctuation and final closing parenthesis.
4. The source’s `300`-police figure, 31-5-2010 publication-event date and three-to-four-month Secretariat timeline are retained. No canonical Tamil change was required.
