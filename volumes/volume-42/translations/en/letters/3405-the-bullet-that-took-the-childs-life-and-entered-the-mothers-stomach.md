# 3405. The Bullet That Took the Child’s Life and Entered the Mother’s Stomach!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This letter contrasts the author’s account of polling in Tamil Nadu with reports and editorial commentary about political violence in West Bengal, then reproduces earlier excerpts from the CPI(M)-linked newspaper Theekkathir concerning Jayalalithaa and the DMK government. Source-supplied English from The Telegraph is reproduced verbatim; surrounding accusations and comparisons remain attributed source material and are not independently adjudicated here. The complete audited Tamil is reproduced below and remains authoritative.

**Source pages:** [PDF 272](../../../pages/page-272.md)–[PDF 277](../../../pages/page-277.md)  
**Date:** 15 May 2009

**Udanpirappē,**

The Election Commission itself has announced, the author says, that the Lok Sabha election in Tamil Nadu was completed peacefully apart from one or two small incidents. Newspapers reported polling of about 67 to 68 per cent in Tamil Nadu.

There were morning reports from a few places of faults in voting machines, but those too were rectified within a short time. Even those minor faults, the letter argues, were the responsibility of the Election Commission and had nothing to do with the government.

For several days, however, some had campaigned by saying that the ruling party would engage in violence during the election, that there would be major disturbances and that Tamil Nadu would come to a standstill. Apart from one or two untoward incidents of the kind that occur during elections, the author argues that there was no large-scale violence that could be described as excesses by the ruling party. He says the election passed peacefully enough to discredit those who had predicted that Tamil Nadu would become a bloodbath.

Even so, he says, the CPI(M) newspaper printed in large type reports supplied by Jayalalithaa — whom the source sarcastically calls the “queen of statements” — under headings such as “Violence by ruling-party men” and “Murderous attack by DMK men.”

The author then turns to events in CPI(M)-ruled West Bengal and says that it is not he but the well-known English daily The Telegraph, published from Kolkata, that carried the following front-page headline on 9 May 2009:

> Tragedy of Sania, 1 year 11 months : Bullet kills baby, then hits her mother!

The letter reproduces these portions in English:

> Nandigram, May 8 : The “politically conscious” State that threw up the highest turnout yesterday also witnessed the death of probably the youngest victim of political violence this election. Sania Khatoun, a year and 11 months old, was in mother Aleya Bibi’s lap last night, being fed her dinner, when alleged CPM supporters entered their home and opened indiscriminate fire. One of the bullets went through Sania’s leg, and then lodged in the stomach of Aleya, 27.
>
> No political party in Bengal could recall anyone as young as Sania being killed in poll violence in recent memory. During last year’s Panchayat elections, Mili Khatoon, 4, was killed by a bomb as she played on the verandah of her home in Murshidabad’s Talibpur village.
>
> No one has been arrested for Sania’s murder because “No one has filed any complaint,” East Midnapore S.P. said.

The source then prints a Tamil rendering of that report. In English, that Tamil passage says that West Bengal, described there as a politically conscious State, had recorded a very high voter turnout, but had also witnessed the death of one of the youngest victims of election-related political violence. It says that one-year-and-eleven-month-old Sania Khatoun was eating while seated in her mother’s lap when CPI(M) supporters were alleged to have entered the house and fired indiscriminately; a bullet passed through the child’s leg and entered the abdomen of her twenty-seven-year-old mother Aleya. It further recalls the death of four-year-old Mili Khatoon in a bomb explosion during the previous year’s local-body elections and says no one had been arrested in Sania’s case because, according to the East Midnapore Superintendent of Police, no complaint had been filed.

The author says The Telegraph did not stop with that report. On 11 May 2009, three days before the letter, it published an editorial under the title “Violent State.”

The source’s Tamil rendering begins:

> “A baby shot dead in her mother’s arms is the new image of the horror of political violence in West Bengal.”

It then says that the episode formed part of what the editorial described as the long legacy of Marxist rule, alleging that political violence had been normalised over thirty-two years and had affected law, administration and other fields. It describes threats, intimidation and violence as principal political weapons in contexts ranging from industrial disputes to elections.

The source also preserves this English sentence verbatim:

> The violence that has rocked the State since the current poll campaign started is thus a result of the political cult that the CPI(M) has nurtured all these years

The Tamil passage continues that, if the government led by that party had failed to prevent or control violence, the editorial attributed the failure not merely to administrative incompetence but also to a political outlook that treated violence as a tactic.

The author comments sarcastically that people receiving such “praise” were the ones publishing allegations of violence and murderous attacks by Tamil Nadu’s ruling party.

He then refers to violence surrounding the proposed Tata car factory in West Bengal, alleging that fourteen people were killed in lathi-charge and firing on farmers who resisted giving agricultural land for the factory. He also refers to newspaper reports that eight people had died in West Bengal during the then-current Lok Sabha election.

The letter next asks what CPI(M) leaders — who were now criticising Tamil Nadu’s ruling party — had said only a few months or years earlier. It specifically asks what they had said in connection with a by-election held in Madurai and says he had kept that material as a reminder.

A Theekkathir passage dated 25-6-2007 is rendered as follows:

When Pratibha Patil was announced as the woman candidate for the country’s highest office, the Presidency, the country rejoiced, the quoted passage says. It accuses Jayalalithaa of calling her selection a “political joke” and urges voters in the Madurai West constituency to answer what it calls the arrogance of Jayalalithaa’s alliance.

It calls the decision made in the national interest a good one and criticises describing it as a “political joke.” It says Jayalalithaa, while campaigning in the Uttar Pradesh election, did not speak against the BJP, and alleges that this indicated an effort to join a communal alliance. It asks voters to take note. It further accuses her of spreading rumours and asks whether she had standing to criticise the grant of pattas for huts in Tamil Nadu.

The letter then reproduces another Theekkathir passage dated 13-8-2006. That passage says the CPI(M) welcomed many public-welfare schemes announced in the DMK government’s budget, while accusing the AIADMK of opposing them unnecessarily and saying it had become unfit even to function as an Opposition party. It refers to Jayalalithaa’s difficulty in saying who owned the bungalow built for her stay at Siruthavur. It criticises the language used by AIADMK Assembly members, rejects the charge that Communists were acting as cheerleaders for the DMK government, and counters that it was the AIADMK government that had acted as a cheerleader for domestic monopoly capitalists and multinational corporate giants. It praises the Chief Minister’s efforts to prevent diversion of good-quality rice from ration shops and accuses the previous Jayalalithaa government of having abetted exploitation in education.

A third quoted Theekkathir passage, dated 4-9-2006, concerns Jayalalithaa’s statement opposing a proposed satellite town in the Vandalur area. The quoted CPI(M) Tamil Nadu State Executive strongly condemns what it describes as an unhealthy and personally hostile attack on Chief Minister Karunanidhi, his family and relatives. It says the attack had turned into a resentment-driven debate using disgusting language. It accuses AIADMK Assembly members of deliberately disrupting proceedings and diverting attention from public issues, says the approach made a mockery of legislative democracy, and adds that the party had not understood the lesson voters had delivered in the May 2006 Assembly election.

Udanpirappē, the author says that CPI(M) leaders and front-line figures had forgotten that they themselves once spoke in these terms before leaving his alliance. He says they were now writing hostile views about his side in their publications.

When friends speak to me about those who attack us, I often say one thing: they have not abused us to the same extent that they once praised us.

That is why, after reading the attacks by those dear friends, I read their earlier praise and console myself.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**15-5-2009**

இத்தகைய பாராட்டுக்குரியவர்கள்(?) தான் தமிழகத்திலே ஆளுங் கட்சியினர் வன் செயல் என்றும், கொலை வெறி தாக்குதல் என்றும் செய்தி வெளியிட்டுள்ளனர். மேற்கு வங்கத்தில் “டாட்டா” நிறுவனத்தின் சார்பில் கார் தொழிற்சாலை ஒன்றினை தொடங்குவதில் - விவசாய நிலத்தை தொழிற்சாலைக்குக் கொடுக்க மாட்டோம் என்று போராடிய அப்பாவி விவசாயிகள் மீது மார்க்சிஸ்ட் அரசு நடத்திய தடியடி, துப்பாக்கி சூட்டினால் ஏற்பட்ட வன்முறை சம்பவங்களில் பதினான்கு பேர் கொல்லப்பட்டார்கள் என்பதை மறக்க முடியுமா? அது மாத்திரமல்ல; தற்போது நடைபெற்ற மக்களவை தேர்தலில் மேற்கு வங்க மாநிலத்தில் எட்டு பேர் பலியான செய்தி ஏடுகளில் வெளி வந்ததை இல்லையென்று கூற முடியுமா?

(மார்க்சிஸ்ட் ஏடு - தீக்கதிர் 4-9-2006)

உடன்பிறப்பே, மார்க்சிஸ்ட் கம்யூனிஸ்ட் கட்சியின் தலைவர்களும், முன்னணியினரும் இப்படியெல்லாம் அவர்களே பேசியதை மறந்து விட்டுத் தான் - நமது அணியை விட்டுச் சென்றார்கள். அவர்கள் தான் இப்போதும் தங்கள் இதழ்களில் நம்மீது காழ்ப்பு வீசும் கருத்துக்களை எழுதி வருகிறார்கள்.

நம்மைப் பற்றி தாக்குகிறவர்களைப் பற்றி நண்பர்கள் கூறும்போது நான் ஒன்றைச் சொல்வதுண்டு. அவர்கள் நம்மைப் பாராட்டிய அளவிற்கு திட்டவில்லையே என்று கூறுவேன். எனவே தான் அந்த அருமை நண்பர்கள் நம்மைப் பற்றி தாக்குவதைப் படித்து விட்டு, அவர்கள் இதற்கு முன்பு எழுதிய பாராட்டுக்களை படித்துப் பார்த்து எனக்கு நானே சமாதானப்படுத்திக் கொள்கிறேன்.

அன்புள்ள,
**மு.க.**
15-5-2009
