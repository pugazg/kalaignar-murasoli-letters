**Udanpirappē,**

Today’s *Dinamani* carries an article under the heading “Only God Knows.” It begins by saying that only when qualified persons occupy responsible offices does honour come to those offices; that respect depends on who adorns an office rather than on someone merely being adorned by it; and that constitutional offices such as President of the Republic, Supreme Court judge, Chief Election Commissioner, Comptroller and Auditor General and Governors bring honour both to the office and the nation only when the right persons occupy them. Do you know who owns this view? S. Gurumurthy. There is no need to explain who he is; it is a truth known to everyone!

According to that writer, all these offices are adorned only when “he” sits in them; only then do they gain honour. In particular, he says that R. Venkataraman entered history only when he occupied the office of President. It seems all the other Presidents did not adorn that office at all! R. Venkataraman, he says, earned the name “Rulebook President.” Shall we use our own Tamil Nadu to give an example of how R. Venkataraman upheld the rules?

In 1990, out of 234 seats in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly, the DMK held 142. Despite such a majority, although the Budget had been presented in the Tamil Nadu Assembly on February 2, the Government was hurriedly dismissed on the night of January 30. January 30 was also the day when the wicked Godse shot and killed Mahatma Gandhi, father of the Indian nation. On that very day, acting on the advice of the AIADMK in Tamil Nadu, then Prime Minister Chandra Shekhar and then President R. Venkataraman together conspired and butchered Indian democracy. It is the President R. Venkataraman, who acted in full accord with that conspiracy, whom the *Dinamani* writer now praises as one who upheld the rules and earned a place in history.

Without even obtaining a report from the Governor, and for the first time in the history of the Indian Constitution, President R. Venkataraman used the single word **“Otherwise”** in Article 356, searched through the nooks and crannies of the law, entered through them and dismissed a popularly elected Government possessing a majority. Tamil Nadu knows this very well.

To state it with further evidence: on 25 December 1990, then Prime Minister Chandra Shekhar flew to Chennai to meet the Sankaracharya. As Chief Minister I went to the airport to receive him, along with then Governor Barnala, the Speaker, ministers and officials. Jayalalithaa, who was then in the Opposition, also came to the airport to receive the Prime Minister. When the AIADMK was in opposition, had Jayalalithaa ever come all the way to the airport to receive a Prime Minister? No. But in 1990 she did. When journalists at Kanchipuram asked Prime Minister Chandra Shekhar about talk that the Tamil Nadu Government would be dismissed, he replied: “I do not know who has spread this rumour. I do not generally issue warnings. If there is confusion somewhere in the country or law and order is affected, we bring it to the attention of the State Government. Nothing else.”

But that very evening Jayalalithaa hosted a tea party for Prime Minister Chandra Shekhar at her residence. She again came to the airport to see him off. There she told journalists that she had pressed the Prime Minister to dismiss the Tamil Nadu Government. The story does not end there.

The next day Jayalalithaa met President R. Venkataraman, who was staying at Raj Bhavan in Chennai. She complained to him too that law and order in Tamil Nadu was bad, that Chief Minister Karunanidhi’s claim that Sri Lankan militants were being arrested was mere eyewash, and so on. Once again she met President R. Venkataraman and submitted a complaint petition alleging that the DMK Government was abetting the activities of the Liberation Tigers.

On 10 January 1991, answering a question from Murasoli Maran in the Rajya Sabha, Prime Minister Chandra Shekhar assured the House: “The Government does not wish to proclaim President’s Rule in any State by using Article 356 of the Constitution.” Yet when the Union Government proceeded in its effort to dismiss the DMK Government and summoned Tamil Nadu Governor Barnala to Delhi asking him for a report, that Punjab lion Barnala refused. In that situation, a National Front delegation led by former Prime Minister V.P. Singh met the President—the same man whom the *Dinamani* writer praises as “R. Venkataraman who upheld the rules”—and asked him not to take an anti-democratic step. Even after that request, it was R. Venkataraman who dismissed the Tamil Nadu Government.

Even after all this, we have no personal enmity toward Mr. Venkataraman. Nor did we have any desire now to narrate these things. They are events that happened and ended long ago. But the *Dinamani* writer pushes us into recalling them. We are not even concerned that they praise R. Venkataraman. But do you know how completely that writer has belittled the others who occupied the same office, treating them as though they were dust?

- President V.V. Giri, he says, blindly signed whatever order Indira Gandhi sent.
- Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed, a member of a minority community, was subjected to the mocking name “rubber stamp,” functioning as a puppet of the ruling party and as a machine that signed without even reading.
- Giani Zail Singh, from a backward community, behaved like a low-grade politician.

After writing in this manner about these eminent persons who sat in the President’s chair, the *Dinamani* writer says that only fifteen years later, when R. Venkataraman was elected President, did the office regain the honour and dignity it had lost. What justice there is in that, “he” himself must explain.

Finally, do you know what this writer says about today’s President, Mrs Pratibha Patil—the first woman chosen to occupy that distinguished office, whom all India wished to see there and for whose election the DMK too made great efforts?

The writer says that someone had remarked that she served the Nehru family without concern for status by cooking, serving tea and coffee to visitors and washing vessels, and that the office was given as a reward of gratitude for “washing ten vessels.” If a *Dinamani* writer can reproduce such words, has not the highest constitutional office in India itself been wrongly disparaged to a degree for which even the Government could file a case?

Even in concluding the article, he writes that perhaps “some servant who does household work well” could be appointed to that office. This letter is written out of the anguish that Dravidian people must at least now understand how much of their characteristic **“kusumbuthanam”**—their sly, needling malice—fills that article, and act accordingly.

**With affection**  
**M.K.**  
**10 June 2012**

• குடியரசுத் தலைவர் வி.வி.கிரி, இந்திரா காந்தி அனுப்பும் உத்தரவு எதுவாக இருந்தாலும், கண்ணை மூடிக்கொண்டு கையெழுத்திட்டார்.

• “ரப்பர் ஸ்டாம்பு” என்கிற கேலிப் பெயருக்கு ஆளானவர். சிறுபான்மையினத்தைச் சேர்ந்த பக்ருதீன் அலி அகமது, ஆளும் கட்சியின் கைப்பாவையாகவும், படித்துப் பார்க்காமல் கையொப்பமிடும் இயந்திரமாகவும் செயல்பட்ட அவலம் நடைபெற்றது.

• பிற்படுத்தப்பட்ட சமுதாயத்தைச் சேர்ந்த கியானி ஜெயில் சிங் தரம் தாழ்ந்த அரசியல்வாதி போலச் செயல்பட்டவர்.

இவ்வாறு குடியரசுத் தலைவர் பதவி நாற்காலியிலே அமர்ந்திருந்த இந்தப் பெருமக்களைப் பற்றி எழுதிய தினமணி கட்டுரையாளர், பதினைந்து ஆண்டுகளுக்குப் பிறகு குடியரசுத் தலைவர் பதவி தனது இழந்த மரியாதையையும், பெருமையையும் மீண்டும் பெற்றது ஆர். வெங்கட்ராமன், குடியரசுத் தலைவராகத் தேர்ந்தெடுக்கப்பட்டபோதுதான் என்று எழுதியிருக்கிறார் என்றால் அது என்ன நியாயம் என்பதை “அவர்” தான் கூற வேண்டும்.
