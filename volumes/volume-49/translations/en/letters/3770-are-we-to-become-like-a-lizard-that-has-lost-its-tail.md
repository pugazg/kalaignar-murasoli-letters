# 3770. Are We to Become Like a Lizard That Has Lost Its Tail?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

Following the DMK's request for, and receipt of, Congress support for its Rajya Sabha candidate Kanimozhi, some people have tried to claim that the DMK has forgotten and abandoned the Sri Lankan Tamil issue. It is true that the DMK first sought support for its Rajya Sabha candidate from the Communist parties, then from the DMDK, the Manithaneya Makkal Katchi and Puthiya Tamilagam, and after that from the Congress. Discussions with every one of these parties were held without any condition and only in relation to this election. Yet some who have practised politics of hostility and hatred towards the DMK from the beginning have started campaigning that the DMK, which broke its arrangement with the Congress a few months ago, has now renewed its relationship with the Congress and washed its hands of the Sri Lankan issue.

Meanwhile, there is an urgent and necessary reason for us to keep in mind what is happening to Sri Lankan Tamils: the Government of Sri Lanka is attempting to amend the Thirteenth Amendment to its Constitution, weaken its very foundation and reduce it to something lifeless.

It was with this issue in mind that I wrote a detailed letter to the Prime Minister at the beginning of this month, on 9 June 2013. I wrote:

> “You are aware that the Thirteenth Amendment to the Sri Lankan Constitution was the direct result of an agreement between India and Sri Lanka. Because Sri Lankan Tamils have had bitter experiences with agreements previously entered into with the Sinhalese, we in India gave them an assurance that this agreement would be implemented. Gotabaya Rajapaksa, the brother of President Mahinda Rajapaksa and Sri Lanka's Defence Secretary, has already called for the repeal of the Thirteenth Amendment.
>
> “It is now the duty and responsibility of the Government of India to intervene directly and ensure that the Rajiv–Jayewardene Accord is fully implemented. This will make the path towards resolving the conflict and achieving ethnic reconciliation easier. At this critical time, India must not remain a silent spectator to the developments taking place in Sri Lanka. I seek your intervention to safeguard the rights and lives of the Tamils in Sri Lanka.”

Prime Minister Dr Manmohan Singh replied immediately, on 11 June 2013:

> “I have received your letter dated June 9 concerning the Thirteenth Amendment to the Sri Lankan Constitution. Although this is a decision to be taken collectively by the various political parties in Sri Lanka, we will certainly examine its implications and convey our views to the Government of Sri Lanka. You are also aware that the Government of India is acting on the view that all sections in Sri Lanka, including Sri Lankan Tamils, must receive their rightful entitlements and live in harmony.”

Both my letter and the Prime Minister's reply were written only after the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam had withdrawn from the United Progressive Alliance. It was while we were outside the alliance that the DMK, without forgetting the Sri Lankan Tamil issue, wrote this letter. The Prime Minister replied to it immediately.

Later, on 18 June 2013, representatives of Sri Lanka's Tamil National Alliance met the Indian Prime Minister in Delhi and asked him to prevent steps that would dilute the Thirteenth Amendment. Newspapers reported that the Prime Minister told them: “We will not abandon the Sri Lankan Tamils; action will be taken to protect them.”

Further, left-wing ministers in Sri Lanka have opposed President Rajapaksa's efforts to dilute the Thirteenth Amendment. They have said:

> “Instead of removing the powers relating to land and the police from the Thirteenth Amendment, the Provincial Council system must be strengthened further and the rights of the Tamils must be given to them. If, instead, the Government lays its hand upon the Thirteenth Amendment and confronts India, Katchatheevu will be lost.”

A conference of left-wing ministers from Sri Lanka's governing side, who are working to protect the Thirteenth Amendment, was held at the headquarters of the Lanka Sama Samaja Party in Colombo. Sri Lankan ministers Vasudeva Nanayakkara, Tissa Vitharana, D. E. W. Gunasekara, Rajitha Senaratne and Reginald Cooray participated.

Minister Tissa Vitharana said:

> “After the war was brought to an end, the hearts of the Tamil people should have been won and national reconciliation established in the country. Even though a suitable opportunity arose for this, some are now arguing that the Provincial Council system, which has been in operation for the past twenty-six years, should be abolished. Had the police and land powers provided for under the Thirteenth Amendment been given to the Provincial Councils, no problem would have arisen in the country. Powers must be devolved through the Thirteenth Amendment and the Provincial Council system implemented. We must demonstrate to the international community that we share power with the minorities and work together with them.”

Minister D. E. W. Gunasekara said:

> “Some say that the Thirteenth Amendment was not introduced in accordance with the wishes of the people of the country. Since 1978, eighteen constitutional amendments have been implemented. None of them was enacted in accordance with the people's wishes. In that situation, it is not right to ask why that question should be raised only in relation to the Thirteenth Amendment. The country's principal political parties, the Sri Lanka Freedom Party and the United National Party, have not acted to resolve the country's central problem—the ethnic issue. Instead, they act only to retain their own political power. They cannot arrive at an agreement on the ethnic issue.”

Although the Government of India has written to the Sri Lankan Government, it is not known whether that will create any impact or pressure upon it. A parliamentary select committee was formed to consider changes to the Thirteenth Amendment. It is said that several parties, including the Tamil National Alliance, have stated that they do not wish to participate in it. It has now been announced that a draft concerning amendments to the provisions of the Thirteenth Amendment will be sent to the Supreme Court of Sri Lanka for its opinion.

It appears that the provisions of the Thirteenth Amendment are to be altered so that no minority—Tamil or Muslim—may receive full powers at the provincial level. Such an act would violate the agreement between the two countries. If provisions of an agreement jointly made by India and Sri Lanka are to be changed, it is in no way just for Sri Lanka to make such amendments without India's consent. If Sri Lanka unilaterally changes the Thirteenth Amendment in violation of the Indo–Sri Lanka Accord, it will bring great humiliation upon India in the international arena.

Meanwhile, *Theekkathir* reported on 28 June 2013 that President Rajapaksa had suddenly announced the postponement of the Northern Provincial Council election in the Tamil-inhabited region of Sri Lanka. Rajapaksa had decided to amend Article 13A of the Sri Lankan Constitution. But the approval of eight Provincial Councils was required. These legal complications had obstructed the President's decision. Opposition groups representing the Tamil and Muslim minorities were strongly opposing the amendment to 13A.

In 1987, India and Sri Lanka entered into an agreement providing for the devolution of powers to Sri Lanka's provinces. The Sri Lankan Government is now seeking to amend 13A in violation of that agreement. Even now, the DMK insists that the Union Government must intervene immediately and exert strong pressure to ensure the full implementation of the Rajiv–Jayewardene Accord as the first step towards resolving the Sri Lankan Tamil issue.

Therefore, the Sri Lankan issue is one matter, the Rajya Sabha election another, and an alliance yet another. But some people are trying to throw everything together and create confusion. When the DMK withdrew from the United Progressive Alliance Government, every DMK minister in the Union Government resigned. Now, merely because the Congress supported the DMK candidate in the Rajya Sabha election, DMK members have not joined the Union Government again. Those who conceal all this and seek somehow to place blame upon the DMK are the ones attempting to fling mud at the movement.

Yet even a newspaper such as *The Hindu* has written an editorial about the Congress supporting the DMK's Rajya Sabha candidate. It was titled “அடைந்தது ஒன்று; இழந்தது ஏராளம்” — “For one more, a lot lost.” By portraying the DMK and the Congress as opportunists, while writing as though the AIADMK leader had moved her pieces with great strategic skill, *The Hindu* has made clear who it is and what its inner purpose is.

As that editorial describes it, did *The Hindu* not see opportunism when Jayalalithaa successively discarded, one by one, all the allied parties that had stood with her in the last election? Why, when the all-India leaders of the Communist Party of India—the party ultimately supported by the AIADMK—met Jayalalithaa in Delhi and sought her support, she did not respect them there by saying that she would support them. She said instead that she would meet them in Chennai. After returning to Chennai, she realised that the DMDK, the DMK and the Communist parties might join together, win two seats and cause one of the five AIADMK candidates to lose. She therefore withdrew one of her own candidates and announced support for the Communist Party of India. What is that called?

Which Communist principle did Madam dislike while she was in Delhi, but suddenly come to like after she returned to Tamil Nadu?

When Jayalalithaa does it, is it statesmanship; but when the DMK does the same, is it opportunism to *The Hindu*?

Why do some people in between, without any need, leap and twitch like a lizard that has lost its tail?

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**30 June 2016**

## Letter-specific notes

1. **Source-date anomaly:** the printed closing date is `30-6-2016`, and the canonical Tamil preserves it. The contents, cited June 2013 events and the letter's position between letters dated 28 June and 2 July 2013 strongly indicate a source printing error, but this translation does not silently correct it.
2. **Thirteenth Amendment / 13A** refers to the amendment to Sri Lanka's Constitution arising from the 1987 Indo–Sri Lanka Accord and establishing the Provincial Council framework.
3. The title's **lizard that has lost its tail** is a Tamil image for agitated, purposeless twitching. It is retained rather than replaced with an unrelated English idiom.
