# 3479. What Benefit Will This ‘Bandh’ Bring?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This letter distinguishes the author’s acceptance of unavoidable protest from his insistence that truthful, non-violent means take priority over tactical success. It responds to the 5 July nationwide bandh called by the BJP, AIADMK and Left parties, preserves the source-reported economic-loss figures and violence claims, and contrasts that action with the author’s account of the Dravidian movement’s announced-arrest protest discipline dating from the 1938 anti-Hindi agitation.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3479](../../../chapters/3479-bandh-thaal-vilaiyap-pogum-payan-enna.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 371](../../../pages/page-371.md)–[PDF 374](../../../pages/page-374.md)  
**Date:** 7 July 2010

**Udanpirappē,**

The Bharatiya Janata Party, the AIADMK and the Left Communist parties joined together in one front and announced that on 5 July they would conduct a “bandh” on a scale that would bring all India to a standstill. The media sharpened that announcement and, as the source describes it, carried and spread sensational bandh reports so that the action would not fail anywhere and so that the religious-political forces of the BJP, the Left front and the AIADMK might secure a great success for it.

The principal leaders of that front told journalists that the bandh should be peaceful. Reports also appeared saying that the country would lose about **₹10,000 crore** because of the bandh, while statements of federations of industry and commerce were reported as estimating a **₹13,000-crore** loss in national output.

In general, if circumstances make a bandh or strike unavoidable, I have never opposed it merely for being such an action.

Whoever is engaged in efforts to attain the stated objective, policy or demand, we have not viewed them with suspicion.

We have not failed to pat them on the back and say “Sabash!” either.

But however much justice there may be in the declared objective or policy, if truth is abandoned in the means used to attain it, or if violence raises its head, then we are people who give second place to achieving the objective and first place to an unwavering, truthful method.

On many issues — the Sri Lankan Tamil question, protest against price rises and others — we have conducted struggles in the name of people’s welfare and people’s rights, sometimes standing independently as the Kazhagam and sometimes with the support of other parties.

Even in those struggles, when we conducted rail-blocking or bus-blocking agitations, we did not announce them as sudden, unspecified events. It would be announced that on a particular date, at a particular place, in the morning or evening, these named comrades would take part in a rail blockade or bus blockade at this time. It would be stated where they would start from and at what hour they would reach the place.

The police too would go there, arrest them and put them in prison. This is not a practice of today or yesterday. The source traces it from the Dravidian movement’s language agitation in 1938 against compulsory Hindi, when Tamil volunteers, on Thanthai Periyar’s instructions, protested at the entrance of the Thondai Mandala High School in Chennai, down to the present.

In one or two protests, because people from other parties entered and created trouble, or because of excesses by the police, even orderly peaceful agitations may exceptionally have turned disorderly. But under no circumstances, the author says, has the Dravidian movement — particularly the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam — been a movement that adopted such a disorderly method as its chosen mode of struggle.

Now, however, the Left, BJP and AIADMK join together. Even though senior leaders in that front such as Advani stressed that peace must not be disturbed in the agitation, either in violation of that advice or because some understood Advani’s advice to mean precisely this —

when one sees, in Patna, a contingent of women marching out to block the road and the gleaming long knives in their hands, one is compelled to wonder whether those who conducted the “Bharat Bandh” actually wanted such scenes.

In any event, as far as Tamil Nadu was concerned, the source says the total shutdown had no support. Apart from scattered stone-throwing at buses and about **200 people** being arrested and kept in custody, there was no other effect. Even newspapers supportive of the front reported that normal life was paralysed mainly in States ruled by the BJP and Left parties.

From this, the author argues, it should be understood that however much major parties such as the AIADMK and BJP line up together in Tamil Nadu, the slogan of price rise and its effects did not affect the people of Tamil Nadu to the same extent as in other States; that the State Government was carrying out its duty of protecting them from such effects; and that this was why the bandh of the BJP-AIADMK-Left alliance, expected to loom so large, did not succeed in Tamil Nadu.

By saying this, we are not waving a white fly-whisk in praise of price rises. But if one considers that in Tamil Nadu, unlike in other States, rice is available at **₹1 a kilogram**, grocery items can be obtained at subsidised prices, and bus fares have not been raised, the full truth becomes clear: the impact of price rises has been kept away from ordinary and poor people, the author argues.

For those who refuse to think about this and merely count the days until their own rule may return, no benefit is going to come from “Bharat Bandh,” road blockades or the brandishing of long knives!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**7-7-2010**

## Letter-specific notes

1. `பந்த்` is retained as **bandh**, matching the source’s political vocabulary.
2. The ₹10,000-crore and ₹13,000-crore loss figures are preserved as source-reported estimates, not independently validated economic measurements.
3. Claims about violence, arrests, State-by-State participation and the effects of Tamil Nadu welfare measures remain source-attributed.
4. The source’s hierarchy — truthful/non-violent method first, tactical victory second — is preserved explicitly.

வீசுபவர்கள் அல்ல நாம்; மற்ற மாநிலங்களைப் போல அல்லாமல்

தமிழ் மாநிலத்தில் கிலோ அரிசி ஒரு ரூபாய் என்பதும் - மானிய
விலையில் மளிகைச் சாமான்களைப் பெற முடியும் என்பதும் - பஸ்
கட்டண உயர்வில்லை என்பதும் - விலைவாசி உயர்வின் பாதிப்பை
சாதாரண - சாமான்ய மக்களிடமிருந்து அகற்றியிருப்பதை
எண்ணிப் பார்த்தால் முழு உண்மையும் புலப்படும்.
எண்ணிப் பார்க்க முன்வராமல்;
எப்போது தங்கள் ஆட்சி வரும் என்பதற்காக மாத்திரம்
நாள்களை எண்ணிக் கொண்டிருப்போருக்கு;
“பாரத் பந்த்” - பாதை மறியல் - பட்டாக் கத்தி வீச்சு -
இவற்றால் எந்தப் பயனும் விளையப் போவதில்லை!
அன்புள்ள,
மு.க.
