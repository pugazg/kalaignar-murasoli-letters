# 3497. Can Advocacy of Violence Be Truth?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation follows the audited canonical Tamil and the locked Volume 44 conventions. It preserves the letter’s political argument about the Nutritious Meal employees’ agitation, A. B. Bardhan’s reported remarks, the Naxalite/Maoist history and the quoted positions from Communist publications without outside reconciliation. The complete audited Tamil is reproduced below and remains authoritative.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3497](../../../chapters/3497-vanmuraikku-vakkaalaththu-vaaimai-aagumaa.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 118](../../../pages/page-118.md)–[PDF 124](../../../pages/page-124.md)  
**Date:** 4 September 2010

**Udanpirappē,**

Comrade A. B. Bardhan, who serves at the all-India level as General Secretary of the Communist Party of India and as a representative of the Left parties, is not merely a person whose nature is to string together calm words and argue even on matters that could provoke anger. He is also a person who does not regard as enemies, and spit venom at, public figures who are not inseparably merged with the Left movements. Therefore, although it is difficult to believe that it was he who spoke in this manner, his interview containing harsh and biting criticism about our DMK and about me seems to have been influenced by poisonous views about us sown by somebody. I am consequently placed in a position where I must offer an explanation.

When I read Bardhan’s reported statement that “the Chief Minister has attacked the Communists out of fear of defeat,” I could only laugh. I have faced so many defeats in my political life. I have neither withered because of them nor attacked anyone in the manner Comrade Bardhan suggests.

In the issue that has arisen between the DMK government and one or two associations of Nutritious Meal employees, we are in a position where we must explain the stand taken by the government. In doing so, we have pointed out that this is one of the agitations being incited by the Communist party here with the election in mind; that it has no public support; and, going further, that a large section of the Nutritious Meal employees themselves — indeed, even those who run various associations for those employees — do not support this agitation. Only a few months ago, many thousands of those employees came to Chennai from across Tamil Nadu and held a conference to thank me. We have also pointed out that all the demands now being put forward by one particular association have not been fulfilled in any State, not even in States ruled by Communists.

When, after all this, such agitations are incited and expanded from road blockades to declarations that the very Secretariat Fort will be besieged, the duty and responsibility to safeguard law and order cannot be avoided by any government, whichever party runs it.

Demonstration —

war-like agitation —

siege of the Fort —

an invasion in buses from all over the State —

if all these are advertised in the newspapers, a date is fixed, and people set out in procession, a government has both the duty and the right under law to prevent them and to arrest those who defy the law. For some reason, they do not appear to understand this.

As far as the DMK government is concerned, whether they are Nutritious Meal employees or any other employees, their demands have never been ignored. Nor have we failed to hold talks with them. Indeed, whenever Pay Commissions have been appointed for Union Government employees and the Union Government has accepted their recommendations, and whenever Dearness Allowance has been increased by the Union Government, Tamil Nadu has been the State that has announced and implemented those recommendations ahead of all the other States. No government employee would come forward to deny that. The phrase that the period of DMK rule is the golden age of government employees has become firmly established.

But without learning in detail how this problem arose, Comrade A. B. Bardhan, who came from Delhi, seems to have taken as truth what the Communists here told him and showered heated words upon us.

Very well; let that pass. Bardhan has also criticised my painful observation that the fire they lit has spread in some States of India in the form of Maoist attacks. He said that there is no connection between the Maoists and the Communists and, as though I had made a baseless accusation, became angry and offered an exposition.

I wish to remind him of one thing.

The Naxalite organisation began in West Bengal after the 1967 election. At that time they were called “Naxalbaris.” Later, when their movement spread to some other States, they came to be called “Naxalites.”

Where did the Naxalites emerge from? From the Communist parties themselves — particularly by breaking away from the Communist Party of India (Marxist), they formed a new movement.

At that time they accused the two Communist parties of having abandoned and buried the great doctrine of Marxism-Leninism by returning to the democratic path and deciding to contest elections to the Legislative Assemblies and Parliament.

They loudly declared that revolution could never be created through democracy or entry into the legislatures, and that the dictatorship of the proletariat could not be brought about that way. They called the two Communist parties revisionists and deviationists.

In the interview he gave at Coimbatore, Comrade Bardhan himself conceded: “Karunanidhi often points to the States ruled by Communists. In some matters that is correct.” If, as he says, there is no relationship whatsoever between Maoists and Communists, why did the Communist parties oppose the Union Government’s decision when it banned the Maoists and declared them terrorists? Did not CPI(M) General Secretary Prakash Karat state that this problem should be solved politically and that a ban would be of no use? Did not Communist Party of India Member of Parliament M. P. Gurudas Gupta say that instead of declaring the Maoists a terrorist movement and banning them, they should have been confronted and defeated politically?

If the Communist parties have no affection for the Maoists, how was the interview given by Sahana Pradhan — Nepal’s Minister for External Relations and a standing-committee member of the Nepal Communist Party — published in the 3-4-2008 issue of *Theekathir*? Was it not published that, in that interview, she said there had arisen a necessity for Maoists to participate in the national mainstream and in general people’s movements? Was that all?

The 14-4-2008 editorial in *Janasakthi* said:

> “With monarchical rule ended, the infant sound of a newly born democracy has begun to be heard sweetly from Nepal. The Maoists are poised to win after receiving a large number of votes. We can firmly believe that Nepal will now gain a new life. At the same time as we congratulate the Maoists, it is also important that they demonstrate by establishing the finest form of communist rule.”

That is not something that can simply be forgotten.

In an article written by C. Mahendran in the 30-4-2008 issue of *Janasakthi*, it was stated: “Nepal had continued to live without any change on the path of a monarchy marked by extremely backward anarchy. It is a truth demonstrated in the world that changes in any country can be achieved only by Communist revolutionaries. No one can forget the truth that in the civil war between the valiant Maoist revolutionaries and the king, the United States of America and European countries supplied arms to the monarchy.” Was that not written in support of the Maoists?

In an article written by Se. Kamarasu in the 9-5-2008 issue of *Janasakthi*, was it not written: “Removing the mask of being the world’s only Hindu State, Nepal is about to wear the crown of a secular Nepali republic. A foundation-stone ceremony is about to be held to establish a democratic federal republic by demolishing the crumbling old fortress of a religious-fundamentalist monarchy. The people have entrusted the great task of building a new Nepal to the Maoists. The Maoist party has emerged victorious as the single largest party”?

Why go that far? Did not Comrade A. B. Bardhan himself, who has now given an interview in Coimbatore, say that “the victory of the Nepal Maoists will bring change in South Asia”?

If the Communists have neither affection nor attachment towards the Maoists, why were all these views published in their journals? This is precisely what I meant when I wrote that the fire they had lit had become “Maoist” attacks in some States of India. What justice is there, then, in everyone from Bardhan to the Communists here leaping at me for that?

We can at least understand that there may be some ulterior motive behind those here pouncing upon us. But should Bardhan, who is capable of thinking and speaking deeply and seriously on any matter, lend support to the calls for violent agitation issued by the Communists here?

**Note:-** At the end of May this year, when journalists met me and asked, “Naxalites and Maoists have recently been attacking members of the public. Handling that problem has become a major question before the Union Government. What is your view?”, I answered:

> “Their ideals may be lofty. Their policies may be admirable. But the methods they are now adopting to fulfil them are completely contrary to humanism. Even today (30-5-2010), if one looks at a newspaper, photographs have been published of those who died when a train was wrecked. Among them is a most pitiful scene — passengers carrying the corpse of a dead child. That child had no difference of opinion whatsoever with the Mao movement. Yet now a new method — an old method, really, but one being practised as though new — is developing in which, without caring about the deaths of men, women, children and others in society, the only concern is that their objective must be achieved.
>
> “It is not good for that attitude to grow. The leaders of those movements must consider whether this method is right within the communist principles that form the basis of their movements. This is not something I began saying today or yesterday; it is a view I have expressed for a long time. I voice that view from one corner of India — from Tamil Nadu — and I hope the Mao leaders will respect it and stand as a supporting strength in protecting humanism.”

Udanpirappē, as I write this letter to you today, I have included this view of mine precisely so that those concerned may also think about it.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**4-9-2010**
