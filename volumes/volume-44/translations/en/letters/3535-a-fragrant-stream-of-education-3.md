**Udanpirappē,**

I continue the account of the achievements and schemes carried out by the DMK government in the School Education Department during the past five years.

## Uniform System of School Education

To implement a uniform system of school education, the **Tamil Nadu Uniform System of School Education Act, 2010** was enacted. The uniform system was implemented from the **2010–2011 academic year** in **Classes 1 and 6 in all schools in Tamil Nadu**, and new textbooks were prepared and supplied.

For **2011–2012**, a common syllabus for the remaining classes under the uniform system has been prepared and textbooks have also been created.

For linguistic minorities in Tamil Nadu whose mother tongues include Urdu, Telugu, Kannada and Malayalam, it was recently announced that, while learning Tamil as a subject, they may additionally study their mother tongue as a subject, and that a minimum pass mark would be prescribed for it as with Tamil.

## Fixing fees in private schools

To regulate fee collection in private schools, the **Tamil Nadu Schools (Regulation of Collection of Fee) Act, 2009** was enacted and brought into force from **7-12-2009**.

## Free bicycles

Under the free-bicycle scheme, from **2006–2007**, **21 lakh 28 thousand 938 students** benefited at an expenditure of **₹487 crore 35 lakh**.

Under the scheme described by the source, students who pass Class 10 are given **₹1,250 for boys and ₹1,500 for girls** by the Tamil Nadu government. Likewise, students who pass Class 12 are given **₹1,750 for boys and ₹2,000 for girls**.

From **2006–2007 through 2009–2010**, a total of **₹20 crore 11 lakh 70 thousand** was given to **1 lakh 24 thousand 702 students**.

A law was enacted on **31-5-2006** making Tamil-language education compulsory from **Class 1 through Class 10**, and it has been implemented.

In States such as Andhra Pradesh, Kerala, Karnataka and Maharashtra, students taking Tamil as a subject in the Class 10 and Class 12 public examinations and securing the first three places in each State are awarded:

- first prize: **₹10,000**;
- second prize: **₹5,000**;
- third prize: **₹3,000**.

## Exemption from examination fees

Students studying through Tamil medium in Classes 10 and 12 in government and government-aided schools are exempted from public-examination fees, benefiting approximately **10 lakh students each year**.

## Additional examination time

After the DMK government assumed office, students writing the Class 10 and Class 12 public examinations were allowed an additional **15 minutes** to read their question papers properly and organise their answer sheets. The letter says this was widely welcomed by students and parents.

## Abolition of special fees

From the **2008–2009 academic year**, the Tamil Nadu government abolished special fees payable by students in government and government-aided schools and assumed an expenditure of **₹21 crore 40 lakh**. Approximately **50 lakh students each year** benefit.

To prevent Scheduled Caste and Scheduled Tribe girl students from dropping out, unmarried girls under age 16 who have passed Class 8 and are studying in Class 9 have **₹3,000 each** deposited in their names in nationalised banks.

Under this scheme:

- in **2008–2009**, **1 lakh 21 thousand 292 girls** benefited at a Union Government expenditure of **₹36.38 crore**;
- in **2009–2010**, **1 lakh 9 thousand 170 girls** benefited at an expenditure of **₹32 crore 75 lakh**.

For **150 students** who came to Tamil Nadu as refugees from Sri Lanka, `“O” level` examinations were conducted with the assistance of the Tamil Nadu Government Examinations Department to help them pursue higher education and obtain employment.

Difficult portions of the Class 11 and 12 syllabi were removed and simplified. The changes in Tamil, English, Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Botany and Zoology have been implemented from **2006–2007**.

## Education Development Day

The DMK government enacted a law in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly requiring the birthday of Perunthalaivar Kamarajar to be celebrated as **Education Development Day**. From **2007–2008**, separate funds have been allocated to every school to celebrate it, honour Kamarajar and spread educational awareness.

The nutritious-meal programme was expanded as follows:

- from **2006–2007**, two eggs a week;
- from **2007–2008**, three eggs a week;
- from **2008–2009**, bananas for children who do not eat eggs under the midday-meal scheme;
- from **2009–2010**, five eggs a week.

The free-textbook scheme, free bus travel for school students, and provision at school itself of caste, residence and income certificates for Class 10 and 12 students continue to be implemented.

## New school buildings through donors

A 1998 DMK-government order allowed donors who provided buildings at their own cost — or equivalent funds — to have a name of their choice inscribed on the building, at these levels:

- government primary school: **2 classrooms or ₹5 lakh**;
- middle school: **3 classrooms or ₹7 lakh 50 thousand**;
- high school: **4 classrooms or ₹15 lakh**;
- higher secondary school: **6 classrooms or ₹20 lakh**.

The previous AIADMK government cancelled that order by a Government Order dated **9-2-2005**. After assuming office in 2006, this government issued an order on **22-6-2006** allowing a donor who gave land, money or a school building to a government school to have a name of the donor’s choice inscribed on the school building.

## Yoga education

Under this programme, yoga is fully taught along with physical education in schools. So far, **7,210 physical-education teachers** have received yoga training, and trained teachers provide yoga instruction to students. **46 lakh 60 thousand 812 students** have benefited.

## Environmental clubs and National Green Corps

The purpose of the environmental club is to create environmental and social awareness among students.

Through the Environment and Forest Department, **1,200 schools** with environmental clubs receive State Government assistance of **₹1,250 each**.

**7,500 schools** with National Green Corps units receive Union Government assistance of **₹2,500 each**.

For implementation, one Assistant Director (Environment) post at District Educational Officer level has been appointed at State level, and district-level coordinator posts at Postgraduate Teacher (Botany/Biology) level have also been appointed.

## Teacher awards

The **Best Teacher Award**, given since 1960 to teachers performing exceptionally in the State, has been given as the **Dr. Radhakrishnan Award** since **1997**.

The award amount was increased:

- from **₹1,000 to ₹2,000** from 1997;
- from **₹2,000 to ₹5,000** from 2007.

Award-winning teachers receive a silver medal and certificate along with the cash award.

## English-language and mathematics laboratories

From **2006–2007** to date, English-language laboratories have been installed through ELCOT in **76 government higher secondary schools** at a cost of **₹3 crore 80 lakh**.

During **2008–2009 and 2009–2010**, computer centres functioning in **1,500 government higher secondary schools** were converted into language laboratories through ELCOT at a cost of **₹1 crore 56 lakh**, to improve students’ English-language proficiency.

During **2008–2009 and 2009–2010**, mathematics laboratories were established in **200 government high and higher secondary schools** at a cost of **₹2 crore**.

In **2009–2010**, a voice/speech training scheme for English-language teachers was introduced at a cost of **₹20 lakh**, and systematic training was given to all English teachers.

## Directorate of Public Libraries

In district library authorities, **1,282 librarians** who had been working as part-time and rural librarians on monthly consolidated pay of **₹1,500** were appointed from **28-10-2006** as Grade III Librarians on the regular pay scale of **₹3,200-85-4,900**.

Because rural libraries had been operating with daily-wage workers for various administrative reasons, **865 rural-librarian posts** have now been filled and those libraries are functioning.

To provide modern library facilities suited to the times, computer and internet facilities have been established in **131 libraries**: the Connemara Public Library, **30 district central libraries**, and **100 libraries** functioning in corporation/municipal areas.

To upgrade library service, **166 part-time libraries** were upgraded as rural libraries and orders were issued to open **169 new rural libraries**. **335 rural-librarian posts** were sanctioned and librarians appointed to manage them.

## Purchase of new books

Each year, good Tamil and English books are purchased for libraries so that people can learn scientific developments and use leisure time productively. From **2006–2007 through the current financial year**:

- Tamil books: **11,837 titles**, costing **₹45.14 crore**;
- English books: **5,466 titles**, costing **₹6.03 crore**;
- total: **₹51.17 crore**.

To help weaker sections and young people take competitive examinations conducted by the Union Public Service Commission, Tamil Nadu Public Service Commission, Railway Recruitment Board and banking recruitment bodies, **50 additional civil-services centres** were established in district central libraries and libraries in municipal areas, in addition to the **150 centres** already functioning.

## New library buildings

To move libraries that had been functioning in rented buildings without adequate facilities into proper buildings, **93 library buildings** were constructed across Tamil Nadu at a cost of **₹10.56 crore**.

## Renovation of the old Connemara Public Library building

The **110-year-old** Connemara Public Library building was renovated by the Archaeological Survey of India with Union/State Government assistance at a cost of **₹1 crore 21 lakh**.

Tamil books had previously been purchased for public libraries in a maximum of **600 copies**. They are now purchased in **1,000 copies**.

## Library renovation conferences

To encourage reading, create library awareness and improve library facilities through public participation, the source lists the following conferences and donations:

1. **Coimbatore, Erode and Nilgiris**, conference at Coimbatore on **29-09-2007**: donations valued respectively at **₹1 crore 90 lakh 43 thousand**, **₹47 lakh 21 thousand** and **₹33 lakh 94 thousand**.
2. **Theni, Madurai and Dindigul**, conference at Theni on **17-02-2008**: donations valued respectively at **₹92 lakh 43 thousand**, **₹1 crore 23 lakh 17 thousand** and **₹1 crore 1 lakh 73 thousand**.
3. **Tiruchirappalli, Pudukkottai, Karur and Perambalur**, conference at Tiruchirappalli on **29-02-2008**: donations valued respectively at **₹1 crore 10 lakh 52 thousand**, **₹78 lakh 94 thousand**, **₹69 lakh 72 thousand** and **₹78 lakh 11 thousand**.

## Welfare Board

A separate Welfare Board for book publishers, booksellers and workers in that field was formed on **10-8-2009**.

- **2,646 members** enrolled;
- **236 people** received **₹3 lakh 38 thousand 900** in welfare assistance.

## Libraries in Samathuvapurams

Library service is being provided in **147 Samathuvapurams** through the Public Libraries Department, the All-Village Anna Renaissance Scheme and development schemes.

**100 rural libraries** were upgraded as branch libraries, and **100 Grade III Librarian posts** were sanctioned to manage them.

## Anna Centenary Library

The Anna Centenary Library was completed at Kotturpuram, Chennai and inaugurated on **15-9-2010**.

The source describes it as an eight-storey building over **3 lakh 75 thousand 770 square feet**, with:

- space for **12 lakh books**;
- seating for **1,200 people** to read at the same time;
- green-building features;
- a restaurant and accommodation for researchers;
- a modern auditorium seating **1,280**;
- an open-air theatre seating **850**.

The library was created at a cost of **₹172 crore**. A further **₹60 crore** was sanctioned for books, e-journals and e-books.

**195 new posts** were created and filled for the Anna Centenary Library.

**Udanpirappē,** this is the compilation of achievements made in the School Education Department over the past four-and-a-half years as a result of the tireless work of brother Thangam Thennarasu.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
10 March 2011
