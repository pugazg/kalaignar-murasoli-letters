# 3440. The ‘Coimbatore’ Conference in the Path of ‘Kanchi’!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> The title preserves the source’s quoted `“காஞ்சி”` / `“கோவை”` pairing and its link to Anna as the “Kanchi leader.” The translation follows the audited canonical Tamil through the detailed recollection of the 1968 Second World Tamil Conference, its statues, language-policy speech, dignitaries, procession, cultural events and closing memories. Names, dates and institutional claims remain source-framed.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3440](../../../chapters/3440-kanchi-vazhiyil-kovai-maanaadu.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 112](../../../pages/page-112.md)–[PDF 118](../../../pages/page-118.md)  
**Date:** 12 December 2009

**Udanpirappē,**

As the World Classical Tamil Conference is to be held from 23 to 27 June 2010, I wish once again to recall the events connected with the Second World Tamil Conference conducted in January 1968 by Perarignar Anna, the “Kanchi leader,” while he was Chief Minister.

As the opening event of the conference, on the evening of 1-1-1968 there was the unveiling of the statue of that worker whose labour was used day after day to straighten all the bends in our lives; the unveiling of the statue of the leader who devoted a large part of his life to make heaven walk upon this Tamil soil; the statue ceremony for the distinguished man whose speech and writing entered our blood and awakened Tamil consciousness in us; the unveiling, on Anna Salai — which was then called Mount Road and which I later had the joy of naming after him — of the statue of that Anna.

Dear friend M.G.R. bought and presented the statue at his own expense. Dr A. Ramasamy Mudaliar unveiled it. Navalar Nedunchezhiyan, who was Education Minister, delivered the welcome address. I, then Public Works Minister, accepted the statue on behalf of the government and delivered the vote of thanks. On the stage I garlanded and honoured sculptor S.P. Pillai, who had created the statue of Arignar Anna.

On that same statue-unveiling day, I released Silambu Selvar’s book *Avvaiyar*. What I said then about the medium of instruction is worth remembering even now. Participating in that function as Public Works Minister, I spoke as follows:

> “The question whether Tamil Nadu is to have Tamil or English has already been decided. It is the objective of the Tamil Nadu government that Tamil alone should be the language of government and the medium of instruction in Tamil Nadu. There is not the slightest change in this. It is only when we speak of a link language that the question ‘Hindi or English?’ arises. I wish to state that the Tamil Nadu government is not a government infatuated with English; Tamil is its objective.
>
> “Valayapathi, Kundalakesi, the *Silappathikaram* and the *Kural* should sing in classical Tamil, blessing it to live and flourish; beneath that, it is only with order and distinction that Anna’s government is eager to proceed. The dispute is only over the link language. Up to Kashmir, Punjab and Delhi, if one asks what the link language is, Hindi is named. But because we proceed on the fundamental principle ‘Every place is our town; everyone is our kin,’ we say that for communication with the whole world, English is the language. We do not accept English as the language that rules us; we welcome it only for communication with the world. Let no one have even the slightest doubt that what we hold is the sceptre of Tamil.”

This view I expressed in 1968 has continued to this day as our language policy. There is no change in it now; there will be none at any time.

On 2-1-1968, as part of the World Tamil Conference, a ceremony unveiling statues of Tamil luminaries was held near the Gandhi statue on the seafront. Under the chairmanship of Minister Navalar Nedunchezhiyan, Muthamizh Kavalar K.A.P. Viswanatham unveiled the statue of Ayyan Thiruvalluvar; under Minister Sathyavani Muthu, Mr S.S. Vasan unveiled the statue of Avvaiyar; under Minister A. Govindasamy, former Chief Minister Bhaktavatsalam unveiled the statue of Kambar; under Legislative Assembly Speaker Adithanar, Bishop Leslie Newbigin unveiled the statue of G.U. Pope; under Legislative Council Chairman Manickavelar, multilingual scholar Appathurai unveiled the statue of Caldwell; under Minister C. Madhavan, Namakkal Kavignar Ramalinganar unveiled the statue of Bharathiyar; under Minister Muthusamy, Dr Mu. Varadarasanar unveiled the statue of Bharathidasan; under Minister Mathiazhagan, Tamilavel P.T. Rajan unveiled the statue of Kappalottiya Tamizhan; under Minister Sadiq Basha, Archbishop Arulappa unveiled the statue of Veeramamunivar; and under my chairmanship, Silambu Selvar M.P.C. unveiled the statue of Kannagi, the queen of chastity.

The statue-unveiling function began with the Tamil Thai Vazhthu sung by Sirkazhi Govindarajan. Perumal Mudaliar, who was director of the Tamil Development Kazhagam, delivered the welcome address, and C.A. Ramakrishnan, then Chief Secretary of the Tamil Nadu government, gave the vote of thanks.

The opening ceremony of the Second World Tamil Conference took place on the Chennai seafront before several lakh people. Dr Zakir Husain, then President of India, inaugurated the conference. Perarignar Anna, then Chief Minister, presided. Navalar, then Education Minister, and Sathyavani Muthu, then Information Minister, delivered welcome addresses. On the same stage, ₹9 lakh was presented to the three universities of Tamil Nadu for Tirukkural research.

I handed ₹3 lakh to Dr A. Lakshmanaswami Mudaliar, then Vice-Chancellor of the University of Madras. Likewise, Food Minister K.A. Mathiazhagan handed ₹3 lakh for Annamalai University to its then Vice-Chancellor Chandra Reddy. Local Administration Minister Ma. Muthusamy gave the grant for Madurai University to Vice-Chancellor T.P. Meenakshisundaranar. M.A.M. Muthiah Chettiar thanked the gathering on behalf of the universities.

Dear friend M.G.R. spoke on behalf of the souvenir committee. Professor Filliozat, president of the International Association of Tamil Research; M. Adiseshiah, a senior official of the United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization; and Thiruchelvam, Minister of Local Government in the Sri Lankan government, delivered greetings. His Excellency Sardar Ujjal Singh, then Governor of Tamil Nadu, released the conference souvenir.

Perunthalaivar Kamaraj had come to the function. On seeing him, Anna himself requested Kamaraj to speak at the gathering. Kamaraj said from that stage:

> “Since the Chief Minister has unexpectedly asked me, I now speak to welcome the delegates. I wholeheartedly welcome all the delegates who have come to the World Tamil Research Seminar Conference. I believe that, as a result of the research seminar, Tamil will gain richness and flourish. The very first World Tamil seminar conference should have been held here. The Malaysian Tamils got ahead of us. When Bhaktavatsalam attended the first conference, he invited them to conduct the second conference in Tamil Nadu. Accordingly, the conference is being held. It must succeed. Wherever Tamils may be, they must live with pride for all time.”

For the Second World Tamil Conference, greetings had also been sent by then Prime Minister Indira Gandhi, Vice-President V.V. Giri, elder statesman Rajaji, Malaysian Minister Sambanthan, Singapore Prime Minister Lee Kuan Yew, Minister Rajaratnam and many others.

As a special feature adorning the World Tamil Conference itself, a grand procession set out on 3-1-1968 from the Island Grounds, where a complex called Poompuhar Nagar had been constructed. Lakhs of people stood on both sides of Anna Salai to watch. Chennai city wore a festival appearance that day. Although the procession began in the afternoon, people had lined the roadsides from eight in the morning.

The procession travelled along Anna Salai, Gemini Point, Dr Radhakrishnan Salai and Beach Road before returning to the Island Grounds. From a special dais erected on Anna Salai next to the College of Arts, President Dr Zakir Husain, Governor Sardar Ujjal Singh, Chief Minister Anna and the ministers watched the procession.

An art exhibition was organised in connection with the Second World Tamil Conference and was opened by elder statesman Rajaji. Chief Minister Anna released the exhibition souvenir.

From 4-1-1968 to 10-1-1968, various cultural programmes were arranged and conducted with great distinction at Poompuhar Nagar on the Island Grounds. Musical events, lecture halls, special dramas and folk dance-and-song programmes were all organised there. Professor presided over the seminar programme held at Poompuhar Nagar. The seminar took place in the Convocation Hall of the University of Madras. Tamil scholars from 40 countries around the world attended.

Although all seven days of events at Poompuhar Nagar remain memorable, I wish now to recall one in which Anna and I were involved.

A reception for a group of Tamil poets was held there. A debate arose over whether Anna was a poet. I myself presided over that function. At the beginning, I referred to Anna as a poet. Anna, who spoke next, said with the modesty appropriate to an assembly that he was not a poet. Even so, I continued:

> “The mark appropriate to a poet is modesty before the assembly. I called Anna a poet. But Anna, with such modesty, said that he was not a poet. That very modesty has shown that he is indeed a poet. For the first time, I have defeated Anna.”

When I said this humorously, the whole hall laughed with delight, and Anna rose and laughed like a little child — is that not a scene that will remain forever in my memory? V. Suba. Manickam, who spoke next on that stage, addressed him at the very opening as “Chief Minister, poet Anna.” Dr Mu. Va., when he spoke, said that by citing a verse from *Manimekalai* in his speech, Chief Minister Anna had proved that he was not merely a poet but a great poet.

In connection with that Second World Tamil Conference, Tamil Nadu film producers made a colour film of the conference and its beautiful procession. I wrote a verse-form narration explaining every scene in that film. When Anna travelled abroad, he took with him that film of the World Tamil Conference procession made with my narration and screened it for Tamils in those countries.

If I were to write in still greater detail about all the other events of that conference, it would take a long time. Arrangements were also made for the distinguished international scholars who had come to the conference to tour Tamil Nadu.

The closing-day event of the Second World Tamil Conference was held on 10-1-1968. Perarignar Anna delivered the closing address. I gave the vote of thanks.

At a time when we are preparing to conduct the World Classical Tamil Conference in Coimbatore in June, I find joy in sharing with you the events of the Second World Tamil Conference conducted by Perarignar Anna and the occasions on which I participated in it, as they rise again in my heart. Will this not also be a useful opportunity for those taking part in this conference and those serving on its various committees to plan what work they themselves should carry out?

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**12-12-2009**

## Letter-specific notes

1. The quoted title pairing `“காஞ்சி”` / `“கோவை”` is preserved in English as **‘Kanchi’ / ‘Coimbatore’** rather than flattened into a generic historical reference.
2. The 1968 language-policy passage preserves the source distinction among language of government, medium of instruction and link language.
3. The long statue and dignitary lists are retained because the source uses them as a historical template for the forthcoming Coimbatore conference.
4. Tamil literary titles and honorific labels are transliterated where an English replacement would erase the source’s cultural function.
