# 3384. Illness and I — Diary (6)

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This sixth and final diary instalment covers 21 February–1 March 2009 and then briefly records the author’s return to the Secretariat on 13 March. It closes the six-part hospital diary with the source’s explicit **(முற்றும்) / (Concluded)** marker. Political, medical and administrative claims are retained as source-framed archival material. The complete audited Tamil is reproduced below and remains authoritative.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3384](../../../chapters/3384-nalivum-naanum-naatkurippu-6.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 150](../../../pages/page-150.md)–[PDF 160](../../../pages/page-160.md)  
**Date:** 15 March 2009

*(Through this serial article I am bringing together various events from the time I was admitted to Ramachandra Hospital, through the surgery and the continuous treatments that followed.)*

★★

**Udanpirappē,**

## 21-2-2009

After Law Minister Duraimurugan read the government statement under Assembly Rule 110 and opposition parties shouted demanding a debate, leading to their removal from the House, I sat down the next morning to write an Udanpirappu letter.

In it I gave the example of 1 April 2002, during AIADMK rule, when opposition Members had all given calling-attention notices but those were not admitted and only a government statement under Rule 110 was read. No one was then permitted to ask questions or speak on it.

After giving that explanation, I wrote in the same letter that whatever had happened had happened, and that from now on everyone should cooperate so the House could function peacefully and properly.

Writing about the court events, I said the government had taken steps for a CBI inquiry; that a proper inquiry would take place; and that action would be taken against those found at fault.

I wrote that we should act without distinguishing between lawyers and police, with the thought that both sides are our own people.

One may wear khaki and the other black — but both are ours.

This evening reports kept coming by telephone that tens of thousands had participated in the “Youth Chain” organised by the Youth Wing for Sri Lankan Tamils.

Then came news that Sivaprakasam, a former area Kazhagam representative from Ward 154 who had come to participate, had set himself on fire and was being taken to hospital in a critical condition.

I told Stalin to make every effort to save his life.

Despite all efforts, he could not be saved.

I directed that ₹2 lakh in assistance be given to his family on behalf of the Kazhagam. General Secretary Professor, Treasurer M. K. Stalin, Rajya Sabha Member Kanimozhi, Organisation Secretaries T. K. S. Elangovan and P. V. Kalyanasundaram, and South Chennai District Secretary Anbazhagan went in person and handed it over.

At the request of Union Home Minister and friend P. Chidambaram, I participated from the hospital by video conference in the foundation-stone ceremony for the Sivaganga Government Medical College.

Brother Duraimurugan was with me in Chennai.

At the Sivaganga function were Union Minister P. Chidambaram, Tamil Nadu Ministers M. R. K. Panneerselvam and K. R. Periyakaruppan, M.P. Sudarsana Nachiappan, and MLAs Ramasamy and Sundaram.

After that function, at the request of Public Works Minister brother Duraimurugan, I participated — again by video conference — in the inauguration of the Tamiraparani–Nambiyar river-linking project.

At the function in Tirunelveli were Speaker R. Avudaiappan, Ministers T. P. M. Mohideen Khan, P. Geetha Jeevan and Radhika Selvi, and MLAs Appavu, Vel DuraI, Vasanthakumar and Rani Venkatesan.

That night Delhi doctor Arvind Jaiswal came to Chennai and examined me in hospital.

He said there was good improvement in my condition, but advised another four weeks of rest and said I could gradually begin sitting, standing and walking.

## 22-2-2009

The morning newspapers carried an interview with Union External Affairs Minister Pranab Mukherjee on the Sri Lankan Tamil issue.

He was quoted as saying that one of the Liberation Tigers’ aircraft had been shot down and that this was a matter of concern; that the LTTE issue could be solved only politically and not through military action; and that the only path to a solution in Sri Lanka was a political settlement reached by both sides sitting together and negotiating.

He said a calm and peaceful atmosphere had first to be created for a settlement; otherwise attacks and counter-attacks would continue.

He said India’s position on Sri Lanka was clear: India was deeply concerned about Tamil civilians trapped in the battle zone, whose normal lives had been badly affected.

He said India had asked the Sri Lankan government to protect the rights of minority Tamils; that the 1987 agreement between the late Prime Minister Rajiv Gandhi and late Sri Lankan President Jayewardene should be implemented; that powers should be devolved; that provincial councils should be given greater authority; and that Tamils and minorities in Sri Lanka should receive legal rights within the country’s constitutional framework without harming its territorial integrity or sovereignty.

India, he said, considered devolution of power the route to a solution and normalcy.

Those words from the Union Minister gave me happiness.

But reports continued that, among thirty-six thousand people temporarily displaced by the war, more than ten thousand were injured or unwell, and that over one lakh people were trapped in the affected area.

So on the twenty-second itself I wrote detailed letters to the Prime Minister and Mrs. Sonia Gandhi.

I said the Tamil Nadu Government was ready to send a medical team and medicines for Sri Lankan Tamils, and asked that urgent steps be taken to send medical teams and medicines to help the affected population.

News also came that the lawyers–police dispute had still not been resolved and that lawyers were refusing to return to work.

Some people, the source says, were acting with great care to ensure the conflict did not end — whether or not they truly cared about lawyers, they seemed concerned above all that the government should get a bad name.

Presenting themselves as intense supporters of the lawyers, they issued statement after statement demanding action against police and saying the government must accept responsibility for the police.

I therefore issued a long statement setting out what I described as the true facts in detail.

At its conclusion I announced that, until both sides expressed agreement to come together, I had decided to undertake a hunger strike while still in hospital.

No sooner was the statement released than, whether or not the two sides united, leaders of allied parties and well-wishers became extremely angry that I should think of fasting in my condition.

Even senior Ramagopalan, whose political views differ from mine, came to the hospital, waited a long time and told me firmly that I must not undertake the fast.

All members of the medical team examined me today.

Injections were reduced and tablets substituted.

It would not be an exaggeration to say that since I entered hospital the doctors had completed a “century” by giving me a hundred injections.

That afternoon, from the hospital, I inaugurated by video link BSNL’s 3G third-generation telephone service.

The first call was received by Union Minister Raja from the Anna Salai telephone exchange in Chennai where the function was held.

## 23-2-2009

There were many telephone calls from the morning, all asking about the hunger strike.

To answer those questions I wrote an Udanpirappu letter for *Murasoli*.

I learned from television that music composer A. R. Rahman had won two Oscars.

I immediately prepared a congratulatory message and had it sent to where he was staying.

The surgical wound on my back was examined again.

Dr. Marthandam removed three of the five stitches today.

## 24-2-2009

This morning Kaviperarasu brother Vairamuthu came to the hospital.

He thanked me for having been among the first to send congratulations to Rahman for the Oscars.

He read aloud to me a poem he had written under the title **“The Sun Is Unwell.”**

It was an excellent poem.

After hearing it, I gave him an affectionate kiss — on his hand!

He left overflowing with happiness, saying that he had received an award greater than Rahman’s Oscar.

The medical team had learned that I was seriously preparing to undertake the hunger strike.

They came and tried to reason with me. I angrily refused.

They then jointly issued a statement to the newspapers saying that if I undertook a hunger strike while recovering from surgery, it could cause a setback in my recovery, and that it would be wiser to consider any fast only after my health had fully returned.

Blood tests were carried out to assess blood levels and liver and kidney function.

I was told that all the results were normal.

## 25-2-2009

Today was the fourteenth day after surgery.

Delhi doctor Jaiswal met me again.

The two stitches that had not yet been removed were removed today.

They said the surgical wound had healed well.

I was made to rise from the bed and stand, and also made to sit in a chair.

The doctors issued a statement saying that I needed another four weeks of rest and could return home on March 1.

Agriculture Minister brother Veerapandi Arumugam issued a statement in the newspapers saying that if I refused to give up my hunger strike, he would himself fast until death.

Tamil leader younger brother Veeramani said in a statement that my hunger strike caused him great mental pain; he appealed to both lawyers and police to cooperate toward a good solution as a humane duty; and he came personally to the hospital to appeal to me.

I had a statement made in the Assembly that injured lawyers and police personnel would each receive ₹5,000 as immediate interim relief.

I also had it announced in the Assembly that the Tamil Nadu Government would not accept the Union Government’s amendment to the Code of Criminal Procedure, which was one of the lawyers’ main demands, and that the State would write to the Union Government about it.

Viduthalai Chiruthaigal leader Thirumavalavan and Ravikumar met me in hospital and enquired about my health.

They said a member of their party from Cuddalore had died by self-immolation for Sri Lankan Tamils and asked that assistance be given to his family.

Brother Ayyappan, the DMK MLA for that constituency, had also submitted a petition.

Accepting the request, I directed that ₹50,000 be given on behalf of the Kazhagam.

Professor today delivered a long and explanatory reply to the general debate on the Financial Statement.

It was a good response, useful to the country, blending humour and civility.

## 26-2-2009

I was determined to go to the Legislative Assembly today.

The doctors firmly stopped me.

The reason I wanted to go was not merely that it was the final day of the Assembly session.

As I wrote that day under the title **“Today Is a Day of Celebration in My Life!”**, this was the day the Assembly passed the reservation Bill for the Arunthathiyar community.

I had planned to go because I wanted to move the Bill myself and have it passed.

Since the doctors prevented me, I got up in the morning and wrote in my own hand the speech I would have delivered before moving the Bill had I been present in the House.

I sent the speech to the Assembly, asked brother M. K. Stalin to move the resolution, and asked that my speech be read in the House.

Copies of my speech were made and given to opposition leaders and to newspapers.

The morning papers reported that Union Government employees had been granted an increase in dearness allowance.

I contacted the Finance Secretary and asked what the position was for our State government employees.

He said by telephone that the same increase could be announced for our employees in two days.

I asked: why after two days? Why not announce it today?

He assured me it could be done.

From the hospital itself I immediately had the announcement prepared and sent to the newspapers.

Accordingly, State government employees were granted the same dearness-allowance increase as Union employees, with retrospective effect.

The very next day several employees’ associations issued statements thanking the government, and some representatives even came to the hospital to thank me.

A new problem then appeared: small ulcers in my mouth and on my tongue.

ENT specialist Dr. Ravikumar and gastroenterology specialist Dr. Palanisamy examined me, conducted further tests and prescribed new medicines.

That evening I was seated in a wheelchair — one that could also be used as a bed or reclining chair — and taken to the ground floor.

I spent more than half an hour in the beautiful garden opposite the hospital, drinking tea with doctors and Ministers.

## 27-2-2009

Because of the ulcers in my mouth and tongue I could not eat properly.

Such is the force of medicines!

This evening too I went downstairs in the wheelchair and rested for a while in the garden.

I met and spoke with important visitors who had come to see me while remaining there.

## 28-2-2009

The doctors had said that after more than a month in hospital I could return home tomorrow, so I was happy today.

Cardiologist Dr. Thanikachalam and hospital owner Venkatachalam asked me to inaugurate, from my room, a newly established cardiac electrophysiology unit in Ramachandra Hospital’s heart department.

I inaugurated it that evening from the room itself.

Afterward came an emotional farewell in which I exchanged affection with the hospital’s doctors, junior medical staff and employees before returning home on March 1.

It impressed upon my mind that this was not merely a hospital, but our own home.

Brother Stalin, Kazhagam Treasurer and Youth Wing Secretary, had built the renovated **Anbagam** with great effort.

He asked me to inaugurate it on my way home the next day, March 1.

I agreed.

## 1-3-2009

I woke early that morning.

The thought was that I was returning home after thirty-three days.

District secretaries told me that receptions had been arranged all along the route and that I should receive them while remaining in the van.

The welcoming crowd extended all the way to Ramachandra Hospital.

Ministers and Kazhagam leaders came to the hospital itself to welcome me and accompany me.

On the way home, remaining in the van, I inaugurated the renovated Anbagam building.

At the entrance to my house my elder sister was waiting to welcome me, seated in another wheelchair.

Today neither my mother nor father is alive.

My eldest sister Periyanayagam is no more.

My beloved Murasoli Maran, whom I raised like a son, is no more.

Only my younger sister remains.

People in our village used to say even when we were children that her hip had bent from carrying me so often.

When I saw that younger sister waiting at the entrance for me, neither of us could speak. We were overcome with emotion.

After returning home and resting for ten days, I went to the Secretariat on 13.3.2009.

Journalists had gathered.

I told them that when I came and sat as Chief Minister for the fifth time in May 2006, the first day I signed files was also the thirteenth — and today too was the thirteenth.

I then signed the file establishing the Ulema Welfare Board.

I met journalists, discussed matters with officials, and met Sudarsanam and Peter Alphonse, who had come on behalf of Congress to congratulate me.

The “machine” that was said to have taken a “rebirth” had now prepared itself to work continuously again.

**(Concluded)**

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**15-3-2009**
