**Udanpirappē,**

“Without preventing the deaths of Eelam Tamils, Karunanidhi thinks of placing flowers on their grave — D. Pandian’s accusation.” That is the `Pena` item published in the 7 June 2012 issue of *Janasakthi*. It was in an interview given to journalists at Tiruvarur that “D.” Pandian levelled this accusation against us.

The Pudukkottai Assembly constituency by-election is to be held on 12 June. It is being held because Muthukumaran, who had been an Indian Communist Party member from that constituency, died in an accident at a young age. In the last general election, when Muthukumaran contested as the Indian Communist Party candidate, that party was an ally of the AIADMK. The AIADMK and the Indian Communist Party are still allies. In that situation, according to political civility and democratic practice, the Indian Communist Party ought to have contested the Pudukkottai by-election on behalf of the AIADMK alliance. But because of the very large majority it obtained in the last general election, the AIADMK does not value its allied parties even as dust. As evidence of that, although the Indian Communist Party had contested and won Pudukkottai last time, the AIADMK did not even call them as a matter of courtesy. It unilaterally announced that the AIADMK would contest and named its candidate.

The Indian Communist Party did not express even a little regret over this. Yet the CPI(M) and some other parties suggested that if the Indian Communist Party fielded a candidate in the constituency it had won last time, the others could treat that person as a common candidate, refrain from contesting and extend support. The Indian Communist Party did not accept even that.

Not only did they, with such great magnanimity(?), surrender this much “self-respect” to the ruling party; yesterday *Janasakthi* itself carried on its front page a photograph of Tamil Nadu Communist leaders D. Pandian and Nallakannu going all the way to the Secretariat and meeting the Chief Minister “as a courtesy.” The two of them also took the wife and children of Comrade Muthukumaran, who had won Pudukkottai and died an untimely death, to meet Chief Minister Jayalalithaa. Jayalalithaa too agreed to meet them, perhaps fearing that the people of Pudukkottai might think about the AIADMK’s ungrateful act and refuse to vote for it in the by-election. That is their party’s problem; it is the problem of that party’s cadres; let it go as it may. But why should D. Pandian go to Tiruvarur and needlessly give an interview saying that “without preventing the deaths of Eelam Tamils, Karunanidhi thinks of placing flowers on their grave”?

Did we truly make no effort to prevent the deaths of Eelam Tamils?

When the final war was taking place in Sri Lanka in 2008–09 and the Eelam Tamil people were shedding blood and life in those dreadful circumstances, did we do nothing to prevent such a situation from arising in Sri Lanka? On 14 October 2008, did the DMK Government not convene an all-party leaders’ meeting on the Sri Lankan Tamil issue and pass a resolution that if the Government of India did not come forward to bring about a ceasefire in Sri Lanka within two weeks, all Members of Parliament from Tamil Nadu would have to resign? Following that, did not the Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha members from Tamil Nadu bring their resignation letters and hand them to me? Was that resolution not sent to the Prime Minister on the very day it was passed through Thambi T.R. Baalu? After examining it, did not the Prime Minister assure Baalu that the Union Government would quickly take every step toward a political solution?

Did the Prime Minister not say in an interview:

> “The ethnic problem in Sri Lanka is a matter of grave concern. A military solution to this problem will not be proper. A political solution must be found through negotiations in a manner that respects Sri Lanka’s sovereignty and the human rights of the Tamils living there. The Union Government will make a fully committed effort to find a solution to the Sri Lankan ethnic problem.”

On 18 October 2008, did Prime Minister Dr. Manmohan Singh not directly speak by telephone with Sri Lankan President Rajapaksa? On 22 October, did Union Minister Pranab Mukherjee not state in Parliament that the Sri Lankan Government had been pressed to provide proper protection to Sri Lankan Tamils? On 24 October, did we not conduct a massive “human chain” in Chennai? On 26 October, did Pranab Mukherjee not come to Chennai and speak with me for more than an hour about the Sri Lankan Tamil issue? Did he not then tell journalists in Chennai that I had insisted that the ceasefire brought about through Norway’s initiative be ensured to continue, and that the Sri Lankan Government had assured humanitarian assistance through the International Red Cross, the United Nations and the Government of India to more than two lakh Tamils suffering in the war zones?

On 12 November 2008, did I not move a resolution in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly for the Sri Lankan Tamils and have it passed? Did Pandian not have the mind to compare that resolution with the resolution Jayalalithaa had earlier passed in the same Assembly against the Sri Lankan Tamil struggle? On 4 December 2008, did I not take leaders of all parties in Tamil Nadu to Delhi, meet the Prime Minister and request that Union Minister Pranab Mukherjee be sent to Sri Lanka over the Tamil issue? Did Pranab not thereafter go personally to Sri Lanka and hold talks with Rajapaksa? On 27 December 2008, did the DMK General Council not pass a resolution on the Sri Lankan Tamil issue?

After Tamil leader K. Veeramani, PMK leader Dr. Ramadoss and Viduthalai Chiruthaigal leader Thol. Thirumavalavan met me on 12 January 2009, did I not send then Union Minister T.R. Baalu and Rajya Sabha member Kanimozhi to explain the situation to the Prime Minister and Sonia Gandhi?

Were all these not efforts by us to prevent the deaths of Eelam Tamils? We who did all this, it seems, did not think of preventing their deaths; but D. Pandian goes and meets Jayalalithaa—who, in an interview published in newspapers on 18 January 2009, said: “Sri Lanka is another country, and therefore there is a limit to interfering in that country’s problem. A country called Eelam has not yet been formed in Sri Lanka. The Sinhala army did not intend to kill Sri Lankan Tamils. When a war takes place, innocent people being killed is natural. No country is an exception to this”—and then accuses me. Is that just?

On 28 March 2009 I wrote to the Prime Minister and Mrs Sonia Gandhi asking India to help bring about an immediate ceasefire in Sri Lanka. On 31 March Sonia Gandhi sent me a long reply saying, among other things, that she wished to assure me of the various measures taken by the UPA Government regarding recent developments in Sri Lanka; that the continuing war and the immense sufferings endured by innocent Tamils there, including mothers and children, caused grave concern; that every effort necessary to protect them and safeguard their welfare was expected of the Sri Lankan authorities; that a ceasefire must, in truth, be the first step; that our Government was in contact with Sri Lanka on many matters, especially the ceasefire; and that only after a ceasefire could innocent people move to safe areas. Was all that not true?

When journalists met me on 5 April 2009 and asked about reports that the army had captured all LTTE-held areas, destroyed its military bases, and that Prabhakaran and his son were hiding in a Tamil-inhabited area, I replied: “For the past six months we have continuously been saying that there must be a ceasefire. Respecting our voice, foreign governments, the United Nations and foreign diplomats have been telling Sri Lanka this. The Government of India has asked them to stop the war. Yet Rajapaksa has not stopped it. We strongly condemn that.” Was that not because the deaths of Eelam Tamils had to be prevented?

On 7 April 2009 I sent a telegram to Prime Minister Manmohan Singh, Mrs Sonia Gandhi, External Affairs Minister Pranab Mukherjee and Home Minister P. Chidambaram asking that “the entire Tamil people in Sri Lanka be saved from being annihilated wholesale, that a ceasefire be ensured there, and that immediate steps be taken for peace talks.” Would D. Pandian not know this? In that final war, it was not only I and not only the Government of India: major countries such as the United States, Britain, Japan and Norway issued statements asking that Sri Lankan Tamils be protected and that Sri Lankan warplanes stop bombing the safe zone. Beyond that, United Nations Secretary-General Ban Ki-moon himself telephoned President Rajapaksa and asked that Sri Lankan Tamils be protected and humanitarian action be taken. A senior UN official also spoke with Rajapaksa at that time.

On 9 April 2009, under my leadership in Chennai, a massive procession was held raising the slogan “Government of Sri Lanka, stop the war.” Rajapaksa announced a two-day ceasefire in Sri Lanka. The Government of India invited pro-LTTE party MPs to come to India for talks. In an interview on 12 April 2009, Chandrahasan, son of Eelam leader Chelvanayakam, said: “Chief Minister Kalaignar’s statesmanlike request was what brought about the Sri Lankan ceasefire.” Union Home Minister and friend P. Chidambaram said in a statement on 14 April that India would continue to press Sri Lanka for the ceasefire to continue. Tamil leader younger brother K. Veeramani stated that Rajapaksa’s Government had announced a 48-hour ceasefire due to pressure from the world’s countries, that the continuous pressure exerted by Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Kalaignar was a principal reason, and that the Government of India had not merely stood watching.

On 21 April 2009, in a telegram sent to Prime Minister Dr. Manmohan Singh, UPA Chairperson Mrs Sonia Gandhi and then External Affairs Minister Pranab Mukherjee, I said: “The war situation in Sri Lanka is dangerous. The sufferings endured by Tamils have reached a very perilous stage. All countries, including the United Nations, have pressured the Sri Lankan Government to announce an immediate ceasefire. To save the lives of lakhs of Tamils in Sri Lanka, I urge that a final warning be issued to the Sri Lankan Government to bring about an immediate and permanent ceasefire.” What was all this for, if not to stop the deaths of Eelam Tamils?

On 21 April 2009 I also wrote an emotional Udanpirappu letter titled “An Appeal to the Union Government to Protect the Helpless Eelam Tamils.” To protect Sri Lankan Tamils and press the Union Government to take immediate steps toward a ceasefire, I issued a statement calling for a one-day strike on 23 April 2009. Seeking cooperation from all parties, I wrote to the leaders of the Congress, AIADMK, CPI(M), CPI, Dravidar Kazhagam, BJP, MDMK, DMDK, Viduthalai Chiruthaigal, Indian Union Muslim League, MGR Kazhagam, PMK, Puthiya Tamilagam and other parties, as well as to the newspapers. The strike took place peacefully and successfully.

On the basis of my telegram, the Prime Minister convened an emergency meeting in Delhi on the night of 22 April 2009 with Union Ministers and officials and asked Sri Lanka immediately to stop the war against innocent Tamils. A high-level group of the Prime Minister’s special envoys went to Colombo to press this directly on the Sri Lankan Government. On the same day, relief materials weighing 936 metric tonnes, worth ₹6 crore 46 lakh 16 thousand, packed in 40,000 parcels, were sent to the Indian High Commission in Sri Lanka for the affected Tamils there. The Indian high-level delegation met Rajapaksa in Colombo and conveyed India’s request that steps be taken immediately to stop the war. On the night of 24 April 2009, then Home Minister P. Chidambaram came to Anna Arivalayam, met me and explained all the details. The Prime Minister too spoke with me by telephone that day and described the Union Government’s efforts. Were these not efforts taken by the DMK, by me as its leader and by the DMK Government to prevent the deaths of Eelam Tamils?

Yet D. Pandian hides all this and gives an interview saying that I merely think of placing flowers on the grave. Is that right?

Let D. Pandian answer what *Dinamani* wrote—that the reason the Indian Communist Party, against the wishes of its cadres, did not field its own candidate in Pudukkottai and yielded the seat to the AIADMK was that the AIADMK had agreed to give it a Rajya Sabha seat. At least from now on, let D. Pandian stop needlessly finding fault with the DMK. **If I elaborate, it will grow; I leave it here!**

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**8 June 2012**

ஒரு சிறு வருத்தம்கூடத் தெரிவிக்கவில்லை. இருந்தாலும் மார்க்சிஸ்ட் கம்யூனிஸ்ட் கட்சியைப் போன்ற வேறு சில கட்சிகள், அந்தத் தொகுதியிலே கடந்த முறை வெற்றி பெற்ற இந்தியக் கம்யூனிஸ்ட் கட்சியின் சார்பில் வேட்பாளர் நிறுத்தப்பட்டால், அவரைப் பொது வேட்பாளராகக் கருதி மற்ற கட்சிகள் எல்லாம் போட்டியிடாமல் ஆதரவு தெரிவிக்கலாம் என்றுகூடச் சொல்லிப் பார்த்தார்கள். அதையும் இந்தியக் கம்யூனிஸ்ட் கட்சி ஏற்றுக் கொள்ளவில்லை.

புதுக்கோட்டையில் இந்தியக் கம்யூனிஸ்ட் கட்சி தனது கட்சியின் தொண்டர்கள் எண்ணத்திற்கு மாறாக; தனது வேட்பாளரை நிறுத்தாமல் அ.தி.மு.க.விற்கு விட்டுக் கொடுத்ததற்கு காரணம்; மாநிலங்களவை உறுப்பினர் பதவி தர அ.தி.மு.க. ஒப்புக் கொண்டதுதான் என்று “தினமணி”யில் எழுதப்பட்டிருந்ததே, அதற்கு தா.பாண்டியன் பதில் சொல்லட்டும்! தேவையில்லாமல் தி.மு.க. மீது தா. பாண்டியன் குறை சொல்வதை இனியாவது நிறுத்திக் கொள்ளட்டும்! விரிக்கிற பெருகும்; விரிஞ்சி விடுகிறேன்!

அன்புள்ள,
மு.க.
