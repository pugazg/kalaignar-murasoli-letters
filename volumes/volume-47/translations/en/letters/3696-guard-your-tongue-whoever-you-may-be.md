# 3696. Whoever You May Be, Guard Your Tongue!

## English translation

Udanpirappē,

Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Jayalalithaa declared at the inauguration of the Pennycuick memorial hall that “I have more concern than Karunanidhi for the maintenance of the Thiruvalluvar statue.” Under the DMK Government itself, on 15 June 2000, I unveiled a statue of engineer Pennycuick in the Public Works Department campus in Madurai to honour the builder of the Periyar dam. That statue remains there even today. It is for the same engineer Pennycuick that a memorial hall has now again been built.

During the DMK Government, we decided at the Cabinet meeting of 31 December 1975 that, besides building Valluvar Kottam in Chennai, a statue of Valluvar should also be installed at the tip of Kanyakumari. Because the DMK Government was dismissed within a month, the decision could not immediately be implemented. Later, during M.G.R.'s rule, efforts were made, but they too did not come to fruition. Jayalalithaa now says I concealed this fact. I have already referred to it many times. In a *Murasoli* letter of 7 October 2003 itself, I wrote without hiding anything that “during M.G.R.'s rule an effort was made to install the statue, but it did not take complete shape.”

When the DMK returned to power in 1989, the inaugural work for installing the Valluvar statue at Kanyakumari took place. Again, because our Government was removed within two years, the work slowed. That is the beginning of the history of the Thiruvalluvar-statue project.

Jayalalithaa now claims greater concern for its maintenance. If, during her five years in office from 1991 to 1996, she had genuinely shown such concern, she could have completed the construction already begun under the DMK Government. She did not.

When the DMK Government returned in 1996, work was accelerated with sculptor Ganapathi Sthapathi and his team at a cost of about ₹10 crore, and the opening celebrations were held grandly on 31 December 1999 and 1 January 2000. G.K. Moopanar, L. Ganesan, N. Sankaraiah, Vazhapadi Ramamurthy, R. Nallakannu, S. Thirunavukkarasu, Kumari Ananthan, R.M. Veerappan, Sivaji Ganesan, Malaysia's Dato Samy Vellu, Sri Lanka's Arumugam Thondaman, Mauritius's Ramasamy Chidambaram Pillai, Singapore's Thirunavukkarasu, Asher of Scotland, Thomas Malten of Germany, Tamil scholars, poets and learned men took part.

Jayalalithaa condemns me saying she has more concern than I do for the maintenance of the statue. Yet after the AIADMK came to power in May 2001, because the statue had been installed by the DMK Government, her Government abandoned its maintenance. In 2003 itself I wrote in *Murasoli* that 65 lights had originally been installed to illuminate the statue, but only eleven were then functioning; thousands visited daily; the Poompuhar Shipping Corporation ferried visitors; about ₹60 lakh a year came to the Government merely through visitor-related revenue; and that even a reasonable portion of this would be enough for maintenance. Because the statue stands by the sea, I also urged that chemical coating be applied regularly to protect it from salt-laden air.

I wrote then that after Thanthai Periyar organised a Tirukkural conference in Chennai around 1942 to explain Valluvar's greatness and the philosophy of the Kural, the Tirukkural moved from scholars' libraries into the hands of ordinary people. Even so, I did not wish to claim Valluvar for the Dravidian movement alone. People in every party, without party distinction, recognise the greatness of the Tirukkural and work for its growth. What is painful is when party rivalry and political bitterness enter even this field. I therefore requested in 2003 itself that the Government continue the annual chemical-treatment work needed to prevent permanent damage to the Kanyakumari statue from sea air.

Having forgotten all this, if Jayalalithaa now claims she has greater concern than I do, will the people of Tamil Nadu fail to recognise who is speaking gigantic falsehoods?

Among the foremost achievements of the DMK Government was the 133-foot, 7,000-ton statue of Ayyan Thiruvalluvar at Kanyakumari, built at a cost of ₹9.65 crore for the whole world to admire. We also planned and completed Valluvar Kottam in Chennai; urged the Union Government to institute an annual Tolkappiyar Award for a nationally distinguished Tamil scholar and a Kural Peedam Award for an internationally distinguished Tamil scholar; and these were announced in continuation of those efforts. Tamil enthusiasts know this well.

Jayalalithaa has said, in her characteristic sharp-tongued manner, that maintenance had been going on and that my statement that no chemical coating had been applied for a year and a half after the change of Government in 2011 was malicious. But if that claim were true, why did even the pro-Government *Dinamani*, which on 31 December 2012 published a half-page report titled “Thiruvalluvar Statue Damaged by Salt Air,” not receive an immediate denial from Jayalalithaa or her Government saying maintenance was in progress? Had the Government denied the report the very next day and said maintenance was being done, there would have been no need for us to announce a DMK protest. Was my statement malicious—or is what Jayalalithaa says now malicious?

She has also said that I erased Tirukkural couplets from State Transport Corporation buses and had my own slogans written instead. When I became Public Works and Transport Minister in Anna's Cabinet in 1967, I was the one who placed Thiruvalluvar's portrait and Tirukkural couplets in Government buses across Tamil Nadu. When some new buses were hurriedly put into service without the Kural being written on them, I called the then minister K.N. Nehru and instructed him, after which the Kural was immediately inscribed on those buses too.

I wrote *Kuraloviyam* on the Kural; summoned painter Venugopal Sharma, gave him ₹10,000, and had the Tamil Nadu Government acquire the rights to his portrait of Thiruvalluvar by paying ₹3 lakh; and I had my book *Tirukkural—Kalaignar Commentary* brought out through Tamil scholar A.Sa. Gnanasambandan. These too were honours rendered to Valluvar.

At the very outset, following a decision of the DMK Cabinet on 30 October 1969, the Government issued an order on 3 November 1969 declaring the day after the annual Pongal festival as “Thiruvalluvar Day” and as a holiday. It was under that DMK order that the Thiruvalluvar Year began to be followed in the Tamil Nadu Government diary from 1971 and in the Tamil Nadu Government Gazette from 1972.

During DMK rule I increased the Thiruvalluvar Award amount from ₹10,000 to ₹20,000 and, in 1998, to ₹1 lakh. Forgetting all this, Jayalalithaa makes diversionary accusations.

If she imagines she has more concern than I do for maintaining the Thiruvalluvar statue, or tries to compete over concern for Tamil and its Classical Language status, she certainly cannot win. For she is the same Jayalalithaa who covered Thiruvalluvar's picture in Samacheer Kalvi textbooks printed during DMK rule with a sticker.

Finally, she ended her speech with a Kural someone seems to have given her:

> “பல்லார் முனியப் பயனில சொல்லுவான் / எல்லாரும் எள்ளப் படும்.”

It means that one who speaks useless words that many dislike will be despised by all. After these lengthy explanations of mine, the people of Tamil Nadu, scholars and those who know the Kural well can decide to whom that golden saying applies.

The couplet she cited occurs in the chapter “Against Speaking Useless Words.” In the same chapter is another Kural that Amma should know:

> “பயனில் சொல் பாராட்டு வானை மகனெனல் / மக்கட் பதடி யெனல்”

That is, one who speaks useless things hoping to gain from them is more appropriately called chaff than a true human being.

I merely provide Jayalalithaa with explanations. She spews hatred. I remind her not to forget Valluvar's words meant for people like her: “யாகாவாராயினும் நா காக்க”—whoever you may be, guard your tongue!

With affection,
M.K.
17-1-2013

எல்லாரும் எள்ளப் படும்.”

அதாவது பலரும் வெறுக்கும்படியான பயனற்ற சொற்களைப்
பேசுபவரை எல்லோரும் இகழ்ந்துரைப்பார்கள் என்ற இந்த
வள்ளுவரின் பொன் மொழி, என்னுடைய இந்த நீண்ட
விளக்கங்களுக்குப் பிறகு, அந்தக்குறள் யாருக்குப் பொருந்தும்
என்பதைத் தமிழ்நாட்டு மக்களும், குறளை நன்குணர்ந்த
சான்றோர்களும், புலவர்களும் நன்கு உணர்வார்கள்.

அம்மையார் கூறும் அந்தக் குறள் “பயனில சொல்லாமை”
என்ற அதிகாரத்தில் இடம் பெற்றதாகும். அதே அதிகாரத்தில்
இடம் பெற்ற, அம்மையார் தெரிந்துகொள்ள வேண்டிய மற்றொரு
குறளும் உள்ளது. அது;

“பயனில் சொல் பாராட்டு வானை மகனெனல்
மக்கட் பதடி யெனல்”

அதாவது பயனற்றவைகளைச் சொல்லி, பயன்பெற
நினைப்பவரை, மனிதர் என்பதை விட, “பதர்” என்பதே
பொருத்தமானதாகும்.

நான் ஜெயலலிதாவுக்கு விளக்கங்களைத் தான் அளிக்கிறேன்.
அவரோ வெறுப்பைக் கக்குகிறார். அவரைப் போன்றவர்களுக்காக
வள்ளுவர் சொன்ன வாசகமான “யாகாவாராயினும் நா காக்க”
என்பதை மறந்து விடாதீர்கள் என்று நினைவூட்டுகிறேன்!
