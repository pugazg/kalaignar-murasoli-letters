# 3381. Illness and I — Diary (3)

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This third diary instalment covers 6–10 February 2009 and records the decision to undertake spinal surgery, formation of the medical team, continuing government work and political writing from hospital. Source-supplied English medical expressions and the *Times of India* headline are preserved as printed. The complete audited Tamil is reproduced below and remains authoritative.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3381](../../../chapters/3381-nalivum-naanum-naatkurippu-3.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 121](../../../pages/page-121.md)–[PDF 130](../../../pages/page-130.md)  
**Date:** 12 March 2009

*(Through this serial article I am bringing together various events from the time I was admitted to Ramachandra Hospital, through the surgery and the continuous treatments that followed.)*

★★

**Udanpirappē,**

## 6-2-2009

In my youth I had a friend named Mayavaram Gandhi, a comrade of the Kazhagam. He is no more. Whenever Periyar came to that region he would stay at Gandhi’s house.

Mayavaram Gandhi had two sons. One was Sampath; the other had been given my own name, “Karunanidhi.” Sampath now works as an assistant to the Minister for Dairy Development.

I had agreed to conduct the marriage of his daughter today at the entrance of my residence. But because I was in hospital, he asked whether he could bring the bride and groom to the hospital itself and have me conduct the marriage there.

So, still lying in bed, I conducted that marriage.

While I was in hospital, General Secretary Professor, Kazhagam leaders and Ministers frequently came to enquire about my health and explain the day-to-day political situation.

My wives Dayalu and Rajathi, and my daughters Selvi and Kani, cared for me like nurses alongside the nurses.

Selvi forgot Bengaluru itself, stayed in the hospital through the night without sleeping and bore all my irritation.

Kani, amid public engagements, Parliament and responsibility for her son Adhi, stayed by my side whenever she found time.

My daughters-in-law and sons-in-law too came whenever they had time and helped the family members caring for me.

Brothers Ponmudy, E. V. Velu and A. Raja stayed in the hospital every night and helped. On other days Arcot Veerasamy, Duraimurugan and K. N. Nehru also stayed there, as did M. K. Alagiri, M. K. Stalin and M. K. Tamilarasu.

The **Anti-Platelet** tablet that had been stopped for the spinal injection was started again.

At about 2 p.m., when I tried to sleep, severe pain struck me. I could not bear it.

Even the doctors were unsure what to do.

Dr. Marthandam thought another MRI scan should be taken, and I was taken for it.

Oh no — another “MRI” again? Family members and friends trembled and became distressed.

The MRI was repeated amid severe pain.

It then showed that the length of the prolapsed disc between L2 and L3 had increased further. The doctors explained that this was the reason the pain had grown worse.

I asked: what use is it to know the cause of the pain; what is the way to cure it?

Dr. Marthandam said, “Spinal surgery is the only way.” The other doctors agreed.

Dr. Mohandas was also called and asked for his view. He too said surgery would be best.

The doctors called the whole family and said that, considering my age, the operation would be very dangerous, but there was no other way to remove the pain and that surgery could be undertaken only with great courage.

The family could not bear to see my suffering, and because the doctors had stated firmly that there was no other way to relieve it, they did not bring themselves to tell me all these facts directly.

Instead they reassured me that the operation was very ordinary, that there was nothing to fear and that I could undergo it.

Kalanithi Maran in particular said, like his father: “Grandfather, there is nothing to be afraid of in this, grandfather. We can do it.”

Their mouths reassured me.

But I have the ability to weigh a person by looking at the face. One glance at all their faces was enough for me to understand the truth.

I could see that tears were waiting to burst from each person’s eyes if I questioned them.

I was placed in the position of having to give courage to all of them.

I said, “It is only an ordinary operation, isn’t it? Why do you all look so frightened?” I comforted them, said I was ready for surgery and asked when it could be done.

At around 8 p.m. all the doctors met again and discussed where the surgery should be done — whether I should be taken abroad or to Delhi.

Neurologist Dr. A. V. Srinivasan, from there itself, used “tele-medicine” to contact a major hospital in the United States and obtained details about the operation.

Finally it was decided to contact Dr. Arvind Jaiswal of the All India Institute of Medical Sciences in Delhi, who was highly skilled in this surgery, and bring him to Chennai.

Brother Dayanidhi Maran immediately contacted Union Health Minister brother Anbumani in Delhi and asked him to send Dr. Arvind Jaiswal to Chennai.

Brother Anbumani immediately spoke with the institute’s Director and sent word that Dr. Jaiswal would be sent to Chennai the very next day.

## 7-2-2009

Dr. Sajan Hegde, a spine specialist working at Apollo Hospital, was asked to examine my condition and give his opinion.

After examining me, he said the treatment being given was correct and that surgery was the appropriate course.

That evening Dr. Arvind Jaiswal arrived from Delhi at Ramachandra Hospital.

While he and Dr. Marthandam were examining me, they asked how far the pain that began in my back extended.

Since this was the Tamil month “Thai,” I joked that it extended up to my “Thai (Thigh)” — my thigh.

Dr. Jaiswal examined me directly and reviewed all the MRI and X-ray images.

Once the decision to operate had been taken, it was felt that the proper course was to form a team of doctors.

A fourteen-member medical team was announced under Dr. Marthandam’s leadership, consisting of:

Dr. Thanikachalam, cardiologist;  
Dr. Arvind Jaiswal, spine specialist;  
Dr. Gopal, family doctor;  
Dr. Karthik Kailash, spine specialist;  
Dr. A. S. Naidu, anaesthetist;  
Dr. Mahesh Wakamudi, anaesthetist;  
Dr. A. V. Srinivasan, neurologist;  
Dr. K. R. Palanisamy, gastroenterology specialist;  
Dr. Raj P. Singh, pulmonary specialist;  
Dr. Mayilvaganan, orthopaedic specialist;  
Dr. Shanmugam, anaesthetist;  
Dr. Bhaskar, anaesthetist;  
Dr. Soundararajan, nephrology specialist;  
and Dr. Marthandam.

The medical team discussed for a long time when the operation should be held.

It was decided to perform it on 11.2.2009 at Ramachandra Hospital in Chennai, and that Delhi doctor Jaiswal should arrive in Chennai by the tenth.

Union Home Minister and dear friend P. Chidambaram came to the hospital today.

He had pressed for a government medical college to be established at Sivaganga, and the government had granted approval.

He had wanted me to come personally and lay the foundation stone and had already obtained a date from me.

After seeing me in hospital, he said I could lay the foundation stone by “video conference” while remaining in hospital, and that if two State Ministers were sent to Sivaganga the same day, the ceremony could be held there.

I agreed.

## 8-2-2009

Even in hospital, despite unbearable pain, I did not fail to read the newspapers every morning and evening.

I had written under the title “There Is Life If We Unite!” that there should be no fratricidal conflict here too over the Sri Lankan Tamil issue, and that at least on this issue everyone in Tamil Nadu should remain united.

In the morning newspapers I saw that some people had not merely ignored my appeal but had spoken and written in a deeply insulting manner about me.

I wrote that I did not feel even an atom of sorrow because of it; if attacking me seemed right to their conscience, I was happy with that.

Whether my body could bear their abuse or not, I wrote that my heart had to bear it so that no pit would be created in the lives of Sri Lankan Tamils.

Jayalalithaa had issued a long statement accusing the Kazhagam government, and me in particular, of doing nothing for farmers.

Using notes already prepared for the Governor’s Address and the reply to it, I wrote a detailed answer to Jayalalithaa’s statement and had it sent to all the newspapers.

Meanwhile, on the advice of Delhi doctor Arvind Jaiswal, neurologist Dr. Meenakshi Sundaram carried out a test called **Nerve Conduction Study and Electro Myography**, intended to determine nerve function.

That examination alone lasted more than two hours.

I telephoned and asked for details about the mobilisation rally organised that day by the South Chennai DMK on behalf of the Sri Lankan Tamils’ Welfare Rights Council.

News came that Thanjai Sultan, who had worked for the beauty and development of every inch of Thanjavur city, had died.

Deeply saddened, I personally wrote a condolence message in my own hand.

Union Health Minister brother Dr. Anbumani Ramadoss came to Ramachandra Hospital and enquired about my health.

## 9-2-2009

Union Minister brother T. R. Baalu met me in hospital early this morning, explained the political situation in Delhi and obtained my advice on some issues.

To examine how my heartbeat was functioning continuously over twenty-four hours, a machine called **Holter E.C.G.** was attached to my body. It was removed only the next day.

To keep the heartbeat regular I was given **Tablet Betaloc**.

I called the Finance Secretary and officers of that department to the hospital and spent the entire morning in a separate room discussing preparation of the Financial Statement.

The meeting began at 10.45 a.m. and continued past noon. Doctors came and reminded me that it was time for examinations and medicines.

Kanimozhi — my daughter and herself a former journalist with *The Hindu* — said that *The Hindu* journalist Radhakrishnan had been staying at the hospital since morning observing my activities and wanted to meet me for five minutes.

I asked that he be allowed in.

The man who came saying “five minutes” spoke with me for more than fifty minutes.

Radhakrishnan of *The Hindu* asked with a sense of personal concern whether I needed to undertake so much work even while in hospital.

I said: it has to be done, doesn’t it?

When the people have entrusted me with such a great responsibility, can I stop working for them merely because I am lying in bed?

When he anxiously asked about my surgery, I said, “All of you are here, aren’t you? Then what is there to fear?” He left with tears in his eyes.

The discussions on the Financial Statement with government secretaries continued in the evening.

Only the important files awaiting my signature were brought to the hospital, and I signed them.

## 10-2-2009

With the thought that I was to undergo a major operation the next day, I asked for the morning newspapers.

The moment I read them, I forgot the operation itself.

There was such an attack on me that day.

Referring to what I had written about Professor and me having once resigned even our Legislative Assembly seats for the Sri Lankan Tamil issue, the leader of one party issued, as usual and with great “concern” for me, a statement asking what benefit had come from resigning those posts and saying there would be benefit only if we resigned from government altogether.

The same leader also wrote that the DMK struggled for Sri Lankan Tamils only when it was in opposition and did nothing when it was the ruling party.

I wrote a detailed list of when and what struggles the DMK had undertaken while in government.

I titled it “Rebuttal and Explanation,” and beneath it added this note:

> “The following rebuttal and explanation was written from hospital. Kindly forgive any errors!”

I then had it sent to the newspapers.

Mrs. Sonia Gandhi, President of the All India Congress Committee and guiding leader of the United Progressive Alliance, telephoned me on the evening of the tenth, enquired about my health and spoke in detail about the operation scheduled for the next day.

She wished that I recover after the surgery and quickly return to work.

On Dr. Thanikachalam’s advice, arrangements were made to take an **Echo-Cardiogram** in my room itself.

Taking those results into account, together with the results of a **64 slice C.T. Angio gram** taken in 2007 and the **Holter E.C.G.** results, Dr. Thanikachalam said the operation could be performed on the eleventh, but that considering my age it was **High Risk**.

That evening the team appointed solely for anaesthesia, under anaesthetist Dr. Mahesh Wakamudi, examined me.

To prevent infection, intravenous medication was begun that evening.

Because surgery was scheduled for the next day, all visitors were to be avoided. My room and the adjoining rooms were cleaned with disinfectant.

Even unavoidable visitors, family members and doctors were permitted near me only after wearing masks.

Nephrologist Dr. Soundararajan came, conducted his examination and gave instructions including how much water I should drink.

The fourteen doctors on the medical team called my family together and explained the operation and its possible consequences in detail. Formal consent and signatures for the operation were obtained through family doctor Dr. Gopal.

On the tenth, the English daily *The Times of India* carried the source-supplied headline:

> **“Ailing but active ‘Kalaignar’ turns Hospital into Chief Minister’s Office”**

The source then reproduces the report in Tamil, saying that on the seventh floor of Sri Ramachandra Medical College Hospital, amid the white coats of doctors, senior government officers could be seen going in and out of the ward where Chief Minister Kalaignar had been admitted, carrying files and discussing schemes with him.

The place, where visitors were restricted, looked like the Chief Minister’s office in the Secretariat.

A senior Finance Department officer was quoted as saying:

> “We hold discussions here. We are discussing the Financial Statement and taking many decisions.”

A doctor was quoted as saying that Ministers, senior DMK members and officials came for important meetings with the Chief Minister; that the visitor list included leaders of political parties, film personalities and party cadres; and that when the Chief Minister was not meeting anyone, he read newspapers line by line or wrote for the party daily *Murasoli*.

The doctor added, in the source’s rendering, that overall he was one of their most active and restless patients.

The report said that he had told officials no file should be kept waiting merely because he was ill.

Because I was to undergo surgery at dawn, my son Alagiri, who was himself in Madurai after eye surgery, came to Chennai and stayed in the hospital.

My son Stalin and the Ministers too remained there.

I will continue this diary tomorrow.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**12-3-2009**

மிகப் பெரிய அறுவை சிகிச்சையை நாளையதினம் செய்து கொள்ளப் போகிறோம் என்ற நினைவுடன் காலை ஏடுகளை வரவழைத்தேன். ஏடுகளைப் படித்தவுடன் அறுவை சிகிச்சை மறந்து விட்டது. அந்த அளவிற்கு அன்றைய தினம் என் மீது தாக்குதல். இலங்கைத் தமிழர் பிரச்சினைக்காக நானும், பேராசிரியரும் சட்டமன்ற உறுப்பினர் பதவிகளையே ஒரு காலத்திலே ராஜினாமா செய்தோம் என்று எழுதியதைப் பற்றிக் குறிப்பிட்டு, ஒரு கட்சியின் தலைவர் “அவ்வாறு பதவிவிலகியதால் என்ன பயன் ஏற்பட்டு விட்டது, ஓட்டு மொத்தமாக ஆட்சியை விட்டே விலகினால்தான் பயன் ஏற்படும்” என்று என்மீது மிகுந்த அக்கறையோடு வழக்கம்போல அறிக்கை விட்டிருந்தார்.

அதே தலைவர், தி.மு.கழகம் எதிர்க்கட்சியாக இருந்த போது தான் இலங்கைத் தமிழர்களுக்காக போராட்டம் நடத்தியதே தவிர, ஆளுங் கட்சியாக இருந்த போது எதையும் செய்யவில்லை என்றும் எழுதியிருந்தார். ஆளுங்கட்சியாக இருந்த போது எவ்வெப்போது என்னென்ன போராட்டங்களை தி.மு. கழகம் செய்தது என்பது பற்றி விவரமான பட்டியலை எழுதினேன். அதற்கு “மறுப்பும் - விளக்கமும்” என்று தலைப்பிட்டுவிட்டு, அதன் கீழே “பின்வரும் மறுப்பும் விளக்கமும் மருத்துவமனையிலிருந்து எழுதப்பட்டது, பிழையிருப்பின் மன்னித்தருள்க!” என்று குறிப்பும் எழுதியதான் ஏடுகளுக்கே அனுப்பச் செய்தேன்.

அகில இந்திய காங்கிரஸ் கட்சியின் தலைவரும், ஐக்கிய முற்போக்குக் கூட்டணியின் வழி காட்டும் தலைவருமான திருமதி சோனியா காந்தி அவர்கள் 10ஆம் தேதி மாலையில் தொலைபேசியில் என்னை தொடர்பு கொண்டு உடல் நலம் விசாரித்ததோடு, அடுத்த நாள் நடைபெறவுள்ள அறுவை சிகிச்சை பற்றி யெல்லாம் விவரமாகப் பேசினார். அறுவை சிகிச்சை முடிந்து நலம் பெற்று விரைவில் பணிக்குத் திரும்ப வேண்டுமென்று வாழ்த்தினார்.

Echo-Cardiogram எடுக்க வேண்டுமென்று டாக்டர் தனிகாசலம் அறிவுறுத்தியதின் பேரில், என் அறையிலேயே அதை எடுப்பதற்கான ஏற்பாடுகள் நடைபெற்றன. இதன் முடிவுகளைக் கணக்கிலே கொண்டும், 2007ஆம் ஆண்டு எடுக்கப்பட்ட 64 slice C.T. Angio gram முடிவுகளைக் கணக்கிலே கொண்டும், Holter E.C.G. முடிவுகளைக் கணக்கிலே கொண்டும் டாக்டர் தனிகாசலம் 11ஆம் தேதியன்று அறுவை சிகிச்சை செய்யலாம் என்றும், ஆனால் என்னுடைய வயதை உத்தேசித்துப் பார்க்கும் போது அது மிகவும் அபாயகரமானது (High Risk) என்றும் தெரிவித்தார்.

மாலையில் மயக்க மருந்து நிபுணர், டாக்டர் மகேஷ் வகாமுடி தலைமையில் மயக்கம் கொடுப்பதற்காக மட்டுமே நியமிக்கப்பட்டிருந்த மருத்துவ குழுவினர் என்னைப் பரிசோதனை செய்தனர். கிருமி தொற்று வராமல் இருப்பதற்காக அன்று மாலையிலிருந்தே நரம்பு மூலம் செலுத்தப்படும் ஊசி மருந்து ஆரம்பிக்கப்பட்டது. மறுநாள் அறுவை சிகிச்சை என்பதால் பார்வையாளர்கள் அனைவரையும் தவிர்க்க வேண்டுமென்று கூறியதோடு - என்னுடைய அறை, மற்றும் பக்கத்து அறைகள் எல்லாம் கூட கிருமிநாசினி கொண்டு சுத்தப்படுத்தப்பட்டது. தவிர்க்க முடியாத பார்வையாளர்களும், வீட்டாரும், மருத்துவர்களும் கூட முகமூடி அணிந்த பிறகே என் அருகே வர அனுமதிக்கப்பட்டனர்.

சிறுநீரகத் துறை நிபுணர் டாக்டர் சவுந்தரராசன் வந்து தனது சோதனையை நடத்திவிட்டு எந்த அளவிற்கு நான் நீர் அருந்த வேண்டும் என்றெல்லாம் அறிவுரைகள் வழங்கினார். மருத்துவக் குழுவினர் 14 பேரும் என்னுடைய குடும்பத்தாரை அழைத்து, அறுவை சிகிச்சை பற்றியும், அதன் பின் விளைவுகள் பற்றியும் விவரமாக எடுத்துக் கூறி, அறுவை சிகிச்சைக்கான ஒப்புதலையும் கையெழுத்தையும் குடும்ப மருத்துவர் டாக்டர் கோபால் அவர்கள் மூலம் முறைப்படி பெற்றார்கள்.

“Ailing but active ‘Kalaignar’ turns Hospital into Chief Minister’s Office” என்ற தலைப்பில் “தி டைம்ஸ் ஆப் இந்தியா” ஆங்கில நாளேடு 10ஆம் தேதி பின்வருமாறு எழுதியிருந்தது.

“ஸ்ரீ ராமச்சந்திரா மருத்துவக் கல்லூரி மருத்துவமனையின் ஏழாவது மாடியில், வெள்ளைக் கோட்டுகளுக்கு

(மருத்துவர்களுக்கு) மத்தியில் முதல்வர் கலைஞர் அனுமதிக்கப்பட்டுள்ள வார்டுக்குள் - உள்ளே செல்வதும், வெளியே வருவதுமாக மூத்த அரசு அதிகாரிகள் கோப்புகளை கையில் தாங்கியவாறு - திட்டங்களை முதல்வருடன் விவாதித்துக் கொண்டிருப்பதைக் காணலாம். பார்வையாளர்களுக்கு கட்டுப்பாடுவிதிக்கப்பட்டுள்ள அந்த இடம், தற்போது தலைமைச் செயலகத்தில் உள்ள முதலமைச்சரின் அலுவலகம் போல் காட்சி அளிக்கிறது.

நிதித் துறையைச் சேர்ந்த ஒரு மூத்த அதிகாரி, “இங்கு நாங்கள் விவாதம் நடத்துகிறோம். நிதி நிலை அறிக்கை மீது கலந்துரையாடி பல முடிவுகளை எடுத்துக் கொண்டிருக்கிறோம்” என்றார். மருத்துவர் ஒருவரிடம் கேட்டபோது, “முதலமைச்சருடன் முக்கியமான சந்திப்புகளுக்காக அமைச்சர்களும், தி.மு.க. முன்னணி உறுப்பினர்களும், அதிகாரிகளும் இங்கு வருகிறார்கள். அரசியல் கட்சிகளைச் சேர்ந்த தலைவர்கள், திரைப்பட பிரமுகர்கள், கட்சியின் தொண்டர்கள் என்று பார்வையாளர்களின் பட்டியல் மிக நீளமானது. முதலமைச்சர் யாரையும் சந்திக்காதபோது செய்தித்தாள்களை ஒரு வரிவிடாமல் படிக்கிறார். அல்லது கட்சியின் நாளேடான முரசொலிக்காக எழுதுகிறார். மொத்தத்தில் அவர் எங்களது மிகவும் செல்லத் துடிப்பான நோயாளிகளில் ஒருவர்” என்றார். தான் சுகவீனமாக உள்ளதால் எந்தக் கோப்பும் காத்திருக்கக் கூடாது என்று அதிகாரிகளிடம் அவர் கூறியிருக்கிறார்.”

என்று எழுதியிருந்தது.

விபந்தால் எனக்கு அறுவை சிகிச்சை என்பதால் - மதுரையில் கண் அறுவை சிகிச்சை செய்து கொண்டிருந்த நிலையிலும் என் மகன் அழகிரி சென்னை வந்து மருத்துவமனையிலேயே தங்கினார். மேலும் என் மகன் ஸ்டாலின் மற்றும் அமைச்சர்கள் எல்லாம் அங்கேயே தங்கிவிட்டனர்.

இந்த நாட்குறிப்பை தொடர்ந்து நாளையும் எழுதுகிறேன்.
