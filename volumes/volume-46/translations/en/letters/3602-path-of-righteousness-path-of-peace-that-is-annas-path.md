**Udanpirappē,**

I had told reporters that if the Kerala Government and the Central Government did not stop the acts of violence taking place in Kerala against the people of Tamil Nadu, and if the violence in Kerala continued, the Kazhagam Executive Committee would meet and take an appropriate decision. Accordingly, on the 9th, the Kazhagam Executive Committee met. To establish Tamil Nadu's firm stand on this issue and draw the immediate attention of the Central Government, we resolved to observe a one-day fast in the corporations and towns of Tamil Nadu on 12 December 2011, from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. We also resolved that, following this, a massive human-chain procession would be held on the 15th, from 3 p.m. to 5 p.m., in the districts of Theni, Madurai, Sivaganga, Dindigul and Ramanathapuram, which receive irrigation facilities from the Mullai Periyar Dam.

In Chennai, the capital of Tamil Nadu, the fast to be held at Anna Arivalayam on behalf of the North Chennai and South Chennai districts will be inaugurated by our Kazhagam General Secretary, the Professor of Self-Respect; and in the evening I will conclude the fast and address the gathering. I would indeed like to sit with you in the fast from 8 in the morning itself! But the Udanpirappugal of the Kazhagam, taking my age into account, have decided on their own, without even asking me, that fasting for the whole day would not be good for my health. Whether or not my body cooperates with the fast, you know and I know that my whole heart will remain firmly rooted in that fast.

On behalf of the Kazhagam we have conducted so many righteous struggles. For the Sri Lankan Tamils, for the Cauvery issue, for the language issue—so many times we have involved ourselves in fasts, human chains, demonstrations and arrests; and we have seen victory. But this time, in our India, which labours every day to honour and safeguard unity, there is the perverse situation in which our Tamils are prevented by a few anti-social elements from even entering the neighbouring State beside us, while the Central Government and Kerala Government are unable to control it and merely watch. It is to draw their immediate attention to this that we have announced this righteous struggle.

When writing the resolution announcing this righteous struggle, we mentioned only to some extent all the fraternal ties between Kerala and us. Many people belonging to Kerala are serving in important positions in Tamil Nadu. Many are senior officials even in the Secretariat. Why, Mr. Prabhakar, I.A.S., who is today the Chief Secretary of Kerala itself, is from Tamil Nadu. When I went to Thiruvananthapuram last year, he came to the place where I was staying and met me. Therefore, should a feeling of difference be allowed to arise between the people of the two States, who have lived with bonds of affection like children of one mother's womb?

The Mullai Periyar irrigation scheme came into operation under a lease agreement for 999 years signed on 29 October 1886 between the then Travancore Government and the Madras Presidency Government.

Under this agreement, approximately 8,000 acres of land were leased to Tamil Nadu. The lease amount was fixed at ₹5 per acre.

Later, on 29 May 1970, two supplementary agreements were made between the Governments of Tamil Nadu and Kerala.

One of those supplementary agreements provided for Tamil Nadu to generate electricity and pay the Kerala Government royalty for that power generation.

The other supplementary agreement provided for raising the lease amount from ₹5 to ₹30 per acre and permitting fishing rights.

In 1979, the Kerala Government expressed fear that the Periyar Dam was in danger. Consequently, the Chairman of the Central Water Commission inspected the dam on 25 November 1979 and stated that the Periyar Dam was in good condition and could be strengthened in keeping with its existing design.

He also stated that measures for strengthening the dam could be undertaken as (1) emergency measures, (2) interim measures and (3) long-term measures, and that for this purpose the water level of the dam could temporarily be maintained at 136 feet.

On 29 April 1980, the Central Water Commission stated that after the emergency measures and cable-installation works were completed, the water level of the dam could be raised to 145 feet.

Nevertheless, the Kerala Government stated that even after the dam was strengthened, water should be stored only up to 136 feet.

Although talks were held many times to find a solution to this issue, no conclusion was reached.

Meanwhile, with regard to this issue, the Tamil Nadu Government approached the Supreme Court seeking to transfer the writ petitions filed in the Madras and Kerala High Courts to the Supreme Court and have them joined with the writ petition already filed there by the Mullai Periyar Environmental Protection Organisation belonging to Kerala.

On 19 May 2000, talks were held between the Chief Ministers of Tamil Nadu and Kerala in the presence of the Union Minister for Water Resources to find a solution to this issue.

At those talks the Central Government decided to constitute an expert committee; the committee was accordingly constituted and submitted its report in March 2001.

The expert committee recommended, among other things, that as a first stage Tamil Nadu could store water up to 142 feet from the existing level of 136 feet, and that Tamil Nadu could complete the remaining strengthening works.

Not only that; the important particulars of the Supreme Court judgment delivered on 27 February 2006 in the Mullai Periyar Dam case were:

1. There was no basis whatsoever for Kerala's contention that the dam had become weak.
2. As recommended by the expert committee, Tamil Nadu could store water up to 142 feet; after completing the remaining strengthening works to the satisfaction of the Central Water Commission, and after the expert committee inspected the dam, Tamil Nadu could store water up to the maximum level of 152 feet.
3. The Kerala Government and its officials should abandon the course of obstructing works carried out on behalf of the Tamil Nadu Government: “we see no reason for the State of Kerala to cause any obstruction / The State of Kerala and its officers are restrained from causing any obstruction.”

But without respecting this judgment even then, the Kerala Government, on the sixteenth day thereafter, amended the Irrigation and Water Conservation Act, brought the amended law into force on 18 March 2006, and fixed the Periyar Dam's water level at 136 feet.

Pointing this out, the Tamil Nadu Government filed a case in the Supreme Court on 31 March 2006. During the AIADMK government, even that case had remained without being registered. After the DMK government assumed office in May, it pursued the matter, and the registration number for the case was obtained only on 31 August 2006.

Following this, on the advice of the Supreme Court, a meeting of the Chief Ministers of the two States was held in New Delhi on 29 November 2006 under the chairmanship of Union Water Resources Minister Professor Saifuddin Soz.

At that meeting, besides explaining the condition of Tamil Nadu's farmers and the safety of the Mullai Periyar Dam, I requested that the Kerala Government consider repealing its amended law. But no decision was taken at that meeting.

Here is the date-wise list of the many letters I wrote regarding the Mullai Periyar issue to the Chief Minister of Kerala, the Union Minister and the Prime Minister of India:

I wrote to the Chief Minister of Kerala on 14 September 2006, 4 October 2006 and 26 October 2006;

I wrote to the Union Irrigation Minister on 31 October 2006, 15 November 2006 and 16 November 2006;

I wrote to the Prime Minister on 23 November 2006;

on that same 23 November 2006, I wrote to the Union Irrigation Minister and the Defence Minister;

on 27 November 2006, I again wrote to the Union Irrigation Minister;

on 2 January 2007, 25 March 2007 and 19 May 2007, I wrote to the Prime Minister.

When I personally met the Prime Minister on 18 December 2007 and explained the Mullai Periyar issue, the Prime Minister asked that the Kerala Chief Minister and I meet once in person and talk, and accordingly we met and spoke.

On 20 December 2007, I again wrote to the Prime Minister.

On 4 February 2008 and 13 July 2009, I wrote to the Chief Minister of Kerala.

On 8 October 2009, I wrote to the Prime Minister. Most of these letters were also published in the newspapers from time to time.

Forceful arguments were placed and argued before the Supreme Court on behalf of the Tamil Nadu Government. It was in connection with this that, on 10 November 2009, the Supreme Court ordered the constitution of an empowered committee of five members. Former Chief Justice of India Dr. A. S. Anand is the chairman of this committee. In the statement I issued in this connection on 14 November 2009, I had asked: What became of the Supreme Court judgment delivered on 27 February 2006 that the water level of the Mullai Periyar Dam could be raised from 136 feet to 142 feet? It is said that a Supreme Court judgment is the ultimate authority in all matters; if so, was that judgment of the Supreme Court accepted? The Kerala Government refused to accept that Supreme Court judgment and brought in and passed a new legislative amendment on 15 March 2006—is that the proper procedure? If a State Government itself acts in this manner, what remedy is there? If every State passes legislative amendments in defiance of court judgments, what will become of democracy? I had asked all these questions.

To this day, no explanation has been received for those questions.

If the Supreme Court itself had questioned this and taken action then, would the present situation in which Tamils are attacked in Kerala have arisen? It is in order that the Central Government, the Kerala Government and the people there should think about all this, and also to press our demands—that, until a conclusion is reached in this issue, responsibility for the security of the Mullai Periyar Dam should be entrusted to the Central security forces; and that the Supreme Court's 2006 direction to the Kerala Government permitting the dam's water level to be raised from 136 feet to 142 feet should immediately be implemented—that the DMK has announced this righteous struggle.

I have abundant confidence that the Udanpirappugal of the Kazhagam, who turn every righteous struggle announced by the Kazhagam into a victory, will make these struggles victorious this time too. Do not forget: this righteous struggle is the path of righteousness—the path of peace—that is Anna's path!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**12 December 2011**

கேட்டு நடவடிக்கை எடுத்திருந்தால் தற்போது கேரளாவில்
தமிழர்கள் தாக்கப்படும் நிலைமை ஏற்பட்டிருக்குமா என்பதைப்
பற்றியெல்லாம் மத்திய அரசும், கேரள அரசும் அங்கேயுள்ள
மக்களும் சிந்திக்க வேண்டுமென்பதற்காகவும், நமது
கோரிக்கைகளான முல்லைப் பெரியாறு அணையின் பாதுகாப்பை,
இந்தப் பிரச்சினைக்கு ஒரு முடிவு ஏற்படும் வரையில், மத்திய
பாதுகாப்புப் படையினரின் பொறுப்பில் விடவேண்டும் -
அணையின் நீர் மட்டத்தை 136 அடியிலிருந்து 142 அடியாக
உயர்த்த அனுமதிக்க வேண்டுமென்று 2006ஆம் ஆண்டு உச்ச
நீதிமன்றம் கேரள அரசுக்கு உத்தரவிட்டதை உடனடியாக நடை
முறைப்படுத்த வேண்டும் - ஆகியவற்றை வலியுறுத்தியும் தி.மு.
கழகம் இந்த அறப்போராட்டத்தை அறிவித்திருக்கிறது. கழகம்
அறிவிக்கும் அறப் போராட்டங்களையெல்லாம் வெற்றியாக
மாற்றித் தரும் கழக உடன்பிறப்புகள் இந்த முறையும் இந்த
போராட்டங்களை வெற்றியாக்கிடுவார்கள் என்ற நம்பிக்கை
எனக்கு நிரம்பவே உள்ளது. இந்த அறப்போர்; அறவழி - அமைதி
வழி - அதுவே அண்ணா வழி என்பதை மறந்து விடாதீர்கள்!
