# 3526. Will Those Who Spew Poison Speak with a Hand on Their Heart?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation follows the audited canonical Tamil and the locked Volume 44 conventions. The letter reproduces allegations from a *India Today* Tamil article and then district-official explanations. Both the allegations and the replies are preserved distinctly; the translation does not turn either into independently verified fact. Survey numbers, area figures, names and source unit forms are retained in source order. The source itself ultimately records specific encroachments in the final Theni explanation and says recovery action was under way; those findings are not suppressed. The source alternates forms such as `எக்டேர்` and `ஏர்ஸ்`, and those unit forms are represented conservatively rather than silently harmonised. The complete audited Tamil is reproduced below and remains authoritative.

**Date:** 12 January 2011

**Udanpirappē,**

When the DMK and the DMK government are welcomed by the public and people are benefiting from its schemes, you and I both know how many people feel resentment and jealousy. As the election draws nearer, a few newspapers have been publishing various reports criticising this government.

You may read them and dismiss them the next moment. I do not. I immediately ask my secretaries to find out whether there is any truth in the report, and I keep asking until I learn the facts. I do not remain at peace until then.

One such report appearing over the past two or three days concerns the scheme to give up to two acres of land free to poor landless people. A five-page illustrated article titled **“A Scheme Going Barren”** appeared in the Tamil edition of *India Today* dated **29-12-2010**. Without first finding out whether what it said was true, the letter says, some opposition parties began alleging large-scale irregularities in the land-distribution scheme.

I therefore contacted the concerned district collectors and asked them to investigate the published allegations directly and send immediate explanatory reports. The collectors, the letter says, conducted inquiries and sent reports. The details follow.

## 1. Bhavani taluk, Anthiyur “A” village

### “Report published as an irregularity”

The article alleged that lands allotted to landless poor people in Anthiyur “A” village of Bhavani taluk — **Survey Nos. 82/6, 82/7, 82/8, 82/9, 106/3, 117/3, 212/6 and 229/2** — were under the control of relatives and close associates of area minister Vellakoil Saminathan. It also said that Kannan, a veterinary inspector in the locality, had land there and that villagers described him as a person close to the minister.

### Explanation of the Erode District Revenue Officer

In the cited survey numbers, a total of **2.94.5 hectares** had been assigned to **eight persons**, and all the lands were in the beneficiaries’ possession and under cultivation. Veterinary inspector Kannan had no land in that village.

With the factual position being so, the report linking minister Vellakoil Saminathan to the matter was, the official explanation said, entirely false.

## 2. Devannagoundanur, Sankagiri taluk, Salem district

### “Report published as an irregularity”

The article alleged that free land allotted in the hill area of Devannagoundanur had fallen into the hands of local politicians. An unnamed local person was quoted as saying that the DMK panchayat president had grabbed the hill land — **Survey Nos. 489/5 through 489/21** — and was running a stone quarry under the name **J.K. Mining and Chemicals**. It further said that although the government had announced free land for **150 people** of the village three years earlier, they did not even know where the land was located.

### Explanation of the Salem District Collector

Survey No. 489 in Devannagoundanur, with a total extent of **4.30.0 hectares**, was classified in village accounts as rock. It was subdivided as Survey No. 489/1, and **0.22.5 hectares** was recorded as a Christian burial ground of the Pentecost congregation. Subsequently, in the rocky poramboke land in Survey Nos. **489/5 to 489/21**, **3.64.5 hectares** was assigned to **15 persons**, and all those lands remained in the possession of the assignees.

The owner of **J.K. Mining and Chemicals**, J. Mohan, had patta land in the same village: **1.54.0 hectares** in Survey No. **18/1B** and **1.91.5 hectares** in Survey No. **183/2**, totalling **3.45.5 hectares**. He had obtained permission from the Commissioner of Geology and Mining in Chennai to extract minerals there for **20 years**.

Those lands had no connection with the lands assigned to the 15 persons. J.K. Mines had made no encroachment upon the assigned lands. There was a distance of **two kilometres** between J.K. Mines land and the land assigned to the 15 persons.

This, the letter says, was the factual information the district collector gave the government after inquiry. Yet the article had relied either on an unidentified person making an irresponsible claim or on a person imagined by the writer, without checking the allegation with anyone in government.

## 3. Sooriyur village, Tiruchi district

### “Report published as an irregularity”

The article alleged that free lands allotted in Sooriyur — **Survey Nos. 50/11, 50/12, 52/3, 52/8, 63/1, 63/3, 63/4, 67/2, 67/12, 67/13, 67/14, 67/16 and 76/15** — were now controlled by DMK General Council member **Navalpattu Viji**; the magazine, the source notes, printed his name as **Valasalpattu Viji**. Local people were said to allege that he had built a structure for **Meena Engineering** on part of the land and that he was very close to Transport Minister K. N. Nehru. Former panchayat president and AIADMK union secretary **Jai Surya** was quoted as saying that DMK people had occupied land allotted to village residents.

### Explanation of the Tiruchi District Collector

In Sooriyur village, Survey Nos. **50/11, 50/12, 52/3, 52/8, 67/2, 67/12, 67/13, 67/14, 67/16, 63/1, 63/3 and 63/4**, totalling **4.58.5 hectares**, had been assigned to **12 persons**, and all were in the beneficiaries’ possession.

Navalpattu Viji had established **Meena Engineering Industries** in Sooriyur on barren land falling under the land-ceiling category. Those lands had no connection with the lands assigned to the 12 persons. No one had encroached upon the freely assigned lands.

With the factual position being so, the official explanation said, publishing a report linking Transport Minister K. N. Nehru on the strength of what an AIADMK union secretary had said was motivated.

## 4. Nilakottai taluk, Dindigul district

### “Report published as an irregularity”

The article alleged that **Anbu**, brother of Revenue Minister and Dindigul district secretary **I. Periasamy**, had occupied free lands announced in the Nilakottai taluk area. **S. Manikandan**, described as a beneficiary who had been announced for free land but could not obtain it, was quoted as saying that Anbu and his friend **Prakash**, a real-estate broker, were grabbing pattas given to poor people and threatening those who asked for them. It was also alleged that these persons had occupied free land along the Dindigul–Madurai highway — **Survey Nos. 4/5 through 4/9** — and started a pickle-manufacturing establishment.

### Explanation of the Dindigul District Collector

In Sandaiyur village, **0.24.0 hectares** of assessed barren land in Survey No. **38/4** had been assigned to **Thondiyammal**, wife of Karuppaiah, and **0.23.0 hectares** of assessed barren land in Survey No. **38/5** to **Santhi**, wife of Shanmugam. They were landless poor agricultural labourers and the lands remained in their possession. No one had encroached upon the assigned land.

There was no pickle-manufacturing establishment on the Dindigul–Madurai highway. One such establishment, far from Dindigul on the Theni–Andipatti road, on the route to Pullimankombai, was being built on patta land belonging to **Jitendra Jain** under the name **“Boros Agro Exports”** and was not yet complete. Further, no land had been allotted to anyone under the free-land scheme along the Dindigul–Madurai National Highway.

With these being the factual circumstances, the official explanation said, publishing the report linking Revenue Minister I. Periasamy by simply believing what Manikandan had said, without checking the truth with anyone, was malicious.

## 5. Pollachi taluk, Coimbatore district

### “Report published as an irregularity”

The article said that in Vadasithur village a carbon-manufacturing company called **Jagobi** had a building that local people claimed stood on land allotted by the government to the poor. A person named Siva was quoted as saying that Survey Nos. **576, 580 and 581**, where the company’s industrial premises were situated, were covered by the free-land scheme.

The company was said to belong to an NRI living in Sweden. AIADMK MLA for the Perur constituency and Coimbatore suburban district secretary **S. P. Velumani** was quoted as saying that the foreign company was operating without permission, causing serious environmental harm and functioning on two-acre land given to landless farmers, and that local people did not even know that two-acre plots had been allotted to many persons in the area.

### Explanation of the Coimbatore District Collector

Under the free-land scheme, no land had been given to anyone in **Mettuvavi village, Pollachi taluk**. **Jagobi Carbons India (P) Ltd.**, a carbon-manufacturing company, was situated in Survey No. **580**, which was privately owned ryotwari dry land. The company had Pollution Control Board permission up to **31-3-2010**. There was no government poramboke land within the company premises.

With the factual position being so, the official explanation said, publishing a sweeping report based on an incorrect claim attributed to AIADMK MLA S. P. Velumani, without inquiring with anyone connected with the government, was motivated.

## 6. Malumichampatti / Othakkalmandapam, Coimbatore district

### “Report published as an irregularity”

The article alleged that land at Malumichampatti — **Survey Nos. 567/25 AB through 567/40 and 738/2 through 738/40** — had been fenced and that a college was being constructed there.

### Explanation of the Coimbatore District Collector

The survey numbers published as the alleged irregularity did not exist in Malumichampatti village, the collector said. The photograph published in *India Today* showed the premises of a private engineering college — **Hindustan Engineering College** — in **Othakkalmandapam village**. The college had been built years earlier on privately owned patta land and was functioning there.

No land had been allotted to anyone under the two-acre barren-land scheme in either Malumichampatti or Othakkalmandapam villages. With the factual position being so, the official explanation said, publishing a report that misdirected readers was improper.

## 7. Theni district / Vaigai Dam area

### “Report published as an irregularity”

The article alleged that DMK Theni district secretary **Vaigai Sekar**, **Giri**, and AIADMK’s **Sannasi** had occupied about **300 acres** around the Vaigai Dam area — **Survey Nos. 1349/5, 1353/6, 1445/1, 1445/2 and 1446/1**. A Theni resident named Siva was quoted as saying that Sekar had arranged for land meant for the poor to be registered in the names of his relatives and had driven several people, including widows, off the land.

### Explanation of the Theni District Collector

In Periyakulam taluk, the villages of **Melmangalam Bit 1 and 2, Jayamangalam Bit 1 and 2, Kullapuram, Silvarpatti and T. Vadipatti**, **543.41 acres** had been assigned to **454 persons**.

No free-land assignment had been made in the name of Vaigai Sekar. In **Melmangalam Bit 2**, Survey No. **1799/2**, patta had been granted under the assignment scheme for **0.62.5 ares** in the name of Vaigai Sekar’s wife **Mahalakshmi**. The land was described as cultivable barren land. In Survey No. **1799/1**, **0.61.0 ares** had been assigned in the name of Sekar’s mother-in-law **Bhagyam**.

Near those lands, apart from Mahalakshmi and Bhagyam, land had been assigned to **14 persons in 17 survey numbers**. The lands given to those 14 people remained in the beneficiaries’ possession and they were cultivating them. Vaigai Sekar had not encroached upon any of those 14 persons’ lands.

In Periyakulam taluk, Melmangalam Bit 2, **2.61.5 hectares** in five survey numbers including **1349/5 and 1353/6** had been assigned to **six persons**. Between these assigned lands were patta lands measuring **0.40.5 hectares** in the name of **Giris Krishna**, **17.29.0 hectares** in the name of his mother **Komalavalli**, and **16.93.0 hectares** in the name of his father **Sudhakar**.

The collector’s explanation then records specific problems. It says **Giri** had encroached upon and fenced **0.62.5 hectares** in Survey No. **1349/5** assigned to **Chitra**, wife of Thangam, and **0.69.0 hectares** in Survey No. **1445/1**, assigned to **Paramasivam**, son of Muthirulan, for which patta had not yet been issued.

It further says that **0.60.0 hectares** in Survey No. **1353/6**, assigned to **Subbulakshmi**, wife of Muthusamy, had been sold by the assignee to a person from Kerala, after which she had left.

In Melmangalam Bit 1, near patta lands belonging to **Sannasi**, **0.97.0 hectares** allotted to **three persons** under the free-land scheme was not in the beneficiaries’ use. Of this, **0.45.0 ares** was under the encroachment of **Pandi**, son of Mookkaiya Thevar.

The district collector, the letter says, had initiated action to recover the land encroached upon by Giri and the land encroached upon by Pandi.

**Udanpirappē,** these are the factual details, the letter says.

With the aim of helping poor landless farmers, the DMK government began this scheme after assuming office and has so far distributed **2 lakh 10 thousand 747 acres** to **1 lakh 75 thousand 798 farmers**.

Even if one does not praise a scheme helping poor farmers, that is one thing. But, the letter asks, **will those who spew poison speak with a hand on their heart?**

Some journalists, it says, are doing something like mixing poison into a drinking-water tank. The people of Tamil Nadu must understand the intention behind such conduct and teach an appropriate lesson both to those with such harmful motives and to the few political parties that magnify their claims. That, the letter concludes, is the way to find a proper remedy for all this.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
12 January 2011
