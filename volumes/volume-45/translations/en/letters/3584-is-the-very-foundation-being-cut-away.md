# 3584. Is the Very Foundation Being Cut Away?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

Under the AIADMK Government we have held public meetings throughout the State under the title “The Plight of Democracy in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly,” met the people and created considerable awareness.

At the massive public meeting held at Thangasala in North Chennai on 25-8-2011, I said:

“Our DMK front-rankers have raised a protest call under the title ‘The Plight of Democracy in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly.’ I wish to make a correction even to that title. Only if democracy exists in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly can we speak of the plight it is suffering. In a place where democracy itself does not exist, how can we lament ‘its plight’? It is for that reason that I wish to add a correction to the title.”

The very foundation of democracy is fearless and free exchange of views at every level, healthy consultation and extensive debate. In today's Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly, this foundation is being cut away and the true form of democracy is gradually being disfigured. We see it; the people of the country too are watching silently. That is why I said that the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly is becoming a place without democracy itself.

If any member wishes to speak in today's Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly, one of three things is required. He must criticise schemes introduced under the DMK Government and implemented with overwhelming public support; or he must insult me—a person who has served continuously as a member of the Tamil Nadu Legislature through its golden-jubilee span and who served five terms and nineteen years as Chief Minister; or he must sing a “hymn of praise” saying, “Mother! Akilandeswari!” There is no place there for those who do not follow these basic requirements. That is why DMK MLAs are expelled so that they cannot come to the House and perform their democratic duty; by doing so, democracy itself is being expelled from the Legislature.

During Jayalalithaa's earlier term in office, it was she who said that the buildings in the Fort St. George complex were insufficient for the needs of the Legislative Assembly and Secretariat, and had become too old and dilapidated to maintain. Different sites were examined, and finally even a *bhoomi pooja* was performed at Kotturpuram.

With the objective of building Legislative Assembly and Secretariat buildings suited to present and future needs and equipped with modern facilities, a site was chosen in the Omandurar Government Estate on Anna Salai. About ₹500 crore of public money was spent and buildings of world standard, praised by neutral observers, were completed. The new Legislative Assembly–Secretariat was inaugurated by Prime Minister Dr Manmohan Singh in the presence of Sonia Gandhi. During the DMK Government itself, the Governor delivered his Address there, the interim financial statement was presented, and the offices of the Chief Minister and other ministers began functioning. Only then did the Assembly election arrive.

After the election, even before the AIADMK assumed office, Jayalalithaa announced—without consulting anyone—that simply because the building had been constructed during the DMK Government, the Legislative Assembly and Secretariat would be moved back to the old Fort St. George. Before taking such a major policy decision, should the Cabinet not have been convened for detailed discussion? Should not even the allied parties have been consulted and their views obtained? Thus, before assuming office and even before the Legislature began sitting, the AIADMK rulers had already taken leave of democracy!

With the socialist objective that village students and city students, privileged students and poor students without facilities should all receive the same quality and equal education, the DMK Government in 2006 set the goal of introducing Samacheer Kalvi in Tamil Nadu. A committee of eminent educationists was appointed; its recommendations were obtained; without haste, another committee was constituted to visit other States and consult there; that committee's views were obtained; legislation for Samacheer Kalvi was enacted; and it was decided to implement the system gradually. It was first introduced for Standards I and VI in the 2010–11 academic year, with a decision to extend it to the other standards from 2011–12.

Meanwhile cases were filed against Samacheer Kalvi before the Madras High Court and the Supreme Court, and only after that was the scheme sustained. Yet this Samacheer Kalvi scheme—implemented with every precaution and with the approval of the courts—was cancelled by the Jayalalithaa Government within just two days of assuming office. At the very first stage, it brought and passed a statutory amendment to cancel the scheme. Even though the Communist parties allied with the AIADMK opposed it, the amendment was passed without allowing extensive debate. The AIADMK has not the slightest faith in equality or equal treatment! Thus it was the AIADMK Government that began, in frantic haste, to expel democracy from the Legislature.

After the date for presenting the financial statement in the Legislative Assembly had been fixed, the Governor's approval obtained and notification issued, the AIADMK Government announced various tax levies amounting to about ₹4,000 crore. Those who respected legislative democracy and the long traditions of the Assembly should have announced those taxes as part of the financial statement itself. Only then would the various parties in the Assembly have had the opportunity to express views and debate them. Instead, the AIADMK rulers crushed the democratically available opportunity and strangled democracy.

The DMK Government enacted the Tamil Nadu Agricultural Labourers–Farmers Social Security and Welfare Act. Under this law, a social-security scheme for agricultural labourers and farmers was launched on 7-8-2006, followed on 18-1-2007 by the establishment of the Agricultural Labourers Welfare Board under the chairmanship of the Revenue Minister. Under this Board, **1 crore 74 lakh 65 thousand** farmers and agricultural labourers were enrolled as members. During the DMK Government, assistance totalling **₹684 crore 2 lakh 55 thousand** was provided to the families of **8 lakh 73 thousand** agricultural labourers and farmers.

The AIADMK Government repealed this law, which provided protection and welfare schemes to farmers and agricultural families, merely out of hostility because it had been enacted by a DMK Government. Even at the introduction stage, though the Communist parties allied to the AIADMK opposed the repeal, the Assembly allowed no debate or expression of views and passed the repealing law. Where is there any place in the Legislature under the AIADMK Government for democratic debate?

On 23-1-2008, His Excellency the Governor of Tamil Nadu announced in his Address to the Legislative Assembly:

“More than five hundred eminent poets, under the leadership of the great scholar Maraimalai Adigal, founder of the Pure Tamil movement, gathered in 1921 at Pachaiyappa's College in Chennai. Considering that Tamils needed a ‘separate year,’ they decided to follow a continuous era in the name of Ayyan Thiruvalluvar, to regard it as the ‘Tamil Year,’ and to place Thiruvalluvar's birth in 31 BCE. The DMK Government accepted that view and ordered it implemented in the Tamil Nadu Government diary from 1971 and in the Tamil Nadu Government Gazette from 1972. Since the first day of the month of Thai, when the Thiruvalluvar year begins, is accepted collectively by Tamil scholars as the beginning of Tamil New Year, the DMK Government has decided to declare and implement the first day of Thai as the beginning of Tamil New Year.”

In accordance with that announcement, the law was passed in the Legislative Assembly on 1-2-2008. The AIADMK rulers repealed in the Legislature, without any debate, this law that constituted one of the worthy markers of Tamil identity. On that day, democracy dissolved into the air!

The Tamil Nadu Legislature has honourable traditions of its own. Under the AIADMK Government those traditions are now taking leave one by one. Chief Minister Jayalalithaa, contrary to those traditions, levels an accusation against the Speaker himself inside the Assembly. It seems that when questions are selected, they must be chosen so that favourable answers can be given! I do not know which rule says so. The Speaker, it seems, failed to select them in that manner. That is the charge against him!

Can history forget that the Speaker himself, inside the Assembly and before everyone, fell at Jayalalithaa's feet and received her blessing? Can Tamil Nadu forget the AIADMK Assembly scene in which Jayalalithaa sat in the Speaker's chair while her “sister not born with her” sat in the Deputy Speaker's chair, both taking pride in it?

**With affection, M.K.**  
8-9-2011
