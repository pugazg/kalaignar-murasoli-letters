# 3447. Some Copper-Plate Records in the History of Classical Tamil — 3: Pavanar’s Fifteen Evidences

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This letter continues the source’s historical narrative through the 1919 non-Brahmin conference, Maulana Abul Kalam Azad, T. P. Meenakshisundaranar, Devaneya Pavanar, Manavai Mustafa and the 1996 Tamil Nadu government initiative. Source-supplied English formulations, especially Pavanar’s fifteen headings, are retained as printed. Historical and linguistic propositions are translated as claims made by the source rather than independently verified conclusions.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3447](../../../chapters/3447-semmoli-varalatril-sila-seppedugal-3-paavanar-kanda-pathinaindhu-saanrugal.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 165](../../../pages/page-165.md)–[PDF 172](../../../pages/page-172.md)  
**Date:** 27 December 2009

**Udanpirappē,**

On 22 June 1919, a non-Brahmin conference was held at the Town Hall in Tiruchirappalli. Among those who took part were Tiruchirappalli lawyer T. C. Thangavelu, Rao Bahadur O. Kandasamy Chettiar, T. A. G. Rathinam, T. V. Subramaniam, Diwan Bahadur P. Ramarayaningar, the Raja of Ramanathapuram, the Raja of Kollengode, the Zamindar of Thottappanayakkanur, T. R. Marudhamuthu Moopanar and other leading figures of the non-Brahmin movement. Resolutions were adopted regarding separate electorates for non-Brahmins, communal representation and inter-caste marriage.

Another important resolution adopted at that conference stated:

> “Tamil, which is rich in ancient literature, should be recognised by the University of Madras and the Civil Service Examiners as a Classical Language on the same footing as Persian, Arabic and Sanskrit.”

Thus, after our predecessors in the Justice Party belonging to the non-Brahmin movement passed a resolution in 1919, and after the Karanthai Tamil Sangam under Tamilavel Umamaheswaranar finally adopted another resolution in 1923 demanding recognition of Tamil as a classical language, the next major voice came from Maulana Abul Kalam Azad.

He was a freedom fighter, one of the leaders of the Indian National Congress, a determined opponent of the partition of Pakistan, a multilingual scholar and poet learned in Urdu, Arabic, Hindi, Persian, Bengali and English, India’s first Education Minister in Pandit Nehru’s Union Cabinet, an introducer of free elementary education, and a founder of the Indian Institutes of Technology. In his inaugural address on 15.3.1951 at the conference convened for the creation of the Sahitya Akademi, he said:

> “The fourteen languages recognised by the Constitution include Sanskrit and Tamil. Sanskrit is, of course, in a class by itself, and is rightly recognised as one of the most developed of classical languages. Tamil also has a rich and ancient literature and its poetry has been and deserves to be translated into foreign languages. We must however, remember that Tamil is really a Classical Language, and most of the achievements of Tamil which entitle it to recognition belong to a past age.”

Thus he pointed out that Tamil was indeed a classical language.

At the All-India Oriental Conference held at Annamalai University, Chidambaram, on 26, 27 and 28 December 1955, Professor T. P. Meenakshisundaranar, in his presidential address, referred to Azad’s 15.3.1951 speech and appealed that Tamil be accorded classical-language recognition. He asked:

> “My appeal is only to give effect to this recognition and if this Oriental Conference of Scholars is not going to give this recognition, who else would?”

The next stage in the history of the classical-language demand arose in 1966, and the person who created it was the linguistic scholar Devaneya Pavanar. After detailed study and research, Pavanar wrote and published in English the rare work *The Primary Classical Language of the World*, setting out evidence intended to establish for language scholars that Tamil was a classical language. In that work he advanced, with supporting arguments, the proposition “Tamil is a highly developed Classical Language of Lemurian Origin” and presented fifteen qualifications which, he said, Tamil possessed:

1. **Lemurian Origin of Tamil**
2. **Phonological simplicity of Tamil**
3. **Catholicity of Tamil**
4. **Tamulic substratum of the Aryan family of languages**
5. **Morphological parity and primitiveness of Tamil**
6. **The presence of the words ‘amma’ and ‘appa’ in almost all great languages in some form or other**
7. **Absence of Nominative case-termination in Tamil**
8. **Separability and significance of all affixes in Tamil**
9. **Absence of morphological gender in Tamil**
10. **Absence of arbitrary words in Tamil**
11. **Traceability of Tamil to its very origin**
12. **Logical and natural order of words in Tamil**
13. **Absence of dual number in Tamil**
14. **Originality and natural development of Tamil**
15. **Highest order of the classicality of Tamil**

The source says that these arguments attracted not only Tamil enthusiasts and scholars but also scholars of other languages, particularly those learned in Sanskrit and English, and led them to accept Pavanar’s case.

In recognition of Pavanar’s mastery of Tamil, his deep attachment to it and his efforts to secure acceptance of Tamil as a classical language, the DMK government established in 1974 a “Directorate of the Etymological Dictionary Project for Classical Tamil” and appointed linguistic scholar Devaneya Pavanar as its first Director.

Following the path of Maraimalai Adigal in seeking to protect Tamil from the impact of other languages, Pavanar also founded the “World Tamil Association.” Though he belonged to the Christian religion, the source says that he lived as a Tamil beyond religion and caste, and gave his children distinctive Pure-Tamil names: Nachinarkkiniya Nambi, Siluvaiyai Vendra Selvarayan, Arungalaivallan Adiyarkku Nallan, Madanthavirtha Mangaiyarkarasi, Manimandra Vaanan and Painthamizh Valartha Pandiyan.

Pavanar was learned in twenty-three languages and stood as a multilingual scholar. To honour his memory, the DMK government nationalised all his works in 1996 and gave ₹20 lakh as compensation to his legal heirs. Through the Union Government, a commemorative postage stamp in his honour was released on 18.2.2006. In Sathamangalam village in Madurai district, the Tamil Nadu government constructed a memorial hall for him at a cost of ₹39 lakh 60 thousand, including a full-length statue, and I inaugurated it on 30.10.2007.

After that, a long interval arose. No major formation of opinion among Tamil enthusiasts or Tamil organisations developed around the demand that Tamil be declared and accepted as a classical language. The demand was not prominently advanced at conferences or seminars.

When my dear friend M.G.R. was Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu, attempts were made in connection with the Fifth World Tamil Conference held at Madurai in 1981 to have Tamil declared a classical language. Valarthamizh Selvar Manavai Mustafa has written in his book *Semmozhi — Ullum Puramum* about those attempts and the outcome they met.

At the conference’s general seminar on the morning of the second day, chaired by Justice Maharajan, Manavai Mustafa received an opportunity to speak. He explained in detail the consequences, as he saw them, of the government not recognising Tamil despite its possessing all the qualifications for classical-language status. M.G.R., after hearing the speech, asked Mustafa to send the government a petition regarding the classical-language demand. Accordingly, in 1982, Manavai Mustafa submitted such a petition for the consideration of the Tamil Nadu government. No one was informed what action the government took on it.

In 1984, in a letter thanking the Chief Minister for conferring the Kalaimamani Award on him, Mustafa again reminded him about the classical-language question. The letter was forwarded to the departmental secretary. Mustafa later wrote in his book, with tears and a sense of helplessness, that the secretary recorded a note on the petition to the effect that if Tamil were made a classical language, as Mustafa requested, Tamil would thereby be included among dead languages, and the file was therefore closed.

Even after the classical-language demand met this “fate,” the source says that no one paid sustained attention to the field and the demand was not advanced at conferences or seminars.

Pulavar T. Sundararajan, General Secretary of the Capital Tamil Sangam, Chennai, later wrote with deep concern that during the Eighth World Tamil Conference held in Thanjavur in 1995, the demand for Tamil’s classical-language status was placed before the then Prime Minister P. V. Narasimha Rao, but the Prime Minister completed his speech without expressing any view on the demand.

The DMK government will always have the credit, the letter says, for making the song beginning “Neerarum kadaludutha nilamadanthai kezhilozhugum,” written by Manonmaniam Sundaram Pillai, the *Tamil Thai Vazhthu* in 1970. In the same way, the DMK government, which works every day to protect Tamil interests, would never stop working for the development of Tamil. As an example, when the DMK government took office for the fourth time, it created a separate ministry for Tamil Official Language and Tamil Culture, which began functioning on 13.5.1996 under Minister Dr Tamizhkudimagan.

Following my instruction that the foremost first task of this newly created department should be to undertake every effort required to seek declaration of Tamil as a classical language, a meeting was held on 20.6.1996 under the chairmanship of Tamil Culture Minister Tamizhkudimagan. Participants included former Anna University Vice-Chancellor V. C. Kulandaisamy, University of Madras Vice-Chancellor P. K. Ponnusamy, former Tamil University Vice-Chancellors S. Agathiyalingam, Avvai Natarajan and C. Balasubramaniam, former Bharathidasan University Vice-Chancellor S. Muthukumaran, Institute of Asian Studies Director G. John Samuel, Manavai Mustafa and the Secretary of the Tamil Development and Culture Department.

At that meeting, a draft report prepared by Institute of Asian Studies Director C. John Samuel concerning the declaration of Tamil as a classical language was considered, accepted with a few changes and approved for recommendation to the Union Government. It was also decided to obtain recommendations from the governing bodies of all universities in Tamil Nadu.

Because the report contained what the committee regarded as strong evidence that Tamil possessed all the basic qualifications required for classical-language status, and because it represented the first effort by the DMK government at the political, organisational and governmental levels to obtain that declaration, I want Tamil enthusiasts and DMK Udanpirappugal to read it and remember it. I shall therefore provide in my next letter the important portions of that report, originally prepared in English, translated into Tamil.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**27-12-2009**

ஆக்கிய பெருமை, கழக அரசுக்கு என்றென்றும் உண்டு. அதைப்
போலவே, தமிழர்களின் நலன் காக்க, நாளும் பாடுபடும் கழக அரசு,
தமிழ்மொழியின் வளர்ச்சிக்கும் பாடுபடுவதை எந்தநிலையிலும்
நிறுத்தாது என்பதற்கு உதாரணம்தான், நான்காம் முறையாக
கழக அரசு பதவியேற்றபோது, தமிழ் ஆட்சிமொழி மற்றும் தமிழ்ப்
பண்பாட்டுத் துறைக்காக ஒரு தனி அமைச்சகத்தை உருவாக்கியது;
அந்தத் துறை, அமைச்சர் முனைவர் தமிழ்க்குடிமகன் அவர்கள்
தலைமையில், 13.5.1996 முதல் செயல்படத் தொடங்கியது.

20.6.1996 அன்று தமிழ்ப்பண்பாட்டுத் துறை அமைச்சர்
திரு.தமிழ்க்குடிமகன் அவர்கள் தலைமையில், அண்ணா
பல்கலைக் கழகத்தின் முன்னாள் துணைவேந்தர் திரு. வா.செ.
குழந்தைசாமி, சென்னைப் பல்கலைக் கழகத்தின் துணைவேந்தர்
முனைவர் ப.க.பொன்னுசாமி, தஞ்சை தமிழ்ப் பல்கலைக் கழகத்தின்
முன்னாள் துணைவேந்தர்கள் - முனைவர் ச.அகத்தியலிங்கம்,
முனைவர் அவ்வை நடராஜன், முனைவர் சி.பாலசுப்பிர
மணியம்; திருச்சி பாரதிதாசன் பல்கலைக் கழகத்தின் முன்னாள்
துணைவேந்தர் முனைவர் ச.முத்துக்குமரன், ஆசியவியல்
ஆய்வு நிறுவனத்தின் இயக்குநர் முனைவர் ஜி.ஜான்சாமுவேல்,
திரு.மணவை முஸ்தபா தமிழ்வளர்ச்சி மற்றும் பண்பாட்டுத்
துறையின் செயலாளர் ஆகியோர் கலந்துகொண்ட கூட்டத்தில்;
தமிழ்மொழியைச் செம்மொழியென அறிவிக்கப்படுவது
தொடர்பாக, ஆசியவியல் நிறுவனத்தின் இயக்குநர் முனைவர்
சி.ஜான் சாமுவேல் அவர்களால் தயாரிக்கப்பட்ட வரைவு அறிக்கை
பரிசீலிக்கப்பட்டு, ஒரு சில மாற்றங்களுடன் ஏற்கப்பட்டு, மத்திய
அரசுக்குப் பரிந்துரைக்கலாம் என முடிவெடுக்கப்பட்டதோடு;
தமிழகத்தின் அனைத்துப் பல்கலைக்கழக ஆட்சிக் குழுக்களின்
பரிந்துரைகளையும் பெறலாம் என்றும் முடிவெடுக்கப்பட்டது.
தமிழ் செம்மொழி என அறிவிக்கப்படுவதற்குரிய
அடிப்படைத் தகுதிகள் அனைத்தையும் பெற்றிருக்கிறது
என்பதற்கான வலுவான ஆதாரங்களைக் கொண்டுள்ள அறிக்கை
என்பதாலும்; தமிழ் செம்மொழி என அறிவிக்கப்படுவதற்கு
அரசியல் ரீதியாகவும், அமைப்பு ரீதியாகவும், அரசளவிலும், கழக
அரசால் மேற்கொள்ளப்பட்ட முதல் முயற்சி என்பதாலும்; தமிழ்
ஆர்வலர்களும், கழக உடன்பிறப்புகளும் அதனைப் படித்தறிந்து

முக்கியப் பகுதிகளை அடுத்த கடிதத்தில் வழங்குகிறேன்.
