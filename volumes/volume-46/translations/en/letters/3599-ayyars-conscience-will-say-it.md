# 3599. Ayyar's Conscience Itself Will Say It!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation follows the locked Volume 46 pilot conventions. Statements about the State Election Commission, election officials, parties and alleged irregularities are preserved as claims made in Kalaignar's source text. Names, dates, vote margins, court directions and quoted interview language are not independently corrected or expanded. The complete audited Tamil follows and remains authoritative.

**Date:** 8 November 2011

**Udanpirappē,**

In an interview, the Tamil Nadu State Election Commissioner said: “Even the Election Commissioners who came from other States to observe the election were astonished that a local-body election could be conducted like this without a State Chief Minister using influence to interfere in election work.” From the fact that he has offered such praise to the Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu, can we not understand very well who he is?

Usually, after a government official is appointed to a post and people observe his actions for some days, they may say that he is not acting neutrally, that he is acting one-sidedly. But in Ayyar's case, the moment he was appointed Election Commissioner, all parties were already saying: “He is an AIADMK man; why are they appointing him Election Commissioner? Local-body elections are coming soon, are they not? That is precisely why the AIADMK government has selected and appointed him—to act favourably toward the ruling party.” Nobody can have forgotten so quickly that such criticism appeared in the newspapers.

Going further, the *Kumudam* reporter who questioned the Election Commissioner himself asked, “There is also criticism that you acted in support of the ruling party, isn't there?” and, “Before and after the election you said Tamil Nadu was a garden of peace. Is that not speech beyond the limits of an Election Commissioner?” From those very questions, can one not understand what kind of official Ayyar is and how far he goes beyond the limits of his responsibility in praising the Tamil Nadu government?

On behalf of the Kazhagam, a complaint petition was submitted even to the Governor about the extent to which the Election Commissioner was favourable to the ruling AIADMK. During the AIADMK government, Mr. Ayyar had been appointed Managing Director of TASMAC, precisely, we said, in order to help the ruling party and those favoured by it. Because this official was supportive of the ruling party in the local-body election, he informed only the ruling party in advance and kept all the other parties in darkness. With nomination filing due to begin on 22 September 2011, he announced the election schedule only on 21 September 2011. When announcing the schedule, he did not state which constituencies were reserved or which were reserved for women. Not merely political parties but voters too struggled without being able to obtain complete details about the election.

While the Model Code of Conduct was in force, the ruling party announced many welfare schemes. Even when this was brought to the Election Commission's attention, the Commissioner took no action. Several Tamil Nadu government officials were transferred in violation of the election code; the Election Commissioner ignored that too.

When the Kazhagam filed a case in the Madras High Court seeking impartial and honest conduct of the local-body elections, Chief Justice M. Y. Eqbal and Justice Sivagnanam, in their judgment dated 14 October 2011, directed that voting at all polling stations within the Corporation limits be fully video-recorded. In other places, besides video-recording sensitive booths, the judges directed the Election Commission to deploy police personnel from other States in numbers equal to the local police. The judgment also asked the State Election Commission to examine the feasibility of recording and live-streaming polling through web cameras and to take appropriate action.

But the Election Commission headed by Ayyar did not act according to these High Court directions. Voters across Chennai themselves saw that video recording was not done at many polling stations. Newspapers wrote about it in detail. Adequate police personnel were not obtained from other States.

In an order issued on 19 October 2011, the Madras High Court judges said that candidates who contested for Chennai Mayor and councillor posts would be bound by the High Court's final judgment; that the recordings from polling stations where video had been made must be submitted to the Court; and that the Election Commission must respond within two weeks. If that is what the Court had to say, one must ask what meaning there is in the Election Commissioner claiming that the election was properly conducted.

Further, during polling, ruling-party men engaged in serious violence in Chennai and in various other cities. Many DMK members were attacked. Even when complaints about these were made to the Election Commission, no action was taken.

Although ₹85 lakh had been allocated for video-recording the polling, only ₹7 lakh was actually spent for that purpose.

*Nakkheeran* reported that even the statistics released by the Election Commission were wrong—that victories by independents had been counted and published as AIADMK victories. Why has the Election Commission not replied to that report even to this day? From that itself, can one not see the calibre of the Election Commission?

The Election Commissioner, who ought to conduct local-body elections neutrally, spoke in his interview like a party man. He said that in a previous local-body election, councillors assembled one morning in a municipality to elect the municipal chairperson; the chairperson was elected, but the person who actually cast the votes was the Municipal Commissioner. When asked why, he said all sixteen councillors lacked the ability to see, and therefore “I helped by voting on their behalf.” Had Ayyar also written which municipality this occurred in, what evidence existed for it, or whether any newspaper had reported such a thing, one could have known what was true! Would a Municipal Commissioner ever say that every elected councillor in an entire municipality lacked sight? Even when telling a lie, should it not at least be made plausible? Is any further example needed to show how one-sidedly today's Election Commissioner behaves? Is it not precisely because this Ayyar would say such things that the AIADMK government searched for him and appointed him to this post!

Ayyar asked in his interview, “Let someone show that even one fraud occurred in this election.” Here are just a few examples of frauds under the Election Commission for which Ayyar served as Commissioner:

- Near Palani, AIADMK men allegedly locked a polling station from the inside and cast bogus votes.
- Near Tirunelveli, an independent candidate was allegedly attacked and chased away and AIADMK men cast bogus votes.
- In Chennai's Thiru. Vi. Ka. Nagar Ward 76, repression and lawlessness were unleashed. In many other places AIADMK men allegedly captured polling booths and engaged in violence; several opposition members were hacked and assaulted.
- Near Mannargudi, people were unable to vote because of the alleged high-handedness of the AIADMK candidate.
- In Tiruppur district, AIADMK men allegedly seized booth slips from government officials and distributed them only to their own party workers.

After all this, the Election Commissioner told reporters that the election had been very peaceful. A demand was made for repolling in 239 polling stations, but instead of listening to that request, repolling was held merely for form's sake in seven booths. Palani Municipal Chairman DMK candidate Velumani was arrested, allegedly at the instigation of AIADMK men.

In Karur district, Krishnarayapuram Panchayat Union Council Ward 6 member K. Muthukumar was shown even in the State Election Commission's own list as having won by 125 votes. But because of AIADMK intervention, a recount was ordered; the DMK candidate and his agents were threatened, and the AIADMK candidate was declared elected.

In Sivaganga district, during counting for Tiruppattur Ward 8 Panchayat Union Council, Ward 9 Panchayat Union Council and Kalaiyarkoil Ward 7 Panchayat Union Council, votes polled for DMK candidates were allegedly added to the AIADMK candidates' totals and AIADMK candidates were declared elected.

In Tharamangalam Union, Salem district, M. S. Krishnan, who contested for the presidency of Desavilakku panchayat, won by 30 votes. But because of AIADMK intervention, the AIADMK candidate was declared elected.

In Valangaiman Union, Thiruvarur district, DMK candidate R. Selvakumar won the Poondhottam panchayat president's post by 14 votes, and this was entered on the State Election Commission website. Later, owing to AIADMK intervention, the AIADMK candidate was declared elected.

In Vellore district's Timiri Union, DMK candidate Mrs. R. Shanthi was first declared winner of the Valayathur panchayat president's election by three votes. Around 11 p.m., it was then said that the AIADMK candidate had received nine more votes, and that candidate was declared elected.

In Tiruvallur district's Minjur Union, DMK candidate T. C. Sakthivel won Union Council Ward 16 by 137 votes. But AIADMK Ponneri MLA Mr. Pon. Raja entered the counting centre, allegedly threatened officials and caused the AIADMK candidate to be declared elected.

In the counting for Cholavaram Union Council Ward 8, the victory of DMK candidate J. Krubavathi was changed and the AIADMK candidate was declared elected.

In R. K. Pettai Union, during the election for District Panchayat Ward 2, election officials and police officials allegedly threatened DMK candidate Ma. Raghu and his agents, removed them from the counting venue and conducted counting with only AIADMK representatives present.

In the Nilgiris district, in Coonoor Union Council Ward 2, DMK candidate Mrs. Saroja was initially declared elected by three votes. At the ruling AIADMK's alleged instigation, a recount was announced. Even in the recount the DMK candidate won by two votes, yet the AIADMK candidate was declared elected.

In Coonoor Union Ward 8, when DMK candidate Mrs. Lakshmi was in a position to win, votes cast for the Rising Sun symbol were allegedly placed in the Two Leaves bundle; officials, yielding to AIADMK threats, stamped those ballots a second time, invalidated them, and declared the AIADMK candidate elected.

As for Chennai, after 2:30 p.m. on 17 October 2011, ministers Senthamizhan and Gokula Indira and MLAs V. P. Kalairajan, Vetrivel and P. Valarmathi allegedly led groups that captured several polling stations and cast bogus votes. DMK lawyers petitioned the Election Commission seeking repolling in those booths where such wrongs had taken place, but the Commission did not even listen.

When the DMK was in government, Dr. Hande, then in the opposition, alleged in the Assembly that a “muster roll” scam had taken place in the Chennai Corporation. I said, “We will investigate it, and if it is true, even though the DMK is governing the Corporation, orders will be issued to dissolve the Corporation.” History records that I later did dissolve the Chennai Corporation and took action including the arrest of DMK Corporation councillors.

Just as “one grain of cooked rice is enough to test the whole pot,” if one example alone is needed to show how Election Commissioner Ayyar conducted himself in the local-body election, it is this: at the 11th Cabinet meeting held on 1 November 2011, a resolution was passed—something unprecedented—congratulating and praising State Election Commissioner Ayyar! Does that not bring to mind the proverb, “If you have seen the mother at the pond bank, do you still need to go home to see the daughter?”

From the list I have given, let Ayyar himself say, “for God to know,” which item is wrong. After that, his own conscience will tell him about his honesty—and his neutrality!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**8 November 2011**
