# 3621. By What Shall This Be Viewed?

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3621](../../../chapters/3621-ethaik-kondu-ithaip-paarppathu.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 233](../../../pages/page-233.md)–[PDF 238](../../../pages/page-238.md)  
**Date:** 5 May 2012

**Udanpirappē,**

“Tughlaq” Ramasamy, answering a question in the issue dated 2 May 2012 on what the DMK's position is on the Sri Lankan question, has said that the DMK's position on Sri Lanka is like a kaleidoscope: with every movement the colour changes, the design changes, and so on. Our daily “Viduthalai” has written an excellent reply to this in an editorial itself.

Viduthalai has asked:

> “Mr. Cho Ramasamy, who has now set out to criticise the DMK's position on the Eelam Tamil question, has not written even a single line about the contradictions that the AIADMK and its General Secretary Jayalalithaa have adopted from time to time on the Eelam Tamil question. Why? Why does he hesitate to point out those contradictions? Or why is he afraid to?”

Do you know what Jayalalithaa first said about the Sri Lankan Tamil question in an interview she gave to the English daily “Indian Express” dated 4 October 1990?

> “The Sinhala army and police are intensely engaged in destroying the Tamil race in Sri Lanka. The Liberation Tigers movement is engaged in a courageous struggle and is fighting bravely against the Sinhala army. During the past two months, the Liberation Tigers have not engaged in any activities in Tamil Nadu that could give rise to complaints. The Liberation Tigers movement is now waging war against the government of a country. This is an act of extraordinary courage.
>
> We must keep in mind that if the Liberation Tigers are destroyed, the entire Tamil race in Sri Lanka will be destroyed. The victory of the Liberation Tigers is the victory of the Sri Lankan Tamils. Instead of doing anything to help them, Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Karunanidhi is concentrating only on protecting himself. All his strength and attention are being devoted entirely to protecting the Chief Minister's chair. If Karunanidhi's attitude is like this, we cannot expect V.P. Singh to act differently.
>
> In the changed circumstances, the only remedy is for the Tamil people to support the Liberation Tigers completely. The Chief Minister must provide every possible assistance within his authority. The Government of India must support the Liberation Tigers one hundred per cent.”

Has Jayalalithaa been conducting herself on the Sri Lankan Tamil question in accordance with what she said in that interview? Does Cho Ramasamy have a conscience? Does he not know the truth? Did this same affection Jayalalithaa had for the Liberation Tigers continue?

The very same Jayalalithaa who told journalists in October 1990 that I was not supportive of the Liberation Tigers, met the then President of India, Mr. R. Venkataraman, on 30 December 1990 and submitted a prepared complaint alleging that the DMK Government was abetting the activities of the Liberation Tigers.

Do you know what Jayalalithaa—who today enjoys the support of Cho Ramasamy—said in 1997, when the DMK was in office and the Sri Lankan Tamil question arose?

> “Karunanidhi is trying to disrupt law and order—the Liberation Tigers have again raised their head in Tamil Nadu.”

Was that not the statement Jayalalithaa issued?

And on 16 April 2002, Jayalalithaa moved and passed this resolution in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly:

> “This Legislative Assembly urges the Union Government to take immediate steps to have the Sri Lankan Government immediately arrest Liberation Tigers leader Velupillai Prabhakaran and hand him over to the Government of India. Further, this Assembly urges the Union Government not to permit any member of the banned Liberation Tigers movement to enter the Indian nation. With the permission of the Sri Lankan Government, our Indian Army must be sent to Sri Lanka and Prabhakaran must be captured and brought back.”

Yet Jayalalithaa appears, to Cho Ramasamy's eyes, to be a person with one consistent policy on the Sri Lankan question. Is that all?

When Rajapaksa's Sinhala forces were killing innocent Tamils in Sri Lanka and processions, public meetings and demonstrations were held in Tamil Nadu condemning it, Jayalalithaa issued a statement on 17 January 2009 saying:

> “The Sri Lankan army does not intend to kill Sri Lankan Tamils. When a war—a battle—is taking place, innocent people will be killed. No country is an exception to this. Wherever a war or battle takes place, innocent people are killed. But what is happening in Sri Lanka today is that the Liberation Tigers are holding the Sri Lankan Tamils, not allowing them to go to safe places, and are forcibly using them as a shield in front of the army.”

Yet she appears, to Cho Ramasamy's pen, to be a person with one consistent policy on the Sri Lankan Tamil question.

When Tamilselvan, who had been the political-wing leader of the Liberation Tigers movement, died, I wrote a condolence poem in “Murasoli” dated 4 November 2007. For that, Jayalalithaa issued a statement saying, “This itself is proof that Karunanidhi has secret links with the Tigers. It is strongly condemnable.” In Cho Ramasamy's view, that Jayalalithaa apparently has a consistent policy, while I am the one said to have a contradictory policy!

Is that all? When an all-party meeting in Tamil Nadu unanimously passed a resolution condemning the genocide of the Tamil race in Sri Lanka and demanding that an immediate ceasefire be announced, it was this very Jayalalithaa who said:

> “The suspicion has arisen in the minds of the people of Tamil Nadu that Karunanidhi is perhaps acting in support of the Liberation Tigers. The Government of India has no authority to stop the civil war now taking place in Sri Lanka.”

She also said that the war taking place in Sri Lanka was a war against the banned terrorist organisation called the Liberation Tigers, and that this was why Karunanidhi wrote elegies when leaders of the Liberation Tigers organisation died. Has Cho Ramasamy conveniently forgotten that all the newspapers in Tamil Nadu published that statement?

For the rights of the Eelam Tamils living in Sri Lanka—for those people not to be set aside there as second-class citizens, but to obtain all rights and live with equality and equal justice—the DMK has continuously raised its voice from 1956 onward, whether in government or in opposition. Are Cho Ramasamy or the AIADMK really qualified to find fault, on the Sri Lankan Tamil question, with that DMK and with me, who am the leader of that movement?

When the “Indian Peace Keeping Force,” after going to Sri Lanka and killing Sri Lankan Tamils themselves, returned to the Indian motherland, I, who was then Chief Minister, refused to go and welcome them. Does Cho Ramasamy not remember that Jayalalithaa criticised me at the time by saying, “It was DMK leader Karunanidhi who did not go to welcome the Indian Peace Keeping Force returning to the country after going to Sri Lanka”?

When what can be called the final-stage war was taking place in Sri Lanka in 2008–2009, on 14 October 2008, as Chief Minister I personally signed and sent invitation letters to the leader of every party for an all-party leaders' meeting. But Jayalalithaa boycotted that meeting, calling it an “eye-wash drama.” I believe Cho Ramasamy knows this too.

I have already written, date by date, about all the actions I have taken since 1956 on the question of protecting Sri Lankan Tamils. In these fifty-five years of my involvement with the Sri Lankan Tamil question, when it comes to the welfare of Sri Lankan Tamils, no one can say that at any time I yielded to anyone. But as for Jayalalithaa's contradictions, Cho Ramasamy himself must tell us: by what shall this be viewed?

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**5 May 2012**
