# 3773. Let Us Raise Our Voice for People of Every Caste to Become Priests!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

In January 1970, Thanthai Periyar announced that he was going to begin an agitation. He sounded the drum of struggle for an agitation based on two demands: that everyone, without caste discrimination, should be able to enter temples up to the sanctum and worship God; and that everyone should have an equal right to become a temple priest, without caste superiority or inferiority.

I issued an appeal to Thanthai Periyar, assuring him that legislation would be enacted to put his objective into practice without the need for such an agitation. In that appeal, I said:

> “I do not expect anyone in this century to argue that only members of a particular community must be priests. At a time when the world is moving rapidly and the principle that no human being is superior or inferior to another by birth is gaining strength, no one should stubbornly cling to such a backward idea. Those who honour Valluvar, Buddha and Gandhiji cannot justly insist that only members of a particular caste may serve as priests before God.
>
> “I have no disagreement with the view that priests must possess certain qualifications, observe the sacred disciplines expected of them and receive the necessary training. Those who receive such training, whatever community they belong to, may qualify and become priests. The Government is considering rules for this purpose.
>
> “At the same time, the Government is also considering whether the children of hereditary priestly families who wish to become priests may be given initial preference. Once such rules are framed and priestly appointments are determined not by birth but solely by qualification, it will also amount in principle to accepting that worshippers may go up to the sanctum without caste discrimination. The place need be entrusted to one person's responsibility only for the protection of the objects within the sanctum; caste colour must not be painted upon it. Not only this Government, but everyone who accepts that all are equal before God must accept this.
>
> “The Government therefore gives its assurance that it will frame rules to bring about this good situation and remove the degrading injustice inflicted upon others in temples in the name of caste. I affectionately request Periyar to accept this assurance and postpone the agitation he has planned.”

In response to my statement and appeal, Periyar agreed to postpone the struggle he had intended to conduct.

In accordance with the assurance I had given Thanthai Periyar, the “Priests Act” was introduced and passed in the Legislative Assembly on 2 December 1970. The Sanatanists who opposed equality became enraged on seeing that law. Would they readily agree to lose the dominance they had enjoyed for generations? They therefore went to the Supreme Court. Twelve writ petitions were filed, claiming that the amendment interfered with the constitutional right of priests to conduct their religious occupation.

The Supreme Court delivered judgment on 14 March 1972. It said that appointing priests in temples was a secular activity without community distinction; that the Government had the right to intervene in such activities; that the legislation challenged by the petitioners did not interfere in religious observances or religious affairs; and that the amendment enacted by the Tamil Nadu Government was therefore valid.

Yet, when it came to what could be done in practice, implementation of the law was blocked. A constitutional amendment was said to be necessary before it could be put into effect. Although this was repeatedly explained to the Union Government and discussed with Union Ministers, the constitutional amendment was never carried out.

Angered by this, Periyar had a resolution adopted at the Dravidar Kazhagam conference held on 8 and 9 December 1973 to conduct a nationwide agitation. But before that agitation could be held, Periyar passed away on 24 December 1973.

When the AIADMK held the unveiling of Periyar's statue near the Anna Flyover in Chennai on 18 September 1977, Annai Maniammaiyar participated on behalf of the Dravidar Kazhagam. Speaking in the presence of the then Chief Minister M. G. R., she pointed out that the Priests Act had been crippled and said with emotion:

> “Periyar had kept within his heart the desire that the Constitution should be amended for this purpose, and had planned an agitation for it. He passed away before he could conduct it. At the time of Periyar's death, Kalaignar Karunanidhi said: ‘We fulfilled so many of Periyar's aspirations. But we could not make this Priests Act rise and walk. We have therefore buried him with a thorn still in his heart.’ Chief Minister M. G. R., please arrange for the Constitution to be amended and the Priests Act implemented.”

At Periyar's centenary celebration on the Chennai seafront in 1978, I spoke with Union Defence Minister Jagjivan Ram on the stage and made this appeal to the Union Government:

> “Periyar had many aspirations. He wanted to see a society without caste discrimination and a society freed from superstition. Though those aspirations were gradually fulfilled, Periyar passed away without seeing this one aspiration fulfilled. On behalf of this vast gathering assembled here like a flood, the appeal I make to the Union Government at this centenary celebration is this: amend the Constitution so that the Priests Act may be implemented.”

But that appeal was not accepted.

Therefore, to remove the thorn that remained in Thanthai Periyar's heart, as soon as the DMK returned to office a Government Order was issued on 23 May 2006. It enabled all Hindus possessing the required qualifications and competence, without caste distinction, to become priests in temples.

At that time, Agnihotram Ramanuja Thathachariar—a Vedic scholar who had struggled for many years for worship in Tamil—said:

> “The decision announced by the newly elected Karunanidhi Ministry that people of all castes may become priests is as authoritative as a Vedic pronouncement. Brahmins used the Agamas to keep members of other castes out of temples. But the truth is that the Brahmin and the Agama themselves are in conflict. The Brahmin has invoked the name of an Agama that is contrary to him in order to deny entry to others.
>
> “In fact, under the Pancharatra Agama there is no caste doctrine at all. Even according to the Agamas, anyone may perform worship. Because Brahmins turned it into their occupation, they did not allow anyone else inside. Ramasamy Naicker once asked me: ‘Thathachariar, may we not take a flower in our own hands and place it before your God?’ Karunanidhi has now enabled that flower to be placed. This is a reform that must be welcomed. Anyone who opposes it is obstructing the people's progress. It is we who must give them good sense and reform them.”

Following the Government Order of the DMK Government, Saiva priest-training centres were opened at Palani, Tiruchendur, Madurai and Tiruvannamalai, and Vaishnava priest-training centres at Chennai and Srirangam. Students were given food, clothing and accommodation free of cost, together with a monthly incentive of ₹500 each.

Under this scheme, 207 students completed training and received certificates: 34 from the Adi Dravidar community, 76 from the Backward Classes, 55 from the Most Backward Classes and 42 from other communities.

After the training was completed, the issue of people of all castes becoming priests was taken to the Supreme Court. Because of that litigation, immediate temple employment could not be created for them.

The AIADMK Government subsequently assumed office in Tamil Nadu. Before the Supreme Court, the Tamil Nadu Government sought and obtained six months to resolve the priesthood issue. Even after those six months ended, the AIADMK Government took no action.

Members of the Association of Trained Priests said:

> “Although the Supreme Court granted six months, the State Government has not so far conducted any consultation. We therefore doubt the State Government's intention. We do not know what the present Tamil Nadu Government intends to do with the Government Order issued in 2006. We will, however, not give up our legal struggle.”

Lawyers too have expressed the view that, under the Constitution, trained priests from all castes have the right to obtain employment in temples.

It is in this situation that the Executive Committee of our parent movement, the Dravidar Kazhagam, under the initiative of Tamilar Thalaivar and my younger brother Veeramani, adopted a resolution and announced a programme of action. It called for demonstrations at every district headquarters on 1 August, demanding the right of people of all castes to become priests and pressing the Tamil Nadu Government to implement it; a conference bringing together those who share this position; seminars in district headquarters; and, as a third stage, a civil-disobedience agitation.

The Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam has supported from the beginning the decision that people of all castes may become priests. I have written this letter to make clear that the DMK also supports this resolution adopted by the Dravidar Kazhagam.

I urge members of the DMK to participate in the struggles conducted under the Dravidar Kazhagam's resolution and take the necessary steps to give those struggles greater strength and momentum.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**14 July 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. **Archakar** means a Hindu temple priest who performs ritual worship. The body uses “priest” for clear English; the source policy and historical campaign are often known as the “all-caste archakar” issue.
2. **Sanatanists** renders `சனாதனிகள்`, Kalaignar's term for defenders of hereditary caste privilege in the religious order.
3. **Agama** refers to bodies of Hindu temple doctrine and ritual regulation. **Pancharatra Agama** is retained as a specific Vaishnava tradition cited in the source.
4. **Thanthai Periyar**, **Annai Maniammaiyar** and **Tamilar Thalaivar** are retained as established movement honorifics.

அறிவித்துள்ளனர்.

இந்த நிலையில்தான் நமது தாய்க் கழகமான திராவிடர்
கழகத்தின் தலைமைச் செயற்குழுக் கூட்டத்தில், தமிழர் தலைவர்,
இளவல் வீரமணி அவர்களின் முனைப்போடு நிறைவேற்றப்பட்ட
தீர்மானத்தில், அனைத்து சாதியினருக்கும் அர்ச்சகர் உரிமையை
வலியுறுத்தும் வகையிலும், தமிழ்நாடு அரசு இதனைச்
செயல்படுத்தும் வகையில் நடவடிக்கை எடுக்கவேண்டுமென்று
வற்புறுத்தும் வகையிலும் ஆகஸ்ட் முதல் தேதியன்று அனைத்துக்
கழக மாவட்டத் தலைநகரங்களிலும் ஆர்ப்பாட்டம்; இதில் ஒத்த
கருத்துள்ளவர்களை அழைத்து மாநாடு ஒன்றினை நடத்துவது; மாவட்டத் தலைநகரங்களில் கருத்தரங்குகள் நடத்துவது;
மூன்றாவது கட்டமாக மறியல் போராட்டத்தை நடத்துவது
என்றெல்லாம் முடிவெடுத்து அறிவித்திருக்கிறார்கள்.

அனைத்துச் சாதியினரும் அர்ச்சகர் ஆகலாம் என்ற முடிவுக்குத்
தொடக்கம் முதல் ஆதரவாக இருந்த திராவிட முன்னேற்றக் கழகம்,
திராவிடர் கழகம் நிறைவேற்றியிருக்கும் இந்தத் தீர்மானத்திற்கும்
ஆதரவாக இருக்கும் என்பதைத் தெரிவிப்பதற்காகத்தான் இந்தக்
கடிதத்தை எழுதியுள்ளேன். திராவிடர் கழகத் தீர்மானத்தின்படி,
இதற்காக நடத்தும் போராட்டங்களில் கழக  உறுப்பினர்கள்
பங்கேற்று அந்தப் போராட்டத்திற்கு எழுச்சியூட்டும் வகையில்
தேவையான நடவடிக்கைகளிலும் ஈடுபட  வேண்டுமென்று
வலியுறுத்துகிறேன்.
