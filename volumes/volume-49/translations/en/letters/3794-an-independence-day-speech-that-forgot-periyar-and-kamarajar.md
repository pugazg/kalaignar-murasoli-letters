# 3794. An Independence Day Speech That Forgot Periyar and Kamarajar!

**Date:** 17 August 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

Yesterday, 15 August 2013, Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Jayalalithaa hoisted the Indian national flag in front of Fort St George and delivered her Independence Day address. Great expectations had been created. It was said that the Chief Minister was preparing numerous new announcements; that her speech was being drafted; that it would surpass even the Prime Minister’s address in Delhi; and that it would contain dramatic advance announcements for the approaching parliamentary election, compelling all India to turn and look at Tamil Nadu. Newspapers supporting the AIADMK published such reports and aroused public interest.

But when one reads the Chief Minister’s speech, it is nothing more than a compilation of the work that the last Budget claimed had been carried out by Government departments during the past two and a half years. There was only one announcement at the end: an increase in the pension for freedom fighters.

Yet the newspapers favoured by the ruling party carried full-page advertisements for the event. Some even carried a full-page advertisement on their front page. During DMK rule, advertisements for this event occupied only a quarter-page. Do you know what information was contained in the present full-page advertisement? Nothing beyond what everybody already knew: “The Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu will hoist the national flag at the Secretariat and deliver an address.”

The advertising did not end with that one page. Another half-page advertisement, accompanied by a full-length photograph of the Chief Minister, announced a ceremony at which she would lay a wreath at the War Memorial and pay tribute to soldiers. No such event had taken place in any previous year. Suddenly it was arranged this year—and received half-page advertisements. Perhaps it was thought that, when the President in Delhi pays tribute to soldiers, how can our State remain without a similar ceremony? The event lasted two minutes, but it received half-page advertisements in every newspaper. It is only the people’s tax money, is it not? Even if advertisements covering two whole pages are issued, can anyone question it?

These were not the only novelties this year. Every year, the flag-hoisting ceremony at the Fort takes place at about 8.30 a.m. For some reason, this year it was held only at 9 a.m. Once before, again under an AIADMK Government, it was said that the time had been altered after consulting the almanac because it was *Rahu Kalam* and it would not be proper for the Chief Minister to hoist the flag then.

The timing of the ceremony at the Fort was not the only change. Every year, on Independence Day and Republic Day, the Governor of Tamil Nadu hosts an evening reception and tea for leading dignitaries at his residence. For the first time this year, that too was changed: the evening tea was moved to 11 a.m. The reason is not known.

*Dinamani* reported that the MLAs of the principal Opposition, the DMDK, and those of the DMK, Communist Party of India (Marxist), Communist Party of India, Congress, PMK, Manithaneya Makkal Katchi and Puthiya Tamilagam did not participate in the reception, while dissident DMDK MLAs Ma. Pa. Pandiarajan and Sundararajan did attend.

On 25 December 2011, Chief Minister Jayalalithaa made an important announcement. To encourage Government employees, she decided that an annual “Good Governance Award” would be presented on Independence Day to the finest employees selected on the basis of their public service and the new methods and initiatives they had used to take Government schemes to the people. The Chief Minister would present a medal and prize money of two lakh rupees.

Newspapers reported that, for this model scheme—said to be the first of its kind in Tamil Nadu and intended to bring out the talents hidden among Government employees—Jayalalithaa had ordered an annual allocation of fourteen lakh rupees: six lakh for the awards and eight lakh for selection and documentation.

But *Dinamani* itself noted that, despite the Chief Minister’s announcement, no individual received the award on Independence Day 2012. On Independence Day 2013, for the second consecutive year, no Government employee received it. It was reported that Good Governance Awards had been given to three Government institutions, but no individual received the award this year either. Perhaps they assumed that no officer would remember the Chief Minister’s announcement!

Next, “protocol” is extremely important when speaking at major occasions such as an Independence Day celebration. Those who hold responsibilities such as that of Chief Minister must be especially careful.

In this year’s speech, the Chief Minister addressed the dignitaries by first acknowledging the Speaker of the Legislative Assembly, then the Ministers, and only after them the Acting Chief Justice of the Madras High Court. Under protocol, she should have acknowledged the Chief Justice before the Ministers. I say all this only so that greater care may at least be taken hereafter.

Last year was worse. She acknowledged the Speaker, then the Ministers, then Members of Parliament and the Legislative Assembly, and only after all of them the Chief Justice of the Madras High Court. It is painful that the Chief Minister’s speech was prepared and placed before her to read without attention even to this.

The speech delivered by Chief Minister Jayalalithaa yesterday appears, with a few changes, to be a copy of the Independence Day speech she delivered last year.

At the beginning of last year’s address she had said:

> “Tamil Nadu’s contribution to the freedom struggle that shattered the yoke of British slavery was immense. I praise and salute the countless martyrs who fought for Indian freedom, including Nerkattumseval Puli Thevan, Veerapandiya Kattabomman, Tiruppur Kumaran, the Marudhu Brothers, Velu Nachiyar, Vanchinathan, Thillaiyadi Valliammai, V.O. Chidambaranar, Mahakavi Bharathi, Pasumpon Muthuramalinga Thevar, Dheerar Sathyamurthi, Subramania Siva, Dheeran Chinnamalai, Maaveeran Alagu Muthukone, Perunthalaivar Kamarajar and Thanthai Periyar.”

In yesterday’s address this year, she said:

> “It would not be an exaggeration to say that the first voices raised from Tamil Nadu against the British for freedom were those of the Palayakkarars. Countless leaders—including Veerapandiya Kattabomman, Puli Thevan, the Marudhu Brothers, Dheeran Chinnamalai, V.O. Chidambaram Pillai, Mahakavi Subramania Bharathiar, Tiruppur Kumaran, Dheerar Sathyamurthi, Subramania Siva, Pasumpon Muthuramalinga Thevar, the foreign-born woman warrior Annie Besant and Maaveeran Vanchinathan—shed their blood and gave their lives to win freedom from the British for us.”

Because it was an Independence Day address and the names of those who laboured for freedom had to be mentioned, one may explain that this year she was compelled to repeat what had been said last year. My sorrow is that important leaders included last year—Perunthalaivar Kamarajar and Thanthai Periyar—were omitted from the Chief Minister’s speech this year. It makes one wonder whether this omission was a deliberate wrong.

The Chief Minister’s speeches both last year and this year then contained compilations of schemes said to have been implemented by the Government through various departments. It is natural for an annual Independence Day address to summarise the achievements of the Government. Most of what appeared in this year’s speech had already been stated two months ago during the Budget and the debates on demands for grants.

For example, page 49 of the AIADMK Government’s Budget statement read in the Assembly on 21 March 2013 said: “Since this Government assumed administrative responsibility in 2011, 51,757 teachers have been selected.” In yesterday’s speech, the Chief Minister repeated: “In the past two years, 51,757 teachers have been appointed.” From her own speech, therefore, we can understand that not even one teacher was appointed by this Government during the intervening five months.

Pages 3–4 of the School Education Department’s policy note last year stated that orders had been issued to fill 49,869 teaching posts in 2011–12 and 13,756 in 2012–13. Added together, these amount to 63,125 teaching posts. Yet the Chief Minister spoke of 51,757 teachers. It is therefore evident that even all the sanctioned posts have not yet been filled.

Further, when the year 2011–12 is mentioned, one must remember that the DMK Government remained in office in Tamil Nadu until May 2011 and that the initiatives taken by that Government made it possible to fill 49,369 teaching posts. If that figure is deducted, one must conclude that the present Government’s efforts newly selected only 2,388 teachers. The Government itself must clarify the true position.

The Chief Minister’s speech simply listed many schemes claimed to be under implementation through other departments.

Last year, she said in her Independence Day address: “A scheme providing a pension of 1,000 rupees under social-security programmes is being implemented.” In yesterday’s speech she again said: “My Government is providing 1,000 rupees in place of the pension of 500 rupees that had been given under the social-security scheme.” Thus, this year too she merely recalled what had already been said last year.

The Chief Minister next said: “I have confidence that we shall establish our rights over Katchatheevu.” An evening newspaper even made this its front-page headline. Similarly, when all newspapers reported Jayalalithaa’s address in 1991, the headline was: “Chief Minister Jayalalithaa vows that she will certainly retrieve Katchatheevu.” More than twenty years have passed. She has served as Chief Minister three more times since then.

What did Jayalalithaa say on 15 August 1991? “This Government is prepared to argue with the Union Government to retrieve Katchatheevu and, if necessary, to fight.” The Jayalalithaa who thundered those words now says only that she has confidence that “we shall establish our rights over Katchatheevu.”

Finally, the sole announcement in the Chief Minister’s Independence Day speech was a modest increase in the pension for freedom fighters and the family pension. Even that was “copied” from last year’s address. Yes, last year too she raised those pensions to some extent. This year she raised them further. It is therefore merely a routine announcement.

Taken as a whole, the Chief Minister’s greatly anticipated and heavily advertised Independence Day speech resembles today’s extremely expensive onion: peel it layer after layer and nothing remains. Read this speech closely, layer after layer, and one finds neither salt nor sweetness—only heat. But just as peeling an onion brings tears, reading this speech and thinking of the condition of the people of the State brings only tears!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**

17 August 2013

ஆட்சியில் எடுக்கப்பட்ட முயற்சிகளின் காரணமாகத்தான் 49,369

தெரிந்து கொள்ளலாம். அதைக் கழித்து விட்டால், இந்த அரசின்
முயற்சி காரணமாக வெறும் 2,388 ஆசிரியர்களை மட்டுமே
புதிதாகத் தேர்ந்தெடுத்திருக்கிறார்கள் என்றுதான் முடிவுக்கு வர
வேண்டியுள்ளது. இதுகுறித்து உண்மை நிலையை அரசுதான்
தெளிவுபடுத்திட வேண்டும்.

மற்ற துறைகளின் மூலமாகச் செய்யப்பட்டு வருவதாகச்
சொல்லிக் கொள்ளும் பல திட்டங்களைத்தான் அந்த உரையிலே
முதலமைச்சர் வரிசைப்படுத்தி யிருக்கிறார்.

கூறப்பட்டவைகளைத்தான் இந்த ஆண்டிலும் மீண்டும்
நினைவுபடுத்தியிருக்கிறார்.

அடுத்து முதலமைச்சர் தனது உரையில் “கச்சத் தீவில்
நமக்குள்ள உரிமையை நாம் நிலைநாட்டுவோம் என்ற நம்பிக்கை
எனக்கு இருக்கிறது” என்று பேசியிருக்கிறார். அதைத்தான்
மாலைப் பத்திரிகை ஒன்று கூட முதல் பக்கத்தில் தலைப்புச்
செய்தியாக வெளியிட்டுள்ளது. இதேபோல்தான் 1991ஆம்
ஆண்டு முதலமைச்சர் ஜெயலலிதா பேசிய உரையை அனைத்து
நாளேடுகளும் வெளியிட்டபோது அதற்குத் தலைப்பு “கச்சத்
தீவை மீட்டே தீருவேன் என்று முதலமைச்சர் ஜெயலலிதா
சூளுரை” என்பதாகும். இருபதாண்டுகளுக்கு மேலாகிவிட்டது.
அதற்குப் பிறகு மூன்று முறை முதலமைச்சராக இருந்துவிட்டார்.

தீவைத் திரும்பப் பெற மத்திய அரசுடன் வாதாடவும், தேவை
ஏற்பட்டால் போராடவும் இந்த அரசு தயாராக உள்ளது” என்று
முழங்கிய ஜெயலலிதாதான், தற்போது “கச்சத் தீவில் நமக்குள்ள
உரிமையை நிலைநாட்டுவோம் என்ற நம்பிக்கை இருக்கிறது”
என்று பேசியிருக்கிறார்.

இறுதியாக முதலமைச்சர் விடுதலை நாள் விழா உரையில்
