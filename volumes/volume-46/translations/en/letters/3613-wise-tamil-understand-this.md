# 3613. Wise Tamil, It Is Enough If You Understand This!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This draft follows the locked Volume 46 conventions. The Maraimalai Adigal/Thiruvalluvar-year chronology, quoted Governor’s Address, statements by Tamil scholars and movement leaders, the Nakkiran–Kuyakkondan story, legislative history, Bharathidasan verse and Kalaignar’s closing appeal are preserved in source order. The complete audited Tamil remains authoritative. This draft awaits separate bilingual review.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3613](../../../chapters/3613-puththiyulla-tamizhaa-nee-purinthukondaal-sari.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 168](../../../pages/page-168.md)–[PDF 174](../../../pages/page-174.md)  
**Date:** 9 April 2012

**Udanpirappē,**

In the letter I wrote yesterday, I said that Jayalalithaa had refused to accept the first day of Thai as Tamil New Year—a decision taken by 500 Tamil scholars under Maraimalai Adigal—and had instead extended a helping hand to the “Aryan illusion”; and that there was said to be an oral Government instruction that the approaching first day of Chithirai should be celebrated grandly. But by yesterday evening itself the newspapers carried not an oral order but an official announcement: that the first day of Chithirai is Tamil New Year’s Day, that it should be celebrated with great pomp and splendour, and that the Chief Minister would participate in a Tamil Development Department function on 13 April and present awards.

As I wrote yesterday, ever since the AIADMK won the general election last May and assumed office, it has behaved with the arrogance that this victory is permanent and that defeat will never return, continuously placing full stops against major schemes implemented under DMK rule. The people of Tamil Nadu know this well.

In the Governor’s Address delivered in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly on 23 January 2008, it was announced that more than five hundred distinguished scholars had assembled at Pachaiyappa’s College, Chennai, in 1921 under Maraimalai Adigal—the great scholar and founder of the Pure Tamil Movement. They resolved that Tamils needed a distinct era; that a continuous year count should be followed in the name of Ayyan Thiruvalluvar; that this should be regarded as the “Tamil Year”; and that Thiruvalluvar’s birth year should be taken as 31 BCE. The Address said that Chief Minister Kalaignar had accepted this view thirty-seven years earlier and ordered it implemented in the Tamil Nadu Government diary from 1971 and the Government Gazette from 1972. Since all Tamil scholars collectively agreed that the first day of Thai, when the Thiruvalluvar Year begins, is the beginning of Tamil New Year, the Government had decided to declare and implement the first day of Thai as Tamil New Year’s Day. Thus the people of Tamil Nadu, who already celebrated Pongal as Tamilar Thirunaal, would thereafter celebrate the same day with double joy as Tamil New Year—planting banana, mango and jackfruit trees, drawing colourful kolams, illuminating homes with rows of lamps, wearing new clothes, singing and dancing in praise of Tamil dignity and self-respect, spreading equality, and showering affection in the joy of Tamil New Year. The announcement was received with loud applause.

Was that one announcement—made under DMK rule in a manner that filled all pure Tamils with joy and pride—not enough to provoke Jayalalithaa? She immediately began counteraction.

At the meeting led by Maraimalai Adigal, distinguished figures including Tamil Thendral Thiru. V. Kalyanasundaranar, Tamil guardian Ka. Subramania Pillai, Saiva elder Sachidananda Pillai, Navalar N. M. Venkatasamy Nattar, Navalar Somasundara Bharathiar and Muthamizh guardian Ki. Aa. Pe. Viswanatham participated. They took three decisions: to follow a continuous year count in Thiruvalluvar’s name; to celebrate that as the Tamil Year; and to obtain the Thiruvalluvar Year by adding 31 BCE to the commonly used calendar year.

Then in 1939, the All-India Tamils Conference met in Tiruchi under Navalar Somasundara Bharathiar. Thanthai Periyar, Karanthai Tamil Sangam President Umamaheswaranar, Professor Ka. Subramaniam, Te. Po. Meenakshisundaranar, Thiru. Vi. Ka., Maraimalai Adigal, P. T. Rajan, Arcot Ramasamy Mudaliar, Revolutionary Poet Bharathidasan, Pattukkottai Azhagiri and many others took part. That conference too declared the first day of Thai as Tamil New Year and Pongal as Tamilar Thirunaal.

Dravidar Kazhagam President, Tamil leader and younger brother Veeramani said that their long-standing demand that the first day of Thai be the beginning of Tamil New Year had succeeded; that this was a new chapter of renaissance in the history of Tamil culture; and that they welcomed and rejoiced in it.

The day after the Governor’s announcement, Thavathiru Kundrakudi Ponnambala Adigalar said that from ancient times the first day of Thai had been a festival bringing awakening and joy to Tamil life through religion, natural life, land and people, and agricultural life; and thanked and praised Muthamizh scholar Chief Minister Kalaignar for implementing as Tamil New Year what great Tamil scholars such as Maraimalai Adigal had declared, beginning on the first day of Thai and bearing Ayyan Thiruvalluvar’s name.

Kaviperarasu Vairamuthu wrote that feeling-filled Tamils danced with joy when the Kalaignar Government announced in the Governor’s Address that the first day of Thai would mark Tamil New Year. Every national people has indestructible identities, he said: Tamils had a territorial identity and an ethnic identity, but their identity in time had remained confused. That darkness of confusion had now broken and light had come. The Dravidian movement had made Ayyan Thiruvalluvar a measure in the world of ideas, and the Kalaignar Government had now made him a measure of time as well. This, he said, was history correcting history.

Tamils in Malaysia now celebrate the first day of Thai as New Year. Dr. Mu. Varadarasanar explained that in ancient times New Year did not begin on the first day of Chithirai; elders celebrated the first day of Thai as the beginning of the year, bought and wore new clothes, renewed streets with fresh earth and red soil, held festivals throughout towns, and in this way celebrated Pongal as New Year.

But in the Assembly on 23 August 2011, Chief Minister Jayalalithaa said there was no evidence for beginning Tamil New Year in Thai, while much evidence existed for beginning it in Chithirai, and claimed that the law had been altered after Karunanidhi had changed it according to his personal wish and hurt popular sentiments.

Now a great danger has arisen, like mixing poison into and ruining a good effort implemented under DMK rule. In the history of Tamil Nadu there has always been a deeply reactionary group that is hostile even to Tamil, that hates, rejects and ignores even the word “classical Tamil.” In earlier times too there were one or two poets who hated Tamil in this way. I once explained this through a story.

In Nakkiran’s time, a man called Kuyakkondan is said to have declared in a debate at the Tamil Sangam, “Aryan is good; Tamil is bad.” Nakkiran became angry and sang a curse: “You who said Tamil is bad and the northern tongue good, may you die.” Kuyakkondan immediately fell dead. Some present then pleaded with Nakkiran to restore him. Nakkiran sang that because Kuyakkondan had declared Aryan good and Tamil bad he had fallen into Yama’s grasp, but by the command of Agastya of the noble Potiyil hills, let classical Tamil restore him—`swaha!`—and Kuyakkondan is said to have risen alive.

At the very time the Tamil Nadu Government’s announcement came, distinguished Tamil epigraphist and scholar Iravatham Mahadevan said: “If we do not have the courage to revise today’s almanacs by scientifically examining astronomy and the present cycle of seasons, what is wrong in celebrating Pongal, the harvest day, as New Year?”

Following the announcement in the Governor’s Address under DMK rule, I introduced the Tamil Nadu Tamil New Year Bill, 2008, in the Assembly on 29 January 2008. On 1 February, during debate on the Bill, E. S. S. Raman for the Congress, Ki. Arumugam for the PMK, N. Nanmaran for the CPI(M), Vai. Sivapunniyam for the CPI, Mu. Kannappan—then there—for the MDMK, and Ku. Selvam for the Viduthalai Chiruthaigal welcomed the Bill, and it was passed.

Thus a decision so widely welcomed and praised was made law under DMK rule and had been in force since 2008. But in Jayalalithaa’s rule, where the thought always seems to be whether anything implemented under DMK rule should be allowed to remain alive, it was changed. The BJP rushed immediately to welcome her announcement. From that alone, one can understand the underlying intention of the decision.

When reporters told me that both Communist parties had opposed Jayalalithaa’s Bill in the Assembly and asked for my view, I replied that I bowed to their Tamil feeling. Later, PMK founder Dr. Ramadoss said in a statement that the AIADMK Government’s decision would hurt those with Tamil consciousness, that suddenly shifting Tamil New Year back to Chithirai was improper, and that it would become a great historical error. Tamil leader Ki. Veeramani also strongly condemned the decision. I thanked them then for that Tamil feeling.

And Revolutionary Poet Bharathidasan himself sang:

> “Tamil, sleeping in slumber!  
> Chithirai is not your Tamil New Year;  
> it is what the Aryan crowd that came here to live off us showed you—  
> sixty years that do not accord with reason.  
> For the Tamil who ruled this earth,  
> the first day of Thai alone is Tamil New Year!”

I wish to recall that too. But when I see all the harms now coming upon Tamil and Tamil Nadu because the people of Tamil Nadu made a mistake without pausing even for a moment to think, my heart swells with pain.

What does Maraimalai Adigal matter! What does Bharathidasan matter! What do hundreds of scholars matter! Why should any of them be respected! Aryanism will only trample underfoot these people, their principles and their Tamil feeling; it will never respect them. The present position taken by the Tamil Nadu Government is itself an example. Wise Tamil—if you understand this, that is enough!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**9 April 2012**

3. The Nakkiran–Kuyakkondan episode remains explicitly presented as the story Kalaignar recounts; it is not independently historicised.
