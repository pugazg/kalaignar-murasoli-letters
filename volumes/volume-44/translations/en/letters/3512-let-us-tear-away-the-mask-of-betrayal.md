# 3512. Let Us Tear Away the Mask of Betrayal!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation follows the audited canonical Tamil and the locked Volume 44 conventions. The Mullai Periyar technical, judicial and inter-State material is translated strictly as the letter presents and attributes it; no engineering or legal claim is independently reconciled here. English passages printed in the source are retained verbatim. The complete audited Tamil is reproduced below and remains authoritative.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3512](../../../chapters/3512-thurogaththin-mugaththiraiyai-kizhippom.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 207](../../../pages/page-207.md)–[PDF 213](../../../pages/page-213.md)  
**Date:** 14 November 2010

**Udanpirappē,**

At every stage of the Mullai Periyar issue, appropriate explanations have been given on behalf of the Tamil Nadu government. Yet, after reading our explanations — whether understanding them, failing to understand them, or deliberately — the Kerala side has made it a practice to issue startling replies intended to divert the issue. Some newspapers in Tamil Nadu publish those replies with an ulterior motive, exaggerating and distorting them. Readers of those newspapers are therefore at times left confused about the Mullai Periyar issue.

Because of recent tremors in Idukki district, the English daily *Indian Express* carried a report on 7-11-2010 under the heading, “Mullai Periyar - Fresh tremors in Idukki raises concerns about dam’s safety”. Likewise, *Dinamani* on 10-11-2010 published a report under the heading, “Tamil Nadu Maintenance Work at Mullai Periyar Intended to Divert the Supreme Court Committee — Kerala Expert Committee Report.” The Tamil Nadu government has continuously carried out maintenance work on the Mullai Periyar Dam in accordance with Supreme Court orders. It is precisely these maintenance works that the Kerala side twists and turns into stories convenient to itself. As soon as Kerala’s objection to the maintenance work came to my notice, our government replied through the Public Works Department. I give this explanation again today so that not only the Kerala government but the people of Tamil Nadu too may know the true position.

From 1998 to 2001, Kerala periodically raised objections about the safety of the Mullai Periyar Dam, and cases were filed in the Supreme Court on behalf of both Tamil Nadu and Kerala. In April 2000, while hearing those cases, the Supreme Court asked the Union Minister for Water Resources to convene a meeting of the Chief Ministers of the two States and seek an amicable solution. When no amicable settlement emerged, the Union Ministry of Water Resources constituted an expert committee on 14-6-2000. The committee examined every aspect of the safety of the Mullai Periyar main dam and the baby dams, including calculations for earthquakes in the dam region, and only then recommended that the reservoir level could be raised to 142 feet. The source reproduces its conclusion as follows:

> “After going through the results of field and laboratory investigations for Baby dam and stress analysis of Baby dam as well as the main dam, all the members of the Committee are of firm opinion that the Mullai Periyar Baby dam and main dam are safe for raising the reservoir water level to 142 ft. (43.28 m)”

Meanwhile, through the Indian Institute of Science (IISc), Bengaluru, the Kerala government submitted a report in 2001 stating that the dam could not withstand earthquakes. That report used the earthquakes at Koyna and Uttarkashi in its calculations. The Central Water Commission rejected those calculations as inapplicable to the Mullai Periyar Dam and filed an affidavit to that effect in the Supreme Court in 2004.

Earlier, when Kerala said similar tremors had occurred, the Central Water Commission sent an expert on 7-2-2001 to inspect the dam; he reported that the dam had suffered no damage. Again, when Kerala said tremors had occurred in the dam area in 2004, Central Water Commission Chief Engineer Dr G. Antony Balan inspected the dam on 9-7-2004 and reported that there was no damage. Taking all these matters into account, the Supreme Court on 27-2-2006 ordered that the water level could be raised to 142 feet. The source prints:

> “There is no report to suggest that the safety of the dam would be jeopardized, if the water level is raised for the present to 142 ft.”

After that judgment, Kerala again stated in an affidavit before the Supreme Court on 30-3-2007 that the dam would not be safe if an earthquake occurred.

Tamil Nadu therefore formed a special expert committee in 2007 to study the safety of the Mullai Periyar Dam, with retired National Water Development Agency Director-General R. K. Sharma as chairman and four experienced engineers as members: retired Uttar Pradesh Principal Chief Engineer P. L. Jatna, retired Orissa Principal Chief Engineer G. C. Sahu, retired chief engineer C. K. Agarwal and retired Andhra Pradesh chief engineer K. V. Subbarao. The committee conducted all necessary studies on the main dam and baby dams according to the methods prescribed by Indian Standards (I.S.I.), took possible earthquakes in the region into account and reported that the dam was sound. Tamil Nadu submitted that report as a document before the Supreme Court.

After Kerala’s repeated earthquake objections had thus been rejected, in 2008 it asked IIT Roorkee to study the safety of the Mullai Periyar Dam in the event of an earthquake and filed that report before the Supreme Court. The report claimed that earthquake intensity in the Mullai Periyar area would be much greater than that specified by Indian Standards. But Dr T. K. Paul and Dr M. K. Sharma, who prepared it, did not appear for examination to substantiate their conclusions. Kerala representative M. K. Parameswaran Nair, who filed their report as evidence, did not provide proper particulars and information during cross-examination about the two reports. The letter therefore calls that report an unproved document.

After cross-examination of witnesses ended and final arguments were under way in 2009, Dr T. K. Paul prepared a second report and gave it to the Kerala government. When Kerala asked that it be received as a document, the Supreme Court rejected the request on 4-11-2009. That report, which the Supreme Court did not accept, stated that the dam would not be safe during an earthquake even when the water level was at 136 feet. Kerala has now submitted the same report to the Empowered Committee appointed by the Supreme Court. Tamil Nadu has asked that it not be accepted. At the committee meeting held on 15-10-2010, it was stated that no decision on this had yet been taken. The letter therefore reiterates that Dr Paul’s report remains unproved and says that, because the case is pending before the Supreme Court, many defects in it cannot be set out here.

The 2001 expert committee had tested core samples from the strengthened Mullai Periyar main dam and baby dams and, after taking their strength into account under Indian Standards, reported that impounding water and earthquakes would not cause damage. Yet unsupported reports about the strength of the construction materials have appeared in the press.

Further, according to the earthquake-zone map prescribed by Indian Standards in 2003, the Periyar Dam lies in Zone-III. On that basis, earthquakes that could occur at the highest level in the area — up to 6 on the Richter scale — were taken into account. The expert committee stated that even with the water level at 142 feet an earthquake would cause no damage to the dam, and the letter says the Supreme Court accepted that conclusion.

Kerala officials have, the letter says, obstructed even routine maintenance work. On one occasion, they objected even when five water-level gauge boards were being repaired and removed them; the boards were later restored. Tamil Nadu placed these matters before the Supreme Court in November 2009. In its order of 10-11-2009, the Supreme Court said there was no bar on repair and minor works. Such work has continued: removing bushes from the earthen dam and spillways, painting gates and dam areas, and repairing damaged portions are routine maintenance works of the kind carried out at every dam. Reports suggesting that such work has only now begun, or that it is being done secretly, are therefore described as incorrect.

The reasons put forward in the press for decommissioning the dam are also rejected by the letter. Even an old dam, it argues, can regain a life like that of a new dam if it is properly examined, consolidated or strengthened — “Retrofitted”. Because the catchment of the Mullai Periyar Dam is densely forested, siltation in the reservoir area is said to be extremely low. The unusable level, “Dead Storage”, is stated to be 104 feet, and the letter argues that siltation therefore cannot be cited as a reason to decommission the reservoir.

The letter further says that an expert committee constituted in 2003 by the Union Ministry of Environment and Forests stated that even if the Periyar Dam water level were raised to its full-capacity height of 152 feet, there would be no environmental damage and that wildlife would benefit. It says the Supreme Court referred to this in its judgment of 27-2-2006 and reproduces the following source English:

> “If the lowest water level even after increasing the water capacity of the dam is maintained at the current level, then the increased high water table will make more area available as Vayals, effectively adding some more area to the existing vayals, thereby increasing the carrying capacity of the reserve for ungulates, elephants and in turn of tigers”

While the case is pending before the Supreme Court, the letter argues, it is neither just nor proper for the Kerala government to convey through the press views contrary to positions already accepted by the Court, or for only some Tamil Nadu newspapers to magnify Kerala’s claims. The time is not far away, it concludes, when the people of Tamil Nadu will remove the mask from those newspapers that print material described as harmful to Tamil Nadu’s public interest and hostile to Tamil Nadu, sell it within Tamil Nadu and conduct their business there.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
14 November 2010

உயரமான 152 அடிக்கு நீரை உயர்த்தினாலும் சுற்றுச்சூழலுக்கு
எந்தவித பாதிப்பும் வராது என்றும், அது வன விலங்குகளுக்கு
உதவியாக இருக்கும் என்றும் தெரிவித்துள்ளது. இதனை
உச்சநீதிமன்றமும் அதன் 27.2.2006 அன்று அளித்த தீர்ப்பில்
குறிப்பிட்டு ஆணையிட்டுள்ளது. (“If the lowest water level even
after increasing the water capacity of the dam is maintained at the current
level, then the increased high water table will make more area available
as Vayals, effectively adding some more area to the existing vayals,
thereby increasing the carrying capacity of the reserve for ungulates,
elephants and in turn of tigers”)

இந்த வழக்கு உச்ச நீதிமன்றத்தில் நிலுவையில் இருக்கும்
பொழுது, உச்சநீதிமன்றம் ஏற்கனவே ஏற்றுக்கொண்ட
கருத்துகளுக்கு மாறுபட்ட கருத்துகளைப் பத்திரிகை வாயிலாக
கேரள அரசின் சார்பில் தெரிவிப்பதும்; தமிழகத்திலே உள்ள சில
ஏடுகள் மட்டும், கேரள அரசின் கருத்துகளைப் பெரிதுபடுத்தி
வெளியிடுவதும்; நீதிக்கும், நியாயத்திற்கும் நல்லதல்ல. தமிழ
கத்தின் பொதுநலனை பாதிக்கும் - தமிழகத்திற்கு எதிரான
செய்திகளை, பத்திரிகையில் அச்சியற்றி; தமிழகத்திலேயே
அவற்றை விற்பனை செய்து, தொழில் நடத்தும் ஒருசில
பத்திரிகைகளின் முகத்திரையை அகற்றி; தமிழக மக்கள், அவற்றை
முழுமையாகப் புரிந்து கொள்ளும் காலம் வெகுதொலைவில்
இல்லை!
