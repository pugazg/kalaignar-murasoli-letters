# 3732. The Beneficiaries Are Going to Know the Truth!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation carries Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English while preserving argument order, political directness, irony, rhetorical questions, figures, names and source attributions. The complete audited Tamil remains authoritative. `Udanpirappē` is retained as his distinctive political-familial address.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3732](../../../chapters/3732-beneficiaries-will-know-the-truth.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 200](../../../pages/page-200.md)–[PDF 206](../../../pages/page-206.md)  
**Date:** 12 April 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

On 21 March 2013, the Finance Minister presented the 2013–2014 Budget in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly. The debate on the demand for grants for Municipal Administration and Water Supply took place on 1 April 2013, and the minister concerned had already replied in the Assembly. Yet yesterday (10 April 2013), Chief Minister Jayalalithaa read a long statement under Rule 110 containing announcements concerning water supply that ought to have been made during the grant debate.

She announced, along with several other measures under Rule 110, combined water-supply schemes costing ₹450 crore in Dindigul district, ₹125 crore in Thanjavur, ₹114.25 crore in Coimbatore, ₹76.44 crore in Tiruppur, ₹797.69 crore in Tiruchirappalli and ₹227.26 crore in Tirunelveli; the conversion of Thanjavur and Dindigul municipalities into municipal corporations; and the upgrading of Karaikudi and Sivakasi as special-grade municipalities. If schemes that should be announced by the minister responsible are not allowed to be announced by that minister during the grant debate, and a few days later the Chief Minister herself reads them under Rule 110, why have a separate minister for that department at all?

Let the Chief Minister read half the schemes if she wishes; should not at least the remaining half be left for the departmental ministers to read, so that they too may have the satisfaction of performing at least half their duty!

Is the reason simply that the newspapers must carry the words “Chief Minister's announcement”? Is the intention that neither the Finance Minister nor the departmental ministers should announce them? When M.G.R. was alive, this same Chief Minister Jayalalithaa wrote in her own hand to then Prime Minister Rajiv Gandhi that, apart from M.G.R., everyone here was a “zero”. Are all the other ministers still “zeros” now?

If the Chief Minister herself reads under Rule 110 not merely the important announcements of this one department but those of every minister, why then have debates on departmental grants and reply speeches at all? Why not decide that every year, instead of presenting the Budget, holding a general debate, debating demands for grants and having ministers reply, the Chief Minister alone will read a Rule 110 statement on one department each day, after which the leaders of the other parties and the Speaker may line up to offer praise?

At the very beginning of her statement, the Chief Minister said that the 3.80-million-litre seawater desalination project at Narippaiyur in Ramanathapuram district had been introduced during her rule, and that the Ramanathapuram Combined Water Supply Scheme had likewise been designed during her earlier tenure and implemented later.

The Ramanathapuram Combined Water Supply Scheme was inaugurated during the DMK government. Not by “video conferencing”; I myself went to Paramakudi and participated in that major function.

After Perarignar Anna's death, while serving as Chief Minister and attending various functions, I went to inaugurate the flower show in the Nilgiris on 21 May 1989. After seeing that flower show, the masses of flowers blooming in the Nilgiris and the rushing waterfalls, a thought occurred to me, and I expressed it at the function itself: “Even as I look at the Nilgiris—at this prosperity and this garden of flowers—and feel happy, I also think with concern about the parched Ramanathapuram district.”

I did not stop with those words. Though Ramanathapuram could not simply be transformed into a prosperous district like the Nilgiris, I worked in office with the determination to change it to some extent and solve its basic drinking-water problem. As a result, when the DMK government took office in 1996, we implemented eleven schemes to convert saline water into fresh water in Ramanathapuram district at a cost of ₹72.20 crore. Through the eleven desalination units established under those schemes, 237 village habitations received drinking water.

After the DMK returned to office in May 2006, the Budget presented in the Assembly on 22 July 2006 announced that work would begin that year on a major ₹671-crore project to bring Cauvery water as a permanent solution to the long-standing drinking-water problem in the villages and towns of Ramanathapuram district. The Ramanathapuram Combined Water Supply Scheme was formulated to put that announcement into immediate effect.

I myself laid the foundation stone and launched the work at a function in Paramakudi on 30 January 2007. In the first phase, drinking water began to be supplied from 27 June 2009 to people in the municipalities of Ramanathapuram, Rameswaram, Paramakudi and Keelakarai and the town panchayats of Mandapam, Sayalgudi, Mudukulathur, Kamuthi and Abiramam. In the second phase, water was subsequently supplied to 32 habitations in the Viralimalai, Alavayal and Ponnamaravathi areas of Pudukkottai district; to Sivaganga municipality and 799 habitations in areas including Tiruppattur, Kalaiyarkoil and Ilayangudi in Sivaganga district; and to various rural areas of Ramanathapuram district. The credit for repeatedly inspecting the work, advising and encouraging officials and completing this great project within two years belongs to the departmental minister and Deputy Chief Minister, Thambi M.K. Stalin.

It was during DMK rule, in 1971, that the Water Supply and Drainage Board was established so that people could receive drinking water without interruption. In the final year of the AIADMK government, 1995–96, ₹213.82 crore was spent on drinking-water works. In the final year of the DMK government, 2000–01, ₹842 crore was allocated for drinking-water works. These figures themselves show the extent of the DMK government's concern and action on drinking-water problems.

The Narippaiyur seawater desalination scheme—described as the largest such project in Asia—was completed in Ramanathapuram district at a cost of ₹51 crore and inaugurated on 27 June 1999. It continues to benefit the district's people. Though announced by the AIADMK government in 1995, no funds were allocated then; funding was provided and the project completed under the DMK government.

Yet Jayalalithaa has told the Assembly that the Narippaiyur scheme too was brought in during her rule. In 1998, under the DMK government, the seawater desalination project at Narippaiyur was launched as the first of its kind in Asia. The ₹46-crore project supplied drinking water to 296 villages in Kadaladi union. BHEL secured the tender in 1998 and operated it under its maintenance for five years; afterward it was maintained by the Doshion company. When inaugurated, it produced 38 lakh litres of drinking water a day for those 296 villages.

To address Chennai's drinking-water problem, after the DMK government took office in 1996 it maintained cooperative relations with the Andhra Pradesh government, completed the long-stalled Krishna water project and enabled drinking water to reach Chennai from Kandaleru reservoir through the Krishna canal.

Likewise, work on the ₹1,929-crore Hogenakkal Combined Water Supply Scheme, with Japanese international cooperative financial assistance, was carried forward at full pace under the DMK government to eliminate the drinking-water problem in Krishnagiri and Dharmapuri districts.

The Minjur seawater desalination project had been initiated under the AIADMK government, but because of litigation it was delayed and no further constructive action was taken to implement it. After the DMK government came to office in 2006, negotiations were held and important decisions taken to accelerate the project. The previously fixed price of the water to be produced at the plant was also reduced to some extent. On 25 February 2007, Thambi M.K. Stalin, then Minister for Local Administration, laid the foundation stone and work began. After successful trial operation, the project was inaugurated on 31 July 2010.

Following Minjur, the Nemmeli seawater desalination project was recently inaugurated by today's Chief Minister Jayalalithaa; but it was the DMK government that undertook all the efforts for that project, sought and obtained funds from the Union government, and completed a large part of the work.

Yet in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly, Jayalalithaa speaks as though the DMK government introduced no drinking-water projects at all and as though she herself brought in Narippaiyur, the Ramanathapuram Combined Water Supply Scheme, Minjur, Hogenakkal and Nemmeli. That is why I am placing all these details before the people.

At this rate, even if she announces under Rule 110 that it was not Karikalan who built the Kallanai but she herself, there would be no reason for surprise! This is not merely Rule 110; it is the fate imposed on the people of Tamil Nadu!

However much Ammaiyar may try to gather every bit of credit for herself, can Jayalalithaa deceive through these artificial announcements the people in those regions who experience the benefits of these schemes every day? Will the beneficiaries somehow fail to know the truth?

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**12 April 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. **Rule 110** is retained as the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly procedural reference used repeatedly in the source.
2. `lakh` and `crore` are retained as Indian public-language numbering units.
3. The source gives both ₹51 crore and ₹46 crore while discussing the Narippaiyur project in successive passages. The translation preserves both figures rather than reconciling them.
4. **Kallanai** refers to the historic Grand Anicut associated in Tamil tradition with Karikalan; the source uses it as a sarcastic climax to the argument about claiming credit.
