# 3588. Education—Must It Be a Bud Left to Wither?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 14 September 2011

**Udanpirappē,**

During these four months since the AIADMK Government assumed office, the work of ruining the School Education Department has continued. No sooner had it come to power than it unilaterally decided that it would not accept the Samacheer Kalvi Act. Although the textbooks had already been prepared at a cost of many crores of rupees, it ordered that they should not be distributed. Using its majority, it passed an amending law against Samacheer Kalvi, announced that new textbooks would be printed, hurriedly called tenders, and conducted itself in such a way that students wasted three months without studying anything. In this manner it damaged the students' education. Of a total of 200 working days for students, 60 working days were wasted.

Because the Supreme Court ruled that Samacheer Kalvi had to be implemented this very year for Standards 1 through 10, this Government, angered and left with no alternative, was forced to accept it unwillingly. Yet it continues to act with a complete lack of concern for students' education. The Chief Minister herself speaks in the Assembly as though the Supreme Court had somehow committed an error. The judges had said that if there were mistakes in the Samacheer Kalvi textbooks, they could be removed. Instead of correcting genuine errors, the Government, out of resentment, tore out, struck out and covered up everything it disliked. Without correcting the actual errors, it advertised: “Look, here is the mistake.”

There have been countless confusions in printing and distributing textbooks. Parents complain that even now Samacheer Kalvi textbooks have not been distributed to many schools, particularly matriculation schools.

In an article written by V.N. Raghavan in the 10-9-2011 issue of *Dinamani*, he states:

> “Although the Samacheer Kalvi syllabus has been implemented, students have been psychologically affected because the model question paper has still not been released. The books have still not reached all students. In Tiruchi district, the problem of textbook shortage differs from school to school. In one school, for example, the Tamil book is unavailable. In another school, the Tamil book has been distributed, but the English book has not been received. Similarly, students in some other schools have not been given textbooks for mathematics, science, social science and other subjects. Ninth-standard students have not received the Tamil textbook in full. The same situation prevails in other districts too. Because books were distributed only after nearly two and a half months of the current academic year had passed, there is difficulty in completing the lessons. Therefore, excluding holidays, lessons that should be taught over 180 days must now be completed in only 90 days.”

Even today's (13-9-2011) English daily *The Times of India* has carried a detailed report saying that students still have not received textbooks for their second-language subject. Reports say that Samacheer Kalvi textbooks have not been supplied in adequate numbers to many schools, that some schools have received no textbooks at all, and that among the thousand schools in Tamil Nadu serving minority students, not one school has been given textbooks for the language subject.

There is said to be confusion over how the question papers for the quarterly examination are to be prepared. Confusion also continues over whether the tenth-standard public examination will be held on the usual date or postponed. Only if that date is known can teaching for the students be planned.

They are only now beginning to conduct the lessons. Teachers have not been given the in-service training required to teach the upgraded Samacheer Kalvi syllabus. As a result, teaching is currently taking place only in name. Yet, even before all this is settled, the Government announces that the quarterly examination will begin on September 22.

The AIADMK Government announced, “We are going to implement all the recommendations made by the Professor Muthukumaran Committee.” But it has so far paid no attention to one of the foremost and most important recommendations of that committee: that one teacher should be appointed for every thirty students. To implement that recommendation, Tamil Nadu would need to appoint 60 thousand new teachers this very year. Although the Government said in one announcement that it would appoint 33 thousand teachers, we can be certain only when that is actually put into practice. Moreover, contrary to the Muthukumaran Committee's recommendation, the Government calculates one teacher for every 35 students and says that only 16,549 additional teachers are needed.

Instead of appointing fully qualified teachers as full-time teachers, it has been announced that they will be appointed as half-day, part-time teachers—and that too at the very low consolidated pay of ₹5,000.

When the AIADMK Government was in office from 2001 to 2006, it brought in a law banning recruitment, and graduates were all affected. At that time, approximately 46 thousand teachers were appointed on consolidated monthly pay of ₹3,000–₹4,000, after obtaining written undertakings from them that they would not even ask for transfers.

But when the DMK Government assumed office in 2006, it granted all those 46 thousand teachers regular time-scale pay with effect from 1-6-2006, thereby enabling each teacher to receive roughly three times the salary they had previously earned.

In addition, after 2006 under the DMK Government, the Elementary Education Department appointed 12,426 secondary-grade teachers and 14,115 graduate teachers, a total of 26,541 teachers. The School Education Department appointed 70 secondary-grade teachers, 17,045 graduate teachers, 4,665 teacher trainers, 3,002 postgraduate teachers, 525 Tamil teachers, 1,131 special teachers, 140 vocational teachers and 1,666 computer teachers, a total of 27,739 teachers.

In the District Institutes of Education and Training, 216 lecturers and 32 senior lecturers were appointed. Altogether, 55,053 new teachers were appointed. Among the teachers appointed from 2006 to 2010, 1,114 persons with disabilities who possessed teacher-education qualifications were appointed as teachers in Government schools on a priority basis.

As for recruitment, when Jayalalithaa was Chief Minister from 1991 to 1996, selection was conducted through the Teachers Recruitment Board and appointments were made according to rank. Secondary-grade teachers were selected in the same way. As a result, graduates who had registered and were waiting were largely affected. Unable to get jobs, they grew older and began looking for other work.

After the DMK returned to office in 1996, appointments were made on the basis of registration seniority.

When Jayalalithaa returned to office in 2001, she introduced the recruitment-ban law. Once again, graduates were affected.

When I became Chief Minister in 2006, 55 thousand teachers received appointments over the next five years on the basis of priority according to registration seniority.

Jayalalithaa has now announced in the Assembly that 14,377 teachers will be appointed. Later, under Rule 110, it was said that 13,300 graduate teachers and 16,549 part-time teachers would be appointed. But neither the Chief Minister nor the School Education Minister has yet announced whether these teacher appointments will be made on the basis of seniority or through examination and interview. Rather than spend time on that, AIADMK ministers are said to be interested only in discovering which former ministers did what, what allegations can be made against them in the Assembly, and how Jayalalithaa can thereby be plunged into delight!

There has also been great confusion this year over the annual transfer of teachers. Under the DMK Government, transfers were carried out through “counselling” in a transparent manner, without concealment, and in a way that left teachers satisfied. But now, after giving transfers first to those they wanted, the Government announces that it is going to conduct “counselling.” If transfers through counselling are offered three months after schools have opened, will anyone show interest? After three or four months, can a teacher who receives a transfer make arrangements to move with the family to a new place?

To put it altogether: whatever else may or may not be happening after the AIADMK Government came to power, the work of ruining the education of Tamil Nadu's student treasures is being carried out with precision. The rulers act with the attitude: what does it matter who studies, and what does it matter if someone does not study and goes to ruin?

The Jayalalithaa Government has not the slightest faith in the ideal, “All are one community; all are of equal worth; all are rulers of this land!”, nor in the principles of equality and equal treatment. It is implementing Samacheer Kalvi in a perfunctory manner. Distribution of the Samacheer Kalvi textbooks is still not properly complete. Large numbers of teaching posts remain vacant. The policy for teacher recruitment has still not been decided and announced. Confusion continues over the method to be followed in teacher transfers.

Because of such confusions and blunders created by half-hearted rulers, the school education of today's young buds, who are to determine the country's future, continues to wither through the neglect of those who ought to care!

**With affection, M.K.**  
14-9-2011
