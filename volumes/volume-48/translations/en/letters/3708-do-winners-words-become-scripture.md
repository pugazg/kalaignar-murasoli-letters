# 3708. Does Everything the Winner Says Become “Scripture”?

> **Translator’s note**
>

It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

I have issued a statement thanking the Supreme Court and the Union Government for the notification of the Cauvery Water Disputes Tribunal's final award in the Central Gazette. I have also already explained the continuous and unceasing efforts made from the very beginning by the DMK and the DMK government on the Cauvery issue. But Chief Minister Jayalalithaa has called this the greatest victory of her thirty-year political life and said that a twenty-two-year-old problem has now been resolved. She summoned reporters and made these claims; some newspapers have gone out of their way to praise her in many ways and even draw cartoons celebrating her. As one who has been deeply involved in the Cauvery issue, I consider it my duty to offer a few explanations.

Even *Dinamani*, which wrote an editorial praising Chief Minister Jayalalithaa, also wrote:

> “This action alone cannot immediately secure Cauvery water. Only if the Union Government and the Supreme Court resolve the legal issues and practical difficulties surrounding the matter can the States concerned, including Tamil Nadu, take the next steps towards asserting their right to a share of Cauvery river water.”

That too must be kept in mind. The same *Dinamani* raised several further doubts. Namely:

1. The final award was delivered in 2007. Karnataka challenged that award, while the governments of Tamil Nadu, Puducherry and Kerala approached the Cauvery Tribunal seeking clarifications. Cases relating to it were also filed in the Supreme Court in 2007. Why did the Supreme Court not take up those cases at the outset and direct the Union Government to publish the Tribunal's award in the Gazette?
2. All four State governments approached the Tribunal in 2007 seeking certain clarifications. After the Tribunal's Chairman resigned on 11 April 2012, a new Chairman was not appointed. What answer is there to the clarifications sought from the Tribunal by the four State governments?
3. The Supreme Court had set a final deadline directing the Union Government to act on the Tribunal's final award by 20 February. Will the Supreme Court itself continue hearing these cases, or will the Cauvery Tribunal hear them?
4. A Cauvery River Water Regulation Authority has to be constituted. When will the Union Government constitute it?

These are the questions *Dinamani* raised on 21 February 2013. We must keep them in mind; the questions it has raised cannot simply be brushed aside.

I also have to offer a few explanations in connection with the interview Chief Minister Jayalalithaa gave yesterday. First, she said that she assumed office as Chief Minister for the first time on 24 June 1991, and that the Tribunal's interim award was issued the very next day, 25 June 1991. Does that not itself mean that Jayalalithaa is acknowledging that the Tribunal's interim award came about only because of the unceasing efforts of the DMK government that had been in office before she came to power?

Jayalalithaa has said that publication of the Cauvery Tribunal's final award in the Central Gazette is the greatest victory she has achieved in thirty years. But who truly worked for the creation of the Tribunal itself, for that body to issue an interim award and later a final award, and for implementation of the interim award? Even if the newspapers conceal it, will not at least some neutral observers think about that?

The day after the Tribunal's final award was issued on 5 February 2007, Jayalalithaa said in her statement of 6 February 2007:

> “It is only because of Karunanidhi's continuing acts of betrayal that the Cauvery Tribunal's award has gone against Tamil Nadu; accepting moral responsibility for this, the minority DMK government must resign.”

It is that very “adverse award” which has now been published in the Central Gazette, and Jayalalithaa calls it the greatest victory of her thirty-year political life. With the same tongue that called it an “adverse award” then, she now calls it a “great victory”. Is this a double tongue? A double position? A deceptive disguise worn to fool the people of Tamil Nadu?

On 21 March 1991 Jayalalithaa said:

> “Even if the Tribunal gives an award, Cauvery water will come to Tamil Nadu only if they themselves open the dam in Karnataka. The Tribunal's award can be effective only if Karnataka agrees and adopts a fair and favourable decision.”

Even after the Tribunal issued its interim award, she was the one who said:

> “Even if the Tribunal gives an award, Karnataka Chief Minister Bangarappa is under no compulsion to implement it.”

And now she says a great victory has come to her. Is this not because of her familiar assumption that people are famous for forgetting?

In her interview on the 20th, Chief Minister Jayalalithaa said:

> “On my instructions the Tamil Nadu government repeatedly approached the Supreme Court and filed various cases, and it was as a result of that that the Cauvery Authority and the Cauvery Monitoring Committee were constituted. My efforts and the efforts of my government alone were entirely responsible for this.”

One does not know whether to laugh or cry on reading this. Would people who have continuously followed developments in the Cauvery issue not be shocked? Is what Jayalalithaa says true?

It was on 7 August 1998, during the DMK government, that nine hours of negotiations took place in Delhi in the presence of Prime Minister Vajpayee, with Union ministers and the Chief Ministers of the four States participating. Only after those talks was the Cauvery agreement reached. During those negotiations, the Karnataka government announced for the first time that it accepted the Tribunal's interim order. The decisions taken at that meeting were to constitute the Cauvery River Authority and the Cauvery Monitoring Committee. Accordingly, the Cauvery River Authority and the Monitoring Committee were constituted on 11 August 1998. Yet in yesterday's interview Chief Minister Jayalalithaa, without the slightest hesitation, stated contrary to the truth that these bodies were created because she had filed various cases in the Supreme Court. And the Tamil Nadu newspapers duly published that claim.

That is not all. After the Cauvery Authority had been constituted, Jayalalithaa said in the Legislative Assembly on 2 April 2002, during the AIADMK government:

> “The Cauvery Authority is a non-functioning authority. We refused to accept that authority and walked out of the BJP alliance. It was the DMK government that betrayed the Cauvery issue by accepting what the Union Government said.”

Jayalalithaa, who described the Cauvery Authority as a “toothless authority” and a “non-functioning authority”, herself attended the Cauvery Authority meeting held in Delhi on 27 August 2002. But instead of remaining until the meeting concluded, she announced a walkout midway and left.

Again, a Cauvery Authority meeting under the Prime Minister's chairmanship had been convened for 29 November 2002. Jayalalithaa, then Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu, said until the last minute that she would attend; then she cancelled the trip itself.

She declared that she had no confidence in the Prime Minister, who was Chairman of the Cauvery Authority. The Supreme Court strongly criticised Jayalalithaa for making such a statement and said that Chief Minister Jayalalithaa should express regret; she did so. Yet the same Jayalalithaa has now told reporters, without any apparent discomfort at loudly stating something contrary to the truth, that she was the one responsible for the Authority being created.

In her interview on the 20th, she said that the Union Government had not wanted to convene a Cauvery Authority meeting for the past nine years. Even if it had not been convened during those nine years, the Monitoring Committee met nine times between 7 October 1998 and 6 September 2001. In a statement issued by the AIADMK government on 21 June 2002, it was said that the Cauvery River Authority had met only three times—on 28 October 1998, 14 July 2000 and 10 October 2001—and that the Cauvery Monitoring Committee had met twelve times.

At a Cabinet meeting held under the AIADMK government on 21 June 2002, they decided and announced that they would not participate in meetings of the Cauvery River Authority. Yet Jayalalithaa now complains that the Cauvery Authority was not convened. In an English statement issued on 21 September 1998, she disparaged the Authority headed by the Prime Minister in these exact words:

> “The Cauvery River Authority headed by the Prime Minister is a very big fraud perpetrated on the farmers of the Cauvery Delta in Tamil Nadu. This Authority headed by the Prime Minister has no such authority or power to order or implement anything regarding the release of Cauvery Water. I categorically state that the new Authority headed by the Prime Minister will not benefit Tamil Nadu even by an iota”

In her interview on the 20th, the Chief Minister also said that the Union Government had made no arrangement to implement the Tribunal's interim order; that in 1993 she therefore undertook a fast unto death; and that she ended the fast only after Union Minister Shukla met her and assured her that the Union Government would constitute the necessary committees to implement the Cauvery Tribunal's interim order.

While Jayalalithaa was in office, Union Irrigation Minister Mr. Shukla wrote to the Tamil Nadu Chief Minister on 9 June 1992 seeking consent regarding implementation of the Tribunal's interim order. Only after six months did Jayalalithaa sign the draft reply giving consent—on 14 December 1992. Do you know the date on which that letter was typed and signed by the Chief Minister? 30 March 1993. Is this one piece of evidence not enough to show the extent of Amma's concern for the Cauvery issue? Yet she now says that she alone struggled to implement the Tribunal's interim order. She calls it a victory achieved after a twenty-two-year struggle. If there are people ready to believe it, those in power can say anything they like, can they not?

Jayalalithaa has also spoken about her hunger strike. On 18 July 1993, she announced that she would undertake a hunger strike asking why the committee had not been constituted. She said she would end the fast only if the Prime Minister ordered the Karnataka Chief Minister to release 205 TMC of water. But when Union Minister Shukla, who came to persuade her, said that two committees consisting of officials would be constituted, she declared that itself a victory—victory!—and ended the fast.

After the Tribunal's interim order was issued in 1991, Jayalalithaa was Chief Minister until 1996. Yet not even in one year did she secure the 205 TMC of water stipulated in the interim order. Only after the DMK came to power in 1996 was an agreement reached in 1998, with Karnataka for the first time accepting the Tribunal's interim order and agreeing to supply 205 TMC of water. It was then too that Karnataka agreed to the constitution of the Cauvery River Authority and the Monitoring Committee. The Tribunal's final award in 2007 also came during a DMK government. It can therefore be clearly understood that no major advance on the Cauvery issue occurred under Jayalalithaa's government.

What did Jayalalithaa, then Chief Minister, say while speaking about the Cauvery issue in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly on 7 November 2003?

> “When the Assembly next meets, I myself will bring a resolution seeking the dismissal of the Karnataka government. The Assembly will meet again in January. If, by then, the Karnataka government has not released the water it must release to us, I will bring the resolution at the next Assembly session as I have said.”

This was spoken by a Chief Minister in the Legislative Assembly. What happened to it?

The people of Tamil Nadu, who have heard the Tamil song **“O great poet born in falsehood and raised in falsehood”**, must surely feel blessed that they have received a Chief Minister “born in falsehood, raised in falsehood and giving falsehood itself as an interview”! But no one has prospered by speaking falsehood, nor has anyone been ruined by speaking the truth. One day Tamil Nadu will certainly understand that.

Does everything the winner says become “scripture”?

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**23 February 2013**
