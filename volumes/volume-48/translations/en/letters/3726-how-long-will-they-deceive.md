# 3726. How Long Will They Go On Deceiving?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 1 April 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

In February last year, two fishermen from Kerala were fishing in the open sea off Kerala when armed guards aboard an Italian vessel passing that way shot and killed them without cause. The Italian side later said they had been killed because the guards mistook them for pirates.

Police registered a case and arrested two naval officers who had come as security guards aboard the Italian vessel. The Union Government and the Kerala government stated categorically that action would be taken against them under Indian law.

The Italian government made a series of diplomatic efforts to secure their release from the case, but those efforts did not succeed. The matter became an issue between the Italian government and the Union Government. While the case was still pending, the court permitted the two Italian naval officers to travel to Italy for the Christmas festival and return. They went and came back. Last month they once again sought permission to return to their country to vote in an election, and the Supreme Court granted permission. They went to Italy accordingly—but did not return to India.

Italy announced that it could not send them back to India. Shocked by this, Left-party and Congress Members of Parliament from Kerala separately met Prime Minister Manmohan Singh in Delhi on 12 March 2013 and pressed him to intervene.

Prime Minister Manmohan Singh then issued a strong warning to the Italian government. Congress leader Mrs. Sonia Gandhi too accused Italy of betraying its undertaking. The Supreme Court took stern action and abruptly barred the Italian ambassador, who had given an assurance on behalf of the two men, from leaving the country. After all this, the Italian government yielded and decided to send the two naval personnel back to India.

That is Italy. Now I come to Sri Lanka.

On the 27th of last month, sixteen fishermen from Tharuvaikulam in Thoothukudi were fishing near Katchatheevu when they were taken away by the Sri Lankan Navy. They were released only in the second week of March.

On the 13th of this month, nineteen Rameswaram fishermen who had gone out to fish were taken away by the Sri Lankan forces. They were produced before the Mannar court; the judge ordered that they be kept in custody until 28 March 2013, and they were taken to Anuradhapura prison.

Again on the 14th, the Sri Lankan Navy detained thirty-four fishermen from Rameswaram. The source says the Tamil Nadu fishermen were severely beaten with iron rods and the butts of guns; their GPRS devices were seized; large quantities of the fish they had caught were taken and thrown into the sea; and their nets were cut.

On 18 March 2013, twenty-five fishermen from Puthukudi village in Pudukkottai district went fishing at sea. Three of them were intercepted and severely assaulted by the Sri Lankan Navy. They returned with serious injuries and were taken to the Aranthangi Government Hospital for treatment.

Whenever Tamil Nadu fishermen are detained in this manner, the Chief Minister immediately writes to the Union Government on behalf of the State government, and External Affairs officials at the Centre make representations to Sri Lanka. This has become a continuing story.

In this situation, the nineteen fishermen detained on the 13th and held in Anuradhapura prison expected to return home when their remand ended on the 28th. Instead, on the 28th the Sri Lankan court extended their remand until 11 April. Learning this, the families of those nineteen fishermen were plunged into great anguish. Their fellow fishermen here have already been protesting for days because their brothers are languishing in Sri Lankan prison; even yesterday they conducted a rail-blockade protest. Does any of this reach the ears of the Union and State governments?

Meanwhile, another report. The Thoothukudi Coast Guard, while on patrol, detained five Sri Lankan fishermen who had allegedly entered Indian waters without authorisation, and cases were registered against them. But in the expectation that if the Tamil Nadu government withdrew the cases against these five Sinhala fishermen the Sri Lankan authorities would reciprocate by releasing the Indian fishermen imprisoned there, the five were released and the cases withdrawn. Yet Sri Lanka extended the Indian fishermen's remand. Fishermen's association leaders have condemned this.

The opposition raised in Parliament the issue of the two Kerala fishermen who were shot dead. Replying, Prime Minister Dr. Manmohan Singh said:

> “Italy's action is unacceptable. There can be no two views that Italy's statement that it cannot send the two naval personnel back is unacceptable. Italy's action has caused an uproar in our country. They have violated every norm. The assurance given by its official representative has been called into question. Everyone must recognise that this is a very important matter and act together.”

The Government of India showed urgency at many levels across the country over the two Italian naval personnel accused of killing two Kerala fishermen. What does that same government say when nineteen Tamil fishermen, whose only “crime” was going to sea to fish, are imprisoned in Sri Lanka and their remand is now extended until 11 April?

Is it fair to look differently at Kerala fishermen and Tamil Nadu fishermen? More than six hundred Tamil Nadu fishermen have, the letter says, been killed by the Sri Lankan Navy. Has the approach, concern and speed shown even once in the Kerala-fishermen/Italian-naval-personnel case ever been shown in the Tamil Nadu-fishermen/Sri Lankan-Navy issue? Arrested Indian fishermen have their custody extended and are locked in prison for another fifteen days, while detained Sinhala fishermen are hurriedly released here. Tamil! Is this your fate? Is this the example proving that a Tamil is a person with no one to turn to?

That is what the situation has become. The reason, the letter argues, is the lack of unity among Tamils.

A report appeared in the newspapers today under the headline: **“Why Did the DMK Walk Out When the Tamil Eelam Resolution Came Up in the Tamil Nadu Assembly?—Pazha. Nedumaran Condemns Karunanidhi.”**

When this same Nedumaran was arrested under POTA during an earlier Jayalalithaa government and lodged in Cuddalore prison with no one to ask after him, I was the one who raised my voice for him. He is showing his gratitude now. Perhaps he fears that this government may put him inside again. If so, let him fall over himself praising every action of the rulers and issuing statements. We are not concerned about that. But while going his way, why is it necessary for him to fall upon us and scratch at us as well? He is said to have condemned me by asking, **“Why did the DMK walk out when the Tamil Eelam resolution came up?”**

All I wish to ask Pazha. Nedumaran is this: when the Tamil Eelam resolution came up, did the DMK walk out—or were its members expelled? Had the House or its members been told in advance that a Tamil Eelam resolution would be introduced? No.

What actually took place was a debate under Rule 55 on a special calling-attention motion concerning the protests by students in Tamil Nadu for the Eelam Tamils. After many had spoken, the Chief Minister, in her reply, unnecessarily piled severe accusations upon me and the DMK. Normally, in replying to a special calling-attention motion, accusations should not be made against anyone. Because blame was repeatedly directed at me, DMK members raised their voices in protest; the Speaker then expelled all the DMK members. Only after that, as the final part of Chief Minister Jayalalithaa's reply and without any advance notice, she suddenly moved the resolution concerning the Eelam Tamils and it was adopted.

All the newspapers reported this. Yet Nedumaran asks why the DMK “walked out” when the Tamil Eelam resolution came and condemns me. It would be good for him to learn from a competent teacher the difference between **“were expelled”** and **“went out.”**

He says the DMK betrayed the Eelam Tamils by walking out. Such are some politicians in Tamil Nadu! Fearing that some danger may come to them under this government, they rush unnecessarily to offer praise. Let them praise; let them survive. But imagining that they can survive there only by attacking us, they load accusations upon us that do not exist. It is because of such attitudes and such harmful approaches, the letter argues, that the Tamil fisherman is attacked and lives without anyone to turn to. Yet these people act as if they alone care for the Eelam Tamils and deceive others.

I am reminded of the line sung by my dear friend M.G.R. in the film *Malaikkallan*, for which I wrote the screenplay: **“How long will they go on deceiving in this country?”**

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**1 April 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. The comparison between the Italian-marines case and the detention of Tamil Nadu fishermen is Kalaignar's political argument. Counts, dates, treatment allegations and descriptions of government action are retained from the audited source and are not independently verified here.
2. **POTA**, **GPRS**, remand, and Rule 55 are retained at the level in which they appear in the source; the body gives enough context without adding external legal exposition.
3. The distinction between `வெளியேற்றப்பட்டனர்` (**were expelled**) and `வெளியேறினர்` (**went out / walked out**) is central to the letter's argument and is deliberately made explicit in English.
4. The final film-song line is translated only to the extent quoted by the source and supplies the title's rhetorical echo.

இந்தச் செய்தி ஏடுகளில் எல்லாம் வெளிவந்திருக்கிறது. ஆனால் நெடுமாறன் தமிழ் ஈழத் தீர்மானம் வந்தபோது தி.மு.க. வெளிநடப்பு செய்தது ஏன் என்று கேட்டு எனக்குக் கண்டனம் தெரிவித்திருக்கிறார். “வெளியேற்றப்பட்டனர்” என்பதற்கும், “வெளியேறினர்” என்பதற்கும் உள்ள வேறுபாட்டை - நல்ல ஆசிரியர் ஒருவரை வைத்து அவர் கற்றுத் தெரிந்துகொள்வது நல்லது. வெளிநடப்பு செய்ததன் மூலம் ஈழத் தமிழர்களுக்கு தி.மு.க. துரோகம் செய்துவிட்டதாம். தமிழகத்திலே இப்படி சில அரசியல்வாதிகள்!! எங்கே இந்த ஆட்சியில் தங்களுக்கு ஏதாவது ஆபத்து வந்துவிடுமோ என்பதற்காக தேவையில்லாமல் ஓடிப்போய்ப் பாராட்டு தெரிவிக்கிறார்கள். தெரிவிக்கட்டும், பிழைத்துப் போகட்டும், ஆனால் நம்மைத் தாக்கினால்தான் அங்கே பிழைக்க முடியும் என்றெண்ணி இப்படியெல்லாம் இல்லாத பழியைச் சுமத்துகிறார்களே, இப்படிப்பட்டவர்களின் இத்தகைய போக்குகளினால்தான் - பொல்லாத அணுகுமுறைகளால்தான் தமிழ் மீனவன் தாக்கப்படுகிறான், நாதியற்றவனாக வாழ்கிறான்! ஆனால் இவர்கள் ஏதோ ஈழத் தமிழர்கள்பால் தாங்கள்தான் அக்கறை கொண்டவர்கள் போல நடித்து ஏமாற்றுகிறார்கள். நான் எழுதிய “மலைக்கள்ளன்” திரைப்படத்தில் அருமை நண்பர் எம்.ஜி.ஆர். பாடிய “எத்தனை காலம்தான் ஏமாற்றுவார் இந்த நாட்டிலே?” என்ற பாடல் வரிதான் ஞாபகத்திற்கு வருகிறது!
