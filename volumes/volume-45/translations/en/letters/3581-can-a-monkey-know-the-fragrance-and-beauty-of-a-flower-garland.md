# 3581. Can a Monkey Know the Fragrance and Beauty of a Flower Garland!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 13 August 2011

**Udanpirappē,**

After the Supreme Court ruled that Samacheer Kalvi must be implemented from this very year, the Tamil Nadu Government, true to the saying “even after falling down, he claims no dust has touched his moustache,” announced that it would implement the scheme. Following that, a circular sent by the Education Department to all Chief Educational Officers instructed that portions to be removed from the Samacheer Kalvi textbooks should be concealed with stickers and, in some places, struck out with black marker pens before the books were distributed. I wish to explain clearly to the people what the Education Department has told all school headmasters must be removed.

First, the *Semmozhi Vazhthu* appearing generally in textbooks of all standards is to be covered with a sticker. Do you know what words in that greeting are supposedly so objectionable that they must be hidden?

> All lives are equal by birth—
>
> Once born, every place is our town, everyone our kin!
> A measure of food, two pieces of clothing,
>
> One dwelling place for all—
>
> Thus we spoke and lived;
>
> Let us labour and live.
>
> “Good and evil do not come from others”—
> That noble saying is our golden saying!
>
> Casting war aside,
>
> To make wealth common,
>
> Showing the path of peace,
>
> A language of love—
>
> The spoken truth of Ayyan Valluvar!
>
> From one-sensed life to six-sensed beings,
> discerning and describing bodily form,
> the unfadingly renowned *Tolkappiyam*;
>
> the high culture voiced by the incomparable *Kural*;
>
> the sounding *Silappathikaram* and *Manimekalai*,
> with *Civaka Cintamani*, *Valayapathi* and *Kundalakesi*—
> ours is Tamil, the Classical Language!
>
> Kamba Nattalvar,
>
> poet-queen noble Avvai,
>
> and thousands upon thousands of poets
> whom every faith accepts and praises—
> the language that is the seed
>
> for every new garment they weave!
>
> Dividing life beautifully
> as *agam* and *puram*,
>
> a sweet language without beginning or end—
>
> a living world language that grows as it is learned—
>
> our language—our language—that alone
> is the Classical Language—the Classical Language—our Tamil language!
> Long may it live! Long may it live!
>
> Long may it live!

Is there even one word here that students must not read—a word that would ruin their future if they did? There is only one offence: I wrote this *Semmozhi Vazhthu*. Look at the Classical Tamil greeting I wrote! At the same time, look also at the following words from the song for the World Tamil Conference conducted at Thanjavur when Jayalalithaa was Chief Minister:

> Like southern Tamil that lives forever,
> like Tamil literature that is ever sweet,
> to add eternal fame to the language,
> long live the gracious Chief Minister!
>
> Avvai, Andal and other noble women
> sang beautifully and nurtured Tamil;
> Mother Chief Minister Jayalalithaa
> nurtures it now from the seat of government.
>
> As Mother India and Mother Tamil
> shine together in harmony,
> a rare jewel who came unbidden—
> long live Mother Jayalalithaa!

In that World Tamil Conference greeting, instead of blessing Tamil, Jayalalithaa's praise was magnified many times over. In the *Semmozhi Vazhthu* used under the DMK Government, is there even a single word praising the Chief Minister? Yet they say it must be torn out!

In the first-standard Tamil textbook, four entire pages—69, 70, 79 and 80—are apparently to be removed! Do you know what is on them? Page 69 has a picture of an apple under the heading “I Will Colour It.” Half the apple is already coloured red; the pupil is to colour the rest. The entire sheet must be removed. Why? Because black and red are the DMK's colours, it seems. Page 70 contains pictures labelled Avvai, kauthari, full moon and bat. I do not know whether perhaps the picture of one of these resembles someone they know! Only the School Education Department can shed light on why that page must be torn out. There is likewise no apparent problem on page 79; the reason is unknown. Page 80 lists the Tamil months. Because the months begin with *Thai*, the whole page has been ordered torn out.

In the third-standard Tamil textbook, page 67 is covered by the circular's direction: “Two pictures, and the scenes of the cultural programme and procession at the World Classical Tamil Conference held from 23–27 June 2010, must be concealed with stickers.” The page merely contains small pictures that look like schoolgirls dancing at the conference's cultural programme and procession. The Government says these must be hidden. I do not know why.

In the third-standard English book, the last paragraph on page 26 must be removed. What does it say? “The World Tamil Conference was held at Coimbatore from 23 June to 27 June 2010.” That is the portion to be erased.

On page 27 of the same book, the passage “The Chief Minister presided over the conference. The conference exhibition was useful” must be erased. It does not even say that I was the conference president; it merely says “the Chief Minister.” Yet the AIADMK Government of Tamil Nadu says it must be erased.

In the fourth-standard Tamil textbook, page 74 says: “Pictures of the Classical Tamil Conference and their explanations must be concealed.” The picture appearing there is of poet Bharathi. They want that hidden.

On page 75, the first paragraph must be removed. The circular says the whole paragraph beginning “Among the classical languages, our refined Tamil is an eminent language” must be struck out with black marker. Do you know what that paragraph says? “Among the classical languages, our refined Tamil is an eminent language. At the first World Classical Tamil Conference conducted by the Tamil Nadu Government at Coimbatore, a procession was held. Decorated floats under the title ‘Iniyavai Narpathu’ took part in that procession. They displayed the antiquity of the Tamil language and the distinction of its culture.” Let the Tamil people decide whether even a single word there praises the DMK or me!

In the fourth-standard Social Science book, page 111's passage on Chennai Sangamam must be covered with a sticker. It says that folk arts refresh people and feast the artistic sense, and that the Tamil Nadu Government conducts Chennai Sangamam every year to develop folk arts and encourage folk artists. The Education Department says this passage must be struck out with black marker. I do not know whether encouraging folk artists is now an offence. They have ordered these lines erased.

In fifth-standard Social Science, the third picture on page 80 must be removed: the new Secretariat building must be covered with a sticker. The image is so small that one can barely tell it is the new Secretariat. If even that must not appear in a textbook, one can understand the depth of resentment occupying the AIADMK rulers' hearts.

In the sixth-standard Tamil book, page 56 carries Abdul Rahman's modern poem “Thirst.” The Government order says the entire page must be stickered over. The poem says: “Gardener who cut the branches of mine that stretched their heads beyond the fence, what will you do with my roots that slip beneath the fence? — Abdul Rahman.” I do not know why this poem must be hidden. Is the anger against Kavikko?

On page 129 of the same book, because the words “Tamil New Year special issue—poem—Welcome, Thai Tamil New Year” appear, the entire page must be removed. On page 130 there is a free travel-pass application form asking the pupil to fill in the student's name, school name, starting place and destination. I cannot discover why the whole page has been ordered removed.

In the sixth-standard English book, page 53's second paragraph, on *thappattam*, must be removed entirely. It explains what *thappattam* is and notes that it existed in the age of *Silappathikaram*. Because the paragraph ends with the sentence, “Have you seen *thappattam* performed at the Chennai Sangamam during the Pongal festival?”, they have ordered the entire paragraph removed.

In the sixth-standard Science book, the picture of a bar magnet on page 81 must be hidden. Why? Apparently because its colours look black and red.

In the sixth-standard Social Science book, page 11 says: “With financial assistance from the recently introduced Kalaignar Life-Saving Insurance Scheme of the Tamil Nadu Government, disadvantaged people everywhere are receiving quality treatment in high-cost advanced hospitals.” That must be covered with a sticker!

On page 12, the words “Anaithu Grama Anna Marumalarchi Thittam” in the third line must be erased with permanent marker. On page 17, the words “Anaithu Grama Anna Marumalarchi Thittam” and “Vazhnthu Kattuvom Project” must likewise be erased with permanent marker. Is even the name “Anna” objectionable under their rule?

On page 24, the words “It must be noted that the person implementing this is Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Kalaignar” must be erased. The book says: “To ensure nutritious food for pregnant mothers, the Government itself provides ₹1,000 a month for three months before and three months after childbirth, a total of ₹6,000; it must be noted that the person who announced and is implementing this good scheme is Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Kalaignar.” If the final words are deleted according to the Government's instruction, the sentence will simply stop at “implementing...” without completion. That, this Government says, is what students must read.

Page 35 contains a picture of a solar eclipse and another showing day and night. The solar-eclipse image must be entirely erased because part of it apparently looks black and red. In the day-and-night picture, the Sun itself must be erased. The AIADMK Government might as well issue an order saying that no textbook may contain a picture of the Sun!

On page 82, the words “Accepting the view of Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Kalaignar, in 2004 the Union Government declared Tamil a classical language. From this the incomparable distinction of Tamil becomes clear” must be erased. Readers themselves can decide what is wrong with these words. On the same page, a map of Tamil Nadu must be hidden. Why? The region inhabited by the Cholas is shown in black, the region ruled by the Cheras in red, and the Pandya region in blue. Because two of these three colours are black and red, the whole map must be hidden!

In the seventh-standard book, page 98 says that Ko.Po. Krishnasamy Pavalar, Kandasamy, N.S. Krishnan, Arignar Anna, Kalaignar M. Karunanidhi, the T.K.S. brothers, R.S. Manohar and others followed in the footsteps of the pioneers and helped Tamil theatre grow. The circular orders that passage struck out with black marker. Under the heading “Pioneers of Tamil Theatre,” the book discusses Parithimar Kalaignar, Sankaradas Swamigal, Pammal Sambandhanar and others in detail and then, in one final paragraph, says the people named above also helped theatre develop. Because my name was included, they have ordered that the names of Anna, N.S. Krishnan and all the others be blacked out too. Is it not shameful that such an order should be issued under a Government that bears Anna's name?

In the ninth-standard Tamil textbook, on page 203, the phrase “the inaugural celebration of the Tamil New Year” must be struck out with black marker. What does the passage contain? Only these words: “Pongal, the festival of Tamils; the festival of the month of Thai; the inaugural festival of the Tamil New Year; the distinctive festival of the Tamil people; the great festival of culture.” The words stating that the first day of Thai is the beginning of the Tamil New Year must be blacked out.

In the tenth-standard Tamil textbook, page 17, the circular orders that the words “was brought to completion by Honourable Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Kalaignar M. Karunanidhi” be struck out with black marker. Why? The text says: “The effort to have Tamil declared a classical language began in 1901 and continued until 2004. In October 2004, the Union Government recognised Tamil as a classical language. This continuing effort was brought to completion by Honourable Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Kalaignar M. Karunanidhi.” They say the final statement naming the Chief Minister must be blacked out.

On page 89 of the same book, the circular says the entire “Let Us Learn” section must be removed. A poem I wrote appears there; the whole thing must go.

Page 100 says: “Read and understand Kalaignar's pleasing prose. Flag Day.......victory will come seeking you and garland you.” The circular orders the passage covered with a sticker. A prose piece I wrote about Flag Day must be hidden because I wrote it.

On page 239, the circular orders the sentence “The Pavendhar Classical Tamil Research Library functions at Fort St. George, Chennai” struck out with black marker. Since this Government removed the Pavendhar Classical Tamil Research Library from Fort St. George overnight, perhaps that is why it wants the sentence erased!

On page 114, it says that the new Secretariat has been functioning at the Omandurar Government Estate on Anna Salai since 13 March 2010. That too must be struck out with black marker.

The entire page 177 of the tenth-standard English book must apparently be removed. What is on that page? A passage concerning the Thanjavur Big Temple must be erased.

In the tenth-standard Social Science textbook, the words “DMK Government” occur in two places, and they have ordered those words erased. I accept that without objection. But from everything above, you can see the nature of all the other portions this Government has removed from the textbooks. In court, Government counsel made a great issue of claiming that page after page in the Samacheer Kalvi textbooks had been filled with praise for the DMK and for me, and that the books therefore ought not be accepted. I have compiled the details here so that everyone may know exactly what words in those textbooks supposedly praised the previous Government and me. I trust that you too will explain these facts to the people and to our student treasures.

**With affection, M.K.**  
13-8-2011

மன்றங்களில் அரசு தரப்பில் வாதாடிய வழக்கறிஞர்கள் தி.மு.
கழகத்தைப் பற்றியும், என்னைப் பற்றியும் பக்கம் பக்கமாக
பாராட்டி சமச்சீர் கல்விப் பாடப் புத்தகங்களில் இடம் பெறச்
செய்துவிட்டதாகக் கூறி, அந்தப் புத்தகங்களை ஏற்றுக் கொள்ளக்
கூடாது என்றார்கள். சமச்சீர் பாடப் புத்தகங்களில் அப்படி
என்னதான் வார்த்தைகள் கடந்த கால அரசையும், என்னையும்
பாராட்டி வந்துவிட்டன என்ற விவரம் அனைவருக்கும் தெரிய
வேண்டுமென்பதற்காகத்தான் இங்கே தொகுத்துள்ளேன். இந்த
விவரங்களை நீயும் மக்களுக்கும், மாணவச் செல்வங்களுக்கும்
விளக்கிடுவாய் என்று நம்புகிறேன்.
