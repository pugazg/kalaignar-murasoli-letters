**Udanpirappē,**

Some people may do many good things for the country and society; yet a certain “few” have an “allergy” merely on seeing them. One among that few is today’s State Secretary of the Marxist Communist Party, Comrade Ramakrishnan. For some reason, he does not like the “DMK.” The DMK is not now the ruling party in Tamil Nadu. Like the CPI(M), the DMK too is now an Opposition party. Yet, for Comrade Ramakrishnan, criticising the ruling party seems less sweet than pouring irritation upon the DMK. We do not know why!

The present ruling party, the AIADMK, does not even value the Marxists. Not only the Marxists: it does not care for or respect even the important parties that allied with it in the last general election and helped bring the AIADMK to power. Still, friend Ramakrishnan does not seem greatly troubled by that.

Yesterday Comrade Ramakrishnan wrote an article in his party’s official daily, *Theekkathir*. He divided the article into two parts. In the first, he poured out as much hostility toward the DMK as he could. In the second, he wrote about the AIADMK almost unwillingly, as though pained that “we have to write about them too.” In a democratic system, it is the duty of the Kazhagam to answer an attack from another party, the Marxist Communist Party. It is in that spirit that I offer the following explanation. I have never had any personal ill feeling toward Comrade Ramakrishnan. I come to the matter.

At the beginning of his article, after referring to the *arappor* conducted by the DMK and to my statement that it had succeeded beyond expectation, Ramakrishnan criticises us: people suffered from steep increases in petrol prices, fertiliser prices, bus fares, milk prices and electricity tariffs; the DMK merely issued statements then, and at most conducted demonstrations, but now it has conducted a jail-filling agitation against land-grab cases filed against former DMK ministers. Having made that criticism, he also answers his own conscience: “It is the right of each party to decide for what issue and in what form it will struggle; we do not wish to interfere in that.”

After himself saying that each party has the right to decide the form of its agitation, he asks why the DMK—having conducted large demonstrations on people’s issues—conducted a jail-filling agitation against cases involving former ministers. The DMK did not ignore people’s issues and suddenly announce a jail-filling agitation the moment cases were filed against former ministers. Cases, arrests and revenge against the DMK did not begin only now. As early as July 2011, almost immediately after Jayalalithaa came to office, arrests of former Kazhagam ministers began.

On 30 July 2011, Veerapandi Arumugam and MLA J. Anbazhagan were arrested; on the same day in Tiruvarur, Treasurer M.K. Stalin and Poondi Kalaivanan; on 2 August, Purasai Renganathan; on 4 August, N.K.K.P. Raja in Erode; on 6 August, Tiruppur City Deputy Secretary T.K.T. Nagaraj; on 10 August, Tiruchendur MLA Anita Radhakrishnan and the Salem Thammampatti town secretary; on 17 August, Pongalur Mani in Coimbatore; on 25 August, former Minister K.N. Nehru, Tiruchi city secretary Anbazhagan, Coimbatore district secretary Pongalur Palanisamy, Paindhamizh Pari and city secretary Veeragopal; on 27 August, Lalgudi MLA Soundarapandian and former town-panchayat chairman A.C. Govindasamy in Kangeyam; on 29 August, former mayor’s husband Gopinath in Madurai; on 1 September, the Tiruchengode municipal chairman; on 9 September, Chennai area secretary K. Dhanasekaran and Jayaraman in Madurai; on 10 September, Karuppasamy Pandian in Tirunelveli; on 11 September, Minnalkodi in Madurai; on 18 September, former Minister Ponmudi in Villupuram; on 19 September, Karur MLA K.C. Palanisamy; on 23 September, former Minister K.P.P. Samy in Chennai; on 2 November, K.P.P. Samy’s brothers and Isari Ganesh; on 16 November, MP Rithish; and on 3 June 2012, once again former Ministers Veerapandi Arumugam and I. Periyasamy, N. Suresh Rajan, K.K.S.S.R. Ramachandran, Madurai city district secretary Thalapathi, former Minister Vellakoil Saminathan, and in Madurai Gurusamy, Attack Pandi, Pottu Suresh, Veerapandi A. Raja and Tiruchi Kudamurutti Sekar.

For a year, Kazhagam front-rankers were continuously arrested. Even after courts granted bail, fresh allegedly false complaints were obtained and new cases filed; if bail was obtained again, the Goondas Act was invoked and they were imprisoned. With no other path left, the DMK convened its Executive Committee and unanimously adopted a resolution condemning not only that revenge but also joining with it the people’s problems. Only then was the jail-filling agitation announced and conducted in due form.

Is it fair, then, for Marxist leader Ramakrishnan conveniently to forget—or hide—all this and accuse the DMK of not struggling for the people, claiming that it conducted the jail-filling agitation only for former ministers? Is that the position of the CPI(M)?

Comrade Ramakrishnan did not stop there. In support of the ruling party’s reply to the DMK, he compiled in his statement the Government’s Assembly figures on how many land-grab complaints had been received, how many cases had been registered and how much land had been recovered. One may even call that his duty! Yet, in his own statement, he says the Government’s figures show that **703 land-grab cases** were received in all and that **only 42 cases were against DMK members**. Those very numbers show how swiftly the Government acts with hostility against DMK members while showing no comparable concern in proceeding against others.

The CPI(M) State Secretary then writes at length about the Salem Angammal Colony issue. By recording what the Pallapatti police inspector and the Salem Revenue Divisional Officer said during DMK rule, he actually confirms what I told journalists—that action had been taken under the Kazhagam Government. As for the later attack on residents of that colony and the present AIADMK Government’s action against Thambi Veerapandi Arumugam, the matter is before the court. The trial is not over.

If, after trial, a judge finds wrongdoing, let legal action follow. But does Ramakrishnan consider it just that the Government itself should become the judge; that, despite knowing that former Minister Veerapandi Arumugam is over seventy-five and a heart patient, it should deliberately file case after case even after a court grants bail, invoke the Goondas Act so that he cannot come out on bail, and drag him by van from Salem prison to Coimbatore prison, Puzhal prison and Vellore prison? Is that revenge or not? If action is taken against even one district secretary of his party, how many condemnatory statements and protests do they conduct? But if the Government repeatedly acts in revenge against a former Kazhagam minister, is it wrong for the DMK to protest?

Even then, the DMK did not agitate only for that. It joined the people’s issues to it. The Executive Committee resolution itself spoke in detail of kuruvai cultivation in the Cauvery Delta becoming a dream; unresolved river-water disputes with Kerala, Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh; increases in rice, groceries, vegetables, bus fares, milk and electricity tariffs; corruption and bribery from teacher transfers to building permissions; disorderly implementation of election promises; judicial censures; and administrative crises overturning the lives of every section. It said that, to divert attention, the AIADMK Government continued “publicity fairs,” “video-conference magic shows,” “arrests of Kazhagam activists — Goondas Act,” and barbaric legal measures squeezing the Kazhagam that courts had rejected and rebuked. Only after writing all this did the resolution announce a jail-filling agitation “condemning all these things.”

Was it right for Ramakrishnan to conceal this and issue his statement? I have given all these explanations so that at least the comrades in that party may think about it.

When comrades and front-rankers in the Indian Communist Party sharply criticise the ruling party, but one or two at the top praise the Chief Minister, we can guess the reason. But why this policy difference in the leadership of the Marxist Communist Party?

Although Comrade Ramakrishnan piled up this many accusations against the DMK in the first part of his statement, the second part gives us some comfort because he also writes about land-grab actions by the ruling AIADMK. Yet even when criticising the ruling party, he joins the DMK—which is not in power—with it and attacks both at the same level. One can only wonder whether this is done out of fear that the ruling-party leadership might otherwise become angry with them.

When the DMK was in office, CPI(M) leaders met me at the Secretariat and complained that people very close to the AIADMK leader had occupied lands allotted to Dalits in Siruthavur village. The Kazhagam Government acted on that complaint, constituted a commission of inquiry, and Justice Sivasubramaniam submitted a report. Why are they silent about the fact that the report now lies without any action under the AIADMK Government? Why is hatred shown only when the name is DMK?

**Is all this politics?**

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**09 July 2012**
