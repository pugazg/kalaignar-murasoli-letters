# 3580. Never Mind; Let Them Dance as Long as They Can!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 12 August 2011

**Udanpirappē,**

The 6-8-2011 issue of *Dinamani*, under the title “Truth Will Burn!”, has as usual written an editorial supporting the ruling party. In it, *Dinamani* asks: “DMK leader M. Karunanidhi repeatedly says that the Tamil Nadu Government is filing false cases against Opposition members. One person may lie; two or three people together may lie. But will people in large numbers from an entire town, from across the whole State, come and lie?” I would like to offer *Dinamani* a few explanations.

The Jayalalithaa Government registered a case against Thiruvarur district DMK secretary Poondi Kalaivanan and moved to arrest him. What offence did this Government say he had committed? On the 29th, lakhs of students took part in the peaceful protest supporting Samacheer Kalvi and boycotted their schools. The son of a Communist Party comrade also took part. Later, while returning home, the bus in which he was travelling collided with a lorry and the boy died. What connection did that have with the DMK district secretary?

When Kalaivanan sought bail, Judge Chandrasekar, who heard the petition, said that the court accepted the submission of advocate Asan Mohamed, appearing for Poondi Kalaivanan, that “this case was filed out of political vendetta,” and ordered his release on bail. So it is not we who are saying that this was a false case. A judge himself has said so.

Next, the AIADMK Government hurriedly registered another case under seven sections against Poondi Kalaivanan, alleging that at about 10 a.m. on 30-7-2011 he had damaged property and belongings at Thiruthuraipoondi. Police travelled all the way to Palayamkottai and arrested him there the moment he came out of prison on bail. Does *Dinamani* call this justice?

When the police tried to remand Kalaivanan after arresting him for the second time, Kalaivanan himself told the judge that on the night of 29-7-2011 he had gone to welcome DMK treasurer M.K. Stalin, who had flown from Chennai to Tiruchi; that he stayed with him that night in Thanjavur; that on the morning of the 30th he was travelling with him in a van when police intercepted and arrested him; and that at the very time mentioned by the police in the second case he was not even in Thiruthuraipoondi. Does *Dinamani* deny this too? Does it deny that the AIADMK Government's complaint accusing him of damaging property in Thiruthuraipoondi was false?

And after all this, if the AIADMK Government has proceeded against the same Kalaivanan under the Goondas Act, can *Dinamani* still say that false cases are not being filed under this Government?

Next, police went to arrest former minister and Erode district DMK secretary N.K.K.P. Raja at about two in the morning. They entered only after climbing over the compound wall. They took him to the judge's residence and asked that he be remanded.

But the Erode judge said that the police had produced neither evidence nor reasons for arresting N.K.K.P. Raja and seeking his remand, and therefore refused to approve the remand. Does this at least make it clear to *Dinamani* that the case against Raja was a false one?

Then there is South Chennai district DMK secretary and MLA J. Anbazhagan. Police rushed to his home at dawn, woke him from sleep and registered a case under eight sections. Do you know who gave the complaint? A person who, in August 2009, had bought sick mills, used forged documents to commit fraud of more than ₹250 crore, been arrested and then released on bail. He had prepared forged documents in the names of more than twenty companies. The case involving him and his associates had been transferred to the CBI. Taking the complaint of such a fraudster, they arrested Anbazhagan in Chennai, took him to Coimbatore and imprisoned him. Does *Dinamani* believe that to be a genuine case?

Former MLA Purasai Ranganathan was arrested by this Government and a case was filed against him. What is the fate of that case? The very same case was already pending before a court. Why this sudden action now? I have merely underlined one or two examples showing that these are false cases. That is all.

In Salem, former minister Veerapandi Arumugam came out on bail and went to the police station to fulfil the condition requiring him to sign every day. While he was there, they registered yet another case against him and arrested him.

In Madurai too, cases have been filed against many DMK comrades—and the Goondas Act has been invoked so that they cannot come out on bail.

In Tiruchi, a deputy district DMK secretary was arrested and detained under the Goondas Act. But what happened to that? He has now come out.

An article published by a weekly supportive of the ruling party begins in its very first paragraph: “Kodaikanal police have achieved the feat of filing an FIR on a complaint that had already been withdrawn.” In that incident, even though the complainants had withdrawn the complaint six months earlier, the police acted on it and arrested several DMK members, including the Kodaikanal Municipal Chairman.

If I call these false cases, *Dinamani* responds with an editorial asking whether every case can be false. What answer will *Dinamani* give to all this? If the complainant is genuine and the truth of the complaint is established, no one can prevent lawful action on that complaint; one can take comfort that the law is doing its duty. But what has been happening in Tamil Nadu for the past three months?

I have already said, and I repeat it now: “Even when genuine land-grab complaints are made against AIADMK members, the police do not register cases. They call the concerned minister, have him speak with the complainants and send them away after a settlement. But if someone who sold land makes a complaint against DMK members, the police register a case and arrest them without even inquiring whether the complaint is true or false.”

The *Dinamani* editorial, after quoting this view of mine, goes on to say: “Since the AIADMK is the ruling party, the police may well adopt this approach; there is no doubt about that.” Is such a statement really appropriate under the title “Truth Will Burn!”?

Instead of coming forward to condemn this lawlessness by ruling AIADMK members in strong terms, writing as though what happens must simply be accepted is equivalent to sounding the death knell for the ideal that “everyone is equal before the law.” Is not the Indian Penal Code common to everyone?

Is there one law for the ruling party and another for Opposition parties—differences here too, like “varna distinctions”?

At Tiruvannamalai, when AIADMK district representative C. Moorthy made a complaint against an AIADMK minister, was that complaint registered by police? Was action taken? Was anyone arrested? What happened? Did not a senior minister summon Moorthy, give him what had to be given, settle the matter and have the complaint itself withdrawn?

Property belonging to the Thyagaraja Temple at Thiruvarur was fraudulently sold. A case was registered against seven people, including a district AIADMK functionary. Yet the accused were not arrested. The temple official who made the complaint has been temporarily suspended. Does *Dinamani* not know all this? This is not the only instance. Many complaints have been made against ruling-party members, and in all of them the police have taken no action.

How are the Jayalalithaa Government's police now dealing with land-grab disputes? What did Additional Director of Police George, IPS, responsible for law and order, say about this?

“We have asked the Police to carefully scrutinise every petition. Verification is done at different levels and a decision to register a case is taken by the concerned Commissioner or Superintendent of Police.”

(The police have been told to examine every complaint deeply. Only after the complaint has been carefully verified at different levels is a decision taken by the concerned Commissioner of Police or Superintendent of Police on whether to register a case.)

Are complaints in fact being investigated and decisions on registering cases made in the manner George described?

DMK comrades are arrested even without complaints; after arresting them, police wander about looking for complaints. If a court grants bail to DMK comrades arrested on one complaint, another complaint is prepared and they are arrested again. Out of anger against DMK comrades and against the courts, they finally fire the cruel weapon called the “Goondas Act.” Those are the examples I have given above.

As Additional Director of Police George said, are they now waiting for complaints to arise on their own? Once a complaint comes, are they conducting the preliminary inquiries needed to examine deeply the integrity of the complainant and the truth of the complaint? Is the decision to register a case being taken by the Commissioner of Police or the Superintendent of Police? Or, listening to orders coming from somewhere else, are things being done only against DMK comrades in frantic haste, overnight, like a pattern splashed about without care?

The encouragement and priority that the AIADMK rulers are now giving to what they call “land grabbing” will produce the following undesirable consequences:

- Since most land-related disputes are matters of “civil litigation,” a habit of taking continuing interest in them will grow within the police.
- As a result, innumerable coercive “katta panchayats” will spring up. Even the rulers, if they wish, will not be able to control them once they cross a certain limit.
- Greed for extracting money will make false complaints multiply like swarms of termites.
- When the police begin showing excessive interest in “land-grab” matters for various reasons and ulterior motives, crimes such as murder, robbery and chain-snatching will proliferate, placing the safety of innocent members of the public in question.
- A cheap weapon called the “land-grab complaint” will fall into the hands of anti-social elements.
- “Land-grab” disputes will move away from the fair scrutiny of law and come under police discretion.
- There will be a danger that, at all times, they become an easy weapon for taking revenge on political parties.

By saying this, I do not mean that “land-grab” complaints should be ignored. Just as crimes such as murder and robbery are investigated, the police must be allowed—without political interference and without targeting only one particular political party—to investigate thoroughly, determine the truth of land-grab complaints and proceed according to law.

The AIADMK rulers dream that, with false complaints labelled “land grabbing” and with police assistance, they can destabilise DMK comrades. Their revenge-driven dream will remain a daydream. Instead of wounding their own hearts over something that will never be fulfilled, it would befit the rulers to think about and act constructively on Tamil Nadu's fundamental problems! It is not good for AIADMK members and the police to waste time and thought creating bad precedents! Never mind; let them dance as long as they can!

**With affection, M.K.**  
12-08-2011

புகார் வந்ததும், புகார் கொடுப்பவரின் நேர்மையையும்,
புகாரின் உண்மைத் தன்மையினையும் ஆழ்ந்து பரிசீலித்திடத்
தேவையான முதல் நிலை விசாரணைகளைச் செய்கிறார்களா?
புகாரின் மீது வழக்குப் பதிவு செய்யலாம் என்ற முடிவு காவல்
ஆணையாளரினாலா அல்லது காவல் கண்காணிப்பாளராலா
மேற்கொள்ளப்படுகிறது? எங்கிருந்தோ வரும் கட்டளைகளைக்
கேட்டு, கழகத் தோழர்களைப் பொறுத்தவரை மட்டும் அவசரம்

அவசரமாக இரவோடு இரவாக அள்ளித் தெளித்த கோலத்தில்
அல்லவா காரியங்கள் நடைபெற்றுக் கொண்டிருக்கின்றன.

“நில அபகரிப்பு” என்று சொல்லி அதற்கு அ.தி.மு.க.
ஆட்சியாளர்கள் அளித்து வரும் ஊக்கமும், முன்னுரிமையும்
பின்வரும் விரும்பத்தகாத விளைவுகளை ஏற்படுத்தி விடும்.

• நிலம் தொடர்பான பெரும்பாலான பிரச்சினைகள்
“சிவில் தாவா” சம்பந்தப் பட்டதால்; அவற்றில்
தொடர்ந்து ஆர்வம் காட்டும் மனோ பாவம் காவல்
துறையில் வளர்ந்துவிடும்.

• அதன் காரணமாக “கட்டப்பஞ்சாயத்துகள்”
ஏராளமாக முளைக்கும். ஆட்சியாளர்கள்
நினைத்தாலும், கட்டுப்படுத்த முடியாத
எல்லைக்கு அவை சென்றுவிடும்.

• பொருள் கறக்கும் பேராசை காரணமாக பொய்ப்
புகார்கள் புற்றீசல் போலத் தோன்றிவிடும்.

• “நில அபகரிப்பு” பிரச்சினைகளில் பல்வேறு
காரணங்களுக்காகவும், உள் நோக்கங்களுக்காகவும்
காவல் துறை அபரிமிதமான
ஆர்வம் காட்ட முற்படும்போது; கொலை,
கொள்ளை, செயின் பறிப்பு போன்ற குற்றங்கள்
மலிந்து, அப்பாவிப் பொதுமக்களின் பாதுகாப்பு
கேள்விக்குறியாகிவிடும்.

• சமூக விரோதிகளின் கைக்கு “நில அபகரிப்புப்
புகார்” என்ற மலிவான ஆயுதம் கிடைத்துவிடும்.

• “நில அபகரிப்பு” பிரச்சினைகள் சட்டத்தின்
நியாயமான பார்வையிலிருந்து, காவல்
துறையினரின் விருப்புரிமையின்கீழ் வந்து விடும்.

• எல்லாக் காலங்களிலும் அரசியல் கட்சிகளைப்
பழிவாங்கிட எளிதாகப் பயன்படுத்தப்படும்
ஆயுதமாக மாறிவிடுகிற ஆபத்து ஏற்பட்டு விடும்.

அலட்சியப்படுத்த வேண்டும் என்பது பொருளல்ல. கொலை,
