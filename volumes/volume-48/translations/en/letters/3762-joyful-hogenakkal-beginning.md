# 3762. A Joyous Beginning for Hogenakkal!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 27 May 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

The 25 May 2013 edition of *Dinamani* carried a report under the headline **“Hogenakkal Combined Drinking Water Scheme — Chief Minister to Inaugurate on May 29.”**

It was during DMK rule that work proceeded at full pace to implement the Hogenakkal Drinking Water and Fluorosis Mitigation Project, with financial assistance from the Japanese international-cooperation institution, in order to provide protected and quality drinking water to people in Dharmapuri and Krishnagiri districts.

Through this project, the following were to benefit:

- the three municipal areas of **Dharmapuri, Krishnagiri and Hosur**;
- ten town-panchayat areas in Dharmapuri district: **P. Mallapuram, Harur, Kadathur, Kambainallur, Karimangalam, Marandahalli, Palacode, Pappireddipatti, Pennagaram and Papparapatti**;
- seven town-panchayat areas in Krishnagiri district: **Bargur, Kaveripattinam, Nagojanahalli, Uthangarai, Mathigiri, Kelamangalam and Denkanikottai**;
- the areas within eight unions in Dharmapuri district: **Morappur, Harur, Pappireddipatti, Palacode, Karimangalam, Pennagaram, Nallampalli and Dharmapuri**; and
- the areas within ten unions in Krishnagiri district: **Bargur, Krishnagiri, Kaveripattinam, Mathur, Uthangarai, Veppanapalli, Kelamangalam, Thally, Shoolagiri and Hosur**.

The project was begun under DMK rule so that nearly **30 lakh people in 6,755 habitations** across these areas could receive protected and hygienic drinking water.

The project would improve the quality of life of people in Dharmapuri and Krishnagiri districts. It would free them from the many problems caused by the drinking water then available and bring them happiness and satisfaction.

It was during DMK rule, in February 1997, that the Hogenakkal Combined Drinking Water Scheme was prepared at an estimated cost of ₹576 crore. It was later modified to include sanitation facilities and sent to the Union Government to obtain financial assistance from the Japanese funding institution.

Field-survey work began in March 1998. An application for a no-objection certificate to draw water from the Cauvery River was made in March 1998, and the Union Ministry of Water Resources granted the certificate in September 1998. But because of the nuclear tests conducted at Pokhran in May 1998, the Japanese funding institution stopped extending financial assistance to any new project in India. During the AIADMK government from 2001 onward, no substantive action was taken to implement the Hogenakkal Combined Drinking Water Scheme.

Only after the DMK returned to office in May 2006 were continuous steps again taken to implement the project.

During the debate on the Rural Development and Local Administration Department's demand for grants in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly on 11 August 2006, the DMK government announced that the Hogenakkal Combined Drinking Water Scheme, using Cauvery water as its source, would be taken up for implementation.

Later, while speaking on the motion of thanks to the Governor's Address in the Assembly on 27 January 2007, the government gave this assurance:

> “Financial assistance from the Japanese international-cooperation institution has been sought for implementing the Hogenakkal project. Only this week, Japanese experts visited Dharmapuri district and carried out a project study. The Union Government has given this project high priority in seeking Japanese financial assistance and has communicated this to the Government of Japan. This communication increases the prospects of obtaining Japanese assistance. Even so, if Japanese financial assistance is not available for some reason, the government will obtain another source of finance and will definitely implement this project.”

Then, when the 2007–2008 Budget was presented in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly on 23 March 2007, it was announced:

> “The Hogenakkal Water Supply Scheme, the only solution to the drinking-water problem of Dharmapuri and Krishnagiri districts, has been recommended by the Union Government for financial assistance from the Japan Bank for International Cooperation. Following this, a team of experts from that bank has recently carried out preliminary studies in these districts. This government will take up implementation of the project as a challenge.”

As a result of the actions that followed these announcements, during DMK rule Local Administration Minister Thambi M.K. Stalin travelled with a team of officials to Tokyo. On 6 February 2008 he met and held discussions with Mr Tshioguchi, Executive Director of the Japan Bank for International Cooperation. As a result, the bank agreed to provide financial assistance both for the Chennai Metro Rail project, estimated at ₹9,700 crore, and for the Hogenakkal Combined Drinking Water Scheme—then estimated at ₹1,330 crore and a long-standing demand of the people of Dharmapuri and Krishnagiri districts.

It was in continuation of this that I laid the foundation stone for the project at a massive function held in Dharmapuri on 26 February 2008.

Nor did matters stop with laying the foundation stone. A formal timetable was fixed for implementing the Hogenakkal Combined Drinking Water Scheme, and work continued accordingly.

Administrative approval to implement the project at a cost of ₹1,334 crore was issued on 25 February 2008 under DMK rule. Later, because project costs were expected to rise, revised administrative approval issued on 28 January 2010 fixed the estimated cost at **₹1,928 crore 80 lakh**.

A project-management consultancy was appointed on 3 October 2008. Work for the Hogenakkal Combined Drinking Water Scheme was to be carried out in five packages. For the first package, pre-qualification applications were received from ten companies and sent to the Japanese international-cooperation institution for approval. For the second, third and fifth packages, pre-qualification applications were received from several companies and evaluated. Tender documents were prepared at the same time.

According to the timetable approved by the Japanese funding institution, tenders were to be invited in July 2009, field work was to begin in December 2009, and the project was to be completed and brought into use by December 2012. Work proceeded during DMK rule according to that timetable without delay.

The people of Dharmapuri and Krishnagiri districts themselves know directly how earnestly the DMK government carried out this work in order to fulfil, within the stipulated period, the promise it had made on the Hogenakkal Combined Drinking Water Scheme.

The AIADMK government conducted closing ceremonies for several good schemes begun under DMK rule. Fortunately, however, it did not obstruct the Hogenakkal Combined Drinking Water Scheme and, even if belatedly, has come forward to complete it. But it is said that even before the work has been completed in full, there is a hurried effort to have the Chief Minister inaugurate it.

Although the present rulers may deliberately try to hide the fact that this project was accelerated during DMK rule, *Theekkathir* in a report published today referred to the efforts made under the DMK government. In particular, it wrote: **“During DMK rule, the work was divided into five packages. ₹1,200 crore has been spent and 90 per cent of the work has been completed.”**

The government's announcement that the Chief Minister will inaugurate the scheme on the 29th itself indicates that the project has not yet been fully completed: it appears that she will inaugurate it after only a portion of the work has been completed, with the remaining works to be finished progressively afterward.

Whatever the circumstances, a project launched through great effort during DMK rule is reaching completion, and the people of Dharmapuri and Krishnagiri districts are going to receive safe, good drinking water. As one who worked for this project, I express my happiness at that.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**27 May 2013**

1. Place names, package counts, habitations, population, dates and financial figures are translated as printed in the source and are not externally harmonised.
2. The Tamil source alternates between a Japanese international-cooperation `institution` and `bank`; the translation follows the wording and context of each passage rather than silently reconciling the institutional history.
3. The source-visible broken forms such as `வாழ்க் கைத்`, `பெறு\nவதற்காக`, `ஒப்பந்\nதப்புள்ளிப்`, `திட்டத் திற்கு` and `கிடைக் கப்` remain preserved in the Tamil layer below.
