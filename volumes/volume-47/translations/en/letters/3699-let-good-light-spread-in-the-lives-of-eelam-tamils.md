# 3699. Let Good Light Spread in the Lives of Eelam Tamils...

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This thought-preserving translation retains the source's advocacy for Sri Lankan Tamils, TESO resolutions, correspondence with Indian leaders, references to military training and rehabilitation, quoted diaspora responses, names and dates. Tamil remains authoritative. `Udanpirappē` is retained as Kalaignar's distinctive address.

## English translation

Udanpirappē,

The sufferings of the Eelam Tamils have not ended; they continue to inflict pain. The Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam has continued, to the extent possible, to do whatever it can with the welfare of Sri Lankan Tamils in mind. Those who love truth will know that from 1956 onward, whether in power or out of power, we have continuously raised our voice for Eelam Tamils.

Although the Government of India has assured us that it will help on our various demands, there are occasions when it acts in a manner that makes some people suspect that, in order to preserve its image among the nations of the world as a non-aligned country, it is yielding on certain matters.

For example, the demand of Tamils with feeling in Tamil Nadu is that Sri Lankan military personnel, whose forces were responsible for killing Sri Lankan Tamils, should not be given training anywhere in India. We have explained and pressed this demand many times before the Government of India.

Yet the Government of India has not been able to state categorically that it will not train Sri Lankan military personnel in India. Yesterday Union Defence Minister A.K. Antony said that rehabilitation work for Tamils in Sri Lanka was not progressing at the expected pace.

Sri Lankan Foreign Minister G.L. Peiris, who is currently visiting India, said that he was not concerned about Tamil Nadu politicians opposing the training given to Sri Lankan military personnel in India, and that the issues raised by those politicians arose from hatred.

Responding, Union Minister Antony said he did not believe the objections raised by Tamil Nadu political leaders were based on hatred. He said that although the Sri Lankan Government had taken some measures, rehabilitation for Tamils was still not at the expected level. He added that India's Government would respect the feelings of Tamil Nadu, avoid military training in Tamil Nadu, while continuing to train Sri Lankan personnel at military establishments in other parts of India.

It was precisely so that international institutions such as the United Nations, which greatly desire unity and peace in Sri Lanka, would understand the reality of the Sinhala-isation of the Eelam region that we adopted fourteen resolutions at our TESO conference.

Those unanimously adopted TESO resolutions were taken by our party treasurer, brother M.K. Stalin, and the leader of the DMK parliamentary group, brother T.R. Baalu, and submitted to the United Nations and the U.N. Human Rights Commission.

We did not stop with passing those resolutions at the Eelam Tamil Right-to-Life Protection Conference organised by TESO. I also sent them to the Prime Minister of India. In that letter I asked the Union Government to help bring before the U.N. General Assembly and the Human Rights Commission a resolution that would provide the Sri Lankan Tamils the rights needed to decide for themselves the political solution they seek.

After reading my letter, the Prime Minister assured me that appropriate action would be taken. He also recalled that India, accepting our request, had supported the earlier resolution brought against Sri Lanka at the United Nations Human Rights Council. A copy of my letter to the Prime Minister was also personally handed by DMK Members of Parliament to UPA Chairperson Sonia Gandhi.

Besides giving assurances directly to our party members, Prime Minister Dr Manmohan Singh wrote back to me stating that the Government of India was showing concern for the welfare and well-being of Sri Lankan Tamils and was giving high priority to their resettlement and rehabilitation. He also wrote that India had conveyed to the Sri Lankan Government the need to advance talks with political representatives of the Eelam Tamils on devolution of powers, and that India would continue its efforts with Sri Lanka to create conditions in which Eelam Tamils could live in future with equal rights, dignity, equal justice and self-respect.

Later, at the TESO consultative meeting held under my chairmanship at Anna Arivalayam on 3 October 2012, we resolved that the Government of India should take steps to bring the plight of Eelam Tamils to the attention of the United Nations, bring the present situation in Sri Lanka quickly to an end, protect their livelihood, and enable them to discuss and decide for themselves the rights they seek.

We have understood the deceptive drama by which the Sinhala Government has continued to mislead the Tamil people since the days of Eelam Thanthai Chelva. That is why we have insisted that the political solution required by Eelam Tamils should be decided and implemented through a referendum. Events at the U.N. Human Rights Council show that countries across the world are beginning, one by one, to understand the inhuman approach of the Sinhala Government.

A few days ago, Member of Parliament Mr Selvaraja from Sri Lanka, Mr Kannan from London and Mr Sivaganesan from Norway sent me information. According to them, after the war ended in 2009, Baby Subramaniam known as Ilangumaran, Yogi, poet Puduvai Rathinagiri, Balakumar and Ilamparithi were among some 18,000 Tamils who were detained. Their present condition is unknown. Yogi's wife, Mrs Yogathinam Yogi, has anxiously raised questions about this. They also said the whereabouts of Eelam poet Puduvai Rathinagiri are unknown, that some say he was killed, and that the life of poet Rathinagiri, who worked actively in the Eelam cause, has itself become a question mark. They asked me to raise this issue.

In the long letter I wrote on 20 January 2013 to Prime Minister Manmohan Singh and Congress President Sonia Gandhi, I asked them to stop the planned encroachments and oppressive actions being carried out by the Sinhala Government in Tamil-inhabited areas of Sri Lanka. I also pointed out the rapid efforts to dilute the Tamil language and Tamil culture and to disrupt and destroy the economic life of Tamils. I wrote in detail that the existing names of 89 Tamil villages in Sri Lanka had been replaced with Sinhala names. The full details of that letter and the names of those villages appeared extensively in newspapers, including English dailies, which gave the report great prominence.

Through the efforts of advocate brother K.S. Radhakrishnan, this news reached Eelam Tamils living in Britain, Canada, the United States and Europe through the Athirvu website. Thousands read it, and it was translated into English and published on English-language websites too. Here are summaries of a few responses sent in just one day:

Sudhakaran, Germany, wrote that he was deeply troubled to learn that the name of the village where he was born and raised might have been changed into Sinhala, leaving him unable to tell his children where he was born, and thanked former Chief Minister Kalaignar for drawing attention to the issue.

Jayasankar, Switzerland, wrote that he had once criticised Kalaignar after reading certain websites, but that what Kalaignar was doing now brought relief to Eelam Tamils.

Thirunavukkarasu, London, said Kalaignar had highlighted an important matter; that the way he continued to work at his age was astonishing; and asked why other Tamil Nadu leaders had not done the same.

Nidarshan, United States, wrote that if the names of 89 Tamil towns were changed into Sinhala, perhaps even “Amma” would have to be called “Amme” in Sinhala. He praised Kalaignar for at least writing a letter and asked where the others were, whether they would not unite even on the Eelam issue, and whether there would ever be a dawn for them.

Nila Sudhan, a former fighter in Eelam, wrote that the place long known in Batticaloa as Kudumi Malai had been changed in Sinhala to “Thoppigala,” and that even Tamil media had begun using the Sinhala name. He said it was fortunate that Kalaignar had taken up the issue and expressed hope that if something came from his effort, they might live there in peace.

Victor, Canada, wrote that Eelam Tamils could never forget that Kalaignar had twice lost power over the Eelam issue, had borne losses and sorrow, and still continued to raise his voice for them with affection.

N. Chitrarasu, Germany, wrote that Kalaignar's actions on the Eelam issue gave them comfort. He recalled that Kalaignar sent DMK Treasurer M.K. Stalin, T.R. Baalu and advocate K.S. Radhakrishnan to the United Nations, the U.N. Human Rights Commission and a conference in Britain to speak for them, and that Kanimozhi raised their cause in the Indian Parliament. “Let what has happened remain what has happened; let what is to happen be good,” he wrote, adding that Kalaignar's initiatives had given them hope of rehabilitation.

Many more such letters continue to arrive. I have mentioned only some of the first ones here.

I am not reproducing these messages for my own glory. I cite only a few to show the current thinking of our Tamil relatives across the world. Our friend Kuganathan Kuganesan, known as Kannan, wrote from London that he had received hundreds of such emails and could attach only a few of them.

We expect the March session of the U.N. Human Rights Council in Geneva to discuss the Sri Lankan Tamil issue in detail. We also expect strong condemnation of the Sri Lankan Government from countries across the world. At that time, the wish and request of Tamils living in Tamil Nadu is that India take a firm stand in support of Sri Lankan Tamils.

It is to discuss all these matters in detail that a consultative meeting of TESO members will be held at Anna Arivalayam on the evening of 4 February. We shall discuss the various issues raised in this letter and take good decisions so that good light may spread in the lives of Sri Lankan Tamils who are wilting in a storm of suffering.

With affection,
M.K.
26-1-2013

கே.எஸ். இராதா கிருஷ்ணன் அவர்களையும் அனுப்பி பிரிட்டன்
மாநாட்டில் கலந்துகொண்டு, எங்களுக்காகக் குரல் கொடுக்கச்
சொன்னார். இந்திய நாடாளுமன்றத்தில் கவிஞர் கனிமொழி
அவர்களும் எங்களுக்காகக் குரல் கொடுத்ததை மறக்க
முடியாது. நடந்தவை நடந்தவையாக இருக்கட்டும், நடக்கப்
போவது நல்லவையாக இருக்கட்டும். கலைஞர் அய்யாவின்
முன்னெடுப்புகளால் எங்களுக்கு மறுவாழ்வு கிடைக்கும் என்ற
நம்பிக்கை ஏற்பட்டுள்ளது.

இதுபோன்ற ஏராளமான கடிதங்கள் வந்து கொண்டே
இருக்கின்றன. முதலில் வந்ததை மட்டும் இங்கே
குறிப்பிட்டுள்ளேன்.

இந்தச் செய்திகளை எல்லாம் ஏதோ என் பெருமைக்காக
நான் இங்கே எழுதிடவில்லை. உலகம் முழுவதிலும் உள்ள
நம்முடைய தமிழ்ச் சொந்தங்களின் எண்ணவோட்டம் எவ்வாறு
உள்ளது என்பதைக் குறிப்பிட்டுத்தான் சிலவற்றை மட்டும்
எடுத்துக்காட்டியிருக்கிறேன். இப்படிச் சுமார் நூற்றுக்கணக்கான
மின்னஞ்சல்கள் தமக்குக் கிடைத்துள்ளதாகவும், ஆனால் அவை
அனைத்தையும் இங்கே இணைக்க முடியாது என்பதால் சிலவற்றை
மட்டும் இணைத்துள்ளோம் என்றும் இலண்டனிலிருந்து நண்பர்
குகதாசன் குகநேசன் என்கிற கண்ணன் அனுப்பியிருக்கிறார்.

வரும் மார்ச் திங்களில் ஜெனிவாவில் நடைபெறவிருக்கும்
ஐ.நா. மனித உரிமை சபையின் கூட்டத் தொடர் இலங்கைத்
தமிழர் பிரச்சினை பற்றி விரிவாக விவாதிக்கும் என்ற நம்பிக்கை
நமக்கு உள்ளது. உலக நாடுகளின் கடுமையான கண்டனங்கள்
இலங்கை அரசுக்கு எதிராக அப்போது தெரிவிக்கப்படும் என்றும்
எதிர்பார்க்கப்படுகிறது. அந்த நேரத்தில் நம்முடைய இந்தியா,
இலங்கைத் தமிழர்களுக்கு ஆதரவாக உறுதியான நிலைப்
பாட்டினை மேற்கொள்ள வேண்டுமென்பதே தமிழகத்தில் வாழும்
தமிழர்களின் விருப்பமும் வேண்டுகோளுமாகும்.

இதைப் பற்றியெல்லாம் விரிவாக விவாதித்திடத்தான்
பெப்ரவரி திங்கள் 4ஆம் நாள் அன்று மாலையில் அண்ணா
அறிவாலயத்தில் “டெசோ” உறுப்பினர்களின் கலந்துரையாடல்
கூட்டம் நடைபெறவுள்ளது. இந்த மடலில் நான் எழுதியுள்ள
பல்வேறு பிரச்சினைகள் குறித்தும் அந்தக் கூட்டத்தில் விவாதித்து;
துன்பச் சூறாவளியில் துவண்டு கொண்டிருக்கும் இலங்கைத்
தமிழர்களின் வாழ்வில் நல்லொளி பரவிட நல்ல முடிவுகளை
எடுப்போம்!
