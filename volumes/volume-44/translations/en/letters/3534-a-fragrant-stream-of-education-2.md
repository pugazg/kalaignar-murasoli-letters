# 3534. A Fragrant Stream of Education - 2

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation follows the audited canonical Tamil and the locked Volume 44 conventions. It preserves the complete school-education achievement inventory, including teacher recruitment, school upgrades, infrastructure, inclusive education, computerisation, student incentives and Rashtriya Madhyamik Shiksha-style source headings without importing outside programme terminology. All source figures and the printed continuation marker are retained in source order. The complete audited Tamil is reproduced below and remains authoritative.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3534](../../../chapters/3534-kamazhum-kalvi-neerodai-2.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 370](../../../pages/page-370.md)–[PDF 380](../../../pages/page-380.md)  
**Date:** 9 March 2011

**Udanpirappē,**

In keeping with the *Naladiyar* lines saying that the beauty of hair, ornamented clothing and turmeric is not true beauty, and that **the beauty of education, arising from the inner sense that goodness is ours, is beauty indeed**, here is a summary of the achievements of our DMK government in the School Education Department.

The allocation for School Education during the AIADMK government in **2005–2006** was **₹4,348 crore 1 lakh**.

During the DMK government in **2010–2011**, the School Education allocation was **₹10,147 crore 56 lakh**. In the interim budget for **2011–2012**, **₹12,674 crore** was allocated to School Education.

To establish schools near habitations and ensure **100 per cent enrolment**:

- **569 new primary schools** were started;
- **2,626 primary schools** were upgraded as middle schools;
- **645 middle schools** were upgraded as high schools;
- **570 high schools** were upgraded as higher secondary schools; and
- more recently, orders were issued upgrading another **344 middle schools** as high schools.

During the five-year AIADMK government from **2001–2006**, **45,987 teachers** were appointed on consolidated pay, receiving roughly one-third of the salary they should have received. The DMK government that assumed office in 2006 placed all those **45,987 teachers** on time-scale pay from **1-6-2006**, enabling each to receive roughly three times the pay they had been drawing.

After 2006, direct recruitment included:

### Elementary Education Department

- **12,426 Secondary Grade Teachers**;
- **14,115 Graduate Teachers**;
- total: **26,541 teachers**.

### School Education Department

- **70 Secondary Grade Teachers**;
- **17,045 Graduate Teachers**;
- **4,665 Teacher Trainers**;
- **3,002 Postgraduate Teachers**;
- **525 Tamil Teachers**;
- **1,131 Special Teachers**;
- **140 Vocational Teachers**;
- **1,686 Computer Teachers**;
- total: **27,739 teachers**.

### District Institutes of Education and Training

- **216 Lecturers**;
- **32 Senior Lecturers**.

Thus, the source gives a total of **55,053 teachers** newly appointed through direct recruitment.

In addition, **1,140 non-teaching staff** and **449 staff on compassionate grounds** were appointed.

Apart from these, **176 agricultural instructors** were appointed to teach agriculture in government higher secondary schools.

For **1,400 primary schools** with more than 70 children, third-teacher posts were sanctioned under the Government Order dated **12-9-2009**, and appointments were made.

Among teachers appointed from 2006 through 2010, **1,114 differently abled persons** with teacher-education qualifications were appointed as teachers in government schools on a priority basis.

For minority and non-minority schools recognised by the government and functioning up to May 1999, **11,307 teacher posts and 648 non-teaching posts** were sanctioned in **February 2011**, at an annual expenditure of **₹331 crore**.

The letter records that this order was welcomed by Christian-community representatives and others led by MLAs **Peter Alphonse** and **M. Appavu**, including Tamil Nadu Christian Goodwill Movement president **Inigo Irudayaraj**, ECI Bishop **Ezra Sargunam**, Chennai-Mylapore Archbishop **A. M. Chinnappa**, Tamil Nadu Minorities Welfare Commission chairman **Vincent Chinnadurai**, Tamil Nadu Minorities Development Corporation chairman **Xavier Arulraj** and others, who met me at the Secretariat, thanked the government and emotionally expressed continuing support for the DMK government.

Graduates in Economics and Commerce who had also obtained B.Ed. degrees were given promotions as middle-school headmasters and Graduate Teachers.

All teacher transfers were conducted only through transparent counselling, which the letter says was welcomed by the teaching community.

To implement education schemes effectively for differently abled students, a new **Deputy Director post at Chief Educational Officer level** was created in the Directorate of School Education.

Personal Assistant posts for **31 Chief Educational Officers** were created at higher-secondary-school headmaster level.

For the first time in the history of the School Education Department, **67 Assistant Elementary Educational Officer posts** were filled in **2009–2010** by direct recruitment through the Tamil Nadu Public Service Commission.

A District Educational Office with Kumbakonam as headquarters was established in **2009** in Thanjavur district, and another new District Educational Office was established at Srivilliputhur in Virudhunagar district in **2010**.

Separate Chief Educational Offices and Elementary Educational Offices were created for the newly formed **Ariyalur and Tiruppur districts**.

A separate District Institute of Education and Training for Dharmapuri district was started in **2007**.

## Government payment of electricity charges for Panchayat Union schools

An order was issued on **4-7-2008** for the Education Department to pay electricity charges for Panchayat Union schools. Accordingly, amounts sanctioned were:

- **2008–2009:** ₹2 crore 97 lakh 8 thousand;
- **2009–2010:** ₹2 crore 56 lakh 17 thousand;
- **2010–2011:** ₹2 crore 50 lakh.

Maintenance works were carried out in **18,299 primary and middle schools** at a cost of **₹2 crore 35 lakh 37 thousand**.

New vehicles were provided to **16 District Elementary Educational Officers** for school inspections.

## Building facilities

Through the Education for All movement in the School Education Department, **30,068 classrooms** were constructed in primary and middle schools at a cost of **₹751 crore 28 lakh**.

With NABARD assistance, at a cost of **₹1,002 crore 25 lakh 81 thousand**, the following were provided in **1,153 high and higher secondary schools**:

- **16,980 additional classrooms** with seating facilities;
- **843 science laboratories**;
- **4,047 toilets**;
- **968 drinking-water schemes**;
- **3 lakh 35 thousand 37 metres** of compound walls.

Buildings were constructed for the District Elementary Educational Offices in Nagapattinam, Cuddalore and Tirunelveli at **₹17 lakh 16 thousand each**, totalling **₹53 lakh 72 thousand**.

New buildings are being constructed for **19 Assistant Elementary Educational Offices** at the locations listed in the source — Pappireddipatti, Avinashi, West Arani, Kalaiyarkoil, Nemili, Senthamangalam, Paramakudi, Sirukaveripakkam, Ponnamaravathi, Karungulam, Manikandam, Thalaivasal, Mulanur, Madurai North, Thoppampatti, Kangeyam, Kariapatti, Veppanthattai and Sethubavachatram — at **₹15 lakh 73 thousand each**, totalling **₹2 crore 98 lakh 87 thousand**.

## School renovation conferences

During the past five years, school renovation conferences were held in **22 districts**, through which movable and immovable assets worth **₹970 crore** were received as public donations for schools.

In **2007–2008**, Activity-Based Learning was introduced in Classes 1 to 4 and later extended through Class 8.

To improve learning under Activity-Based Learning and creative-education methods, **colour television sets and DVD players** were supplied to **36,150 primary and middle schools** at a cost of **₹26 crore**.

Seating facilities were provided to **9 lakh 24 thousand 490 children** in Classes 6, 7 and 8 at a cost of **₹46 crore**.

The DMK government provided five eggs a week with the nutritious meal, free bus passes, abolition of special fees for Tamil-medium students, abolition of tuition fees and abolition of examination fees. The letter says these measures sharply reduced school dropout rates:

- primary education: **12 per cent in 2001–2002 → 1.02 per cent in 2009–2010**;
- middle-school education: **13 per cent → 1.88 per cent**.

Through the Education for All movement, **1,621 bridge centres** operate on school campuses for urban children who have been out of school for more than a year, street children and migrant children, enabling them to study for **6 to 12 months**.

Children studying at these centres receive midday meals, uniforms, footwear, school bags and teaching-learning materials.

For children who have never attended school, dropouts, hill-area children and urban disadvantaged children, **231 residential centres** operate across the districts. Activity-Based Learning is used from Classes 1 to 5 and creative education from Classes 6 to 8.

These centres are run by **177 voluntary organisations and women’s self-help groups**, benefiting **9,454 children**.

Residential centres for children with intellectual disabilities operate in Coimbatore, Salem, Ramanathapuram, Perambalur, Dindigul and Cuddalore districts, benefiting **265 children**.

**2 lakh 94 thousand 182 school dropouts** have been enrolled in regular schools.

## Welfare of differently abled children

To provide quality inclusive education suited to life for differently abled children aged 6 to 14, works costing **₹52 crore 27 lakh** included:

- enrolment of **90,543 differently abled children** in government and alternative schools;
- assistive devices for **75,174 children**;
- surgeries for **4,476 children**;
- home-based education and training for **22,000 severely affected children**;
- special training for all primary and middle-school teachers to provide quality inclusive education;
- special training for parents of differently abled children;
- resource-training centres in **412 blocks**;
- **333 day-care centres**;
- ramps in **1,144 government schools** at a cost of **₹73 lakh**;
- **826 special teachers** and **133 physiotherapy specialists** working in the programme.

To make learning easier through computer-assisted teaching in primary and middle schools, **26,900 laptops** were supplied to **7,500 primary and middle schools** at a cost of **₹142 crore 15 lakh**.

Two computer laboratories were established in every district, and **37,207 teachers** working in middle, high and higher secondary schools received computer training.

A total of **41,110 computers** were supplied to **1,980 government higher secondary schools** and **2,131 government high schools**, at a cost of **₹233 crore 77 lakh 81 thousand**.

From **2009–2010**, a Computer Science textbook was supplied free to all Class 6 students in government high and higher secondary schools. This benefited **5 lakh 75 thousand Tamil-medium students** and **75 thousand English-medium students**.

From the **2008–2009 academic year**, a new scheme awarded laptops to students securing first marks in the Class 10 public examination. During the two years **2008–2009 and 2009–2010**, **2,205 students** received laptops at a cost of **₹4 crore 74 lakh 64 thousand**. The letter says this encourages students to pursue higher education.

From **2009–2010**, incentives for State-level students securing the first three places in Classes 10 and 12 while taking Tamil as the first-language subject were increased:

### Class 12
- first prize: **₹50,000**;
- second: **₹30,000**;
- third: **₹20,000**.

### Class 10
- first prize: **₹25,000**;
- second: **₹20,000**;
- third: **₹15,000**.

## Directorate of Matriculation Schools

### Free Tamil textbooks

From **2007–2008**, students in Classes 1 to 12 in Matriculation / Matriculation Higher Secondary Schools have been given free Tamil textbooks at an annual cost of approximately **₹2 crore 25 lakh**.

### Strengthening the Directorate

A new Joint Director post was created to strengthen the Directorate of Matriculation Schools. An officer was appointed and powers appropriate to the post were delegated by Government Order dated **16-12-2008**.

Following completion of the Education for All movement’s work, the **Education for All at the secondary level scheme**, as named in the source, has been implemented from **2009–2010**.

### New high schools

**200 middle schools** were upgraded as high schools, at **₹58 lakh 12 thousand per school**, with a total expenditure printed as **₹1 crore 16 lakh 24 thousand** in the source. The translation preserves that printed total without arithmetic correction.

### School grant

For all government / corporation / municipality / welfare-department high and higher secondary schools in the State, a school grant of **₹40,000 each** was sanctioned to **4,627 schools**, totalling **₹18 crore 50 lakh**. Laboratories and libraries for Classes 9 and 10 were improved, and water, electricity, telephone and internet facilities were provided.

### In-service training for teachers

**15,000 teachers** teaching secondary classes received **five days of in-service training** at a cost of **₹1 crore 50 lakh**.

### Model schools

In **44 educationally backward unions** across **13 districts** — Ariyalur, Cuddalore, Dharmapuri, Erode, Krishnagiri, Karur, Perambalur, Namakkal, Salem, Sivaganga, Tiruppur, Tiruvannamalai and Villupuram — **18 model higher secondary schools** were started in **2010–2011**, one in each of the selected unions, at **₹3 crore per school**, for a total cost of **₹54 crore**.

### Girls’ hostels

In the same 13 districts and 44 educationally backward unions, **44 girls’ hostels** were established, one per union, at a cost of **₹18 crore 70 lakh**.

Recurring expenditure of **₹14 lakh 27 thousand per hostel per year** is to be sanctioned. Each hostel admits **100 girl students**.

கல்வியில் பின்தங்கிய 44 ஒன்றியங்களில் ஒரு ஒன்றியத்திற்கு
ஒரு மாணவியர் விடுதி வீதம் 44 மாணவியர் விடுதிகள் 18
கோடியே 70 இலட்ச ரூபாய் செலவில் ஏற்படுத்தப்பட்டுள்ளன.
இவ்விடுதிகளுக்குத் தொடர் செலவினமாக ஆண்டு

கொள்ளப்பட்டுள்ளனர்.
