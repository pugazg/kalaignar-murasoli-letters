# 3372. This Is Always Your Life!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This letter combines autobiographical reflection, recovery from spinal surgery, a contemporaneous statement on the Madras High Court conflict and a declaration that the author’s life belongs to the Tamil people. Political and legal claims are preserved as the author’s source framing. The complete audited Tamil is reproduced below and remains authoritative.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3372](../../../chapters/3372-idhu-eppodhum-ungal-uyir.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 66](../../../pages/page-066.md)–[PDF 69](../../../pages/page-069.md)  
**Date:** 1 March 2009

**Udanpirappē,**

I wonder how deeply Arignar Anna, who gave us the historic saying, “The law is a dark room; the lawyer’s argument is the lamp that gives light within it,” would have been pained if he were alive today to see the undesirable events now taking place.

Father Periyar, that world-renowned warrior, conducted countless struggles to spread the principles of the Self-Respect Movement and to surround and oppose actions contrary to self-respect.

Gandhiji, the pure-hearted one who led civil-disobedience struggles against British rule, issued a statement calling for the Chauri Chaura agitation to be brought to an end. That statement continues to shine before the world as an explanation of his path of righteousness.

Such great leaders, honoured by the people in Tamil Nadu and across India and honoured even today, must forever remain guides to the youth of today and of the future — through their writings, their thoughts and their actions. Only events worthy of such guidance should endure and become pathways for the advancement of society.

I have continued to work with that thought. Eighty-five years ago I was born in Thirukkuvalai, a small village in the Thanjavur region, also known as Thirukkolili, praised in the hymns of the Thevaram and Thiruvasagam, into an ordinary, humble family of musicians. Even in my childhood, the sweat of agricultural labourers who were beaten down by the local landlords, and the tears shed by their eyes, shaped me, moulded me into a figure, wrapped me in the red flag and placed me in the hands of Periyar, the lion of thought.

It placed me under the embrace of Anna, the treasure of classical Tamil.

The shower of affection from elder brother Pattukkottai Azhagiri became a shower of praise and made me Kalaignar.

My affection for Annal Jeeva filled my heart. Even after he passed away, that affection did not pass away.

Turning all these things over in my mind, I am now returning, as though I have taken another birth, after recovering from what is described as a dangerous operation. I offer my thanks and blessings to the medical friends who gave me life again: the Ramachandra Hospital team under orthopaedic specialist Dr. S. S. K. Marthandam; Dr. Arvind A. Jaiswal of Delhi; Dr. S. Thanikachalam; Dr. Mayilvaganan; Dr. Karthik Kailash; Dr. I. S. Naidu; Dr. Mahesh Wakamudi; Dr. Bhaskar; Dr. P. S. Shanmugam; Dr. Raj P. Singh; Dr. A. V. Srinivasan; Dr. Soundararajan; Dr. K. R. Palanisamy; Dr. P. Gopal; and to all my close friends and Udanpirappugal.

Yet because I bear the responsibility of governing the State, even in this physical condition there are those who keep pricking my chest with needles, as though determined to trouble me. Thinking of them, I wonder whether they possess something called a heart at all — or, as Anna said, whether there is a pit where the heart ought to be. With such thoughts surging tonight in Ramachandra Hospital, one small wave — indeed, an angry wave — is the following statement I issued last night, 27.2.2009, concerning the events at the Madras High Court:

> “At the Madras High Court, lawyers began what they called a peaceful struggle for the protection of Sri Lankan Tamils. Then came a case that lawyers had thrown eggs at Mr. Subramanian Swamy inside that court. The matter grew into violence in the form of clashes between police and lawyers, and finally the issue went all the way to the Supreme Court.
>
> “There was a demand that the DMK government be dismissed. The Supreme Court threw that demand out.
>
> “In accordance with the Supreme Court’s order, this government has transferred certain police officers from Chennai to other places.
>
> “What more?
>
> “The Supreme Court has announced the commission headed by retired Supreme Court Justice Srikrishna. The government is waiting for the commission’s advice.
>
> “Can the lawyers alone not show patience in this matter?
>
> “The government is also allocating funds so that compensation, as specified, may be given and received by both sides.
>
> “When the government says it is ready to implement all these Supreme Court orders, is it not proper that all lawyers too accept that Supreme Court view and return to the courts on March 2? Otherwise, are we not forced to conclude that this is not a struggle over the Sri Lankan Tamil issue, but a struggle against the DMK government here?”

**Udanpirappē,**

The Financial Statement of our government, read in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly on 17.2.2009 by Finance Minister Professor, has immersed every section — the poor and humble, ordinary people, farmers, working labourers, traders, government employees and teachers — in a sea of happiness. Thinking of this again and again fills me with pride.

In the mirror of my thoughts, some political leaders who once shone as colourful new figures, some who once appeared as warriors of principle, now seem to have abandoned assessment, performance, character and civility, and appear even in my dreams like Gandharis roaring, “Not only the government — what if he himself were gone?”

They treat my tears and worries as trivial.

As long as I live, I wish to do something for the poor and destitute, for the people at the bottom who have been reduced to insects and lowly creatures — and to do it in a way that enables them to live and to gain self-respect. As long as there is a Tamil sky, the song I sing beneath it for the Tamil people must continue to sound. Even if I am no more, it must continue to sound. I will live holding that thought.

I am eighty-five now!

Though the life gained through this surgery may be written as “my life” —

dear Tamils,  
my beloved Udanpirappugal,  
then,  
now,  
and forever,  
this is your life!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**1-3-2009**

## Letter-specific notes

1. The quoted statement on the Madras High Court conflict is translated as the author’s contemporaneous political/legal framing; no external adjudication is imported.
2. The closing declaration deliberately turns “my life” into “your life”; that rhetorical ownership is preserved.
3. The autobiographical references to Periyar, Anna, Jeeva and Pattukkottai Azhagiri are translated without added historical explanation.
4. The source’s heart/pit image echoes Letter 3366 and is retained.

நிதி நிலை அறிக்கை - ஏழையெளியோர், சாதாரண, சாமான்ய மக்கள், உழவர்கள், உழைக்கும் பாட்டாளி மக்கள், வணிகர்கள், அரசு அலுவலர்கள், ஆசிரியர்கள் என எல்லாத் தரப்பினரையும் மகிழ்ச்சிக் கடலில் ஆழ்த்தியிருப்பதை எண்ணியெண்ணி இறும்பூது எய்துகின்றேன்.

என் எண்ணக் கண்ணாடியில் - வண்ணப் புதுமையாக - ஜொலித்துக் கொண்டிருந்த அரசியல் தலைவர்களில் ஒரு சிலர், கொள்கை வீரர்களில் ஒரு சிலர் - மதிப்பாய்வு வெளிச்சம் காட்டி விட்டு இப்போது நடிப்புக்கு - பாத்திரத்திற்கு - நாகரிகத்திற்கு விடை கொடுத்து விட்டு; “ஆட்சி மட்டுமல்ல, அவனே போனால்தான் என்ன” எனக் கர்ச்சிக்கும் காந்தாரிகளாக அல்லவா என் கனவிலும் வந்து காட்சி தருகிறார்கள்.

என் கண்ணீரை - கவலையை - துச்சமாகக் கருதுகிறார்கள்.

இருக்கும் வரையில் ஏழை பாழைகளுக்கு - பொட்டுப் பூச்சிகளாய், புன்மைத் தேரைகளாய் ஆக்கப்பட்டுள்ள அடித்தட்டு மக்களுக்கு - எதையாவது செய்ய வேண்டும், அதையும் அவர்களை வாழ வைக்கும் வண்ணம் செய்ய வேண்டும், தன்மானம் பெறும் வண்ணம் செய்ய வேண்டும், தமிழ் வானம் இருக்கு மட்டும் - அதில் தமிழ் மக்களுக்காக நான் பாடும் கானம் - ஒலித்துக் கொண்டே இருக்க வேண்டும், நான் இல்லாவிட்டாலும், அது ஒலித்துக் கொண்டே இருக்கும் என நினைத்துக் கொண்டே வாழ்வேன்.

அகவை 85 இப்போது!
