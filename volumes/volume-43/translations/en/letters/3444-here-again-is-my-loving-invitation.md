# 3444. Here Again Is My Loving Invitation!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> The letter is an open invitation to participate in the World Classical Tamil Conference and includes the full exchange of letters between the author and *Dinamani* editor K. Vaidyanathan. The source’s affectionate political-literary register and the chariot-rope metaphor are retained.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3444](../../../chapters/3444-meendum-itho-en-anbazhaippu.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 143](../../../pages/page-143.md)–[PDF 148](../../../pages/page-148.md)  
**Date:** 21 December 2009

**Udanpirappē,**

The World Classical Tamil Conference is to be held in Coimbatore city from 23 to 27 June 2010. To attend to the arrangements for conducting it with order and distinction, 21 committees have been constituted with scholars, elders, intellectuals, Tamil enthusiasts, spiritual thinkers and people from the world of arts.

They are the Conference Leadership Committee, Conference Advisory Committee, Conference Special Souvenir Committee, Conference Research-Seminar Organisation Committee, Conference Arrangements Supervisory Committee, Reception Committee, Conference Venue Arrangement Committee, Public-Hall Programmes Committee, Procession Committee, Hospitality Committee, Tamil Internet Conference Committee, Conference Coordination Committee, Exhibition Organisation Committee, Arts Programmes and Tourism Committee, Accommodation Arrangements Committee, Public Relations and Publicity Committee, Coimbatore City Development Committee, Medical and Health Committee, Transport Arrangements Committee, Security Arrangements and Rescue-Work Committee, and Research-Seminar Organisation Assistance Committee. Their constitution has been announced through the newspapers, and all these committees are about to begin their respective work.

As far as my memory allowed, I have included many people as members of these committees. Apart from them, there may be many others of equal ability, and it may have been impossible to include everyone; one or two names might therefore have been left out. I request that they need not take this wrongly.

Some who have been placed in one committee may even feel that they could have worked more effectively in another. What is to be held is a conference for our classical language. Keeping in mind that everyone has a part in it, I invite everyone, with a sense of right, to undertake service for Tamil.

Two days ago, younger brother and poet Pa. Vijay met me. He had many drawings and several books in his hands. Because there was little time, he showed them to me in great haste. He had himself engaged an artist to prepare drawings of how the conference frontage might look, and he also showed me several pictures suggesting possible cover designs for the conference special souvenir. On seeing all that, I was astonished. I immediately decided that he should be involved in conference work.

Some people abroad have already begun making preparations to participate and are contacting us by telephone and letters, saying that they are eagerly waiting for June to arrive.

There is another experience of mine connected with this list. Although the *Dinamani* newspaper often writes critically about our Kazhagam and the DMK government, it generally remains within a limit. At times it has not hesitated even to write editorials praising the work of the DMK government, and we have reproduced such editorials in *Murasoli* too. In the matter of press freedom, it has every right. I myself have enjoyed caricatures published attacking me on many occasions.

I have a special affection for the editor of that newspaper, Mr K. Vaidyanathan. Though he may hold views different from mine in political policy, I know that he has deep involvement in matters concerning Tamil, Tamils and Sri Lankan Tamils. I continually read the literary criticism he writes each week in *Dinamani* under the pen name “Kalarasikan.” I know very well that Vaidyanathan is among those who, like me, have wished that a World Tamil Conference should be held.

For that reason I felt that he should have a place in the World Classical Tamil Conference. Not only he: I wished Mr N. Ram of *The Hindu*, Mr Sivanthi Adithan of *Dina Thanthi*, younger brother Kalanithi Maran of the Sun Television group, Mr Krishnamurthy of *Dinamalar* — who is to be included in a research-seminar explanatory committee yet to be announced — Mr Ramachandra Adithan of *Maalai Murasu*, Mr Gopanna of *Desiya Murasu*, Gopal of *Nakkheeran*, and Ramesh Prabha of Kalaignar Television also to be included in these committees, and accordingly I had them included.

Meanwhile, *Dinamani* editor Mr K. Vaidyanathan had written me a letter dated 16 December. Thinking that he would not take it amiss if I published it, I reproduce it here. The letter reads:

> **To my most respected Chief Minister,**
>
> Affectionate greetings.
>
> I know well the affection and regard that you have for me in the depths of your heart. Nor am I unaware that you have enough concern and trust in me to take a certain right over me.
>
> From the days forty-five years ago, when as a schoolboy in half-trousers I sat in the front row at Madurai Tilakar Grounds to hear your speech, until today, I have stood enthralled by your writing and your love of Tamil, like a snake held spellbound by the magudi.
>
> My enthusiasm and urgency that a World Tamil Conference should be held are by no means small. You know how much anguish I felt that no World Tamil Conference had been held during your tenure. Many times I have suffered at the thought that, if this conference were not held, despite your boundless attachment and affection for Tamil, an indelible blemish like a stain on the moon would remain upon your fame.
>
> The World Classical Tamil Conference is also my dream. That it should be held with distinction while you are Chief Minister is a matter of pride for Tamil, for the Tamil people and for Tamil Nadu.
>
> No one can know and understand me better than you. More than saying that I was happy to be made a member of the Public Relations and Publicity Committee of the World Classical Tamil Conference in a manner that brought honour to me, it is true to say that I was moved.
>
> Whatever work I take up, I involve myself in it wholeheartedly, like a horse with blinkers. At present, my thoughts, actions and writing are centred entirely on *Dinamani*. I cannot think about or act upon anything else. I therefore wish, with regret, to tell you that I am not in a position to make use of the opportunity given to me. Please do not think that I am breaking your trust or disobeying an affectionate command.
>
> *Dinamani* will certainly extend its full cooperation and contribution to the World Classical Tamil Conference. Dr M. Rajendran, Vice-Chancellor of the Tamil University at Thanjavur and my dear friend, and I often exchange views about it. Let there be not even the slightest doubt that my constructive contribution to the success of the conference will continue even without my being a member of the committee.
>
> The World Classical Tamil Conference is not your dream alone; it is our dream too. I need not stress to you that in that service to Tamil, instead of sitting on the dais as a member, “your Vaidyanathan will be there” as a Tamil enthusiast making his contribution.
>
> Thank you. Greetings.
>
> Ever affectionately,  
> **K. Vaidyanathan**

Udanpirappē, when I saw this letter, I did not think, “He has refused to be in the committee we announced.” Within a few minutes of receiving it, I wrote him a reply. Here is that reply:

> **Dear Sir,**
>
> Greetings. I have seen your emotion-filled letter. Though outward politics may present one appearance, I am greatly moved to see the feeling mixed in the inner light and the firmness of our relationship.
>
> In the effort for the World Classical Tamil Conference, it is enough if people like you, whose attachment to the Tamil people never changes course, even if not among those pulling the chariot, are among those who come holding its rope. It was with that thought that your name was included in the announcement of the committees. I again request you to accept without refusing and help add distinction to the conference.
>
> With affection,  
> **M. Karunanidhi**

Within a few hours of sending my letter, I received a good reply from Mr Vaidyanathan. That reply follows:

> **To the most respected Chief Minister,**
>
> Affectionate greetings. I received the letter you wrote following mine. I am greatly delighted by the affection and trust you have shown toward me. How can I continue to refuse your request?
>
> Even so, I believe and wish that, rather than in outward activities, I can work wholeheartedly and enthusiastically in more inward fields such as research seminars, research compilations and relations with international Tamil scholars.
>
> With the request that you accept my view and make the necessary arrangements,
>
> Thanks and greetings.
>
> Ever affectionately,  
> **K. Vaidyanathan**

Udanpirappē, having accepted my request, he who at first declined to come will, like others who seek to add strength to flourishing Tamil, join together with a joyful heart and rejoice as the classical-language flag is raised in Coimbatore. When we Tamils across the world have the rare opportunity to experience a delight like tasting inexhaustible nectar mixed with honey, even the absence of one or two people feels to me like a deficiency. That is why I have published this correspondence reflecting that concern.

If there are others with a full heart like his who can remove such a deficiency, my loving invitation once again extends to them too.

What is to be held is the World Classical Tamil Conference. Every person born a Tamil has a right in it. Therefore, without thinking that your names do not appear in one of the various committees that have been constituted, I request each of you to take responsibility and work for the conference’s success.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**21-12-2009**

## Letter-specific notes

1. The complete Vaidyanathan–Karunanidhi correspondence is retained because the letter’s argument is built through that exchange.
2. `தேரிழுப்போர்களில் ... தேரின் வடத்தைத் தாங்கிக் கொண்டு` is rendered through the source’s chariot-and-rope image rather than reduced to generic “participation.”
3. Newspaper, television and committee names are kept in source order without supplementing their historical roles from outside knowledge.
