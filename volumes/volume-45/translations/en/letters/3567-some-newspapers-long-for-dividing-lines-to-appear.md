# 3567. Some Newspapers Yearn: Won't Dividing Lines Appear?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Source pages:** [PDF 231](../../../pages/page-231.md)–[PDF 235](../../../pages/page-235.md)  
**Date:** 14 June 2011

**Udanpirappē,**

On the evening of **10.6.2011**, a meeting of the members of the DMK High-Level Action Plan Committee was held at the Murasoli Maran complex in Anna Arivalayam. Copies of the ten resolutions passed at that meeting were made and immediately distributed to journalists through the party's Organisation Secretary.

As I was leaving after the meeting, I was told that journalists were waiting and was asked whether I would meet them. I asked in return: “You have already given them the resolutions, haven't you? Then what for?” When I was told that the journalists were waiting to ask me some questions, I agreed and went to the press room.

There was twice the usual number of journalists there! When I saw that a few journalists who normally never attended these press meetings had also come, I understood that they had come for some particular purpose.

I said:

> “Copies of the resolutions that were passed have been given to each of you. If you wish to ask anything about those resolutions, or anything connected with them, please ask. I will answer.”

Thereafter **31 questions** were asked at that press meeting, and my answers to each of them appeared in the newspapers. I wish to recall only some of those 31 questions here.

1. Do you think the Union Government is using the **CBI** as a political weapon?

2. When you spoke in Tiruvarur, you said that the case filed against Kanimozhi was a case filed by the Union Government. Why did you not mention that in your resolution?

3. You spoke of a **“bad friendship.”** Whom were you referring to?

4. A majority of cadres in your party want the relationship with the Congress Party not to continue, do they not?

5. Can we expect your General Council to decide whether the DMK's relationship with the Congress Party will continue or not?

6. There is a question among the cadres as to whether the relationship with the Congress Party should continue. What is your answer to that?

7. An official newspaper of the Congress Party has written that the **2G spectrum issue** itself was the reason for the election defeat. What do you say about that?

8. Was the spectrum issue a reason for this defeat, or was it not?

9. They say in Delhi that you are going to join hands with the **BJP**. Is that so?

10. There are reports that **Dayanidhi Maran's** name is going to figure in the CBI's third charge-sheet. What do you say?

11. They are saying that Dayanidhi Maran should resign. What is your view?

12. You did not support Dayanidhi Maran, did you?

13. **Venkaiah Naidu** has said that the Emergency will return. What is your view on that?

14. Can the General Council you are convening in July be taken as a deadline you have set for the Union Government?

15. Would you say that the Congress Party has never respected the DMK?

16. Do you think your **Self-Respect** is being protected within the United Progressive Alliance?

17. Do you feel regret that the Congress Party did not help you in any way in the 2G matter?

18. Your cadres say that the Congress Party is the reason **A. Raja and Kanimozhi** are in prison. What do you say?

19. How has the CBI acted in the 2G case?

20. You have criticised the CBI in your resolution. Should that be taken to mean that you have criticised the Prime Minister himself?

21. Would you say that under no circumstances is there any problem in your alliance with the Congress?

22. **Ghulam Nabi Azad** has said in Delhi that they can do nothing in the 2G case. What do you say?

Udanpirappē, at this point the printed source gives the malformed wording **`பொத்தம் 31 கேள்விகளில் 22 1 கேள்விகள்`**—literally presenting the count as **“22 1 questions”** out of 31—before continuing with the argument: were these not questions asked with the intention of somehow breaking the relationship between the Congress Party and the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam, getting me to say something criticising the Congress Party, magnifying it, and creating a quarrel between the two?

Why this concern, this haste, this eagerness among journalists who are supposed to gather news without likes or dislikes, with neutrality, and carry it to the people without adulteration? Had one or two questions been asked on this subject, we could have understood the purpose behind them. But when **22 out of 31 questions** were of a kind that could sow discord between the Congress Party and the DMK, does it not make one wonder whether journalists themselves should engage in conduct that undermines press ethics?

Ten resolutions had been passed by the High-Level Action Plan Committee and copies of all those resolutions had been given to all the journalists. Yet without asking even a single question about those resolutions, a few particular journalists—almost as if they had planned, discussed and decided it beforehand—raised question after question only about the DMK–Congress relationship. Think, then, about the kind of environment in which we will have to function in the future!

During the period of DMK government, whether it was the Governor's Address or the Budget, each time those documents were prepared I made sure that the welfare of journalists found a place in them. Not only that: because I wanted journalists to live in homes of their own, I issued orders allotting low-cost house plots to them in Chennai and other cities. Having worked to this extent for the welfare of journalists, I felt only sorrow that they should now deal with us with such a different state of mind and disposition.

But those who asked these questions were not all the more than fifty journalists seated at the press meeting. In particular, **five or six people**, instead of sitting together, sat at different places and took turns asking all these questions. The other journalists seated there would surely have understood both what those five or six were doing and how I answered them. Yet all they could do was listen quietly to those irritation-spitting questions; they could do nothing else.

Nor did it stop with questions at the press meeting. In the **Dinamani** dated **12-6-2011**, there was a large article whose very title was **“Why Is the DMK Hesitating?”** It wrote that Karunanidhi was prepared to break relations with the Congress, but that the Members of Parliament and Union Minister **M.K. Azhagiri** wanted to postpone the alliance break. See the extent to which efforts are being made to break this alliance.

In the **Dinamalar** of the same 12th, another large article appeared under the title **“Cadres Shocked by Karunanidhi's Decision.”** At the end of that article it was written: **“DMK cadres have begun to lament that the situation has become so bad that it seems they are expected to endure however much they are beaten.”**

No DMK cadres are lamenting.

It is the newspapers **Dinamani** and **Dinamalar** that are crying out and lamenting because of the DMK's decision. They write articles because they are disappointed that their wish has not been fulfilled. At press meetings they raise question after question. Poor fellows—what can they do? They do what their proprietors tell them to ask and to write.

Why do their proprietors tell them to do so? Only then will they receive the **“kozhukattai” called advertisements**. Their lives will prosper.

**Long live Manu Dharma—Long live Press Dharma!**

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**14-06-2011**

## Letter-specific notes

1. This letter deliberately repeats the 10-6-2011 press-meeting material also printed in Letter 3565. The repetition is source text and is translated in full rather than deduplicated.
2. PDF 233 directly preserves the anomalous printed form `பொத்தம் 31 கேள்விகளில் 22 1 கேள்விகள்`. The English explicitly exposes that malformed count instead of silently repairing the Tamil; the subsequent source sentence itself returns to `31 கேள்விகளில் 22 கேள்விகள்` on PDF 234.
3. The title's `கோடுகள்` is rendered as **dividing lines** in the context of the letter's repeated claim that some newspapers sought a rupture between the DMK and Congress. The closing `மனு தர்மம்` / `பத்திரிகா தர்மம்` parallel is retained as **Manu Dharma / Press Dharma**.
