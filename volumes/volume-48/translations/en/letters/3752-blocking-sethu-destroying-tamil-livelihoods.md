# 3752. A Roadblock for “Sethu”! An Act That Will Ruin Tamil Livelihoods!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 10 May 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

In the judgment delivered on 6 May 2013 by the Supreme Court Bench comprising Justices K.S. Radhakrishnan and Dipak Misra on permitting the Kudankulam Nuclear Power Plant to function, the Court is quoted in the source as saying that the petitioners claimed their right to life would be taken away if the nuclear plant operated, but that nuclear power plants were not established to take away the right to life; they were intended to secure the conditions for life. The judgment, as cited here, said that projects of this kind should receive importance in the public interest and in order to guarantee the right to life. If that is kept in mind, the Sethu Samudram project too is a project intended to secure people's means of living and is, moreover, wholly directed towards public benefit.

When the inauguration of the Sethu Samudram project was held in Madurai on 2 July 2005, the then Chief Minister Jayalalithaa said in a lengthy statement:

> “It was on my insistence that in 1998 this project was taken up as a priority project, and the preliminary Environmental Impact Assessment study was entrusted to the National Environmental Engineering Research Institute in March 1998. The Institute's report was also submitted in August 1998. In this way, I have been an important reason for ensuring that the Sethu Samudram Canal Project became a reality.”

In other words, she claimed as though she herself had been responsible for the Sethu Samudram project coming into being and sought credit for it at that time. The amusing contradiction is that the same person has now gone to the Supreme Court saying that the very same project should not proceed.

Jayalalithaa said in her statement that the report was obtained from the National Environmental Engineering Research Institute in 1998 because of her insistence. She appears not to know what happened after that. Since the report then given was considered insufficient, the Tuticorin Port Trust once again asked the same institution for a detailed report, and a more elaborate report was obtained from it.

When litigation over the project arose in November 2004 at the instigation of “some people,” and the National Environmental Engineering Research Institute was asked whether the report it had submitted was a detailed one, the institution categorically stated that it had filed that detailed report only after conducting a complete study. This firm clarification itself shows, the author argues, how completely contrary to the truth was Jayalalithaa's claim that the project had been taken forward without proper study.

Jayalalithaa had also said in that lengthy statement that even though the rules framed by the Union Ministry of Environment and Forests required the Ministry to obtain a no-objection certificate from the State Government, the Union Ministry had abruptly granted environmental clearance for the project without waiting for Tamil Nadu's no-objection certificate.

The source then reproduces the following English passage exactly:

> “N.O.C. from the State Government or Tamilnadu Pollution Control Board is not a statutory requirement for consideration of environmental clearance for a project for which such clearance is to be accorded by the Central Government as per the Environmental Impact Assessment Notification dated 27-1-1994. Neither the notification, which has the force of law, nor Schedule IV which is appended to it and lays down the procedure for public hearing stipulates such No Objection Certificate”

The Tamil parenthetical explanation that follows says that, under the environmental rules dated 27 January 1994, a no-objection certificate from the State Pollution Control Board is not a statutory requirement for a project whose environmental clearance is to be granted by the Union Government, and that neither the legally enforceable notification nor its appended Schedule IV governing public hearings stipulates such a certificate.

In the case filed by O. Fernandes opposing the Sethu Samudram project, the source quotes an important portion of the judgment delivered on 17 December 2004 by Madras High Court Chief Justice Markandey Katju and Justice V. Balasubramanian. It says that the petitioner had rushed to court seeking to stop a project brought forward in the national interest; that it was well known that the Sethu Samudram Canal Project could bring major benefits to the country because ships then had to sail around Sri Lanka to reach the Bay of Bengal; that a shipping canal across the Palk Strait could save substantial money and time and reduce fuel costs for shipping companies; that it could aid trade and improve transport and development around Tuticorin port and coastal towns; and that, like the Suez Canal, it could avoid a long circuitous route.

The cited judgment further says that district collectors had already held public inquiries and sent reports; that, in the Court's view, the Sethu Samudram Canal Project should be enabled to reach early completion; and that scientific and technological progress in the country should not be obstructed merely by invoking environmental protection.

Using the name of a government to oppose and obstruct the Sethu Samudram project on the basis of personal likes and dislikes is, the author argues, an act that damages the lives and progress of the people. Was it the Samacheer Kalvi scheme? Was it a scheme begun under DMK rule? Then it must not be allowed. Was it the Tamil Nadu Secretariat in the Omandurar complex, a building constructed under DMK rule at a cost of more than ₹600 crore? That too must not continue. If they say a hospital will instead be established there, Karunanidhi personally loses nothing. But if the Secretariat and Legislative Assembly had continued to function there, people might have spoken of who built it and under which government. What, then, will people passing that building say now after it has been kept unused for two years despite having been built from the taxes they paid?

The Chief Minister speaks as though she alone cares about the police. Yet how many months have passed since a multi-storey building costing ₹100 crore was completed in Chennai for the office of the Police Commissioner? Because it was built under DMK rule, it has been left without an inauguration. What loss does Karunanidhi suffer from that? Police personnel who see it, the author imagines, will say that although ₹100 crore was spent to build it, “this lady has no heart to open it.”

The same is true, he says, of the Maduravoyal–Port elevated-road project. Because the Prime Minister participated in laying its foundation stone during DMK rule, that project too has been obstructed after many crores had already been spent and is lying stalled. What will the people of Tamil Nadu, who have watched all this, think of this government?

When more than about ₹800 crore had already been spent and the Sethu Samudram project works were progressing rapidly, is it right for some people to intervene with what the author calls an imaginary reason and try to hold a closing ceremony for the project? It is to ask for justice and establish the truth that the DMK is conducting **Ezhuchi Naal** protest meetings on the 15th. Even vindictiveness has a limit. Let it be shown in personal matters if they wish; how can it be right to display it in public works meant to benefit the people?

One enters public life to do good for others. Even if one does no good, is office meant for doing harm? The people of Tamil Nadu, the author says, are learning from experience how the present rulers behave out of political vindictiveness. Yet many opposition parties that ought to explain this to the people hesitate even to speak of it, thinking, “Why invite unnecessary trouble?” If anyone boldly raises a voice against the government, instead of accepting it as part of democracy, the rulers take various actions against them.

Do the newspapers at least write about these matters? They too have their difficulties. Last month, an English daily carried a prominent front-page report about an alleged ₹200-crore tender scandal in the Transport Department. Nobody took notice. The next day, after the DMK wrote about it in detail, the government announced that it would file a case against that newspaper. The following day the paper, apparently thinking “Why invite trouble?”, stopped publishing such reports attacking the government.

The Sethu Samudram project is one that could raise the livelihoods of people throughout the southern districts. It is a project demanded by many people for many years; political parties and their leaders have raised their voices for it. Any government should demand that such a project be completed; would any government ask that it should not be implemented? Yet that is the strange and painful situation now occurring in Tamil Nadu. At the meetings the DMK will hold on the 15th, this must be explained to all the people of Tamil Nadu. It is with that in mind that I have gathered these explanations and given them to you.

Make these views clear to the people of Tamil Nadu as well! Whoever says whatever they may, let our work continue as always!

On the 15th, I will address the Ezhuchi Naal meeting in North Chennai. Likewise, the DMK's frontline leaders and speakers will address meetings in various cities across Tamil Nadu. I ask you, with a sense of rightful closeness, to make all those meetings highly successful.

**(Concluded)**

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**10 May 2013**
