# 3558. The Film Industry and the DMK!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 30 April 2011

**Udanpirappē,**

One of our senior writers, and one of the thinkers who has captured my mind, is **Solai**. Whatever he writes, I read it without likes or dislikes, in the same even frame of mind; at this point the source prints the word **`பொக்கம்`**. I do not merely read it. Without leaving room for condemnation, I turn it over in thought; like the swan that, from a mixture of milk and water, takes only what it needs, I have never wavered from that position. In that spirit, I had the opportunity to read in the weekly **Nakkheeran** an article he wrote about the Tamil Nadu film industry.

My children—Azhagiri, Stalin and Tamilarasu—have grown into pillars of the movement through service and continuing sacrifice. Some people cannot tolerate the fact that they and their children also earn money and fame in the film world. Even while remaining my friends, they shoot poisonous arrows at me. I want to make the country understand this, not in the style of **“I will answer song with song,”** but by examining the matter carefully and asking that it be understood clearly.

From **1945** until now, I have written screenplay and dialogue for more than **75 films**. In my first films as screenplay-and-dialogue writer—**Abhimanyu** and **Rajakumari**—my dear friend M.G.R. appeared as the hero. After him, my beloved friend Sivaji acted as the hero in **Parasakthi** and attained great fame. Those who understand the film world clearly cannot have forgotten the connections I had, and still have, with film artists, directors, producers, musicians, poets and workers.

In the speed of his prose, Solai says that someone—a major producer—has complained at the height of his anger that only if this government changes will the cinema industry see a dawn. Solai has even stated his name openly. I, however, do not wish to publish that name.

Solai also does not fail to say that people in cinema circles complain that theatres are not available to release films that have already been produced.

What can be done? There was once a time when, because there were no people to make films, studios and theatres were closed and their premises converted into marriage halls, hotels and warehouses. Now we have reached a situation in which people wander about saying that there are no theatres available to release films.

I know the tears shed by the producers of **Ponnar-Sankar**, for which I wrote, who said that they too could not find theatres anywhere and had to roam from place to place before they could secure theatres.

At a theatre in Cuddalore, **Ponnar Sankar** was being screened to house-full shows; yet on the very second day, the film was forcibly removed. It is only fair to assume that Solai may not know all those reports.

Solai writes that, in a village in the Reddiar Chathiram union of Dindigul district, an elderly woman asked, **“How is it that Karunanidhi's grandsons and granddaughters are all making cinema films?”** Poor grandmother! Apparently she did not know that A.V. Meiyappa Chettiar made films and that later his sons A.V.M. Murugan, A.V.M. Kumaran and A.V.M. Balasubramaniam, A.V.M. Saravanan, his son Guhan and daughter-in-law Nithya were all in the film industry; that after Nadigar Thilagam Sivaji, his sons Ramkumar and Prabhu and Prabhu's son Dushyanth worked in films; that Superstar Rajini, his beloved daughter Aishwarya and son-in-law Dhanush are in the film industry; that Kalaignani Kamal, his brother Charuhasan, his daughter Shruti, and Suhasini, Mani Ratnam and Anu Hasan are all involved in this field; that producer R.B. Choudary and his children Jiiva and Jithan Ramesh are in it; and that character actor Sivakumar and his children Suriya and Karthi are involved in the film industry. That the Reddiar Chathiram grandmother did not know all this is the amusing part.

That is not all. My friend, poet Kannadasan, wrote and published as a book accounts of how many film producers in earlier times produced films but could not complete them and committed suicide, and how many became penniless and ended up on the street simply because they had made films. It would be good if **Nakkheeran** acquired and published that work.

If my sons, daughters or grandchildren enter politics, why is it called hereditary politics; and if they enter the film industry, why must meanings be invented for that too? What is the reason? Why this burning resentment?

To the producer who, like the sage Durvasa, became angry and demanded a change of government, I would like to recall what he himself had repeatedly said on many platforms until not very long ago. They are as follows:

To encourage Tamil films produced at low cost, the DMK government first introduced on **12.12.1996** and has continued to implement a scheme giving eligible Tamil films exemption from entertainment tax for the first **four weeks** after release. Before that, entertainment tax had continued to be collected under the previous government.

The DMK government reduced entertainment tax on new films from **54 per cent to 40 per cent in 1989**; from **1.8.1998**, it reduced it from 40 per cent to **30 per cent**; and in **2000**, to **25 per cent**.

For films released more than ten years earlier, it reduced entertainment tax from **40 per cent to 20 per cent on 1.8.1998**.

The DMK government also reduced entertainment tax on dubbed films in the same way it was reduced for other Tamil films. Composition-tax assessments too were reduced accordingly.

Through these tax reductions, as early as **1996**, the DMK government provided tax concessions of about **₹60 crore a year**, on a scale unprecedented in the history of Tamil cinema.

Under the Sales Tax Act, the DMK government waived the tax payable on transfer of film rights from **1.4.1986 to 11.11.1999**, and from **12.11.1999** reduced sales tax on rights transfers from **11 per cent to 4 per cent**. From **1.4.2000**, the **4 per cent** sales tax on film leases was abolished altogether.

It is under this government too that, when films are given Tamil titles, the entertainment tax is fully waived.

For shooting at sites under the Archaeology Department and at places containing art monuments, the DMK government in **1996** reduced the shooting fee from **₹5,000 to ₹1,000 per place per day**, and for other places from **₹2,500 to ₹500**.

Under the DMK government, the shooting rent for Rajaji Hall had been **₹1,000 a day**. In **2003**, the Jayalalithaa government raised it to **₹1 lakh**. After the AIADMK's defeat in the **2004 Parliamentary election**, it was reduced to **₹25,000**. On **25.6.2006**, the DMK government reduced it further to **₹10,000**.

For shooting locations classified as **Category 1**, the fee fixed at ₹10,000 under the Jayalalithaa government was reduced to **₹5,000**; for places under **Category 2**, the fee fixed at ₹5,000 under the previous AIADMK government was reduced to **₹3,000**.

On **25.6.2006**, the DMK government also ordered that shooting charges for the small screen need be only **50 per cent** of the charges for the big screen.

In **1996**, the DMK government introduced, and has continued to implement, a relief scheme under which, if stunt performers acting in Tamil films lose their lives during a stunt scene, their families receive **₹1 lakh**, and if they are injured to the extent of becoming disabled and unable to work, their families receive **₹50,000**.

For film-industry and television producers, artists, technical assistants and workers to build houses, **96 acres of land** have been allotted at **Paiyanur**, near Siruthavur in Kanchipuram district.

A **Film Industry Welfare Board** has been created to protect the welfare of workers in the film industry.

On **9.7.2008**, from the **₹25 lakh** I received as remuneration for story and dialogue for the film **Uliyin Osai**, after income tax, **₹18 lakh** was directly distributed to senior artists working in various sections of the film industry.

Udanpirappē, my friend Solai conveyed through his article the thinking of a few people in the film industry. I thank him for giving me the opportunity to convey this explanation to all of them, and I have listed here just a few achievements carried out for the film world under the DMK government.

If some in the film industry think this government must change precisely because it committed the “crime” of doing all these things, then the responsibility for explaining matters to them lies with my friend Solai himself.

I regard both the arts and literature as weapons needed for my political struggle for ideals. Some people, without taking even a step toward the struggle for ideals or laying their head down for it, are drawn into activity only by the fields of art and literature. It is natural that some among them, without reason, develop envy and jealousy toward me. But for that reason I will not bury my **ideal (`இலட்சியம்`)** and wander in search of **lakhs (`இலட்சங்கள்`)**.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**30-04-2011**

## Letter-specific notes

1. PDF 170 prints the source form `பொக்கம்`. Its intended word is not established by the audited source alone, so the English explicitly flags the printed form rather than silently substituting an expected Tamil word.
2. The closing `இலட்சியம்` / `இலட்சங்கள்` wordplay contrasts an “ideal” with “lakhs”; the English keeps both senses visible.
3. All tax rates, dates, fees, family names and film-industry examples are source-derived and are not reconciled with outside records.
