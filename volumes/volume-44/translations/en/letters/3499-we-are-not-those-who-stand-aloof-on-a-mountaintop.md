# 3499. We Are Not Those Who Stand Aloof on a Mountaintop!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation follows the audited canonical Tamil and the locked Volume 44 conventions. All health-sector figures, quoted press/medical assessments, the source’s insurance-scheme accounting and source-specific numerical or phrasing anomalies are retained without outside reconciliation. The complete audited Tamil is reproduced below and remains authoritative.

**Source pages:** [PDF 133](../../../pages/page-133.md)–[PDF 139](../../../pages/page-139.md)  
**Date:** 18 September 2010

**Udanpirappē,**

Some people must keep finding fault with us, with our government, with somebody or something. Otherwise they cannot sleep. Whatever medicines doctors may or may not discover, they should first come forward to find a medicine for this disease. They keep issuing statements lamenting that government hospitals do not have this medicine or that medicine, that there are no doctors, that the whole department is not functioning properly and has become merely a business. Even without answering that particular statement, I wish to take it as an opportunity and, from today, explain how extensive the work being carried out for the welfare of the people is in the various sectors.

At a time when good people across the country are praising Tamil Nadu’s public-health schemes as models for India — particularly the “Kalaignar Medical Insurance Scheme” being implemented in Tamil Nadu — a few people continue to find fault with that scheme. Before we even answer such fault-finders, yesterday’s (15-9-2010) issue of *The Hindu* carried a long article by Divya Gupta praising Tamil Nadu’s declining maternal mortality rate, infant mortality rate and total fertility rate, and the DMK government’s schemes such as the Dr. Muthulakshmi Reddy Maternity Financial Assistance Scheme, Kalaignar Insurance Scheme and free emergency ambulance scheme. The article was a summary of a research report prepared by the “Save the Children” movement, which creates awareness on issues relating to child mortality, for a conference on United Nations development goals to be held in New York.

The article stated:

> “Tamil Nadu is the State with one of the lowest maternal mortality rates in India. The mortality rate, which stood at 380 per lakh in 1993 (under AIADMK rule), had fallen to 111 in 2008 (under DMK rule). Finding that many pregnant women were dying because proper medical assistance was not available at the time of delivery, the Tamil Nadu government introduced maternity emergency-assistance and newborn-care centres throughout Tamil Nadu, with appropriately trained doctors, treatment facilities, blood-storage banks and twenty-four-hour service. The Dr. Muthulakshmi Reddy Maternity Assistance Scheme introduced by the Tamil Nadu government greatly helped in preventing maternal mortality.
>
> “Tamil Nadu uses better practices in implementing government schemes than other States in India. Firm measures are taken here to monitor how schemes function. Even on government holidays, public-health centres are ready with personnel to provide medical services. Nurses are ready to provide service twenty-four hours a day even on Sundays.
>
> “The 108 ambulance facility implemented by the Tamil Nadu government operates at least 385 ambulances, at the rate of one for 385 community development blocks. These ambulances operate free of charge for emergency medical assistance, including for pregnant mothers. No one seeking ambulance assistance needs to wait more than twenty minutes. To that extent the 108 ambulance service is performing a great service for the people. Although there are schemes such as the Integrated Child Development Scheme implemented by the Union Government, the Tamil Nadu government’s welfare schemes function in such a way that anyone can see them with their own eyes wherever they go.”

Medical expert Saradha Suresh, interviewed by the article’s author, said: “One important difference I have seen personally is that these health centres function well in Tamil Nadu. In other States they do not function at all.”

At a time when journalists and doctors are praising the Tamil Nadu government’s Health Department in this manner, some people issue statements saying that the entire Health Department in Tamil Nadu is no good — like Gandhari in the *Bharatham* taking the stone-child and striking her stomach with it.

During the last four years, hospital buildings have been constructed at an estimated cost of more than about ₹1,100 crore. Modern medical equipment worth more than ₹600 crore has been purchased and supplied to hospitals. Because of this, the public know that the number of patients coming to government hospitals instead of private hospitals has been increasing.

The DMK government has allocated ₹236 crore a year for medicines. As a result there is no shortage of medicines in government hospitals.

So far, 6,772 doctors, 7,242 nurses, 1,767 village health nurses and more than 10,000 allied medical personnel and ministerial staff have been appointed in government hospitals.

Under AIADMK rule in 2005–2006, the number of deliveries in primary health centres was only 81,190. Because the quality of primary health centres was improved under DMK rule, the number of deliveries taking place there increased by more than three times. In 2009–2010 the number of deliveries in primary health centres was 2,98,253.

In medical-college hospitals alone, 5,57,294 minor and major operations were performed last year. The source prints that 79,13,344 in-patients received treatment, and that figure is retained as printed. Up to June 2010 this year, 2,80,016 minor and major operations had been performed.

Therefore, under the “Kalaignar Insurance Scheme,” it is not only operations conducted in private hospitals; operations for various illnesses continue to be performed in government hospitals as well.

Nevertheless, considering the severity of the illness, the immediate need of the patient and the waiting lists in government hospitals for special operations, private hospitals have been integrated through this scheme only as an additional service — a `(Supplementary Facility)` — so that the public may benefit.

Through this scheme, the services of about 650 private hospitals have become available to the public free of cost. The proof is the beneficiaries who have been freed from illness through about 1,93,000 operations conducted during the last fourteen months.

The Kalaignar Insurance Scheme was introduced by this government because many people were registered and waiting for operations and poor patients’ lives had to be saved through immediate surgery. Because treatment is provided in government hospitals for all diseases, the insurance scheme covers fifty-one life-saving disease categories where waiting lists are heavy, such as heart surgery, cancer and kidney-transplant surgery. Within these fifty-one categories, treatment is provided for about 630 sub-types. As set out in the announcement, treatment under this scheme is available not only for heart, cancer and kidney operations but also for fractures, accidents, neurological conditions, congenital defects, reproductive-related illnesses, ear, nose and throat diseases and all such categories.

Although ₹1 lakh has been fixed as the limit under the scheme, the insurance company, with government approval, has entered into what the source prints as `ஒரு புரிந்துணர்ந்து ஒப்பந்தம்` with hospitals so that every treatment may be performed within that limit. Accordingly, fees have been fixed for each treatment and published on the website for everyone to know.

Under this scheme, identity cards have been given to 1.33 crore families. Through these family identity cards, about 4.50 crore members of the public can benefit. There is not a shred of truth in the statement by a few “statement-creatures” that ₹3,000 crore has been paid to a private insurance company. On the basis of the proportion of enrolled members, the annual premium was calculated at ₹469 per family. So far, for five quarters, only ₹722 crore in premium and ₹74 crore in service tax — ₹796 crore in all — has been paid. The insurance company has so far paid ₹515 crore to hospitals for those who underwent operations.

There is no room here even for the word commission. The insurance company implementing the scheme was finalised through an open tender, and the entire design of the scheme was discussed and decided at every official level.

People affected by chikungunya, cholera, typhoid, smallpox, jaundice, swine flu and similar diseases receive proper treatment in government hospitals themselves. Moreover, people affected by these diseases do not require surgery. Since quality treatment for such diseases is available in government hospitals, there was no need to include them in the insurance scheme. For preventing diseases listed in the source as diphtheria, polio, tuberculosis, measles, jaundice, whooping cough, mumps and `இரண ஜென்னி`, the Union Government sends vaccine medicines to all States. The State government does not purchase these vaccines.

To protect doctors and health workers treating swine flu from contracting it, the Union Government has sent preventive vaccine to Tamil Nadu. Doctors and hospital workers are being given this vaccine. With the good intention of protecting the public from swine flu, government officials spoke with vaccine-manufacturing companies approved by the Union Government, and a scheme for giving the vaccine to the public at a low price was started at the Guindy King Institute and is being implemented. At a time when no other State in India had started such a scheme, Tamil Nadu alone began low-cost swine-flu vaccination.

While this vaccine is being given to the public at a low price, this government has decided that it should also be given at government general hospitals in district headquarters and that even this low charge need no longer be collected from those below the poverty line; orders were issued for immediate implementation.

Tamil Nadu’s Health Department, government hospitals and primary health centres are functioning well. The Union Government and other States praise the health schemes implemented in Tamil Nadu and are taking steps to implement them elsewhere in India. Yet people in Tamil Nadu who have no concern for the people’s welfare try to create panic by finding fault even with the welfare schemes being implemented by the government. Their continuing criticism helps us provide truthful explanations to the people. Therefore let us welcome even their abuses, answer them and create clarity among the people!

Forgetting that service to the people is service to Mahesan, we are not those who climb a mountain and stand aloof!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**18-9-2010**

பொதுமக்களுக்கு குறைந்த விலையில் இந்தத் தடுப்பூசி போடப்
பட்டு வரும் நிலையில், மாவட்டத் தலைநகரங்களிலே உள்ள
அரசு பொது மருத்துவமனைகளில் இந்தத் தடுப்பு ஊசி போடவும்,
வறுமைக் கோட்டிற்கு கீழே உள்ளவர்களிடம் இந்தக் குறைந்த
தொகையையும் கூட இனி வசூலிக்கத் தேவையில்லை என்றும்
இந்த அரசு முடிவெடுத்து, உடனடியாக நடைமுறைப் படுத்திட
ஆணையிட்டுள்ளது.
தமிழகத்தில் சுகாதாரத்துறையும், அரசு மருத்துவமனைகள்
மற்றும் ஆரம்ப சுகாதார நிலையங்கள் சிறப்பாக செயல்பட்டு
வருகின்றன. மத்திய அரசும் மற்ற மாநிலங்களும் தமிழ்நாட்டில்
செயல்படுத்தப்படும் சுகாதாரத் திட்டங்களை பாராட்டி
இந்தியாவிலுள்ள பிற மாநிலங்களில் செயல்படுத்த நடவடிக்கை
எடுத்துவருகிறார்கள். ஆனால் தமிழ்நாட்டில் மக்களின்
நலனில் எந்தவிதமான அக்கறையும் இல்லாதவர்கள் அரசு
செயல்படுத்திவரும் மக்கள் நலத்திட்டங்களையும் குறை கூறி
பீதி உருவாக்கிட முயற்சி செய்கிறார்கள். அவர்கள் தொடர்ந்து
இவ்வாறு குறை கூறிக்கொண்டே இருப்பது; நமக்கும் மக்களுக்கு
உண்மையான விளக்கங்களை அளிக்கப் பயன்படுவதால்
அவர்களின் தூற்றுதல்களையும் வரவேற்போம், பதில் அளிப்போம்,
மக்களுக்கு தெளிவு ஏற்படுத்துவோம்! மக்கள் தொண்டே மகேசன்
தொண்டு என்பதை மறந்து விட்டு; மலையேறி நிற்போரல்ல நாம்!
