# 3525. Read the History of the Raja of Panagal!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation follows the audited canonical Tamil and the locked Volume 44 conventions. The autobiographical school-memory and Justice Party/Panagal Raja history are preserved as the letter presents them and are not externally reconciled. The long Periyar quotation contains historically loaded caste terminology; it is translated in its source-era quoted form rather than silently modernised. `நான்டிடெயில் (துணைப்பாடம்)` is rendered transparently as **“non-detail” (supplementary reader)** because the source itself supplies the parenthetical explanation. The complete audited Tamil is reproduced below and remains authoritative.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3525](../../../chapters/3525-padiththiduveer-panagal-arasar-varalaaru.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 299](../../../pages/page-299.md)–[PDF 301](../../../pages/page-301.md)  
**Date:** 7 January 2011

**Udanpirappē,**

When I took steps to place the portrait of the Raja of Panagal in the Legislative Council and the Legislative Assembly, a few officials, overcome with surprise, asked in bewilderment: **Who was the Raja of Panagal?**

I told them.

When I was thirteen years old, in **1937**, studying in the Fifth Standard at Tiruvarur High School, a book titled **“History of the Raja of Panagal”** had been prescribed to me as a **“non-detail” (supplementary reader)**. That small book of **45 pages** had been written by **Mani. Thirugnanasambanda Mudaliar**. Its address was printed as **“Ottrumai Office,” Saidapet, Madras**, and it sold for **three annas**.

I memorised all **thirteen chapters** and all **45 pages** of that book and could recite them in class. My teachers, Ramasamy Chettiar and Muthukirutta Nattar, praised me for it.

The letter says that in **1917**, in the then Madras Presidency, the Raja of Panagal became leader of the Justice Party, described as the “Niyaya Katchi,” and subsequently, in **1923**, became the Presidency’s **“Prime Minister.”** It says he held those positions for three years and died in **1928**.

When the Raja of Panagal died, Thanthai Periyar wrote an obituary editorial. The letter reproduces it in substance as follows:

> At a critical moment, the Raja of Panagal’s life came to an end. If, while a great and skilfully conducted war were advancing under the sign of victory, the soldiers turned waiting for their commander’s order and received the news that the commander had died, how their hearts would tremble — we need not say that our Tamil people would have trembled in the same way.
>
> For the self-respect, liberation, equality and human dignity of nearly **20 crore people** who had been pushed down, oppressed and regarded as degraded — those described as “untouchables,” “lower castes,” “degraded castes” and “Shudras,” upon whom “degradation by birth” and “slavery by birth” had been imposed — the Raja of Panagal entered upon and worked at a task like swimming against a powerful, fast-flowing flood.

**Udanpirappē,** it was during the Raja of Panagal’s period in government, the letter says, that the Tamil system of medicine was given great importance.

History, it says, also records that he introduced on behalf of the government a highly praised **“Hindu Religious Reform Act”**, overcoming fierce opposition from some self-interested people, in order to protect the dignity of temples and put an end to the degrading conduct of trustees.

The courage, ability and knowledge of that great man, and his compassion for the poor — these were gathered together in the Fifth-Standard supplementary reader on the **Raja of Panagal** that I studied.

I recently had that book searched for. It was located in the Mannargudi District Library in Tiruvarur district, and Tiruvarur District Collector **M. Chandrasekaran, I.A.S.**, sent it to me.

If one reads that little book of only **45 pages**, one can learn, the letter says, about the service rendered to society by the Justice Party and about the dignity, integrity and justice observed by those who held responsibility on behalf of that party.

It is the portrait of such a great man that has now been placed in the new Legislative Assembly and Legislative Council in Chennai.

Until that old picture arrived, until the historical notes concerning the Raja of Panagal came into my hands, I suffered greatly because I could not share these truths with you. Now this book has reached my hands — like manure placed at the root, like water poured upon it — easing my pain and giving me renewed enthusiasm to accomplish further achievements.

I strongly believe that if this book is published as a low-priced edition, people will gain access to the truths about the beginning of the Justice Party, its history and the need for it.

Therefore, **Udanpirappē**, I want you too to read the Fifth-Standard supplementary lesson on the Raja of Panagal that I read and learned from in those days. You, my Udanpirappu, should read it at once.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
7 January 2011
