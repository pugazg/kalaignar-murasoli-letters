# 3701. When Will It Take Vishwaroopam?

## English translation

Udanpirappē,

In the DMK election manifesto issued for the 2006 Tamil Nadu Assembly election, we promised the people that an “Elevated Highway” scheme would be implemented in parts of Chennai suffering severe traffic congestion.

To implement that promise, after the DMK Government assumed office on 13 May 2006, within a month—on 5 June 2006—I wrote to Prime Minister Manmohan Singh requesting approval for an elevated road to Chennai Port to ease traffic congestion in the city.

The request was accepted. Approval was granted for what was to be India's longest elevated expressway, linking Chennai Port with the Maduravoyal junction, initially at an estimated cost of ₹1,655 crore. Prime Minister Manmohan Singh laid the foundation stone for the massive project on 8 January 2009 at a function held under my chairmanship. On that occasion he said: “This Project is one more important mile-stone in the ambitious road programme of the Government that will transform the Country’s transport sector, give a boost to industry and integrate the economy.” The project cost was later fixed at ₹1,815 crore.

The project was planned through the National Highways Authority of India, with a major part of the expenditure coming from Union Government funds. To enable uninterrupted container traffic from Chennai Port, the Tamil Nadu Government gave in-principle approval on 22 June 2007 for an elevated expressway along one bank of the Cooum from the port to Maduravoyal.

Later, at NHAI's request, the State Government approved on 7 March 2008 a route running along the Cooum from Chennai Port to Koyambedu and above Poonamallee High Road from Koyambedu to Maduravoyal. The expressway was to have 17.5 kilometres of elevated road and 1.5 kilometres at ground level, for a total length of nineteen kilometres.

The ₹1,815-crore road was to be built by NHAI under the Build, Operate and Transfer model. It was announced that the four-lane elevated road would be completed within three years and would be India's longest elevated road.

For land acquisition, rehabilitation and resettlement of people affected by the project, Chennai Port Trust and the Tamil Nadu Government agreed to share expenditure equally, fifty-fifty. Tamil Nadu's share was expected to be ₹130 crore. The State Government also assured full cooperation with NHAI in obtaining pollution-control clearances and shifting drinking-water lines, sewer lines and electricity poles. The Tamil Nadu Slum Clearance Board was appointed the nodal agency for rehabilitation and resettlement.

To review the work periodically, the State Government constituted on 14 September 2008 a high-level committee headed by the Chief Secretary and a subcommittee headed by the Principal Secretary of Highways and Minor Ports.

Vehicles bound for Chennai Port were being trapped in traffic, causing serious delay, fuel wastage and unnecessary expense. The elevated road would allow containers to move rapidly and without obstruction from Chennai Port to other regions through the Golden Quadrilateral, avoiding waste of time and fuel. Goods from other regions could likewise reach the port quickly. As a further benefit, traffic congestion on other Chennai roads would be greatly reduced.

In March 2011, the Union Environment Ministry granted permission for the project to continue.

After holding closing ceremonies for DMK Government schemes one after another, the AIADMK Government looked for what else it could stop. In March 2012 it ordered work halted on the Chennai Port–Maduravoyal elevated road, a Union Government project begun during DMK rule and proceeding rapidly at a cost of ₹1,815 crore. When the project had earlier been challenged in court, the First Bench of the Madras High Court, comprising Chief Justice Iqbal and Justice Sivagnanam, had ruled in August 2010 that it could proceed. The present State Government did not attach much importance even to that.

This was a project for which every approval had been properly obtained and which the Prime Minister himself had launched. Once implemented, a journey taking half an hour could be completed in five minutes and traffic movement would become easier. The State Government itself had fully examined and approved the project before it began. The Union department secretary wrote all these details to the Tamil Nadu Chief Secretary and came in person to explain them. With the Prime Minister's approval, Cabinet Secretariat official T.A.K. Nair came to Chennai and explained to Chief Minister Jayalalithaa why the project needed to be completed quickly. Yet the AIADMK Government continued to block it.

On 16 May 2012, when poet Kanimozhi spoke in the Rajya Sabha about the Tamil Nadu Government blocking the Maduravoyal project, AIADMK members opposed her. Because the State Government had placed obstacles in the project's path for nearly a year without reasonable basis, the project cost rose and public tax money was put at risk of being wasted. Grass and weeds grew at work sites.

As public opposition to the Government's obstruction increased day by day, the State was pushed toward changing its decision. On 29 January 2013 *The Hindu* carried a report titled “Ray of hope for Port - Maduravoyal Project - State wants NHAI to realign elevated expressway along Cooum and resume work.” It said the Tamil Nadu Government had begun efforts to continue the project and had written to NHAI. The report also noted: “The Tamil Nadu Government is yet to complete its share of the work in terms of relief and rehabilitation of dwellers along the Cooum River and acquisition of the land.”

NHAI, for its part, remained firm that it had done nothing contrary to the agreement with the Tamil Nadu Government. About ₹500 crore had already been spent and roughly twenty per cent of the work was complete.

After the AIADMK came to power, many projects like this were blocked because of political animosity, wasting people's tax money. The Chennai Port–Maduravoyal project had been discussed in detail, debated by the Union and State Governments, approved, and then inaugurated by the Prime Minister himself. But what of that? For those who were prepared to turn the new Secretariat and Assembly complex—opened by the Prime Minister and Sonia Gandhi themselves—into a hospital, would Maduravoyal be important?

Jayalalithaa often proclaims that she will take Tamil Nadu forward on the path of development and make it the leading State. Had she allowed this major infrastructure project to continue, it could have been completed before the end of 2013. Instead, with the single motive that the DMK should not receive credit, the AIADMK Government blocked the project, delayed it and squandered public tax money, while claiming concern for the State's development.

Will people fail to understand that such claims are hollow? When will all these truths assume their full *Vishwaroopam*? I do not know now—but they will not remain unknown.

They will certainly become known!

With affection,
M.K.
1-2-2013
