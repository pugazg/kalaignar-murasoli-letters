# 3765. I Will Work; I Will Keep Working!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

All of you, my Udanpirappugal, came together and conducted the celebration marking my ninetieth year with a grandeur and distinction unlike that of any previous year. For the past two or three days, I have not slept properly. Members of my household repeatedly asked me about it. Even some of my affectionate younger brothers, who have been beside me morning and evening during these two or three days, asked, “What is it, Anna? Are you unable to sleep?”

I alone know why sleep has not come. Yes—it is nothing else. Even after all these years, the Tamil people continue to show me such boundless affection! Was I not born in some village, and that too in a very ordinary family? Maran, who was my very life, was the first graduate in our family. Until then, no one in our family had been a graduate. Our family had no special distinction of its own. Yet I have served as Chief Minister five times. I have contested twelve Legislative Assembly elections and won continuously without being defeated even once. Following the passing of Perarignar Anna, I was unanimously elected President of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam in 1969, and I have continued in that responsibility ever since.

Newspapers recently reported that Tamil Nadu has a population of seven crore. Among these seven crore people, there may be a few—so few that they can be counted on one's fingers—who regard me as an enemy. All the others have shown me affection. Even some who opposed me for a time later showed me affection. Why? What is the reason? What distinction do I possess as my own? Only one thing: work. From childhood, I have worked without interruption. I have spoken at meetings. I have written.

When I say that I have written and spoken, do I mean casually? Under the title *Nenjukku Neethi* (*Justice for the Heart*), I have continuously written my autobiography together with the history of the movement. Five volumes of *Nenjukku Neethi* have so far appeared as books. I have also completed the sixth volume as a weekly serial in *Murasoli*, though it has not yet appeared as a book. These six volumes cover events from my birth in 1924 up to 2002. Do you know how many pages these six volumes alone contain? 4,616 pages. Yes—this one work alone runs to six volumes and 4,616 pages.

Likewise, twelve volumes of my important speeches in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly have so far been published. Do you know how many pages they contain? 6,077 pages. In addition, all my poems have been collected under the title *Kavithai Mazhai* (*Rain of Poetry*): 1,107 pages. The recently published *Sirukathai Poonga* (*Garden of Short Stories*): 670 pages. The historical novel *Romapuri Pandian*: 705 pages. *Thenpandich Singam*: 453 pages. The two volumes of *Muthukkuliyal* together: 1,062 pages. *Pudaiyal*: 380 pages. *Sanga Tamil*: 484 pages. *Ponnar Shankar*: 543 pages. *Kuraloviyam*: 648 pages. *Kural Urai*: 285 pages. *Tolkāppiya Poonga*—of which fourteen editions have so far appeared—551 pages.

Do you know the total number of pages of my speeches and writings that have appeared in print? 17,082 pages. Even I feel overwhelmed and astonished: have I written so many pages? And that is not all. More than one hundred smaller books written by me have also been published. In addition, twelve volumes of the letters I wrote in *Murasoli* have appeared. Thambi Ila. Pugazhendi has brought together my speeches delivered at educational institutions and published them as a large volume.

It is not only writing. At the age of ninety, there is hardly a major city, small town or village in Tamil Nadu where I have not spoken. In places outside the State, by roadsides while travelling, in Chennai, at conferences, at public meetings, and even at the entrance to my own home—the number of marriages I have solemnised is itself close to twenty thousand.

That is not all. Though I have been the leader of a State party in Tamil Nadu politics, I have interacted with leaders across India to such an extent that there is hardly an all-India leader who does not know me. Beginning with the period when Madam Indira Gandhi was Prime Minister, every Prime Minister who followed has dealt with me affectionately. I have never behaved in a way that would make anyone show hatred towards me. Not only Prime Ministers and Union Ministers, but all the leaders who sat in the Opposition at the Centre have respected and appreciated me. Leaders in the various States of India also know me well.

Even after severe pain developed in my back, even after undergoing surgery for it, and even now, when I am unable to walk and travel in a wheelchair, I have neither stopped nor postponed any of my work. I have not stopped attending meetings, nor have I stopped writing. Amid my political work, I have also been involved in the arts and written stories and dialogues for about seventy-five films. The people of Tamil Nadu know that most of them ran successfully. Many people in Tamil Nadu memorised the dialogues I wrote; those dialogues were also among the first film dialogues to be published in book form. Despite all this, I have never been arrogant. I have never treated anyone with disregard. I have never spoken as though I could simply pick something up and overturn it at will.

It was for such a person as me that the celebration of my ninetieth year was held. Though my birthday fell on the 3rd, a month earlier Thambi Kaviperarasu Vairamuthu had obtained my consent by saying that, on the 1st, he would invite ninety poets and honour me in their presence. He arranged that event beautifully. He personally checked and conducted every detail. He brought together ninety poets of Tamil Nadu. Senior poets and writers including Kulothungan—Va. Se. Kuzhanthaisamy—Kavikko Abdul Rahman, epic poet Vaali, Kalyanji, Manushyaputhiran, Kavithaipithan, Va. Mu. Sethuraman, Kanimozhi, Pa. Vijay, Ilaya Bharathi, Palani Bharathi, Salma, Kalapriya, Andal Priyadarshini and Krushangini—ninety poets in all—came to me one by one, praised me and offered their greetings.

On the morning of the 1st, the Women's Wing held a birthday seminar under the leadership of Sister Noorjahan Begum. Though Noorjahan Begum is now the head of the Women's Wing, I have known her from the days when, whenever I travelled to Madurai by train, she would stand at Dindigul railway station with twenty women and raise the cry “Long live Kalaignar!” so loudly that it echoed across the whole station. An affectionate younger sister! At that seminar, Thambi Duraimurugan, Thambi T. R. Baalu, leader of the DMK parliamentary party, Rajya Sabha member and poet Kanimozhi, and Thambi Cumbum Selvendran participated and delivered excellent speeches.

On 2 June, a function was held to release the fifth volume of *Nenjukku Neethi* and *Sirukathai Poonga*. The General Secretary, Perasiriyar, who takes the lead and lends distinction to my birthday celebrations every year, was unwell. Before leaving for the function that evening, I therefore went to his home, met him and received his greetings. Justice Mohan presided over the function and released the books. There, he poured out his affection for me like the waterfalls of Courtallam. Our Dr Nannan, who received the first copy, expressed his views with both a sense of right and deep feeling. Kaviperarasu Vairamuthu marvelled at and praised the distinction of the books in his own characteristic way. Until then, I had heard Manushyaputhiran's views only through newspapers and television; that day was the first time I heard him in person. It was as though he had reflected for a long time, shaped a profound research essay and was reading it aloud—yet he presented it there without a single note in his hand. Thambi Duraimurugan, who delivered the welcome address, once again proved before everyone, in a way that stirred them, that he is indeed my younger brother.

From five in the morning on 3 June until I went to bed at around midnight, the rain of affection that the Tamil people poured upon me—how am I ever to repay it? Every year on my birthday, I have met comrades of the movement at Anna Arivalayam. But how did such a vast crowd gather this year? The glass entrance door of Anna Arivalayam was shattered to pieces. The previous night, our friends at Arivalayam had neatly erected temporary barricades to regulate the crowd. By the next morning, all of them had broken amid the press of people. At one point, fearing that the crowd might press in on me, we had to hurry into a nearby room.

The birthday public meeting was held at the YMCA grounds in Royapettah. It had been arranged there because obtaining police permission would have been difficult at other places. It is an enormous ground. I must make particular mention of the South Chennai District Secretary, Thambi J. Anbazhagan, who organised the meeting, and of the Udanpirappugal who worked under his leadership. I had intended to mention them at the public meeting itself. They had created the setting so splendidly that one wondered whether Anna Arivalayam itself had been lifted from its location and placed there. Such is the South Chennai District Secretary's “devotion” to me.

When he organised my birthday meeting the previous year, he had created a stage resembling the setting in which I sit and speak at my Gopalapuram home. Those who watched the stage on television from distant places felt as though I were speaking while seated at home in Gopalapuram. J. Anbazhagan's father, Pazhakadai Jayaraman, used to construct stages filled with such novelty. Once, he built an entire stage using fruit. His son J. Anbazhagan has surpassed his father in constructing this public-meeting stage. He reproduced the rooms of Arivalayam there, just as they exist in the original, and brought every leader attending the meeting into the room made to resemble mine and showed it to them. He even placed there a statue of Anna like the one at the entrance to Anna Arivalayam. From the high stage, what we saw was not merely an assembly of people, but a sea of people.

The public-meeting venue was only one kilometre from my home. Yet along that short route, nine receptions had been arranged under the leadership of the different area units of the movement. We left at 6.30 in the evening and could reach the YMCA grounds only at 8 p.m.—or rather, the new Anna Arivalayam that had been created there. Thambi Thalapathi, the Treasurer of the movement, who brought both the birthday arrangements and the Arivalayam arrangements to completion with discipline and order, reminded me of myself in the days of Anna, when I used to organise such programmes.

On the public-meeting stage, the words of greeting and appreciation spoken by Tamilar Thalaivar and Dravidar Kazhagam President, my dear younger brother Veeramani; Thambi Thol. Thirumavalavan, leader of the Viduthalai Chiruthaigal movement; Professor Khader Mohideen; Arulalar R.M.V.; and Professor Thambi Suba. Veerapandian still continue to resonate in my ears.

Meanwhile, on the 2nd, on behalf of the Youth Wing, former Mayor Thambi Ma. Subramanian conducted a Muthamizh seminar at Kamarajar Arangam under the leadership of Kaviperarasu Vairamuthu, with Professor Suba. Veerapandian, Dindigul Leoni and Advocate Arulmozhi. Each speaker spoke with a distinction of their own and in a manner that seemed to surpass the others. Ma. Subramanian has arranged events across different parts of the city for many consecutive days to mark my birthday. That first event arranged by him at Kamarajar Arangam became a complete event and a fitting culmination in itself.

The celebrations are still nowhere near their end. Districts are competing with one another in announcing one hundred events, a full month of events and continuous programmes. For the past two or three days, the question that has kept me awake is this: how am I to repay all this? When they pour so much affection and love upon me, what are we going to do for them? With my mind and through my actions, I will continue to work. I will work, I will work, and I will keep working.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**6 June 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. **Perarignar Anna**, **Perasiriyar**, **Kaviperarasu**, **Kavikko**, **Tamilar Thalaivar** and **Thalapathi** are established honorifics or political-cultural forms used in the source. They are retained where replacing them with generic English titles would diminish the relationships Kalaignar is invoking.
2. **Muthamizh** refers to the traditional threefold conception of Tamil expression: *iyal* (literature), *isai* (music) and *natakam* (drama).
3. The titles of Kalaignar's Tamil works are transliterated and, where useful, followed by a brief literal English rendering.
