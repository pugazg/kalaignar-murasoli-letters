# 3753. Industrial Growth in Tamil Nadu: Then and Now!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 11 May 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

I searched and searched through the newspapers to see whether, regarding the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly proceedings of 7 May 2013, any importance had been given to details such as which department's demand for grants was debated, who spoke in that debate, and what reply the minister of that department gave. I could find nothing. What is happening in Tamil Nadu? As they say in English, it is a **“One Man Show”**—that is what is happening here. In that pattern, on some days even Question Hour does not take place in the Assembly, but the Chief Minister's statements under Rule 110 appear every day, and they occupy page after page in the newspapers.

On 7 May 2013, the Chief Minister read announcements relating to the Industries Department. The demand for grants for that department had already been debated in the Assembly and replies had already been given by the ministers concerned. Yet it was the Chief Minister who read the announcements concerning that very department in the Assembly the next day.

As soon as the Chief Minister finished reading her Rule 110 statement, there were words of praise from the Republican Party, Kongu Peravai, Forward Bloc, Samathuva Makkal Katchi, Manithaneya Makkal Katchi, Congress, Communist Party of India and Communist Party of India (Marxist). **“It would not be surprising if, because of the Chief Minister's announcements, Tamil Nadu becomes the Manchester of India”**—that was the praise from the Communist Party of India.

And the praise did not end there. Industries Minister Mohan said, **“Our Amma is capable of accomplishing what is difficult. She is the one who drove away even the tsunami. She announces such good schemes because Tamil Nadu must earn the name of the foremost State. She brings light.”** Then another minister of the department rose and said that Amma was governing with determination so that everyone should receive everything and there should be no deprivation, and that these schemes were the crowning touch to that good governance.

The Speaker put the full stop to it by saying that Amma, who worked day and night for Tamil Nadu's advancement, had issued these new announcements to take the State still further forward in industry, and that she had come like the morning star and was bringing light to Tamil Nadu.

Nowadays, this is what happens in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly: Question Hour ends; the Chief Minister reads statements under Rule 110; representatives of all parties welcome and praise them; only after the minister and Speaker add their tributes does the scheduled debate on the demand for grants begin. Those who might question, oppose or criticise with an alternative view must not be present; that, the author argues, is why DMK members were expelled in violation of democratic norms.

Do you know the projects the Chief Minister announced that day for industry?

- A paperboard-manufacturing plant in the Srirangam constituency with an investment of ₹1,200 crore.
- In Nagapattinam district, a deep-sea port, a floating liquefied natural gas terminal and a 500-MW gas-based power plant, to be implemented with an investment of ₹3,500 crore.
- Nine new industrial parks in nine districts.
- A multi-storey industrial complex at Thirumazhisai through SIDCO.

The announcements continue like this. When I read the announcement about the multi-storey industrial complex at Thirumazhisai, a doubt arose in my mind. I had written about it only last month.

Soon after the AIADMK came to power in 2011, the Chief Minister read a statement under the same Rule 110 on 9 September 2011. It announced that housing would be created on 311 acres at Thirumazhisai and that a satellite town would be developed near Chennai, and—as now—the newspapers gave the announcement prominent coverage. But before the Chief Minister made that announcement, the very same scheme had already appeared in the policy note placed before the Assembly by Housing Minister R. Vaithilingam on 25 August 2011. The Chief Minister later read the same thing as a Rule 110 announcement.

What became of that announcement? Has the satellite town at Thirumazhisai been established? The answer has to be no. Even a news report published on 5 May 2013 said that the project was facing problem after problem.

At a time when the Chief Minister has issued so many industrial announcements in the Assembly, I think it appropriate to recall a few of the measures taken during DMK rule, in order to answer the question of whether nothing at all had been done for industry under the DMK government.

After the DMK government was formed in 2006, a high-level committee under the Industries Secretary was constituted on 20 July 2006 to identify industrially backward **Blocks** and determine the methods by which industries could be started there.

To assist industrial and commercial growth in Tamil Nadu, a **“Special Task Force”** comprising representatives of industrial and trade associations was constituted under the Chief Minister on 8 November 2006.

A new industrial policy was formulated and released in 2007 with objectives including creating additional employment in Tamil Nadu; raising the manufacturing sector's share in State Gross Domestic Product from 21 per cent to 27 per cent; doubling Tamil Nadu's annual export value to ₹1 lakh 40 thousand crore; raising the State to an unmatched position in innovation and high technology; and enabling farmers to earn better income.

In 2008, for the first time in India, a separate policy for micro, small and medium enterprises was formulated, with objectives that included increasing their competitiveness and progressively improving performance, encouraging agro-based industries so farmers could earn additional income, and setting an annual growth rate of more than 10 per cent for MSMEs.

Before preparing the Industries Department demand for grants for 2009–2010, Deputy Chief Minister Thambi M.K. Stalin convened a meeting at the Secretariat in Chennai on 13 June 2009 of associations representing micro, small, medium and large entrepreneurs in Tamil Nadu and discussed measures required for the State's industrial growth. Similar meetings were subsequently held in Tirunelveli, Coimbatore, Tiruchirappalli and Erode.

The source states that the Union Government's **Central Statistical Organisation**, in its 2011 publication of the Annual Survey of Industries for 2008–09, reported that Tamil Nadu had a total of 26,122 factories and therefore ranked first in India in number of factories; that Tamil Nadu also ranked first in the number of factory workers, with 17 lakh 74 thousand 19 people employed in factories alone; that Tamil Nadu ranked third in **total investment**; third in **gross industrial output**; and third in **net value added** among factories.

The **“Wall Street Journal”**, in its issue dated 8 July 2010, is cited as having highly praised the Tamil Nadu government's performance. The source says the journal observed that Tamil Nadu performed better than other Indian States in providing the land, roads and electricity needed by automobile-manufacturing industries and that more than twelve government-related permissions for starting or expanding an industry were provided at a single place through a single-window system.

The source further says that the BIZCON report issued by an organisation printed as **“ASSO CHAMP”** highlighted Tamil Nadu as one of India's three best States in successfully attracting investment.

According to the Centre for Monitoring Indian Economy's report for the October–December 2010 quarter, the source says Tamil Nadu ranked third among Indian States in industrial growth. It states that at the beginning of AIADMK rule in 2001—that is, up to May 2001—the industrial investment proposed in Tamil Nadu was ₹1 lakh 83 thousand 49 crore 37 lakh; by May 2006, at the end of five years of AIADMK rule, it had risen by only about ₹8,742 crore to ₹1 lakh 91 thousand 791 crore 98 lakh.

But after the DMK government was formed in 2006, the source attributes to the new industrial policy, encouragement for industrial growth, a transparent and industry-friendly approach to investors, and infrastructure developed to global standards an increase of ₹5 lakh 73 thousand 765 crore 94 lakh over five years, bringing total investment by December 2010 to ₹7 lakh 65 thousand 557 crore 92 lakh—nearly four times as much—and producing what the author calls a quiet industrial revolution.

During the five years of AIADMK rule from 2001 to 2006, the increase in industrial investment over the preceding DMK government, the source says, was only ₹8,742 crore 61 lakh. But during the five years of DMK rule from 2006 to 2011, the increase over the preceding AIADMK period was ₹5 lakh 73 thousand 765 crore 94 lakh.

The Chief Minister also referred to **SIPCOT** in the statement she read in the Assembly. It was the DMK government of 1971, the letter recalls, that established SIPCOT itself in order to create developed industrial estates with all basic facilities and provide them to industrial investors.

Because the Chief Minister's statement claimed that it was her government that had been acting energetically in the industrial sector, I have given only this brief note on the initiative shown and the progress made in industry during DMK rule. If I were to list the factories that were started during the DMK period, that would require a separate letter.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**11 May 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. Intentional source English—including **“One Man Show”**, `Blocks`, **“Special Task Force”**, `Central Statistical Organisation`, **“Wall Street Journal”** and `Centre for Monitoring Indian Economy`—is retained in the translated discussion.
2. The source prints the association/report reference as **“ASSO CHAMP”** and **BIZCON**. Those forms are preserved rather than silently normalised to an outside institutional spelling.
3. Industrial rankings, investment figures and quoted assessments are translated as presented in the source and are not independently reconciled or updated.
4. `சிப்காட்` is rendered as **SIPCOT**, the established source acronym used for the industrial-development corporation.

வருகிறது என்று கூறிய காரணத்தால், கழக ஆட்சிக் காலத்தில்
தொழில் துறையில் எந்த அளவிற்கு முனைப்புக் காட்டப்பட்டது,
முன்னேற்றம் ஏற்பட்டது என்பதற்கான சிறு குறிப்பினை மட்டும்
தந்துள்ளேன். எந்தெந்த தொழிற்சாலைகள் கழக ஆட்சிக்
காலத்தில் தொடங்கப்பட்டன என்பதைக் கூறவேண்டுமென்றால்
அதற்குத் தனியாகத்தான் எழுத வேண்டும்.
