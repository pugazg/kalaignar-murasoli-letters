# 3493. Will the One That Did Not Understand at First Understand Now?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation follows the audited canonical Tamil and the locked Volume 44 conventions. The source’s chronology of the proposed all-India medical entrance examination, Tamil Nadu’s earlier abolition of entrance tests, GST correspondence and the Supreme Court impleadment petition is retained without outside correction. The source-title form `எடுகொளாவது` is preserved in the Tamil appendix; the English title is deliberately conservative and does not silently substitute another Tamil reading. The complete audited Tamil is reproduced below and remains authoritative.

**Date:** 20 August 2010

**Udanpirappē,**

On 14-8-2010 the newspapers carried a report that the Union Government had informed the Supreme Court of a decision to introduce, from the next academic year, an all-India common entrance examination for admitting students to medical colleges. As soon as that report appeared, on 15-8-2010 I wrote to Prime Minister of India Dr. Manmohan Singh and to the Union Minister for Health and Family Welfare explaining that the Union Government’s decision would seriously affect Tamil Nadu students, particularly poor and rural students. I also conveyed the matter through our parliamentary party leader, younger brother T. R. Baalu.

In that letter I wrote:

> “The Union Government has informed the Supreme Court, when the case pending there came up for hearing, that on the recommendation of the Medical Council of India an all-India entrance examination for admission to medical courses will be conducted from 2011–2012. Taking the welfare of rural students into account, the Tamil Nadu government enacted a law, with the assent of the President, abolishing entrance examinations for professional and medical education from 2007–2008. Rural students who are disadvantaged socially and economically have been benefiting from this. The decision now announced by the Union Government is considered an intrusion into the States’ right to administer their educational system. The Tamil Nadu government has for many years been implementing 69 per cent reservation. Under the new system announced by the Union Government, a situation will arise in which that reservation system cannot be implemented. Therefore, the Tamil Nadu government cannot accept the conduct of an all-India common entrance examination for admission to medical education.”

I wrote this, and the news was reported in detail in all the newspapers.

After I wrote on the 15th, Jayalalithaa suddenly woke up four days later. Without thinking about why she had remained silent for so many days, she had a long statement written, and spent most of it accusing me and making unnecessary criticisms about me.

Although I wrote to the Prime Minister and the Union Minister on the very day I saw the news in the newspapers, Jaya has asked in her statement whether writing a letter was enough and whether the Chief Minister should not have gone in person. When there is a government, issues arise every day. Every time an issue arises, a Chief Minister cannot immediately go to Delhi and keep meeting the Prime Minister and the Minister concerned, especially while a session of Parliament is in progress.

For example, as soon as news came about the proposed constitutional amendment for implementing the Goods and Services Tax system, I wrote to the Prime Minister on 29-7-2010. I sent a copy of that letter to the Union Finance Minister as well. At the meeting subsequently held in Delhi, our Finance Minister, the Professor, and Commercial Taxes Minister younger brother Ubayadullah participated and strongly presented our position. Considering the importance of the issue, I again wrote personally to the Prime Minister and the Finance Minister on 17-8-2010; and for yesterday’s meeting in Delhi I sent our Law Minister, younger brother Duraimurugan, in person to speak there on the issue.

Likewise, when news came that a biometric method was to be introduced in the caste-wise census, on 14-8-2010 I wrote detailed letters about it to the Prime Minister of India and to Mrs. Sonia Gandhi, guiding leader of the United Progressive Alliance. A Chief Minister therefore cannot go personally to Delhi every day whenever a new issue arises.

It took Leader of the Opposition Jaya four days even to write a statement about this issue. If, even in that statement, she writes about me rather than the issue, anyone can understand that her attention is less on the problem than on the need to write against this government.

Jayalalithaa has also advertised in her statement as though it was she who abolished the entrance examination in Tamil Nadu. To state the truth, it was during Jaya’s rule that, without properly abolishing the common entrance examination through lawful procedures, an order was hurriedly issued on 9-6-2005; that order was held invalid by the Madras High Court on 27-6-2005. Again, an order issued during Jaya’s rule on 18-2-2006 was struck down by the High Court on 27-2-2006.

Only after the DMK government assumed office in May 2006 was it announced in the Governor’s Address on 24-5-2006 that a committee would be constituted to examine the procedure required to abolish the common entrance examination. In continuation of that announcement, a committee headed by former Anna University Vice-Chancellor Dr. M. Anandakrishnan was constituted on 7-7-2006.

After considering that committee’s recommendations in accordance with law, the appropriate legislation to abolish the common entrance examination altogether was brought in under the DMK government. After the legislation was passed in the Assembly, the President’s assent was duly obtained; it was then made law and has been in force from 7-3-2007. This law too was challenged, and the Madras High Court, which heard the case, upheld and confirmed the law on 27-4-2007. As a result, over the past two or three years poor and rural students have been benefiting in large numbers.

Even in the present issue, I did not stop after writing to the Prime Minister and the Minister concerned on the 15th. In the case now pending before the Supreme Court, the Tamil Nadu government yesterday filed a petition seeking to be impleaded as a party. The petition says that, since the Tamil Nadu government has taken a policy decision and completely abolished entrance examinations for professional courses such as engineering and medicine, the Medical Council’s decision conflicts with the State government’s position, and therefore the Tamil Nadu government should be added as a party.

That petition is to come up for hearing on the 10th of next month before Supreme Court Justices Raveendran and A. K. Ganguly. This news too has appeared in the newspapers and has been reported on television.

When the Tamil Nadu government has taken efforts to this extent on the issue, I ask the people of Tamil Nadu to understand how someone who calls herself Leader of the Opposition conceals all of it and acts against every step taken by the government — and how, after spending all these days resting in Kodanadu and Siruthavur, she now engages in such unnecessary acts because an election is approaching. Will the one that did not understand at first understand now?

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**20-8-2010**

## Letter-specific notes

1. The source-title form `எடுகொளாவது` is scan-audited but lexically opaque in this context; the English title therefore uses the conservative “the one” rather than silently replacing the Tamil with a guessed word.
2. The medical-entrance-examination, GST and caste-census sequences are kept in the source’s chronology and are not reconciled with outside records.
3. `பேராசிரியர்` is translated as **the Professor**, as printed, without supplying an outside-source personal name.

ஆனால் 2006ஆம் ஆண்டு மே திங்களில் தி.மு. கழக அரசு
பொறுப்பேற்ற பிறகுதான், பொது நுழைவுத் தேர்வு முறையை
ரத்து செய்ய எடுக்கப்பட வேண்டிய வழிமுறைகள் குறித்து
ஆராய ஒரு குழு அமைக்கப்படும் என்று 24-5-2006 அன்று
ஆளுநர் உரையிலேயே அறிவித்து, அதன் தொடர்ச்சியாக
அண்ணா பல்கலைக் கழக முன்னாள் துணைவேந்தர், டாக்டர் எம்.
அனந்தகிருஷ்ணன் தலைமையிலே ஒரு குழு 7-7-2006 அன்று
அமைக்கப்பட்டது.

அந்தக் குழு அளித்த பரிந்துரைகளை சட்டப்படி பரிசீலித்து,
பொது நுழைவுத் தேர்வு முறையை அறவே நீக்குவதற்கான உரிய
சட்டத்திருத்தம் தி.மு. கழக ஆட்சியிலே கொண்டு வரப்பட்டது.
இந்தச் சட்டத் திருத்தம் சட்டமன்றத்தில் நிறைவேறிய பிறகு -
குடியரசுத் தலைவரின் ஒப்புதலை முறைப்படி பெற்று, அதன்
பின்னர் இது சட்ட மாக்கப்பட்டு 7-3-2007 அன்று முதல் இந்தச்
சட்டம் அமுலிலே இருந்து வருகிறது. இந்தச் சட்டத்தையும்
எதிர்த்து வழக்கு தொடரப்பட்டு - அந்த வழக்கினை விசாரணை
செய்த, சென்னை உயர்நீதி மன்றம் 27-4-2007 அன்று இந்தச்
சட்டத்தை ஏற்று உறுதி செய்தது. அதன் காரணமாக கடந்த
இரண்டு மூன்று ஆண்டு காலமாக ஏழையெளிய, கிராமப்புற
மாணவர்கள் பல்வேறுகணக்கில் பயன் பெற்று வருகிறார்கள்.

தற்போதைய பிரச்சினையிலேகூட, பிரதமருக்கும், துறை
அமைச்சருக்கும் நான் 15ஆம் தேதி கடிதம் எழுதியதோடு விட்டு
விடாமல், இது தொடர்பாக உச்சநீதிமன்றத்தில் நடைபெற்று
வரும் வழக்கில், நம்மையும் ஒரு வாதியாகச் சேர்த்துக் கொள்ளக்
கோரி, தமிழக அரசின் சார்பில் உச்சநீதிமன்றத்தில் நேற்றைய
தினம் மனு ஒன்றும் தாக்கல் செய்யப்பட்டுள்ளது. அந்த மனுவில்
தமிழக அரசு கொள்கை முடிவெடுத்து, பொறியியல், மருத்துவம்
