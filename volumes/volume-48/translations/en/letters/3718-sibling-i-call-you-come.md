# 3718. Udanpirappē! I Call You—Come!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

Only one day remains before the general strike we are to conduct on behalf of TESO for the Eelam Tamils.

The Eelam Tamil issue is being discussed intensely at the U.N. Human Rights Council, a focal point of the nations of the world, and both inside and outside the Indian Parliament, with many different views being exchanged.

The United States has brought a resolution of condemnation before the U.N. Human Rights Council. Even before that, the Council itself prepared a thirty-eight-page report, submitted on 11 February 2013 by its head, Mrs. Navaneetham Pillay. Some of the important points stated in that report, as reproduced here, are:

- During the war in Sri Lanka, many thousands of people—adults, boys and girls—went missing. Sri Lanka has not properly carried out measures to trace them and restore them to their places. Therefore an international investigation into the missing is necessary.
- The military remains concentrated in Tamil-inhabited areas. Why has it not been withdrawn?
- Because the military has not been withdrawn and is instead being concentrated further, fear of sexual violence prevails among Tamil women and girls living there.
- Relief work and resettlement that should have been carried out after the civil war have not been properly completed.
- The Sri Lankan government, which said it would investigate human-rights violations, has not conducted a full investigation. It has not even properly carried out the resettlement of displaced people.
- A credible international investigation must be conducted into the war crimes and human-rights violations said to have been committed by Sri Lanka.

When the U.N. Human Rights Council itself has levelled accusations to this extent, when we are suffering day and night thinking of the continuing sorrows of the Eelam Tamils, are not some actions of the Union Government—which ought to console us and extend a supporting hand to them—instead plunging us into a fire of anguish? I must refer particularly to the dry, unfeeling remarks of our Hon'ble External Affairs Minister Salman Khurshid.

Speaking to journalists in the Parliament complex, he said:

> “At the U.N. Human Rights Council meeting in Geneva, after the Sri Lankan government holds talks with the United States regarding the resolution being brought by the United States, the Government of India will decide whether or not to support that resolution.”

He further said that he had asked Sri Lankan External Affairs Minister G.L. Peiris to explore the possibility of holding talks with the United States and reaching an amicable solution over the resolution; that the Sri Lankan government should explore possibilities of preventing the United States from moving a resolution against it; and that until then those concerned should wait. Such remarks are like driving a spear into an already burning wound. The minister's response is like a commander who ought to stand at the front of our ranks and fight for us instead standing in the front rank of the opposing army and showering them with advice.

It is not only journalists. In Parliament, the DMK, AIADMK, BJP and other parties sharply criticised the Sri Lankan government and advanced strong arguments.

Speaking in Parliament, Thambi T.R. Baalu, leader of the DMK parliamentary party, poured out the suffering in these words: during the war in Sri Lanka, 90,000 women were made widows; about two lakh men and children disappeared; 1.20 lakh people took refuge abroad; and the Sri Lankan civil war was worse than the Second World War and the civil wars in Bosnia, Iraq and South Sudan.

Thambi Dayanidhi Maran said with emotion:

> “When India dropped food packets for Tamils in Sri Lanka during Rajiv Gandhi's government, I felt proud to be an Indian. Channel 4 television has exposed war crimes by the Sri Lankan military. Young people and Tamils living abroad are closely watching the actions of the Government of India. The whole world is watching what India is going to do. India must take firm action in this matter.”

Speaking for the BJP, one of its senior leaders, Mr. Yashwant Sinha, said India should ask the Sri Lankan government immediately to withdraw the military stationed in the Northern Province; India should call for an impartial international investigation conducted by people from outside Sri Lanka into the alleged genocide; and India should not merely be one of the countries supporting a resolution against Sri Lanka at the U.N. Human Rights meeting—India itself should be the country taking the resolution forward.

Why, even our friend K.S. Alagiri, speaking for the Congress party, said:

> “Tamils throughout the world are raising their voices demanding a political solution for Sri Lankan Tamils. They believe that India alone can make it happen. Therefore India must support the resolution being brought by the United States.”

Speaking for the Viduthalai Chiruthaigal Katchi, Thambi Thol. Thirumavalavan declared:

> “Sri Lanka has never been a friendly country in the way we imagine. When Sri Lankan leaders come here, we give them a red-carpet welcome; when our leaders go there, they are subjected to attacks. Sri Lanka has humiliated India at various stages. Therefore India must change its position toward Sri Lanka.”

Mr. Thambidurai for the AIADMK, Mr. Lalu Prasad Yadav for the Rashtriya Janata Dal, Mr. Mulayam Singh Yadav for the Samajwadi Party, CPI(M) member P.R. Natarajan, CPI member Lingam, Saugata Roy for the Trinamool Congress and Dara Singh for the Bahujan Samaj Party also raised their voices there for the Sri Lankan Tamils.

But from those from whom we expected a firm and definite announcement, we have so far received only disappointment. Replying to the debate, Salman Khurshid said there were many problems in interfering in the internal affairs of a friendly country and pressuring it to grant political rights, and that acting like a **“big brother”** or **“super policeman”** was not appropriate to India's foreign policy.

Congress spokesperson Abhishek Singhvi, speaking in Bengaluru, said:

> “The Government of India is weighing this issue in a neutral manner. We will not interfere in the internal problem of any country. In particular, we will not interfere in the internal affairs of Sri Lanka, a neighbouring country and a valued friend of India.”

These statements have caused enormous sadness and anguish among Tamil people across the world.

Even in the Prime Minister's reply to the President's Address, no firm assurance was given regarding the resolution against Sri Lanka. He said that the Sri Lankan government should hold talks with Tamil leaders in Sri Lanka.

If a foreign policy of never intervening in another country's internal affairs had been followed rigidly, could Pandit Nehru have intervened in South Africa's racial-apartheid problem? Could Mother Indira Gandhi have extended a helping hand to liberation leader Mujibur Rahman in Bangladesh and supported liberation there? Will Tamils around the world, who closely observe history, not ask such questions?

After the minister's reply in Parliament, members of several parties, including the DMK, walked out of the House.

Ananthapadmanabhan, Chief Executive of Amnesty International India, has said that there is sufficient, demonstrable evidence of Sri Lankan war crimes and that the United Nations Human Rights Council should conduct an independent investigation.

David, described here as a director of a British human-rights organisation, has said that cruelty against Tamils, ill-treatment of Tamils and persecution of Tamils continue in Sri Lanka. The United States has twice sent fact-finding teams to Sri Lanka, and those teams too said that the relief work was not satisfactory.

Thus, the letter argues, international organisations, investigations and testimony all indicate that President Mahinda Rajapaksa must bear responsibility and answer for war crimes, human-rights violations and genocide alleged to have occurred in Sri Lanka.

It is to place all of this before the country, to press for a change in the Union Government's approach, to demand concern for the Eelam Tamils, and to exert the necessary pressure that TESO has announced a twelve-hour general strike on the 12th—from 6 a.m. to 6 p.m. I ask again and again that all Tamils take part with one mind and make the strike a success.

Udanpirappē, whom else am I to go and ask but you? Whether you are a party worker, a labourer, a teacher, a student, a farmer, a government employee—whoever you may be—I ask you as your elder brother, my voice trembling, with the right that comes from our bond as Tamil kin.

You were born a Tamil. Recognise without fail that this is a moment when the entire Tamil people must unite and cooperate, raise your broad shoulders and set out! Can you not plead peacefully, along the path of moral protest, with those going to their offices; make a calm appeal to them; stand together with them; and make this one-day moral strike a success? You can! You certainly can!

Let your work shine like the dawn breaking through the darkness that has gathered against us. It is only one day—not even a whole day, but from six in the morning until six in the evening. For those who shed blood in Tamil Eelam, for those who lost their lives, and for those caught in the cruel grip of poverty and suffering—yes, for our Udanpirappus—this one-day strike is the penance we undertake in order to stand by them in their hour of need.

Among crores of Tamils, wherever those who live here or elsewhere may be, let us undertake this solemn offering with the resolve that their hearts may find comfort. With the hope that dawn will come—not only for the Tamils here but also for the Eelam Tamils living in Sri Lanka—let us observe tomorrow's strike on the path of moral protest, the path of love and the path of peace, and make it a success. Come, my beloved Udanpirappē, dearer to me than life itself—I call you. Come!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**11 March 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. The six bullet points at the beginning are translated as Kalaignar's summary of the U.N. Human Rights Council report he cites; the translation does not silently supplement or correct that summary.
2. **“Big brother”** and **“super policeman”** reproduce the images attributed in the source to External Affairs Minister Salman Khurshid.
3. Parliamentary figures, casualty/displacement counts and statements attributed to political leaders and international organisations are retained exactly at the level of the audited source and are not independently verified here.
4. The closing section uses religious-sacrificial metaphors such as `தவம்` and `வேள்வி`; they are rendered as **penance** and **solemn offering** while preserving the letter's mobilisation rhetoric rather than turning it into theological commentary.

தொழிலாளியாக இருந்தாலும் - அல்லது ஆசிரியராக இருந்தாலும்
மாணவராக இருந்தாலும் - விவசாயியாக இருந்தாலும் - அரசு
அலுவலராக இருந்தாலும் - யாராக இருந்தாலும் “தமிழ்ச் சொந்தம்”
என்ற உரிமையோடு தம்பி என்ற முறையில், தழுதழுத்தக் குரலில்
- தமையன் நான் கேட்கிறேன்!

தமிழனாகப் பிறந்த நீ; ஒட்டுமொத்தத் தமிழினமும்
ஒன்றுபட்டு ஒத்துழைக்க வேண்டிய தருணமிது என்பதைத்
தவறாமல் உணர்ந்து தடந்தோள் உயர்த்திப் புறப்படுக!
அலுவலகம் செல்வோரை அறவழியில் கெஞ்சிக் கேட்டு -
அமைதியாகக் கோரிக்கை விடுத்து - அவர்களும் நீயும் இணைந்து
நின்று இந்த அறவழி, ஒரு நாள் வேலை நிறுத்தத்தை வெற்றி
பெறச் செய்ய முடியாதா? முடியும்! நிச்சயமாக முடியும்! எதிராகக்
கப்பியிருக்கும் இருள் கிழித்து; வெளிக் கிளம்பும் உதய விடியலாக
உன் பணி திகழட்டும்; ஒரு நாள் தான்! ஒரு நாள் கூட அல்ல;
காலை 6 மணி முதல் மாலை 6 மணி வரை; தமிழ் ஈழத்தில்
ரத்தம் சிந்தியவர்களுக்கு - உயிர் இழந்தோர்க்கு - வறுமையின்
கோரப்பிடியில் சிக்கித் தவிப்போர்; ஆம்; நம் உடன்பிறப்புகளுக்கு
உற்றுழி உதவிட நாம் இயற்றும் தவம்தான் இந்த ஒரு நாள்
வேலை நிறுத்தம்!
கோடிக்கணக்கான தமிழர்களில் - இங்கு வாழ்வோர் -
அவர்கள் எங்கு வாழ்வோராயினும்; அவர்கள் இதயம் குளிர, நாம்
இயற்றப் போகும் வேள்வி இது என்ற சங்கல்பத்தோடு விடியும்,
விடியும், இங்குள்ள தமிழர்க்கு மட்டுமல்ல; இலங்கை வாழ் ஈழத்
தமிழர்களுக்கும் நல்ல பொழுது விடியும் என்ற நம்பிக்கையோடு
நாளை நடைபெறும் அறவழி வேலை நிறுத்தத்தை - அன்பு
வழி வேலை நிறுத்தத்தை - அமைதிவழி கடைப்பிடித்து
வெற்றியடையச் செய்வோம்; வாராய் என்று என் உயிரினும்
மேலான அன்பு உடன்பிறப்பே, உன்னை அழைக்கிறேன் வா!
