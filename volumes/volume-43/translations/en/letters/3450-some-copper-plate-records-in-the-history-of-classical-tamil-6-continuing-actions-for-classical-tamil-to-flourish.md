# 3450. Some Copper-Plate Records in the History of Classical Tamil — 6: Continuing Actions for Classical Tamil to Flourish!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This letter traces political, governmental and organisational activity from 2000 through 2003. It includes an extended 2003 speech by the source author on the claimed qualifications and expected consequences of classical-language recognition. Numerical, historical and linguistic propositions are retained exactly as source-framed claims; they are not reconciled with outside information.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3450](../../../chapters/3450-semmoli-varalatril-sila-seppedugal-6-semmoli-sirandhida-thodar-seyalpaadugal.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 191](../../../pages/page-191.md)–[PDF 198](../../../pages/page-198.md)  
**Date:** 30 December 2009

**Udanpirappē,**

On behalf of the DMK government, reminder letters were repeatedly sent to the Union Government asking it to take immediate action to declare Tamil a classical language. On 13.6.2000 I sent a reminder to Prime Minister Vajpayee. In a letter I sent him as Chief Minister on 18.12.2000, I wrote:

> “I wish to draw your kind attention to the letters I have already sent. The Tamil Nadu government’s request that the ancient and richly endowed Tamil language be proclaimed a classical language has remained pending with the Union Government for a long time. I respectfully request that the Union Government consider this truthful and just request of the State government and issue the appropriate orders.”

The Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam decided that it was urgently necessary to continue carrying the demand for Tamil’s classical-language status forward not only at the governmental level but also at the party and political levels.

The DMK had repeatedly stated in its election manifestos that all the official languages of the States should be made official languages of the Union Government and that, if making all State languages official languages at the Centre could not immediately be achieved, Tamil — a member of the Dravidian language family with rich literary and cultural resources — should at the first stage be made one of the Union’s official languages. In its 2001 Assembly election manifesto, the DMK for the first time included the promise: “The DMK will insist that Tamil be declared a classical language.” It did not stop with an election promise; the party drew up programmes of action for fulfilling it and entered the field.

On 4 and 5 May 2002, the Capital Tamil Sangam and the Bengaluru Tamil Sangam jointly held a conference with three major slogans: Tamil as an official language, Tamil as a classical language, and the *Tirukkural* as the national book. The conference adopted a resolution stating that Tamil ranked foremost among languages because of its antiquity, literary and grammatical richness, living youthfulness and character as a mother language; that it was regrettable that the Union Government continued to delay despite the long-standing collective feeling of Tamils demanding that Tamil be declared a classical language; and that the conference strongly urged the Union Government to make the declaration immediately without further delay.

On 13 April 2003, the Tamil Language Academy held the Tenth National Languages Conference in Chennai. At that conference I was presented the “Ilakkiya Kadal Award.” Since the speech I delivered on receiving the award was filled with thoughts that you, Udanpirappē, should remember regarding Tamil as a classical language, I give its important portions below:

> “What everyone has spoken about here is that Tamil should be made a classical language. More appropriately than saying it should be ‘made’ one, it should be said that it must be ‘called’ one. For Tamil is already a classical language. No one has two different views about that. Our demand today is that it should be called a classical language.
>
> “If we ask whether Tamil possesses the qualifications to be called a classical language, linguistic experts have specified eleven qualifications for a language to be so called. Tamil stands as the one language in the world to which all eleven qualifications fully apply.
>
> “The total number of major world languages is 600. Of them, 300 possess literature and grammar. Six languages have a history of more than two thousand years: Tamil, Chinese, Sanskrit, Latin, Hebrew and Greek. Of these, Latin and Hebrew may be regarded as nearly dead languages. But the State of Israel has undertaken an effort to give life to Hebrew and has achieved some measure of success.
>
> “Greek, which had reached the boundary of death, may be said to be gaining new life after that country achieved independence. Sanskrit was never a spoken language. It is a language used only in writing. Yet it cannot be denied that Sanskrit is one of the oldest languages in India. My *Kuraloviyam*, already written in Tamil, has been translated into that northern language, Sanskrit, and has been released today.
>
> “If we next take Chinese, it is a language with picture-writing, pictorial characters. But Tamil alone has the power to express human thought and delicate feelings without fuzziness, firmly, correctly and precisely — this is not merely my statement; Dr Emeneau, praised as the father of linguistics, has said so. Such is the distinction of our language.
>
> “Just as knowledge of Latin, Hebrew and Greek is needed to understand Europe through language, knowledge of both Tamil and Sanskrit is needed to understand India. These two languages stand as mother languages for the languages of India.
>
> “In the book *World History* written by Pandit Nehru, he accepted that what existed at Mohenjo-daro and Harappa was Dravidian civilisation. Our Tamil language belongs to the people of such a civilisation. It is that language which we now petition to have declared a ‘Classical Language.’
>
> “For those who ask what benefit there would be if this petition succeeds and Tamil becomes a classical language, this is my explanation.
>
> “If the Union Government declares Tamil a classical language, the University Grants Commission of India will accept it. Thereafter separate Tamil departments will be established in universities throughout India. Research into Tamil language, art and literature will proceed with vigour. If the University Grants Commission accepts Tamil as a classical language, universities throughout the world will accept it. The present condition in which Tamil exists merely as one among the Oriental languages in world universities will change and separate departments of Tamil will be created.
>
> “Just as the Union Government declared a Sanskrit Year, an opportunity will arise to declare a Tamil Year. Just as the Union Government allocated ₹15 crore to celebrate the Sanskrit Year, a situation may arise in which funds are allocated for Tamil and it is celebrated. Encyclopaedic volumes describing the artistic, literary and cultural distinctions of Tamils may be created. Many Tamil–English dictionaries may appear. The Union Government may bear the expenditure. Opportunities will arise to devise and implement many more projects giving momentum to the growth of scientific Tamil.
>
> “Not only in Indian languages but also in English, French and other languages, Tamil literature and modern works will be translated and spread throughout the world.
>
> “It was to achieve this that, when the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam government was in office, we engaged in many efforts. All documents relating to the demand already placed before the Union Government that Tamil be made a classical language should be taken up and examined, all the recommendations studied, and this conference’s resolution that Tamil be made a classical language — the idea contained in resolutions we have step by step been pursuing since 1918 — should be implemented. On behalf of this conference I make that request.”

This speech of mine on Tamil’s classical-language status created great feeling and welcome among Tamil enthusiasts. It was also published extensively in English newspapers and drew the attention and consideration of those in the Union Government.

At a conference jointly conducted by the Thiruvananthapuram Tamil Sangam and the Capital Tamil Sangam on 3 and 4 May 2003 at the Thiruvananthapuram Tamil Sangam premises, a resolution stated that among the languages of the world Tamil, Greek, Latin, Sanskrit and Chinese were five ancient languages; that Greek, Latin and Sanskrit had gone out of common use yet had been accorded classical-language status; that the Government of India was refusing to declare Tamil — possessing antiquity, literary richness, the character of a mother language and living vitality — a classical language; and that Tamil should be accepted as a classical language and given the status due to it.

Again insisting on the demand that Tamil be declared a classical language, the Karanthai Tamil Sangam, through the efforts of its secretary S. Ramanathan, adopted a resolution on 20.7.2003. A procession followed. More than five thousand people — Tamil scholars and distinguished Tamil figures, teachers and students of the Karanthai Tamil Sangam educational institutions — took part.

On 18.8.2003, the Capital Tamil Sangam, Delhi Tamil Sangam and other Tamil organisations conducted a hunger strike in front of Parliament. On behalf of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam, Members of Parliament and Union Ministers T. R. Baalu, A. Raja, A. K. S. Vijayan, A. D. K. Jayaseelan, T. Venugopal, S. Agniraj, Adi Shankar, A. Krishnasamy, V. Vetriselvan, Professor Sankaralingam, S. Viduthalai Virumbi and R. Shanmugasundaram participated.

Regarding the hunger strike, Kumari Ananthan — chairman of the disciplinary committee of the Tamil Nadu Congress and president of the Tamil Nadu Gandhi Peravai — stated on 12.8.2003 that Tamil becoming the medium of instruction and a classical language had been a long-standing demand; that his literary organisation had celebrated the Bharati centenary for 52 weeks and the Bharathidasan centenary for 52 weeks; and that he would participate in the fast on its behalf.

Similarly, my dear friend R. M. Veerappan, president of the M.G.R. Kazhagam, stated on 13.8.2003 that Tamil scholars had urged that Tamil be made a classical language as early as ninety years before; that for several years Tamil scholars had, without party difference, conducted many agitations pressing the Union Government; that Tamil people and all parties supported the hunger strike to be held in Delhi; and that the M.G.R. Kazhagam would participate.

After the Delhi hunger strike demanding that Tamil be declared a classical language, the agitation committee submitted a petition on 19.8.2003 to Prime Minister Vajpayee and President Abdul Kalam asking that Tamil be accepted as a classical language.

Perungkavikko V. M. Sethuraman, organiser of the International Tamil Relations Forum; Pulavar T. Sundararajan, General Secretary of the Capital Tamil Sangam; Pulavar Arivudainambi; Professor Aaru. Alagappan; Dr T. K. S. Kannan; Bengaluru Tamil Sangam president Subramanian; and Delhi Tamil Sangam president Mukundan urged the Prime Minister and President that, since the Union Government had already declared Sanskrit, Persian and Arabic classical languages, Tamil too should be declared a classical language in view of its richness, distinctiveness and antiquity, and that the demand raised over the previous hundred years should be fulfilled. Prime Minister Vajpayee told them that the demand was already under consideration by the Union Government.

In a statement on 2.9.2003 about the demand, Dravidar Kazhagam president K. Veeramani said that linguists, scholars, political-party leaders, leaders of social organisations and Members of Parliament had all, with one voice and without a dissenting view, appealed to the National Democratic Alliance government led by Vajpayee to declare the ancient and rich Tamil language a classical language — but that the demand remained like a stone dropped into a well.

He further referred to an unofficial news report suddenly appearing in the press: that Union Human Resource Development Minister Murli Manohar Joshi had asked ministry officials to make a recommendation on declaring Tamil a classical language; that the officials had advised him that only a dead language no longer in use could be declared classical; and that although Tamil possessed every form of richness, it could not be so declared because it remained in use. Veeramani asked whether there could be a greater absurdity than saying Tamil was disqualified from classical-language status because, compared with Sanskrit, it was a rich living language, and called upon Tamils throughout the world and within India to treat this as a question of rights.

The source concludes that it cannot be denied that history has taken note of the negative approach and delay shown toward the demand by some leading figures such as Murli Manohar Joshi within the BJP, which held a major role in the National Democratic Alliance government at the Centre, attributing that approach to what the author describes as an enduring hostility toward the Dravidian language family, and that this delayed the declaration of Tamil as a classical language.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**30-12-2009**
