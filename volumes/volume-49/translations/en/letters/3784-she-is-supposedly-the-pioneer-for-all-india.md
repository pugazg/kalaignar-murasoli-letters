# 3784. She Is Supposedly the Pioneer for All India!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 29 July 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

Chief Minister Jayalalithaa announced yesterday, 27 July 2013, from Kodanadu that the Mettur Dam would be opened on 12 August for the samba crop. This confirms that the kuruvai crop has been abandoned this year.

This is precisely why, in a statement issued as early as 12 June, I asked the AIADMK Government at least then to wake up and clarify immediately: Will there be a kuruvai crop this year or not? Will water be available for cultivation or not? What are the farmers to do? What will the Government do to protect the livelihood of farmers and agricultural labourers?

A month after I asked whether the kuruvai crop would exist, the Chief Minister has now answered: there will be no kuruvai this year; there will only be samba.

Her statement has appeared in every newspaper. The very first paragraph made me feel compelled to write this letter. It reads:

> “With the aim of bringing about a second Green Revolution in Tamil Nadu, the Tamil Nadu Government under my leadership has been a pioneer for the whole of India in strengthening irrigation sources, introducing new technologies into agriculture and creating awareness among farmers.”

Jayalalithaa says that the Tamil Nadu Government under her leadership is a pioneer for all India! If I mention this, she will immediately say I am anguished and jealous. As I have already written, she takes pride in repeatedly saying “the Government under my leadership.”

One may congratulate oneself when good is done under one’s leadership. But cultivation failed last year. This year there has been no kuruvai so far. Do you know when the Mettur Dam—normally opened on 12 June—was opened last year? Only on 17 September.

Because the dam was opened so late, Delta farmers asked that water be supplied until the end of February. Normally the dam remains open for Delta irrigation until 28 January. But what did the Government that continually calls itself “the Government under my leadership, a pioneer for India” do? It stopped the irrigation release at eight in the evening on 21 December—before January had even begun—and closed the gates.

If the Mettur Dam was not opened on schedule in 2012, was it opened as usual on 12 June at least in 2013? No. On 27 July, the Chief Minister announced from Kodanadu that it would be opened on 12 August. Yet in the very first paragraph she says Tamil Nadu is a pioneer for India in agriculture. This, apparently, is the definition of “pioneer”!

The Chief Minister further says that, immediately after assuming office in 2011, instead of opening the Mettur Dam for irrigation on 12 June, she ordered it opened for the first time six days early, on 6 June.

The Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam remained in office in Tamil Nadu until 13 May 2011. Had its term continued into June, the water would have been released then too. The AIADMK came to office in May. What miracle did they perform within twenty-three days to open the dam on 6 June? When the dam has filled, it must be opened. What new achievement lies in that?

Was it not because the water level had risen through the efforts taken under the DMK Government that they could open the Mettur Dam on 6 June? The Chief Minister says that this early release enabled Tamil Nadu to achieve a record food production of 101.52 lakh metric tonnes in 2011–12 and earn an award from the Union Government. Can she conceal that this distinction resulted from the many constructive efforts continuously undertaken by the DMK Government up to 2011?

The child was born to the DMK Government in 2011. Is it right for the AIADMK Government merely to name that child and claim the glory?

Chief Minister Jayalalithaa also says in her statement: “My Government has caused the Cauvery Water Disputes Tribunal’s final award to be published in the Union Gazette and continues to struggle for the establishment of the Cauvery Management Board and the Cauvery Water Regulation Committee.” Once again it is the boast of “my Government.”

Was her Government responsible for publishing the Tribunal’s final award in the Union Gazette? It may be true that publication occurred during her term. But may she claim the credit?

While hearing the case, it was the Supreme Court judge who asked why the Tribunal’s final award had still not been published in the Union Gazette and, on the Court’s own initiative, ordered its immediate publication. It was because of that direction that the award was gazetted.

The case may have been filed by Jayalalithaa, but was it filed with a prayer seeking publication of the final award in the Gazette? It was not. The unshakeable truth is that the judge independently directed publication.

Jayalalithaa herself acknowledged this in an interview on 20 February 2013. When a journalist asked:

> “Madam, you have charged that the DMK has not done anything to get the Cauvery final Award published.”

She replied in English:

> “I hold the same charge against them and what has compelled the Central Government to notify the Award is the Supreme Court. So we are grateful to the Supreme Court for giving us Justice.”

This appeared in her own party newspaper.

Thus Jayalalithaa herself told journalists that it was the Supreme Court which directed publication of the final award in the Union Gazette, and that gratitude was due to the Court for delivering justice. Yet do you know what she said in the same breath? She called it “a magnificent victory achieved by me and my Government through twenty-two years of continuous and tireless struggle.”

The Tribunal’s final award was delivered only on 5 February 2007, during the DMK Government—six years earlier. Yet the Chief Minister spoke in the style of “I am the song; I am the emotion,” declaring it the magnificent victory of herself and her Government after twenty-two years of tireless struggle.

Not only that: a felicitation was held at Thanjavur on 9 March 2013. For what? To honour the Chief Minister for having “fought single-handedly” and secured Cauvery water. They presented her with a statue titled “Ponniyin Selvi.”

Jayalalithaa narrated the story of Karna and Duryodhana and, in her accustomed manner, praised herself as a generous giver like Karna and as the one person possessing a helpful heart. Newspapers printed page after page of that speech. What has happened now?

Was Cauvery water secured in accordance with the Tribunal’s final award? Did it arrive at the proper time last year? Was the Mettur Dam opened on its appointed date? Was there a kuruvai crop last year? When kuruvai failed, did even the confusingly announced relief reach the farmers properly? Has the Union Government immediately established the Cauvery Management Board and the Cauvery Water Regulation Committee? Nothing has occurred except the strange spectacle of a victory celebration.

To the person who praises herself as the cause of publication of the Tribunal’s final award in the Union Gazette, I ask this: when the award was delivered on 5 February 2007, did the AIADMK or Jayalalithaa accept it as a good award? Did she not issue a statement saying that Karunanidhi’s continuous acts of betrayal had caused the Tribunal’s final award to be harmful to Tamil Nadu, and that the minority DMK Government must resign accepting moral responsibility?

If so, will they at least admit that Jayalalithaa caused an award which she herself called harmful to Tamil Nadu to be published in the Union Gazette?

On 21 March 1991, did Jayalalithaa not belittle the Tribunal by saying that even if it gave an award, Cauvery water would reach Tamil Nadu only if Karnataka itself opened the dam, and that the award could be useful only if Karnataka agreed to a fair and favourable decision? Did she not mockingly say that even if the Tribunal delivered its award, the Karnataka Chief Minister was under no compulsion to implement it?

Why, on 2 April 2002, in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly itself, did she not describe the Cauvery River Authority as an inactive and toothless authority, and say that the AIADMK had left the BJP alliance without accepting it—thereby lowering the standing of the authority headed by the Prime Minister?

Later, at the AIADMK Cabinet meeting held on 21 June 2002, did the Government not decide and announce that it would not participate in meetings of the Cauvery River Authority, insulting the Authority itself? Did she not later reverse that decision and attend those very meetings?

Even earlier, in a statement dated 21 September 1998, did Jayalalithaa not say:

> “The Cauvery River Authority headed by the Prime Minister is a very big fraud perpetrated on the farmers of the Cauvery Delta in Tamil Nadu. This Authority headed by the Prime Minister has no such authority or power to order or implement anything regarding the release of Cauvery Water. I categorically state that the new Authority headed by the Prime Minister will not benefit Tamil Nadu even by an iota.”

One could continue writing much more. I have been compelled to recall these past events only because the announcement on opening the Mettur Dam claims that “the Tamil Nadu Government under her leadership is a pioneer for all India,” and because her statement says that she and her Government caused the Cauvery Tribunal’s final award to be published in the Union Gazette.

I write this letter to place the true position before the people—not because I wish to find fault with anyone.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**29 July 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. **Kuruvai** and **samba** are major paddy cultivation seasons in the Cauvery Delta.
2. **Ponniyin Selvi** here is an honorific presented through a commemorative statue; literally, “daughter of the Ponni,” an old name for the Cauvery.
3. The source reproduces Jayalalithaa’s English interview reply and her 1998 statement in English; both are retained verbatim.
4. Missing word spaces in `என்பதைப்` on PDF page 162 and `மத்திய அரசின்` on PDF page 164 were restored after visual comparison with the scans.
