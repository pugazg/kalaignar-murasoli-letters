# 3868. I Give You the List; Spread It Far and Wide!

## Translator's Note

This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The Tamil below is a source-checked derived reading copy. Clear OCR corruption in bullet markers, numerals, dates, punctuation, and word breaks has been repaired; no listed programme or figure has been silently invented. The canonical source file remains unchanged and authoritative.

`Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

## English Translation

Because Chief Minister Jayalalithaa goes from town to town at her campaign meetings listing what she claims to have achieved for Tamil Nadu in the past three years, I am obliged to list some achievements of the DMK government from 2006 to 2011. This is only a summary, not the complete list.

* Cooperative loans of Rs 7,000 crore waived for 2,240,739 farming families.
* Crop-loan interest reduced from nine per cent in 2005-06 to seven, five and four per cent in successive years, then abolished in 2009-10. Since 2006, crop loans totalling Rs 8,838.06 crore were given to 3,671,372 farmers.
* Paddy procurement raised from Rs 600 a quintal in 2005-06 to Rs 1,050 for ordinary varieties and Rs 1,100 for fine varieties in 2010-11.
* 117 Uzhavar Sandhais revived and forty-five new ones established.
* The government subsidised fifty per cent of crop-insurance premiums. Participation grew from 100,000 farmers in 2005-06 to 901,643 in 2009-10; Rs 974 crore in compensation was paid.
* Sugarcane prices rose from Rs 1,014 a tonne in 2005-06 to Rs 2,000.
* Rs 5.25 crore in loans and interest owed by Adi Dravidar farmers to TAHDCO as of 31 March 2006 was waived.
* Interest and penalty interest on non-farm mortgage loans was waived, with discharge upon repayment of principal.
* The Cauvery-Gundar river-linking project was launched at Rs 189 crore and the Tamiraparani-Karumeniyar-Nambiyar project at Rs 369 crore.
* Food-grain production rose from 6.117 million tonnes in 2005-06 to 7.101 million in 2008-09 and 8.429 million in 2009-10.
* 27,294 farmer self-help groups received Rs 27.294 crore in revolving funds, while 32,940 groups received crop loans of Rs 402.56 crore.
* Old motors were replaced free for small and marginal farmers and with a fifty per cent subsidy for large farmers under a five-year energy-saving programme.
* Free electricity was provided to 105,494 handloom weavers, 90,547 power-loom weavers and 239,511 farmers who had paid special connection charges; phased free connections for another 200,000 pump sets were ordered.
* Thirty-five unorganised-worker welfare boards enrolled 21,355,884 members, and assistance of Rs 1,011.628 crore went to 2,141,692 worker families.
* A Coconut Farmers' Welfare Board chaired by S. Rajkumar Mandradiar was formed on 27 August 2010.
* Free colour televisions were planned for 17.28 million families at Rs 3,742.4259 crore; 16,259,526 had received them.
* Twenty-nine lakh families received free gas stoves and connections at a cost of Rs 661 crore.
* 179,002 landless poor agricultural families received 212,995 acres free, and 830,495 poor families received free house-site pattas.
* Kamarajar's birthday was celebrated in schools as Education Development Day.
* 7.3 million children aged two to fifteen received nutritious meals with eggs five days a week, and bananas for those who did not eat eggs.
* Special fees were abolished for more than five million Tamil-medium pupils in government and aided schools, and public-examination fees for 1.1 million Class 10 and 12 students.
* Tuition fees were abolished for more than 375,000 arts and science undergraduates and, from 2010-11, for MA and MSc students.
* Each year, 10,000 poor dropouts received job-oriented community-college training and Rs 1,000 assistance through the Open University.
* Free bus passes went annually to 2.482 million school students and 435,000 college students.
* Free education for poor women was extended from undergraduate through postgraduate study.
* Entrance examinations for professional courses were abolished.
* First-generation professional-college students admitted through single-window counselling had Rs 20,000 tuition waived: 67,405 first-year and 10,750 lateral-entry students benefited.
* Engineering admission cut-offs were substantially lowered.
* Five Anna Universities of Technology were established in Chennai, Coimbatore, Tiruchirappalli, Tirunelveli and Madurai.
* Fourteen new government arts and science colleges opened after 2006 at Orathanadu, Perambalur, Valparai, Surandai, Kulithalai, Lalgudi, Mettur, Pudukkottai, Theni, Tiruvannamalai, Villupuram, Pennagaram, Tirupattur and Vedaranyam.
* Six new medical colleges were established under the “one medical college per district” policy, and government engineering colleges in twelve previously unserved places.
* Tamil was made compulsory through Class 10.
* The Central Institute of Classical Tamil was established in Chennai, fulfilling a century-old dream; the World Classical Tamil Conference was held in Coimbatore in June 2010.
* The thousandth anniversary of Rajaraja's Thanjavur Big Temple was celebrated in September 2010.
* Works of 113 Tamil scholars were nationalised, with Rs 7.61 crore in compassionate grants.
* Aid to indigent artists doubled from Rs 500 to Rs 1,000 monthly. Since 2006, 9,563 artists benefited, with 5,041 currently receiving aid.
* Monthly pensions of Rs 3,000 plus medical allowance went to 999 Tamil scholars, and family pensions to 528 heirs; pensions likewise went to 339 border defenders and 166 heirs.
* Renovation and consecration works costing Rs 523 crore were completed at 4,724 temples.
* Marriage assistance under all schemes, including the Moovalur Ramamirtham Ammaiyar scheme, rose from Rs 10,000 to Rs 25,000; 467,419 poor women received Rs 882.06 crore.
* Under the Rs 6,000 maternity-assistance scheme, 2,576,612 poor women received Rs 1,389.42 crore.
* 12,904 unmarried poor women over fifty received Rs 500 monthly.
* The Varumun Kappom preventive-health programme was revived; 17,705,085 people benefited through 18,742 camps.
* Three nurses were posted for round-the-clock service at 1,421 existing and 116 new primary health centres. Deliveries there rose from 82,532 in 2005-06 to 298,853 in 2009-10.
* Government support of Rs 20,000 to Rs 1 lakh was provided for children's heart surgery. Under two schemes, 3,264 children received surgery at twenty-eight private hospitals at a cost of Rs 17.10 crore.
* The statewide 108 emergency ambulance service began with EMRI on 15 September 2008 with 445 vehicles and served 808,907 people.
* A new medical-insurance scheme provided government employees up to Rs 2 lakh over four years.
* Kalaignar Insurance for Life-Saving Treatment began in July 2009. 13.4 million families registered, and 303,328 poor people received surgery costing Rs 781.40 crore.
* Nalamana Tamizhagam promoted screening and awareness for heart disease, diabetes and cancer; a free hearse service carried home those who died after hospital treatment.
* Twenty-seven MoUs worth Rs 62,349 crore promised about 235,464 jobs; action was taken through twenty-four orders for fifty-one factories, twelve of which opened.
* 401,704 educated unemployed young people received Rs 284 crore assistance; about 509,336 young people received new government jobs.
* 270,000 young people who failed to renew employment registration were allowed renewal with seniority from 2001.
* Assistance for destitute elderly people, widows and persons with disabilities rose from Rs 200 to Rs 400 in 2006 and Rs 500 in 2010; 2,371,370 people received it. The manifesto promised Rs 750.
* Maintenance assistance for severely disabled persons rose from Rs 200 to Rs 500, benefiting 10,000 annually.
* The Mahalir Thittam begun by the DMK in 1989 produced 554,538 women's self-help groups with Rs 9,032 crore in loans.
* Since 2006, 115,493 new women's groups with 2.695 million members received Rs 7,756.13 crore in economic credit; 281,883 groups received Rs 281.88 crore in revolving funds.
* 96,699 urban self-help groups received Rs 96.70 crore in revolving-fund subsidy, and 79,643 young members received job-linked vocational training.
* Under Anaithu Grama Anna Marumalarchi Thittam, infrastructure works costing Rs 2,549 crore covered all 12,618 village panchayats.
* Similar works costing Rs 280 crore covered 561 town panchayats, while thirty financially weak municipalities received Rs 75 lakh each.
* Rs 793 crore owed by corporations and municipalities to the government was waived.
* Local-body revenue sharing rose from eight per cent, Rs 2,112 crore, in 2006-07 to ten per cent, Rs 4,030 crore, in 2010-11.
* Improvements and maintenance covering 57,787 kilometres of roads were completed at Rs 12,094 crore.
* 4,945 kilometres of single-lane roads were widened to intermediate lanes, 2,611 kilometres to two lanes and 168 kilometres of two-lane roads to multiple lanes. Apart from thirty-two kilometres that could not be widened, State highways had no remaining single-lane stretches; another 11,219 kilometres were improved.
* 1,046 bridges and 3,800 minor bridges were built for Rs 881 crore; 3,226 of Tamil Nadu's 4,676 kilometres of National Highways became four-lane roads.
* Head tax, surcharge and water cess were abolished. Dry-land tax fell from Rs 15 to Rs 2 an acre and wet-land tax from Rs 50 to Rs 5.
* Erode, Tiruppur, Vellore and Thoothukudi became corporations; Ariyalur and Tiruppur became new districts; three new revenue divisions and nine new taluks were created.
* 12,137 new buses, and another 3,000, were introduced without fare increases.
* Muslims received 3.5 per cent internal reservation and Arunthathiyars three per cent.
* A law enabled people of every caste to become temple priests, and 216 people from various castes were trained.
* Ninety-five new Periyar Memorial Samathuvapurams were added to 145 existing ones. Sixty-five had opened and thirty were nearing completion.
* The world-class Anna Centenary Library, costing Rs 179 crore, opened at Kotturpuram on 15 September 2010.
* The new Assembly-Secretariat complex at Omandurar Government Estate opened under a Rs 910-crore sanction.
* The Rs 100-crore Adyar Tholkappia Poonga and the Rs 8-crore Semmozhi Poonga were created.
* The Minjur desalination plant opened on 31 July 2010; the Rs 908-crore Nemmeli plant was under construction.
* Chennai Metro work proceeded at an estimated Rs 14,600 crore with Japanese assistance.
* The Rs 1,929-crore Hogenakkal and Rs 630-crore Ramanathapuram combined water schemes advanced, and the Prime Minister laid the foundation for the Rs 1,655-crore Chennai Port-Maduravoyal elevated road.
* The anti-conversion law was repealed to protect religious freedom.
* The Third Police Commission under R. Poornalingam was formed; 278 of its 444 recommendations had been implemented.
* 212,981 nutritious-meal workers received time-scale pay and pensions.
* TESMA and ESMA were repealed, withdrawn benefits restored, and Sixth Pay Commission recommendations implemented from 1 January 2006 at an annual additional cost of Rs 5,155.79 crore.
* A historic Rs 11,093 crore in arrears was sanctioned for employees and teachers.
* One-person committee recommendations took effect from 1 August 2010 at Rs 200 crore, benefiting nearly 200,000 employees and teachers.
* Teachers' pay anomalies were addressed with Rs 163 crore in additional concessions, benefiting 213,000 teachers.
* The monthly transport allowance for disabled employees in government, local bodies and aided institutions rose from Rs 300 to Rs 1,000 from 1 October 2010.
* Kalaignar's Housing Scheme aimed to replace 2.1 million huts with concrete homes in six years. In the current year, 300,000 houses were planned at Rs 75,000 subsidy each, costing Rs 2,250 crore, and work orders began on 15 August 2010.
* The first house was handed over at Vallampadugai near Chidambaram on 9 October 2010. So far 77,000 houses had been completed and 60,486 occupied.
* Eligibility cards went to another 1.2 million beneficiaries; in phase two, 300,000 more houses were approved and work orders issued for 298,162.

Udanpirappē, I have gathered here only the headings of what the DMK government accomplished in five years. You know, and the people know, that these were not mere announcements but things delivered to Tamil Nadu's people. When the Chief Minister goes from town to town listing what the AIADMK government claims to have achieved in three years, I trust you will turn this list into leaflets, carry it to the voters and perform that duty. That is why I have written it here.

With affection,

M.K.

21 March 2014
