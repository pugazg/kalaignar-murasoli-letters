# 3594. Then and Now, the Position Is the Same!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

Thanthai Periyar would often say, “The kitten has come out of the bag!” In just that way, on the issue of Sri Lankan Tamils too, Chief Minister Jayalalithaa's true state of mind and course of action have now become known to everyone. When Tamils came together and argued that the death sentences of Perarivalan, Santhan and Murugan should be commuted, and asked that a unanimous resolution be adopted on behalf of the Legislative Assembly, Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Jayalalithaa at first stubbornly refused. Finally, after witnessing the ethnic awakening of the Tamils, she had that resolution adopted in the Assembly. While doing so, however, Jayalalithaa said in her speech that under Article 161 of the Constitution neither the State Chief Minister nor the State Governor had authority in this matter. To this day, Jayalalithaa has repeatedly maintained the same position—that the State Chief Minister has no authority in this matter. Even so, merely because she had the resolution passed in the Assembly, some people thanked her and sang her praises, saying that “Amma” had saved the lives of all three!

But in the statement she read under Rule 110 in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly on 30 August 2011, she herself said that His Excellency the Governor had given approval on 21 April 2000 after accepting the decision of the Cabinet headed by Karunanidhi. If the Tamil Nadu government and Jayalalithaa genuinely have concern to save these three lives by following the same course that we followed, I and several other parties in Tamil Nadu had requested that the Tamil Nadu Cabinet adopt a resolution and send its recommendation to the Governor. Yet Jayalalithaa has not come forward to do so even now. When she has not come forward to convene the Cabinet and pass such a resolution, the petition now filed in the High Court on behalf of the Tamil Nadu government asks that the plea filed by the three persons sentenced to death seeking commutation of their death sentences be dismissed. From this alone, I hope that those who had been “singing Jayalalithaa's praises” will clearly see that “the kitten has come out of the bag.”

Yesterday, MDMK General Secretary Vaiko, speaking to reporters, recalled precisely what had happened under the DMK government and said that today's Tamil Nadu Cabinet should pass a resolution seeking commutation of the death sentences of the three and recommend it to the Governor. Even while recalling what I did then and what I have said now, he has vented his anger by calling Jayalalithaa and me “two sides of the same coin”—and that too must not be forgotten. If Vaiko sees as two sides of the same coin me, who made him a Rajya Sabha member three times in recognition of the work he did in the Kazhagam and kept him close and appreciated him, and Jayalalithaa, who made him wait until the very last moment and then cut his throat politically, it only shows that he has still not changed. What can one do? Is there not a proverb that says, “When the cat grows weak, even the mouse claims it as a brother-in-law”?

Another senior advocate who argued in the High Court for the prisoners facing execution, along with Mr. Ram Jethmalani, was Mr. Colin Gonsalves. *The Indian Express* reported him as saying:

> “The Chief Minister has the power to reduce Capital Punishment to life in Jail, and it is the right time to convene a Meeting and take a Cabinet Decision; ....... Jayalalithaa can exercise her power to stay the death sentence under Article 161 of the Constitution .... said one of the three advocates, Colin Gonsalves, who appeared before the High Court for the convicts.”

**Tamil rendering printed immediately after the English quotation in the source:**

> “The Chief Minister has the authority to reduce the death sentence to life imprisonment, and this is the right time to convene the Cabinet and take a decision on it..... Jayalalithaa can exercise the authority under Article 161 of the Constitution to stay the death sentence..... said Mr. Colin Gonsalves, one of the three advocates who argued before the Madras High Court.”

In the statement she read in the Legislative Assembly under Rule 110 on 29 August 2011, Chief Minister Jayalalithaa referred to the decision taken at the Cabinet meeting held under the DMK government on 19 April 2000: clemency could be shown to Nalini alone among those convicted in the Rajiv Gandhi assassination case by commuting her death sentence to life imprisonment, while the mercy petitions of the others could be rejected. Without realising it, she thereby acknowledged that fundamentally it was the DMK government that was responsible for Nalini's death sentence being reduced to life imprisonment. The Supreme Court confirmed the death sentences of these four, including Nalini, on 11 May 1999. Their review petition before the same Supreme Court was dismissed on 8 October 1999. The Governor rejected the mercy petitions they submitted to him on 17 October 1999, doing so on 27 October 1999. In the case filed against this in the Madras High Court, the Court on 25 November 1999 set aside the Governor's rejection order and directed that a fresh order be issued after obtaining the advice of the Cabinet. It was in those circumstances that the DMK Cabinet met and took its decision on 19 April 2000.

In the same statement read by Jayalalithaa, she also said: “The Tamil Nadu government, by its letter dated 28-4-2000, forwarded to the Central government the mercy petitions submitted by these three persons to His Excellency the President.” Through her own statement, Jayalalithaa has thus revealed that it was because the DMK government forwarded those mercy petitions to the Central government on 28 April 2000 that the sentences of the three were not carried out and they were able to remain in prison through these eleven years. It was because the President's office had taken no decision for the past eleven years on the petitions sent after the Cabinet decision that the advocates have now cited that very delay as one of their grounds before the High Court, and the Court has stayed the sentences.

Do you know what Jayalalithaa said in a statement published in the 23 October 2008 issue of *Namadhu MGR*?

> “After I assumed office as Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu in 1991, I urged the Central government that, for peace to prevail in Tamil Nadu, the Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam, a terrorist organisation, should be banned. It was because of my strenuous efforts that the Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam was banned by the Central government on 14-5-1992. V. Prabhakaran has been declared a proclaimed offender by the designated court in the Rajiv Gandhi assassination case. Activities supporting such a banned organisation are now taking place again in Tamil Nadu. Film directors Seeman and Ameer, who participated in the demonstration at Rameswaram condemning the massacre of Sri Lankan Tamils, have expressed several objectionable, anti-national views against India's sovereignty. Karunanidhi has so far taken no action whatsoever against those expressing views against India's sovereignty. ......... If a government headed by me were now in office in Tamil Nadu, I would certainly have arrested those who spoke in support of banned organisations. During my rule, I took action under the ‘POTA’ Act against people who spoke in this manner and imprisoned them. Now, with the foolish confidence that the ‘POTA’ Act has been repealed, some have begun openly expressing anti-national views. Even without ‘POTA’, action can be taken under the existing Unlawful Activities Prevention Act against those who speak against India's sovereignty. But the Chief Minister of the minority DMK government has no mind to take action. Whenever Karunanidhi comes to power, speeches supporting the Liberation Tigers openly take place in Tamil Nadu. Supporting such a banned terrorist organisation is an anti-national act. There can be no difference of opinion on this.”

That was the kind of statement Jayalalithaa issued in 2008.

I wish to recall once again the statements of two people who had earlier been sentenced to death and were saved during DMK rule.

In particular, Comrade Thiyagu was one of the comrades of the Left movement who was sentenced to death and later saved. I reproduce what he said as it was reported:

> “In accordance with the party's decision, a man named Muthuthangappa from the Orathanadu area was killed in 1970. Three of us—Comrades Rengasamy Lenin, Gurumurthy and I—were arrested in the case and lodged in Tiruchirappalli Central Prison. The case against us was tried in the Sessions Court, and in 1972 all three of us were sentenced to death. We appealed to the Madras High Court, but the High Court too confirmed our death sentences. Kalaignar was then Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu. We petitioned the Governor to commute the death sentences confirmed by the High Court. The Governor sought the Tamil Nadu government's views on the matter. Kalaignar then convened his Cabinet, discussed my mercy petition, decided that the sentence could be reduced to life imprisonment, and recommended this to the Governor. Accordingly, the Governor cancelled our death sentences and reduced them to life imprisonment. Thus we, who had been about to kiss the hangman's noose, were saved.
>
> “Mercy petitions can be sent any number of times to the President of the country and the Governor of the State. Even if mercy petitions are rejected by the President, the State government can recommend to the President or the Governor that the sentence be reduced. Debates over whether there is or is not authority under our Constitution have always occurred. That is what is happening now. We say the authority exists. Jayalalithaa says she has no authority. Since the implication is, ‘If I had the authority, I would save them,’ why cannot Jayalalithaa at least make an appeal to the President, who does have the authority, asking that the three be saved? A person who says she has no legal authority can at least do this.”

Comrade Thiyagu said this, and *Nakkheeran* published it.

Like Thiyagu's, the death sentence of Pennadam poet Kaliyaperumal was also cancelled. He is no longer alive; he died in 2007. In an interview given to *Nakkheeran*, his younger son Chozhanambiyar explained:

> “In a case involving the killing of a police informer, my father Kaliyaperumal, my brother Valluvan, I, and seven members of my family were arrested. The trial was held in the Cuddalore Sessions Court. In 1971 Judge Singaravelu sentenced my father Kaliyaperumal and my elder brother Valluvan to death. The other five of us received life sentences. Only my brother Valluvan appealed to the Madras High Court, and the High Court itself reduced his sentence to life imprisonment. At that time Comrade Kalyanasundaram was a Member of Parliament. When he met V. V. Giri, who was President, and spoke to him about Kaliyaperumal, Giri suggested that signatures be collected from the people and sent. Accordingly, we held a conference in Pennadam, collected one lakh signatures and sent them to the Tamil Nadu Governor in 1974. He forwarded the petition to Kalaignar, who was then Chief Minister. Kalaignar discussed it in his Cabinet, said that the State government's authority could be used to reduce poet Kaliyaperumal's sentence, took a decision accordingly and recommended it to the Governor. Accepting the State government's recommendation, the Governor cancelled my father's death sentence and reduced it to life imprisonment. Therefore, if a State Chief Minister has the will, anyone can be saved from the death penalty. The question now is whether the State Chief Minister has such a will.”

Keeping these two statements in mind, I had said that, as one of those who had been instrumental in securing the release of some people from the grip of death, I was pressing both the Central and State governments.

Instead of wasting time by clinging firmly to what someone has told her—that she has no authority under Article 161 of the Constitution to save these three—Jayalalithaa should at least now recognise the authority available to her and come forward to save the lives of Perarivalan, Santhan and Murugan, who are suspended between the gallows and continued life, and thereby fulfil the heartfelt wish of Tamils across the world. Showing obstinacy and allowing time to pass in this matter will only cause setbacks.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**30 October 2011**

**Note printed after the closing in the source:** I stated many years ago that, in general, I do not accept the “death penalty” itself. I have expressed this view many times without regard to party differences—particularly even in the Dharmapuri matter!

## Letter-specific notes

1. **Udanpirappē** is retained as Kalaignar's characteristic political-familial address.
2. The recurring idiom `பூனைக்குட்டி வெளியே வந்துவிட்டது` is translated as **“the kitten has come out of the bag”**, preserving Kalaignar's metaphor and its sense of a concealed position becoming exposed.
3. The proverb `பூனை இளைத்தால் எலி கூட “மச்சான்” முறை கொண்டாடும்` is translated close to its imagery rather than replaced with an unrelated English proverb.
4. The Colin Gonsalves passage printed in English in the Tamil source is reproduced verbatim, including its source capitalization and ellipses. The Tamil parenthetical rendering that follows is also translated in full rather than summarized.
5. Long quotations attributed in the letter to Jayalalithaa, Thiyagu and Chozhanambiyar are translated from the audited canonical Tamil as printed; no outside versions have been substituted.
6. The parenthetical note printed **after** `30-10-2011` is preserved as source text rather than treated as material outside the letter record.
