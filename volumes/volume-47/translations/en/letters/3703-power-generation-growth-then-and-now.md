# 3703. Power Generation Growth—Then and Now!

## English translation

Udanpirappē,

Replying to the Governor's Address, Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Jayalalithaa read in the Assembly a lengthy explanation prepared by officials on power generation. She said that during the AIADMK Government from 2001 to 2006 steps had been taken to establish a 500-MW thermal power station in North Chennai; 1,000-MW thermal power stations at Vallur jointly with the National Thermal Power Corporation; 1,000-MW thermal power stations at Thoothukudi jointly with Neyveli Lignite Corporation; a 500-MW pumped-storage project at Kundah; and a 95-MW gas-turbine power station at Valuthur in Ramanathapuram district. She then claimed that the present power shortage was caused because the DMK Government from 2006 to 2011 failed to complete these projects.

Take North Chennai Thermal Power Station Stage II. Although a Government Order had been issued on 26 June 2001 for Unit 1, estimated at ₹2,475 crore and intended to generate 600 MW, nothing happened on that project during the five years until the AIADMK left office. It was only under the DMK Government, from 18 February 2008, that discussions were held with BHEL and the work was accelerated. The Chief Minister concealed in the Assembly that the project had remained idle during her Government.

For Unit 2 of North Chennai Thermal Power Station Stage II, a Government Order was issued under the DMK Government on 14 July 2008 for a 600-MW unit at an estimated cost of ₹2,175 crore, and work began.

For Mettur Thermal Power Station Stage III, the DMK Government issued a Government Order on 2 May 2007 for a ₹3,100-crore, 600-MW project and work proceeded. It is this project that has now begun generating electricity.

On 12 July 2002, the Tamil Nadu Electricity Board entered into a Memorandum of Understanding with the National Thermal Power Corporation in Chennai for 1,000 MW. The Chief Minister cites this as something begun during her Government. But only the MOU was signed under the AIADMK; no subsequent work took place. During the DMK Government, the capacity was increased to 1,500 MW and, at an estimated cost of ₹8,000 crore, BHEL began rapidly implementing the Vallur project from 13 August 2007.

Likewise, an MOU between Neyveli Lignite Corporation and the Tamil Nadu Electricity Board had been signed at Thoothukudi on 19 June 2003, but no work took place under the AIADMK Government. Under the DMK Government, from 28 January 2009, BHEL rapidly implemented works for a 1,000-MW project costing ₹4,909 crore.

It was also during the DMK Government, on 14 December 2009, that an order was issued to modernise cooperative and public-sector sugar mills and produce 183 MW of cogenerated power at an estimated cost of ₹1,126 crore.

Under the AIADMK Government, a Government Order had been issued on 27 March 2002 for the Bhavani Kattalai Barrage Hydro Electric Project III, estimated at ₹396.60 crore and intended to generate 30 MW. But the work was accelerated only during the DMK Government.

On 26 October 2007, an MOU was signed for the Udangudi project, a joint venture between BHEL and the Tamil Nadu Electricity Board, to generate 1,600 MW at an estimated cost of ₹8,700 crore. Land for the project had already been acquired during DMK rule. Generation had been planned to begin with the first 800-MW unit in January 2013 and the second 800-MW unit in July. But after the AIADMK came to power in 2011, implementation slowed. The Tamil Nadu Government has now announced that it will itself execute the project. If this does not remain merely an announcement and actually begins, that would be good.

On 8 December 2006, a Government Order was issued for the Ennore Thermal Power Station expansion, a ₹3,136-crore project to generate 600 MW, and work proceeded. Generation had been planned to begin in August 2013. But after the AIADMK came to power in 2011, the project was shelved.

On 5 January 2009, an order was issued for a thermal power station at the Ennore Special Economic Zone (SEZ), Kattupalli, to generate 1,600 MW at an estimated cost of ₹9,613.18 crore, and preliminary work was under way. Under the present AIADMK Government, this project is not being pursued rapidly with due attention.

Brother Singara Rathinasabapathi, who worked for many years in the Electricity Board before retiring and who serves as general secretary of the Electricity Corporation Labour Progressive Federation and treasurer of the federation, has already explained these generation projects in detail to the public on television.

Jayalalithaa says in one paragraph of her reply that the DMK Government did nothing on power projects begun under the AIADMK, and in the very next paragraph says that “less than 50 per cent of the work had been carried out on the North Chennai Thermal Power Station project approved during my Government in 2005.” By her own admission, therefore, about fifty per cent of the work was completed during DMK rule. It is because the DMK Government showed such concern that 600 MW from that project is expected from April this year. Likewise, another 600 MW is expected from May from the second unit of the North Chennai Thermal Power Station begun during DMK rule.

The 600-MW Mettur thermal project begun during DMK rule is yielding 608 MW. Ironically, the day after the Chief Minister spoke happily in the Assembly about this project, there was news that a tube fault had occurred in the new Mettur unit and that the trial run had been stopped.

The Vallur thermal project too progressed rapidly during the DMK Government and its first unit has begun generation. Yet Jayalalithaa described even that in the Assembly as though it had come about through her efforts.

Overall, projects implemented through great effort during DMK rule have been read out by Chief Minister Jayalalithaa as though they were achievements of her last nineteen months. Still, perhaps it is a benefit to Tamil Nadu that she did not shut down these power-generation schemes as she did other projects begun during DMK rule. The people of Tamil Nadu know well, however, that she continued them not because of concern that the problem should be solved, but because of the opposition shown by the public.

Jayalalithaa further said in her reply that because “power projects totalling 2,500 MW had been initiated during my Government, the DMK Government had to spend about ₹7,000 crore on power projects.” In saying so, she unintentionally admits both that nothing had been spent during her own Government on those 2,500 MW of projects and that the DMK Government, instead of shelving them merely because they had originated under Jayalalithaa, spent about ₹7,000 crore on them.

Amma also says that Tamil Nadu had no power shortage during the AIADMK Government from 2001 to 2006 and that the shortage arose only under the DMK Government. But the reason Tamil Nadu did not face a shortage during that AIADMK period was that, in the preceding DMK Government from 1996 to 2001, eleven projects had created capacity for about 1,200 MW: the Basin Bridge diesel power project, Thirumoorthy mini-hydel project, Mukurthi mini-hydel project, Samalpatti diesel power project, Thirumakottai gas-turbine project, Pillaiperumalnallur power project, Samayanallur diesel power project, Aliyar mini-hydel project, Neyveli Zero Unit project, Valuthur gas power project and Perunchani mini-hydel project. It is therefore clear that DMK Governments paved the way for the growth of the Electricity Board.

Finally, one more fact. In statement after statement, Jayalalithaa repeatedly speaks as though I left the Tamil Nadu Government burdened with enormous debts. Explanations have been given many times, yet she repeats the same charge. The DMK returned to power for the fifth time only in May 2006. Even before that, on 31 March 2006—after Jayalalithaa's five-year Government—the Tamil Nadu Government's total debt liability already stood at ₹57,457 crore. Likewise, in 2005–06 Jayalalithaa left office after placing a debt burden of about ₹5,000 crore on the Electricity Board.

When we came to office, we did not issue statement after statement crying, “Jayalalithaa has left ₹57,000 crore of debt on Tamil Nadu and ₹5,000 crore of losses in the Electricity Board,” and blaming others. Nor did we try to escape responsibility by shifting blame. A Government inevitably has to borrow. Particularly in a developing country like India, welfare schemes needed by the people often can be implemented only through borrowing.

Jayalalithaa's habit has become to throw dust and abuse in her own style on every one of these matters; for us to give factual explanations to each charge; and, even after that, for her to repeat what she had already said and confuse and divert everyone. The Tamil people, who know the difference between a green snake and a green creeper, understand this too. Yet because it is my duty to explain the truth again, I have written this letter.

With affection,
M.K.
11-02-2013
