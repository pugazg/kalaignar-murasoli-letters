# 3579. Is a Tax-Free Budget Not a Distilled Falsehood?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 6 August 2011

**Udanpirappē,**

In the second paragraph itself of the financial statement presented to the Assembly by Finance Minister Mr Panneerselvam of the Jayalalithaa government, it is stated that “the State Government is submerged in a debt burden exceeding one lakh crore rupees.” Seeing that, I read the entire statement expecting that he would set out various plans and ideas for reducing the State's debt burden. But at the end I found that the government proposes to borrow **₹17,261 crore** in 2011-2012. I was greatly disappointed. Those who had repeatedly spoken as though the DMK government had borrowed all the money and placed the debts upon Jayalalithaa's head before leaving office must also have been disappointed.

I have already explained the State Government's debt figures many times. It is wrong to keep saying that the DMK government placed a debt burden of ₹1.25 lakh crore upon this government. As early as 31-3-2006, after five years of Jayalalithaa's rule, the Tamil Nadu Government's total debt liability was **₹57,457 crore**. That was the debt burden Jayalalithaa left behind for the DMK government. The DMK government did not impose any tax to reduce that debt burden. But within just three months of returning to office Jayalalithaa has imposed taxes amounting to about **₹4,000 crore**—hurriedly, even before the financial statement was read in the Assembly. After imposing taxes before presenting the financial statement, what meaning is there in now boasting loudly that this is a budget without taxes?

Finance Secretary K. Shanmugam, IAS, in the customary interview after the financial statement, said that the State Government expects tax revenue of **₹10,191 crore** from excise duties in the current financial year; that excluding revenue from alcoholic beverages, sales taxes will bring **₹7,755 crore**; and that including tax revenue from liquor sales the total will be **₹17,810 crore**. Yet *Dinamani*, reporting this, printed on its front page in large type: “Surplus Budget Without Taxation,” singing its “praise.” Is that right?

Even today's *The Hindu*, under the headline **“State may have to go in for heavy borrowings this year”**, points to the Jayalalithaa government's need to borrow:

> “The AIADMK Government’s first budget presented to the Assembly on Thursday shows that it may have to go in for heavy borrowings this year to fund its ambitious social welfare programmes. The projection of net borrowings is Rs. 17,261 crore 2011-12 to fill the resource gap”

In that same report *The Hindu* also recalls:

> “When the State’s public debt was projected to cross Rs. 1 lakh crore at the end of last fiscal, the AIADMK, then in the Opposition, had warned that the DMK regime was pushing the State into a debt trap”

In other words, it reminds readers that when the State's debt was projected to cross ₹1 lakh crore at the end of the previous financial year, the AIADMK had accused the DMK government of pushing the State into a debt trap—and thereby also points to Jayalalithaa's earlier loud claims.

When the Jayalalithaa government assumed office, it was widely advertised that she had signed seven files. Finance Minister Panneerselvam sets out those schemes in detail in the third paragraph of the financial statement. Why, then, did he forget to mention one of those seven—that maternity leave would be increased to six months?

Whenever the DMK government announced free-distribution schemes, Jayalalithaa mocked and ridiculed them as schemes that would keep people poor. Yet in the present financial statement she herself has announced free schemes. Apart from these free schemes, almost all the other schemes in the financial statement are either Union Government schemes or schemes of the DMK government.

To give examples: Union Government schemes include the National Agricultural Development Scheme, National Horticulture Mission, National Fisheries Development Board funding scheme, Jawaharlal Nehru National Urban Renewal Scheme, Rajiv Housing Scheme, Education for All Scheme, National Secondary Education Scheme, National Rural Health Scheme and Swarna Jayanti Employment Scheme. World Bank schemes include the Water Resources–Land Resources Project, Dam Safety Project and Tamil Nadu Health Project. There are also the Green Project with Japanese assistance and the Chennai Outer Ring Road Project, and schemes for drinking water, rainwater and underground sewerage through the German Development Bank.

There are many examples showing that the AIADMK government has now begun to enjoy the benefits of schemes initiated during DMK rule. Paragraph 6 of the financial statement says, “Tamil Nadu is a front-runner in e-governance initiatives.” Paragraph 50 says, “At the global level Tamil Nadu is regarded as a State suitable for manufacturing-sector industrial investment.” Paragraph 56 states: “Tamil Nadu has the highest number of micro, small and medium enterprises in the country. To encourage micro, small and medium enterprises, SIDCO has identified 2,256 acres of land at 25 locations for allotment.” Thus the financial statement itself praises the DMK government.

It has also been announced that schemes started by the DMK government—such as the river-linking scheme, special public-distribution scheme and skill-development scheme—will continue under the AIADMK government. Though unavoidable circumstances compel this government to continue these schemes, it is also eager to hold closing ceremonies for many good schemes begun under DMK rule.

To give one example: in 2008, to reduce the workload of the Chennai City Police and improve policing, the Chennai Suburban Police Commissionerate was established. But the present AIADMK government has announced that it is going to close the Chennai Suburban Police Commissionerate. *Dinamalar* itself commented today that doing so would increase the Commissioner's workload, create administrative complications and cause police personnel to be thrown from one end of the city to another during transfers.

They have also held a closing ceremony for the free colour-television distribution scheme. During DMK rule, **1 crore 62 lakh 80 thousand** colour television sets were procured at a cost of **₹3,716 crore**, and **1 crore 58 lakh 8 thousand 285** sets were distributed. To provide sets to those who had not yet received them, the DMK government initiated action to procure another **10 lakh** colour television sets at an estimated cost of **₹249 crore**. Because the AIADMK government has now closed that scheme, it must earn the anger of those who were waiting to receive those television sets.

Likewise, for people living in huts—with the good intention that Tamil Nadu should have no huts—the housing programme was named the “Kalaignar Housing Scheme.” Even when my name was given to it, I said it should not be done. But because some did not listen to me and named the scheme after me, the AIADMK government has moved to put a full stop to the scheme itself. Of the **3 lakh concrete houses** begun in the first year, **1.5 lakh** have been completed, while the remaining **1.5 lakh** are waiting at a stage when they should now be completed. Yet the present financial statement shows no arrangement for doing so.

After cancelling the scheme bearing my name, this government has announced another scheme of its own and says **₹1.80 lakh** will be given per house. Because the DMK government had fixed the assistance at **₹75,000**, they have, out of boastfulness, announced ₹1.80 lakh. When the DMK government raised the assistance under this scheme to ₹75,000, we also announced that houses built under the Indira Gandhi Housing Scheme—which greatly benefits Scheduled Caste communities—would receive a State Government subsidy of ₹75,000. But instead of giving ₹1.80 lakh to houses under the Indira Gandhi Housing Scheme too, the AIADMK government has announced assistance of only **₹1 lakh**. That becomes discrimination by this government against the oppressed community.

Paragraph 65 of the financial statement says, “The new generating capacity added during the past five years was only 206 MW.” Yet paragraph 68 says, “The generating capacity expected from new projects during 2011-2012 is **3,280 MW**.” This 3,280 MW is to come from projects begun during the previous five years of DMK government; it is not electricity generated by the AIADMK government in these three months. During DMK rule, projects were begun to generate a total of **4,183 MW**. The truth is that these projects begun under DMK rule are going to benefit the AIADMK rulers.

At the World Classical Tamil Conference in Coimbatore, **₹100 crore** was allocated and a Government Order issued to implement resolutions such as the “Tolkappiyar Peravai.” Yet the present financial statement contains no announcement about it. Under the heading “Tamil Development,” only **₹58 lakh 50 thousand** is shown as an allocation. It is not known whether the schemes announced at the Classical Tamil Conference will be implemented, or whether they too will meet the same fate under AIADMK rule as the Pavendhar Classical Tamil Research Library.

Paragraph 143 of the financial statement says: “The State Government has constituted 34 welfare boards for various categories of workers. Of these, 17 welfare boards providing social security to unorganised workers are administered by the Labour Welfare Department. **51.37 lakh** workers have been registered with these 17 welfare boards. So far **₹356.59 crore** has been given to **13.85 lakh** beneficiaries for marriage, maternity, surgery, education, pension, accident and death relief and similar assistance. Since these welfare boards are not functioning effectively, this government will restructure them and improve their operations.”

Simply reading those figures tells us whether these boards had been functioning. Yet because these boards were all begun during DMK rule, what is likely to happen is that, after announcing they will “restructure and improve” them, this government will hold closing ceremonies for them too.

So one may close one's eyes, read the AIADMK financial statement, praise it and rejoice! That is all!

**With affection, M.K.**  
6-8-2011
