# 3445. Some Copper-Plate Records in the History of Classical Tamil — 1

> **Translator’s note**
>
> `செப்பேடுகள்` literally evokes inscribed copper plates and is used here as an image for durable historical records. The letter begins a series on the history of Tamil’s recognition as a classical language. Long nineteenth- and early-twentieth-century quotations from Parithimar Kalaignar are rendered for meaning in English while their exact historical Tamil, spelling and punctuation are preserved in the audited Tamil appendix.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3445](../../../chapters/3445-semmoli-varalatril-sila-seppedugal-1.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 149](../../../pages/page-149.md)–[PDF 156](../../../pages/page-156.md)  
**Date:** 25 December 2009

**Udanpirappē,**

You may be thinking that I have come here for rest. Not for rest — no! I am using this rest with the eagerness to build, through letters, a ceremonial gateway for the World Classical Tamil Conference that is to be conducted in an incomparable manner. This is the pile of stones I am gathering to build that gateway!

Including the Second World Tamil Conference led by Arignar Anna after he became Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu in 1967, eight conferences have so far been held under the name “World Tamil Conference.”

Because the Second World Tamil Conference, held in Chennai with such distinction that those who saw it marvelled, produced a great transformation in the inner feeling and intellectual capacity of Tamils, Arignar Anna spoke with pride and dignity:

> “The greatness, advancement, distinction and excellence of classical Tamil, the language of the Dravidian people, are gaining a special place in the view of the world.
>
> “Many masters of language are greatly praising the distinctive qualities of Tamil — the youthful and refined qualities fitting a classical language.
>
> “Tamil is extremely ancient in time. It has properly acquired rules and frameworks of grammar and literature. It is pure — unsullied. It possesses a very rich vocabulary capable of expressing thoughts precisely. It has great elegance in conveying feelings subtly, finely, honestly and gracefully. It has forms of expression capable of stating thoughts, arrangements, policies and principles without deviation or distortion.
>
> “The treasury of Tamil contains an abundance — an abundance, a very great abundance — of words capable of giving pointed comment, elaboration and explanation with heat, taste, subtlety and intellectual refinement. Its resources do not diminish however much we draw from them. The wealth and power of our Tamil language do not run out however much we give from them.
>
> “Tamil is sweet. It is beautiful. It is powerful. It has an inexhaustible spring of richness in words and meaning, and the elegance and refinement of civilisation. It is ancient. It is a mother language! It possesses the astonishing power of an ever-youthful vitality that never perishes. We are living at a time when town and world alike have begun to agree that all this is truth, not mere praise!”

Now, what we have announced for five days from 23 to 27 June 2010 in Coimbatore, the splendid city of the Kongu region, is the “World Classical Tamil Conference.” The initial stage of conference work has been completed.

The dates and venue have been fixed. Arrangements are being made so that the conference will shine with beauty; so that research sessions will possess depth of meaning, give knowledge to future generations and adorn Mother Tamil with new ornaments of further splendour; so that public sessions will create unprecedented feeling and enthusiasm and send them like a flood among Tamils throughout the world; so that the exhibition will be a sight to behold, remaining in the memory of the eyes and radiating light for all time; and so that the procession, taking visible form through tableaux that speak of Tamil civilisation, culture, many-sided character, antiquity and the greatness and tenderness of Tamil literature praised by the world, will astonish not merely as a procession through a town but as though it were a procession around the world.

To conduct all this appropriately, 21 committees have been constituted by bringing together many people of distinction.

To state it plainly, in the sense of being a **World Classical Tamil Conference**, what we are going to conduct will be the first conference. Because our Tamil language fully possesses all the qualifications for being a `செம்மொழி` — a classical language — the demand that all should accept it as such was voiced not today or yesterday, but more than a century ago.

That voice — the Tamil voice — slowly but firmly, with the passage of time, became the voice of Tamil scholars, the voice of Tamil enthusiasts, the voice of Tamil society and a powerful voice in the political arena. Even though ears that would not hear failed to respect it, those with hearing and cultivated character honoured it, recognised the dignified justice of that voice, and Tamil was officially recognised and proclaimed a classical language at the level of India on 12.10.2004.

That day was a high and noble day in the history of a struggle for rights lasting more than a century — an emerald festival day that the great Tamil people can never forget!

Classical Tamil, older than the oldest antiquity and new even to later newness, whose natural origin cannot be known, has grown and flourished with ever-youthful vigour. At this fitting moment when the World Classical Tamil Conference has been announced, it is appropriate, because of the meaningful relationship we share, that I recall and tell you the evolutionary history through which Tamil was proclaimed a classical language; that you should learn it; and that, storing what you learn in memory and telling it to your relatives and other Udanpirappugal, you should gain a new awakening and joy.

In 1887, the ancient Tamil scholar V. Ko. Suryanarayana Sastri, known as Parithimar Kalaignar, wrote in the conclusion of his book *History of the Tamil Language*:

> “Let us show briefly that, like Sanskrit, Latin and Greek, Tamil too is a high, independent classical language. A language that holds leadership among the many languages spoken in its country and possesses greater excellence than they is a high language. Examined by this criterion, Tamil, having leadership over Telugu and the other languages and possessing greater excellence than they, is itself a high language.
>
> “A language possessing the strength to function independently, without the help of the other languages current in the country where it is spoken, is called an independent language. Ordinarily, its assistance to other languages will be great while the assistance they give it will be small. If the assistance of Tamil were removed, Telugu and the others could not function; but Tamil can function independently and sweetly, without the slightest difficulty, even without their assistance. Many Indian philologists have agreed upon this. Therefore Tamil is an independent language. Now, what is a classical language?”

After thus insisting that Tamil is both a high language and an independent language, Parithimar Kalaignar answered the question “What is a classical language?” as follows:

> “The rule is that a pure language possessing refined character and distinguished civilisation is called a classical language. It is certain that these linguistic qualities are present in Tamil. What does this mean? Refined character consists in standing corrected and developed so that, without tangled constructions of word or meaning, the listener can clearly and firmly understand the meaning intended by the speaker, with old forms passing away and new forms entering.
>
> “See that this quality is fully present in Tamil. In keeping with the maturity of a country’s civilisation, words must arise and bring civilised excellence to its language. When such words arise, words of its own language should predominate rather than words of foreign languages. These qualities too belong to high, independent Tamil. Thus Tamil is also a pure language. Therefore it is certain that Tamil is a classical language. Is it not for this reason that from ancient times Tamil has been spoken of by melodious poets as `Senthamizh`?
>
> “Thus, however one investigates it, it is certain that the Tamil language — sweeter than nectar and shining with distinction in the South — is a high, independent classical language. Instead of counting our precious Tamil language, endowed with so much elevation and excellence, together with the inferior languages of this country, it is proper that, just as Sanskrit is regarded as the high independent classical language of the North, Tamil should be regarded as the high independent classical language of the South.”

Thus, more than a hundred years ago, he decisively established Tamil as a classical language and raised his voice for it.

After deep study, Parithimar Kalaignar continued to insist upon this position until the end. In 1902, in an article titled “High Independent Classical Language” in the monthly *Senthamizh*, published by the Madurai Tamil Sangam with Professor Raghava Iyengar as editor, he explained:

> “It is certain that, however our Tamil language — sweeter than nectar and shining with distinction in the South — is examined, it is a high, independent classical language. Rather than counting our precious Tamil language, possessing such elevation and excellence, together with the inferior languages within the country, it is proper to frame rules by treating Tamil as the high independent classical language of the South, just as Sanskrit is treated as the high independent classical language of the North.”

That is why the house in which Tamil scholar Parithimar Kalaignar lived — door number 4-219, on a 2,508-square-foot site in Vilacheri village near Thiruparankundram in Madurai district — was ordered on 16.11.2006 to be taken into government ownership and maintained as a memorial house.

To acquire the privately held house, ₹3 lakh 84 thousand 534 was sanctioned on 1.12.2006. The old house was renovated by the DMK government at a cost of ₹7 lakh 90 thousand, given a new appearance as a memorial house, and a bust of Parithimar Kalaignar was installed at its entrance.

On 31.10.2007, I opened the renovated memorial house, whose antiquity had been preserved, and unveiled the bronze bust erected in front of it. After viewing his biographical notes and rare photographs displayed there, I wrote and signed in the visitors’ book:

> “Long live the fame of Parithimar Kalaignar, the first Tamil who raised his voice demanding classical-language status for Tamil!”

Because Parithimar Kalaignar was the first Tamil to demand classical-language status for Tamil, the DMK government gave him several other honours too. All the books created by Parithimar Kalaignar were nationalised, and on 2.12.2006 his legal heirs were given ₹15 lakh as compensation.

The request of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam government was accepted by the United Progressive Alliance government, and on 17.8.2007 a commemorative special postage stamp for Parithimar Kalaignar was released.

Wishing to remedy the absence in Tamil of a work on the grammar of drama, Parithimar Kalaignar studied Tamil, Sanskrit and English and wrote and published a work titled *Nadakaviyal*.

While he was working as a teacher, the University of Madras planned to remove the Indian languages, including Tamil, from its course of study. Parithimar Kalaignar and Mr Purnalingam Pillai went from house to house among Tamil scholars and, through their efforts, stopped the university’s plan; Tamil continued as a language of study at the university.

Parithimar Kalaignar, who laid the foundation for Tamil to be proclaimed a classical language, and the Tamil scholar who began a century-long history of language rights, lived for only about 33 years, yet he holds an enduring fame in the history of the Tamil language.

Suryanarayana Sastri, Parithimar Kalaignar, exposed what he described as the deceptive practices of Sanskrit scholars — tearing away their face-covering, removing the mask they wore and bringing their disguise into the light. The passage in his book *History of the Tamil Language* is one that all Tamils — particularly those who came through the path of Periyar and Anna — should unfailingly remember. It reads:

> “The Sanskrit scholars, having understood the customs and conduct of the Tamils, began composing works in Sanskrit in accordance with them. Because they were trained in spiritual texts and possessed knowledge of the arts, they composed puranas for the sacred places of the Tamils; and they gradually established among the Tamils the fourfold caste system of ‘Brahmins, rulers, merchants and cultivators,’ which had not existed among them.
>
> “‘Like a system different from the order of the earlier creation,  
> you established in this country the fourfold caste divisions’
>
> — see also the *Kapilar Agaval*, which cries out thus toward the Aryans. Further displaying their cleverness, they established themselves among Tamil kings as ministers and superior officials; translated many rare matters that had existed among the Tamils; and before the Tamils themselves knew, presented those matters as though they had first known them and as though they had come to Tamil only from Sanskrit.”

Is not what Parithimar Kalaignar set out with examples more than a century ago reflective of the current of thought of Periyar and Anna? That is why, calling Suryanarayana Sastri — Parithimar Kalaignar — the first Tamil, I wrote from the feeling of my heart, openly blessing: “Long live his fame!”

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**25-12-2009**

- என நான் எழுதிக் கையெழுத்திட்டேன்.

தமிழுக்குச் செம்மொழித் தகுதி கோரிய முதல் தமிழன்
பரிதிமாற்கலைஞர் என்பதால் தான், கழக அரசு வேறுசில
சிறப்புகளையும் அவருக்குச் செய்திருக்கிறது. பரிதிமாற் கலைஞர்
படைத்துள்ள நூல்கள் அனைத்தையும் அரசுடைமையாக்கி,

அவரது மரபுரிமையர்க்கு 2.12.2006 அன்று 15 இலட்ச ரூபாய்
பரிவுத் தொகையாக வழங்கப்பட்டது. திராவிட முன்னேற்றக்
கழக அரசின் கோரிக்கை ஐக்கிய முற்போக்குக் கூட்டணி அரசால்
ஏற்கப்பட்டு, 17.8.2007 அன்று பரிதிமாற் கலைஞர் நினைவு
சிறப்பு அஞ்சல் தலை வெளியிடப்பட்டது.
தமிழில் நாடக இலக்கண நூல் இல்லாத குறையைப் போக்க
விரும்பி, தமிழ், வடமொழி, ஆங்கிலம் ஆகிய மும்மொழிகளையும்
ஆராய்ந்து, நாடகவியல் என ஒரு நூல் எழுதி வெளியிட்டவர்
பரிதிமாற்கலைஞர்.
இவர் ஆசிரியராகப் பணியாற்றிய காலத்தில், சென்னைப்
பல்கலைக்கழகம் தமிழ்மொழி உட்பட உள்நாட்டு மொழிகளைப்
பாடப்பகுதியிலிருந்து நீக்கிட திட்டமிட்டபோது; பரிதிமாற்
கலைஞரும், திரு.பூர்ணலிங்கம் பிள்ளையும், தமிழறிஞர்களின்
வீடுதோறும் சென்று, முயன்று பல்கலைக்கழகத்தின் திட்டத்தைத்
தடுத்தனர்; தமிழ்மொழி தொடர்ந்து பல்கலைக் கழகப்
பாடமொழியாக நீடித்தது.
தமிழ்மொழியைச் செம்மொழியென்று பிரகடனப்
படுத்துவதற்கு அடித்தளம் அமைத்த பரிதிமாற்கலைஞர் -
நூறாண்டுக்கால மொழி உரிமை வரலாற்றைத் தொடங்கி
வைத்த தமிழறிஞர் - ஏறத்தாழ 33 ஆண்டுகள் மட்டுமே வாழ்ந்த
அப்பெருமகனார் - தமிழ் மொழி வரலாற்றில் நிலைத்த புகழைக்
கொண்டுள்ளார்.
சூரியநாராயண சாஸ்திரியார் எனும் பரிதி மாற்கலைஞர்
அவர்கள்; வடமொழியாளரின் ஏமாற்று வேலைகள்
எப்படிப்பட்டவை என்பதை - அவர்களது முகத்திரையைக் கிழித்து,
அணிந்திருந்த முகமூடியைக் கழற்றி - அவர்களது வேடத்தை
வெளிச்சம் போட்டுக் காட்டியிருக்கிறார். “தமிழ்மொழியின்
வரலாறு” என்ற அவரது நூலில் உள்ள அந்தப் பகுதி, தமிழர்கள்
அனைவரும் - குறிப்பாக பெரியார், அண்ணா வழி வந்தவர்கள் -
தவறாது நினைவு கூரத்தக்கதாகும். அது பின்வருமாறு:
“வடமொழியாளர் தமிழர்களது ஒழுக்க வழக்கங்களை
உணர்ந்து அவற்றிற்கேற்ப வடமொழியில் நூல்கள் வகுப்பான்
புகுந்தனர். அவர்களெல்லாம் ஆன்மநூற் பயிற்சிக்கு

அமைந்திருக்கிறதல்லவா? அதனால் தான் சூரியநாராயண
