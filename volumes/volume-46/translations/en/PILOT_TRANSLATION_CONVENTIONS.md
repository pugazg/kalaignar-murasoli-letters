# Volume 46 — English Translation Pilot Conventions

## Status

**LOCKED after bilingual review of pilot letters 3592–3594, subject only to the documented 3594 file-level correction being applied before main-batch release.**

These conventions derive from the three-letter pilot and its bilingual alignment review. They govern subsequent Volume 46 English translation work.

## Locked conventions

1. Retain **`Udanpirappē`** as Kalaignar's characteristic political-familial address; do not flatten it into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.”
2. Retain the stable closing **`With affection, M.K.`** where the Tamil source has `அன்புள்ள, மு.க.`.
3. Preserve Kalaignar's **thought and argument order before stylistic elegance**. English may be made readable, but claims must not be reordered, compressed, broadened, softened, or supplemented.
4. Preserve rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, sarcasm, argumentative turns and political directness rather than neutralising them into academic prose.
5. Preserve source-supplied English **verbatim**, including unusual capitalization, punctuation and ellipses when they are part of the archival source text.
6. When the source prints an English passage and then separately prints a Tamil rendering/explanation, **represent both passages in English**, even where their substantive content overlaps. Do not replace the Tamil passage with `in substance`, a summary, or an editorial omission.
7. Translate long quotations from the fidelity-cleared canonical Tamil as printed. Do not silently substitute an outside edition, newspaper text, court judgment, speech transcript or other external version.
8. Preserve names, dates, counts, monetary figures and source-specific legal/institutional terminology. Use conservative English equivalents and explain genuinely source-specific terminology in letter notes when necessary.
9. Preserve Indian public-language quantities such as **lakh/crore** and source-specific legal/land units such as **kani/ground** unless the source itself supplies a conversion or a later project-wide decision explicitly changes this rule.
10. Preserve Tamil idiom/proverb imagery where replacing it with an unrelated English proverb would erase Kalaignar's phrasing. A brief note may explain the rhetorical force where needed.
11. Source text visibly printed after a formal signature/date remains part of the archival letter record and must be translated in its source position or explicitly labelled as a post-closing source note.
12. The complete audited Tamil remains authoritative and is reproduced with the bilingual record according to the established Volume 46 format.
13. Do not repair source numbering anomalies, heading anomalies, historical spelling, factual assertions or internal inconsistencies in translation. Preserve them and document where necessary.
14. Translation verification is a separate gate from drafting. A letter is not `verified` merely because an English draft exists.
15. Bilingual alignment checks title, salutation, closing/date, paragraph/argument sequence, substantive claims and qualifications, quotations, names, figures, dates, institutional terminology, rhetorical force and duplicated/parenthetical source material.

## Batch rule after pilot

Once the documented 3594 correction is physically present in the English letter file and pilot statuses are synchronized, normal translation may proceed in **five-letter batches**, beginning with **3595**. Each batch remains draft translation until its own bilingual/source-check gate is completed.
