# 3572. “Even 100 Karunanidhis Could Not Do It!”

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Source pages:** [PDF 261](../../../pages/page-261.md)–[PDF 265](../../../pages/page-265.md)  
**Date:** 19 July 2011

**Udanpirappē,**

Even during the period when the Communist Party of India had a relationship with the DMK, when that party's cadres and leaders were united with us in a spirit of comradeship, its State Secretary, Comrade **D. Pandian**, used to associate with us with a kind of **“allergy.”** Even before the general election, he was the one who acted with great concern and enthusiasm for the Communist Party of India to establish a relationship with the AIADMK; the cadres and frontline workers of that party know this very well. But how firmly he stands by any particular opinion is open to question.

Even last week, speaking at an event, he said that nothing was happening under the new AIADMK government other than changes of ministers. I had pointed out that this was reported from his party's official newspaper **Janasakthi** to English newspapers. In response to my comment, he issued a denial saying that Janasakthi's reporter had not attended that event at all and that the speech had been published after seeing reports in other newspapers. His speech had also appeared in the AIADMK-supporting weekly **Thuglak**, and D. Pandian issued a denial of that too. But after D. Pandian had participated, spoken and left, an AIADMK minister who spoke at the same event replied that there was nothing wrong in Jayalalithaa changing ministers. That too appeared in Thuglak and made it crystal clear to the world whether what D. Pandian had said was true or not.

Within two months of Jayalalithaa's government taking office, it imposed taxes amounting to **₹4,200 crore**. All parties expressed condemnation. Yet some newspapers published what D. Pandian was said to have answered about it in an interview.

In particular, **Dina Thanthi** reported the State Secretary of the Communist Party of India in Tamil Nadu as justifying the ₹4,200 crore tax burden on the public by saying:

> “The previous government announced that the Tamil Nadu government had a debt of ₹98,000 crore. But it has now come to light that the Tamil Nadu government has a debt of ₹1 lakh 25 thousand crore. Since the government has to pay principal and interest on this, imposing taxes on commodities is unavoidable.”

Fortunately, when **Janasakthi** published this interview, it explained that the party was collecting details about which of these taxes would affect which manufacturing industries, was discussing them, and would announce its position once that process was complete.

Asked by a weekly magazine about the many criticisms that had arisen over the sudden taxes imposed by the Jayalalithaa government, D. Pandian replied:

> “These are taxes imposed to pay interest on the debt taken by former Chief Minister Karunanidhi. During the previous government, Karunanidhi loaded a debt burden of ₹1.25 lakh crore onto the State. To clear it and set the situation right, even this tax increase is not enough. A tax should be imposed on Karunanidhi too. The last time the AIADMK came to power, Karunanidhi had similarly pushed Tamil Nadu into a huge debt pit. It will take the AIADMK government a year just to fill and repair this pit. Therefore Karunanidhi has no standing at all to speak about the tax increase.”

When the interviewer merely asked what he had to say about the many criticisms, he unnecessarily sprang at me and clawed at me; and he answered not even that the DMK government, but I personally, had placed the debt burden on the AIADMK government, and said on that basis that there was nothing wrong in imposing new taxes. Whether D. Pandian's view that there was nothing wrong in the AIADMK government imposing taxes of ₹4,200 crore is also their view is something comrades **Nallakannu, Gopu, Sivapunniyam, Subbarayan** and others in that party must say.

Not only that. Even after the government had explained its debt position many times, D. Pandian says that I had somehow loaded a debt of **₹1.25 lakh crore** onto the government. AIADMK Chief Minister Jayalalithaa made the same accusation, and I gave a detailed reply to it on **1-7-2011**. Even before that, D. Pandian himself had made the same accusation, and I gave a detailed reply to him too on **13-2-2011**. I do not know whether D. Pandian read any of those explanations. Even so, so that he may at least now understand, I shall state it once again.

The DMK government did not leave this government with a debt burden of ₹1.25 lakh crore. As early as **31-3-2006**—that is, after the five years of rule of Jayalalithaa, whom D. Pandian admires—the Tamil Nadu government's total debt liability was **₹57,457 crore**. That was the debt burden Jayalalithaa left behind for the DMK government. To reduce that debt burden, the DMK government imposed no tax on Jayalalithaa and no tax on D. Pandian.

Further, in his interview D. Pandian imagined and said, as though the DMK government had told some great falsehood, that Finance Minister Perasiriyar had stated that the Tamil Nadu government had ₹98,000 crore in debt, whereas it had now been discovered that the debt was ₹1 lakh 25 thousand crore.

In the interim financial statement placed before the Assembly by the DMK government on **5-2-2011**, page 55 states:

> “The State government's total debt, including liabilities such as provident funds, was ₹57,457 crore on 31-3-2006. It is estimated that this will rise to ₹1,01,541 crore on 31-3-2011.”

D. Pandian may at least now spell it out, read it and understand. Therefore no one in the DMK government wrongly stated that the debt was ₹98,000 crore. Even while replying to the debate on the interim financial statement on **10-2-2011**, then Finance Minister Perasiriyar said that the AIADMK government had left behind a debt of **₹56,000 crore**, and that it had now risen to approximately **₹1 lakh crore**. Thus no incorrect statistic was supplied on behalf of the DMK government.

To say still more: at the end of **2005-2006**, after Jayalalithaa had ruled for five years, debt liabilities were **22.29 per cent** of Gross State Domestic Product; the DMK's interim financial statement itself made clear that after five years of DMK government this ratio would fall to **19.58 per cent in 2010-2011**.

Finally, the weekly asked D. Pandian:

> “Touch your conscience and tell us... in its position on Samacheer Kalvi, are all the ruling party's actions satisfactory?”

Under the DMK government, several committees were appointed; they travelled to several States and studied how Samacheer Kalvi was being implemented there; three years of consultations at various stages were completed; and even after that, without showing haste, it was decided to implement the scheme step by step. In the first year, Samacheer Kalvi was therefore introduced only for **two classes**. But under the AIADMK government, an announcement was made on the first day and an amendment Bill was introduced and passed in the Assembly the very next day. The court itself asked why there was such haste.

Yet D. Pandian replied:

> “Jayalalithaa did not wish to introduce a half-baked Samacheer Kalvi scheme in the hurried manner of the DMK. Therefore the AIADMK's position on this issue is the correct one.”

He concluded by saying that even **100 Karunanidhis together could not become one Jayalalithaa**.

That is true! Jayalalithaa has made even the State Secretary of the Communist Party of India give an interview to this extent. Yes, once in the Legislative Assembly Jayalalithaa said that the **“tin box”** itself had been invented in this country so that Communists could shake their collection boxes. To become a Jayalalithaa who pours out such showers of abuse—**even 100 Karunanidhis could not do it!**

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**19-7-2011**
