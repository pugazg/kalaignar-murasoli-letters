# 3443. They and We: A Comparison!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> The letter contrasts the DMK’s conduct toward the Marxist Communist Party with what the author describes as the latter party’s conduct toward the DMK. The translation preserves the source’s legal and political claims, names, dates and quoted statements without independently reconciling them.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3443](../../../chapters/3443-avargalum-naamum-or-oppidu.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 138](../../../pages/page-138.md)–[PDF 142](../../../pages/page-142.md)  
**Date:** 20 December 2009

**Udanpirappē,**

My secretary told me a piece of news this afternoon, 18-12-2009. Comrade Varadarajan had himself telephoned to say that the Marxist Communist Party wished to hold a conference in Dindigul city on the 19th and 20th and a procession on the 21st, but that the police had not granted permission. He also said that the Chief Minister of Tripura would participate in the conference and requested that the matter be brought to the Chief Minister so that the necessary arrangements could be made.

As soon as my secretary told me this, I immediately instructed him to contact the concerned police officials and have permission granted for both the conference and the procession.

After that, when I entered the *Murasoli* office, today’s *Theekkathir* newspaper was before my eyes. On its front page, enclosed in a box, was the following news item:

> “Comrade P. Mohan, a member of the State Committee of the Marxist Communist Party, former Member of Parliament from Madurai and the candidate who contested on behalf of the Marxist party, had filed a case in the Madras High Court seeking a declaration that M.K. Alagiri’s victory from the Madurai constituency in the 2009 Parliamentary election was invalid. Following P. Mohan’s death, A. Lazar, a member of the Tamil Nadu State Executive Committee of the Marxist Communist Party, who had filed his nomination as the substitute candidate, will be added as the petitioner and will continue to conduct the case.”

Let the case proceed. Let the court pronounce its judgment. Our Kazhagam is prepared to accept that judgment. But I thought of a few matters in order to compare the attitude of some persons in the leadership of the Marxist Communist Party with the attitude we have shown.

On 23-4-1997, when the DMK was in government, Mrs Leelavathi, a Madurai Corporation councillor belonging to the Marxist Communist Party, was murdered. On 24-4-1997 a special calling-attention motion was brought in the Assembly regarding that murder. Replying to the motion, I said:

> “Mrs Leelavathi, who was murdered in Madurai, was a hard-working member of the Marxist Communist Party and a public-service worker. She has been murdered. In the complaint given at the police station, her husband Mr Kuppusamy has stated that his wife Leelavathi had been elected as a councillor in the Madurai Corporation election; that Mrs Valli, wife of a person named Karumalaiyan, had contested against her in the same ward and been defeated; that thereafter Karumalaiyan and his brother Muthuramalingam — the latter being secretary of the DMK’s 59th ward — came to Leelavathi’s house, quarrelled and threatened her; and that on the morning of 23-4-1997, when Leelavathi went to a shop, these two persons and four others attacked her with sickles and dangerous weapons, as a result of which she died on the spot. A case has been registered at the police station and Karumalaiyan has been arrested. Police teams have been formed to arrest the other accused and an intensive investigation has been undertaken.
>
> “What I wish to tell this House is that even though the persons involved in this murder belong to the ruling DMK, the government and the Police Department have acted without showing any favour or indulgence and without discrimination.”

I did not stop with saying that. Although the accused in the case belonged to the DMK, and although the DMK was then the ruling party, the case proceeded properly and the DMK accused were produced before court. They were not even allowed to come out on bail.

While the case was proceeding, the Marxist Communist Party made a request to me: the government prosecutor should not appear; if the government prosecutor argued the case, the DMK accused might escape; therefore the advocate nominated by their party should be appointed to appear. I accepted even that request and appointed the advocate named by them to conduct the case. The case proceeded and those DMK accused were convicted by the court. This is what happened.

At this point I also wish to remind you of an incident that occurred two years earlier under the AIADMK government. A Marxist Communist Party comrade named Thangayya was killed at Kudavasal. Those accused in that case were protected in the very car of a minister who was then in office. It was only after the DMK government came to power that action was taken in that case. I trust that our friends in the Marxist Communist Party have not forgotten all this.

I wish to mention one more incident. In March 2008, an election to the Rajya Sabha was held. In the previous Rajya Sabha election in 2007, in accordance with the agreement reached at the time of the 2006 Assembly election, the DMK voted for Communist Party of India candidate Comrade D. Raja and ensured his victory.

At the time of the 2006 Assembly election, no agreement had been reached with the Marxist Communist Party regarding the Rajya Sabha election. Even so, out of the spirit of comradeship the DMK had toward its allied parties, in the Rajya Sabha election held in March 2008 it came forward to give its 28 surplus votes in support of the Marxist Communist Party candidate.

Not only did we come forward to support him; I myself signed as proposer of the nomination of Comrade T.K. Rangarajan, who contested the Rajya Sabha election on behalf of the Marxist Communist Party.

In an interview at that time, Rangarajan thanked and praised Kalaignar for having made it possible for him to enter the Rajya Sabha. He also said:

> “So far, on behalf of the Marxist Communist Party in Tamil Nadu, P. Ramamurthi, M.R. Venkataraman and Nallasivam have been elected as members of the Upper House in Delhi. Now I, T.K. Rangarajan, have been elected as a Marxist member. All four of us were elected to the Upper House with the support of the DMK. Chief Minister Kalaignar not only proposed my nomination but assured us that, if there was a contest in this election, further DMK votes would also be given. Chief Minister Kalaignar sincerely wished to protect the unity of the DMK alliance parties. Keeping this in mind, he made allocations in past Assembly, Parliamentary and Upper House elections.”

That statement still stands recorded in the newspapers.

This letter is written not merely about the Marxist Communist Party. It is to make clear the extent to which the DMK, whether other parties are in alliance with us or not, acts toward them with affection, accommodation and consideration; and at the same time to enable the comrades of those parties themselves, particularly the Marxist Communist Party, to know how their party acts.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**20-12-2009**

## Letter-specific notes

1. The source uses the Leelavathi murder case and the 2008 Rajya Sabha election as the principal comparison; both are retained in the source’s own framing.
2. The legal claims and quotations are translated from the audited canonical Tamil and are not independently verified or harmonised with outside records.
3. `தி.மு. கழகம்` is rendered as **DMK** and `மார்க்சிஸ்ட் கம்யூனிஸ்ட் கட்சி` as **Marxist Communist Party**, following the source’s own terminology.
