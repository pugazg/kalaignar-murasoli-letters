# 3772. The Rajya Sabha Election — An Analysis!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

As the terms of six Rajya Sabha members from Tamil Nadu were due to end on 24 July, the Election Commission announced that the election to fill those seats would be held on 27 June.

The party-wise strength in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly at that time was as follows:

- AIADMK alliance — 151 seats: AIADMK 147, Samathuva Makkal Katchi 2, Republican Party 1 and Kongu Munnetra Kazhagam 1;
- DMK — 23;
- DMDK — 22;
- CPI(M) — 10;
- CPI — 8;
- DMDK dissidents — 7;
- Congress — 5;
- PMK — 3;
- Puthiya Tamilagam — 2;
- Manithaneya Makkal Katchi — 2; and
- Forward Bloc — 1.

At the beginning of the Rajya Sabha election, after excluding the seats held by the DMK, DMDK, Congress and PMK, the remaining 181 Assembly votes were broadly on the side of the AIADMK alliance. With those 181 votes, that alliance could easily win five seats. But one of the six seats falling vacant was the seat held by D. Raja of the Communist Party of India.

In truth, Jayalalithaa initially had no intention of giving that seat again to D. Raja. Because Tha. Pandian of the CPI had been speaking as an intense supporter of Jayalalithaa, she apparently wished to give the seat to him as a reward. The CPI leadership, however, wanted D. Raja to continue in its one seat. Jayalalithaa neither wished to allot a seat to the CPI nor announced one at first.

That was not all. Senior CPI leaders such as A. B. Bardhan and Sudhakar Reddy came from Delhi and waited for days to meet the Chief Minister. She did not give them even the courtesy of a meeting, but said that she would see them in Delhi. Before leaving Chennai for Delhi, however, she announced that the AIADMK would contest five seats and named its five candidates, thereby disappointing the CPI.

When Bardhan and Sudhakar Reddy met Jayalalithaa in Delhi and reminded her that one of the vacant seats was the CPI's “sitting seat”, asking that it be allotted to them, she replied firmly: “Does a sitting seat have to be given merely because it is a sitting seat? I have announced five AIADMK candidates. It cannot be changed.” When they appealed again, she ended the discussion by saying: “Contest somewhere else and win. Best wishes.”

Meeting the press afterwards, Jayalalithaa dramatically announced in Delhi that the AIADMK would contest all forty constituencies in the next Lok Sabha election and would have no alliance with anyone. The Communist parties were left squirming on hearing that announcement.

Jayalalithaa had first named five AIADMK candidates because she believed that, even without giving a seat to the CPI, the AIADMK could win all five seats by securing the votes of the DMDK dissidents, Puthiya Tamilagam, the Manithaneya Makkal Katchi and some secret votes from within the DMDK.

It was when the Communist parties had been disregarded by the AIADMK that the DMK, which had initially made no decision about contesting, tried to discuss the Rajya Sabha election with the CPI(M) leadership in Delhi. When DMK Parliamentary Party leader T. R. Baalu met comrades Sitaram Yechury, Prakash Karat and other Communist leaders, they explained the position: “We too are dissatisfied with Jayalalithaa's political conduct, and she has announced that her party will contest all forty seats in the next Lok Sabha election. But if the CPI announces that it is contesting this election, our agreement with them will compel us to support their candidate.”

A meeting of the DMK's legislators was held in Chennai on 13 June 2013. Some legislators said that certain DMDK members had indicated that their leader Vijayakanth was prepared for an arrangement with the DMK, and that his brother-in-law too supported such an arrangement. When journalists met me that day, I said: “No decision has been taken on the Rajya Sabha election. We will speak to all parties.”

The AIADMK then removed Saravana Perumal, one of its five announced candidates, and named K. Thangamuthu of Thanjavur in his place. Without remaining firm even on that change, it later withdrew Thangamuthu too and announced that the seat would be allotted to CPI candidate D. Raja.

The same Jayalalithaa who had loudly declared in Delhi during the first week that “we will contest all forty constituencies; there will be no alliance with anyone” changed her mind within one week and announced AIADMK support for the CPI candidate.

Did any Tamil Nadu newspaper criticise these contradictions? Did *The Hindu*, which wrote an editorial describing the DMK and the Congress as “opportunists”, criticise them? No.

Once the AIADMK announced support for the CPI, the CPI(M) was left with no alternative and was compelled to support the CPI candidate.

The reason the AIADMK took that decision was obvious. If the DMK, DMDK and the Communist parties joined together and contested in coordination, they could easily win two seats and one AIADMK candidate would be defeated.

Only after it became clear that the Communist parties were not available did discussions take place between the DMDK and the DMK.

DMK candidate Kanimozhi filed her nomination on 15 June 2013. DMK Treasurer M. K. Stalin spoke to Sudheesh of the DMDK. Although the DMDK side initially said that a good outcome could be reached, after its party meeting it suddenly decided to contest and announced that decision. A. R. Elangovan of Salem filed his nomination on behalf of the DMDK. They also went to Delhi seeking Congress support for their candidate. The DMK, for its part, approached the PMK, the Congress, the Manithaneya Makkal Katchi and Puthiya Tamilagam.

The Manithaneya Makkal Katchi met on 18 June 2013 and contacted Treasurer M. K. Stalin. When the DMK contacted Dr K. Krishnasamy, leader of Puthiya Tamilagam, he too assured the DMK of support. The DMDK contacted both parties, and the AIADMK side also spoke to them.

At the same time, Stalin, Duraimurugan and Jagathrakshakan met Anbumani Ramadoss of the PMK. He gave an assurance and said that a decision would be taken and announced at the PMK's meeting on 21 June. At that meeting, however, the PMK announced that it would boycott the election. The Manithaneya Makkal Katchi and Puthiya Tamilagam nevertheless met me in person and confirmed that they would vote for the DMK candidate.

Only after this did the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam speak to the Congress. At the same time, the DMDK too submitted a letter seeking support through the Tamil Nadu Congress Committee President, and that letter was sent to the Congress high command. The All India Congress Committee leadership announced its support for the DMK candidate. That is the actual record of the relationships and positions taken by the parties in this Rajya Sabha election.

At this point, I also wish to recall how the DMK approached Rajya Sabha elections in the past. Anbumani of the PMK has asked me to explain whether the DMK, by seeking Congress support in this election, has forgotten the Eelam Tamil issue. Should I not answer him?

During the 2004 Lok Sabha election, it was agreed that the PMK would be given one seat in the next Rajya Sabha election. When the Rajya Sabha election was held on 28 June 2004, the DMK could easily have won one seat with the support of the Communist parties. Instead of taking that seat for itself, the DMK gave all thirty of its votes to the PMK and the Congress. We wholeheartedly enabled Dr Anbumani Ramadoss—the very person who now questions me—and Sudarsana Natchiappan of the Congress to be elected to the Rajya Sabha. It was because the DMK helped Anbumani win that Rajya Sabha seat that he was later able to serve as a Union Minister. This alone shows the extent to which the DMK gave up a seat of its own in a spirit of cooperation.

Even when agreements were reached with the parties for the 2006 Assembly election, the agreement with the PMK allotted it thirty-one Assembly constituencies; it contained no provision that the PMK must be given a Rajya Sabha seat.

When the CPI pointed out that the CPI(M) had been allotted more Assembly seats than it had, the DMK reassured it by promising one seat in the next Rajya Sabha election. Keeping that word, in the 2007 Rajya Sabha election the DMK fielded CPI candidate D. Raja and ensured his victory through firm and honourable support.

When the 2008 Rajya Sabha election was held, the DMK had ninety-six members in the Assembly and could have contested and won three seats. We nevertheless decided that the DMK would contest two seats, the Congress two and the CPI(M) one. Comrades N. Varadarajan and G. Ramakrishnan asked that I myself propose the nomination of CPI(M) candidate T. K. Rangarajan, and they even took a photograph with me. G. K. Vasan and Sister Jayanthi Natarajan won on behalf of the Congress.

I wish to give one more example. In the 1996 Assembly election, when the All India Congress Committee decided to ally with the AIADMK, G. K. Moopanar formed the Tamil Maanila Congress, entered into an alliance with the DMK, and together we won a great victory in Tamil Nadu. Later, believing it improper to continue in Rajya Sabha seats that they had obtained as Congress nominees while now belonging to the TMC, Moopanar, Jayanthi Natarajan and Peter Alphonse resigned their Rajya Sabha memberships on 9 September 1997.

A by-election was held to fill those three vacancies. The DMK then had 165 Assembly members and could have fielded three DMK candidates and won all three seats. But the DMK did not act with such selfishness. We spoke to Moopanar and said that the DMK would not field even one candidate; the TMC could field all three, and the DMK would ensure their victory. We thus enabled Peter Alphonse, Jayanthi Natarajan and Abdul Khader to win on behalf of the TMC. This too is an example of the DMK's friendship and willingness to accommodate allied parties.

Nor has the DMK, because of its present decision, forgotten or abandoned the Eelam Tamil issue. We have not abandoned it, and we never will.

Even after I clearly said that the Rajya Sabha election is one matter, the Sri Lankan Tamil issue another, and an alliance yet another, some journalists have continued to write as though the DMK has abandoned the Sri Lankan Tamil cause.

They are unable to bear the fact that the DMK won this Rajya Sabha election with the support of the Congress, the Manithaneya Makkal Katchi and Puthiya Tamilagam. They write in this manner only to ease their own inner resentment.

With that intention, *Dinamani* asked Union Minister V. Narayanasamy: “Karunanidhi has said that this cannot be called an alliance with the Congress. What do you say?” He answered very clearly: “The DMK sought Congress support in the Rajya Sabha election, and the Congress gave it. That is the extent of the matter. Only our party high command can answer questions concerning an alliance.”

Kanimozhi met Congress President Sonia Gandhi and Prime Minister Manmohan Singh in person and thanked them for their party's votes in the Rajya Sabha election. Yet *Dinamani* devoted nearly half a page to the headline: “DMK will support the Union Government—Kanimozhi assures Manmohan Singh and Sonia.” After the meetings, Kanimozhi had told journalists only: “On behalf of the DMK, I thanked them for supporting my victory in the Rajya Sabha election.” But claiming to rely on “Congress high-command circles” and “Prime Minister's Office circles”, *Dinamani* wrote that Kanimozhi had assured them the DMK would support bills introduced by the Union Government and decisions taken by it during the monsoon session of Parliament. *Dinamani* was once called *Dina-p-pulugu-mani*—the daily bell of falsehood. It keeps proving that name true.

To put an end to such propaganda, Mukul Wasnik, the Congress leader in charge of Tamil Nadu, gave an interview in Chennai. He said clearly: “The decision to support the DMK in the Rajya Sabha election was taken after consulting all leaders and considering all circumstances. Claims that we supported Kanimozhi because the DMK agreed to support the Food Security Bill, the Land Acquisition Bill or similar measures are false.”

Let me give another recent example. Two weeks ago, the Janata Dal (United) Government led by Nitish Kumar won a confidence vote in the Bihar Legislative Assembly. In the 243-member Assembly, Nitish Kumar needed the support of 122 members, while his party had only 118. Four Congress members, four independents and one CPI member—none of whom had until then been in alliance with the JD(U)—supported the Government. Does that mean that the JD(U) thereby formed a future alliance with the Congress and the CPI? Thambi T. K. S. Elangovan explained this in an article in *Murasoli*.

Therefore, the propaganda conducted with an ulterior motive by some people, particularly some newspapers—that the DMK has abandoned all its principles, acted opportunistically, disregarded all other parties and renewed its relationship with the Congress, given up the Sri Lankan Tamil issue, or committed some wrong merely to obtain one Rajya Sabha seat—is nothing but deceit intended to smear the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam, throw dust upon it and gain favour with Jayalalithaa.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**3 July 2013**
