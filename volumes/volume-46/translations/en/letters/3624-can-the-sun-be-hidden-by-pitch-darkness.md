# 3624. Can the Sun Be Hidden by Pitch Darkness?

**Udanpirappē,**

There is someone called the Finance Minister in Tamil Nadu! His authority seems confined to reading the Budget book in the Assembly. Now and then he rises only to attack the opposition and thereby show that he exists. Suddenly he has issued a statement!

At the end of every Assembly session, the report of the audit authority is tabled. It examines accounts of departments and public-sector undertakings and points out deficiencies. Newspapers then report them. When this year's audit report appeared, our Finance Minister hurriedly issued a statement saying that everything in it concerned the previous Government, apparently fearing that the observations might look like charges concerning his own department. Poor man! Until the Assembly ended he did not get the chance to read a statement under Rule 110 and have it appear in the newspapers. Now he has found an opportunity.

Minister Panneerselvam says the Auditor's report has created an impression as though allegations concern the functioning of the Government headed by Amma. No one who read the report appears to have received such an impression; perhaps only Minister Panneerselvam did.

An audit report is an annual routine under every Government. Audit officers have issued many reports about accounts during AIADMK rule too, and those reports also pointed out deficiencies. If the Finance Minister asks his departmental secretary, he will explain all this!

The Minister says the report states that Tamil Nadu's public-sector undertakings as a whole suffered severe losses. Public-sector undertakings are generally not run solely for profit. Not every PSU earned profits under AIADMK rule either. If tariffs and prices are raised brutally and the people squeezed, PSUs can be made profitable—but that is not the character of a people's welfare Government.

Chief Minister Jayalalithaa said in her statement of 20 November 2011 that the previous DMK Government was responsible for losses in all Government PSUs. The Finance Minister has now simply “copied” that. Let me give examples.

SIPCOT: under Jayalalithaa's rule it lost ₹11.76 crore in 2001–02 and ₹29.15 crore in 2002–03. Under DMK rule it earned profits of ₹56.69 crore in 2006–07, ₹96.54 crore in 2007–08, ₹90.38 crore in 2008–09 and, before audit, ₹93.11 crore in 2009–10.

Tamil Nadu Industrial Investment Corporation—TIIC—is another PSU. Up to 31 March 2010 it had lent an aggregate ₹7,672 crore to 104,949 enterprises. In 2009–10 alone it created employment for about 15,000 people. It had operated profitably for seven consecutive years and earned about ₹40 crore in 2009–10, its highest profit compared with preceding years, representing growth of 36.58 per cent over the previous year.

Tamil Nadu Newsprint and Papers Limited is another. Its profit under Jayalalithaa's rule in 2004–05 was ₹39 crore; under DMK rule it was ₹163 crore in 2007–08, and net profit for 2009–10 was estimated at ₹155 crore.

Tamil Nadu Cements Corporation earned a net profit of ₹26.08 crore in 2009–10. Tamil Nadu Magnesite Limited, established in 1979, was expected to earn approximately ₹19.28 crore profit, subject to audit, in 2009–10, with a similar amount estimated for 2010–11.

The AIADMK Government repeatedly says the Tamil Nadu Electricity Board suffered a ₹10,294.64 crore loss in 2010–11; the Finance Minister repeats it. Had the DMK Government raised electricity tariffs as the AIADMK Government has now done, there might not have been a ₹10,000-crore loss. But the DMK Government believed that even if the Board incurred losses, people should not be pushed into hardship. We still think so.

Panneerselvam's Budget itself began by saying the State was submerged in a debt burden exceeding ₹1 lakh crore. I read on expecting plans to reduce it, only to find at the end that the Government proposed borrowing ₹17,261 crore in 2011–12.

It is wrong to keep saying the DMK left this Government a ₹1.25-lakh-crore debt burden. On 31 March 2006, after five years of Jayalalithaa rule, Tamil Nadu's total debt liability was ₹57,457 crore—the debt Jayalalithaa left to the DMK Government. The DMK did not impose taxes to reduce it. Yet within a year of returning to power Jayalalithaa imposed roughly ₹4,000 crore in taxes, raised milk prices, bus fares and electricity tariffs, and then blamed the previous Government.

Panneerselvam boasts that the revenue deficit was ₹2,728 crore in 2010–11, that the AIADMK increased revenue, eliminated the deficit in 2011–12 and produced a ₹536.54-crore revenue surplus. But before the AIADMK assumed office, Professor presented the DMK Government's interim Budget on 5 February 2011. Page 51 clearly estimated that by the end of 2011–12 the revenue deficit would disappear and there would be a ₹439-crore surplus. The Finance Minister conveniently conceals this and boasts as though only AIADMK skill created the surplus. It makes one laugh!

For, as in “the same moon came then; the same moon has come today,” the Finance Secretary under the DMK Government is the Finance Secretary under the AIADMK Government too! Reduce certain revenue expenditures and a revenue surplus appears. A surplus alone does not mean the overall finances have been repaired.

What matters is the final position of the finances as a whole. In the DMK Budget, the fiscal deficit for 2011–12 was estimated at ₹13,507 crore, or 2.32 per cent of Gross State Domestic Product. In Panneerselvam's Budget of 26 March 2012, the fiscal deficit for 2012–13 is ₹19,832.13 crore, or 2.87 per cent of GSDP. One need only read the AIADMK Government's own Budget to understand whether it has strengthened the State's finances.

Paragraph 137 of that Budget says total State debt at the end of 2012–13 would be ₹1,35,060.47 crore, but the debt-GSDP ratio would be only 19.6 per cent, below the Thirteenth Finance Commission's permitted 24.8 per cent. This itself shows the DMK Government had not violated the fiscal indicator. Yet the Minister says the DMK left a debt burden.

On 8 August 2011, when an Assembly member expressed concern about State debt, Jayalalithaa herself intervened and said total debt was ₹1,18,610 crore; at the end of 2011–12 it would be only 20.39 per cent of GSDP, below the Thirteenth Finance Commission's 24.50-per-cent ceiling. After Ammaiyar herself said the debt was below the Commission's limit, is it fair for her and her minister now to say the DMK left an enormous debt burden?

They say they must reduce the debt left by the DMK. A year has passed since they came to power—how many thousands of crores have they repaid? Forget repayment: did they at least refrain from borrowing? Their own Budget boasts that they borrowed only ₹12,873.81 crore in the current year and that next year's borrowing would be “contained” at ₹18,387.47 crore. Yet they forget that during the five AIADMK years before 2006 their Government itself borrowed more than ₹50,000 crore and repeatedly accuse the DMK.

Since Panneerselvam unnecessarily dragged the DMK Government into his statement on the audit report, I wish to remind him of something.

Who is T. Theethan? He was Accountant General (Audit) II, Tamil Nadu. Until 2 August 2004 most people in Tamil Nadu did not know his name; by 6 August it had become known across India—courtesy of none other than today's Chief Minister.

The Comptroller and Auditor General's report for the year ending 31 March 2003 was placed in the Tamil Nadu Assembly on 31 July 2004. Theethan, the officer who had signed it, reportedly explained some details to journalists on 2 August. He gave year-wise figures for Government losses from 1999–2000 to 2003–04; he did not identify them by ruling party, and even stated the loss for 1999–2000 when the DMK was in office.

Jayalalithaa, then Chief Minister, declared it shocking that the State audit officer had spoken to reporters, unprecedented in independent India and contrary to the Constitution. In response to a single officer she issued not merely a statement but a full-page Government advertisement condemning him—something without precedent.

The Government spent ₹52,14,560 merely on advertisement charges in Tamil Nadu newspapers. And it did not stop there. The same full-page statement was advertised in the *Statesman* from Calcutta, the *Pioneer* from Delhi and Chandigarh, the *Financial Express*, *Hindustan Times*, *Times of India* and *Economic Times*, spending ₹1,22,81,100 of Tamil Nadu taxpayers' money. When some newspapers now report that more than ₹25 crore has been spent on advertisements in Tamil Nadu and across India celebrating one year of AIADMK rule, perhaps they will say the one crore spent over an audit officer was not much!

Nor did they leave that officer alone. Then AIADMK parliamentary party leader P.G. Narayanan and MP Malaichamy filed a case in the Madras High Court alleging that the auditor had unnecessarily given an interview and published in newspapers matters meant for the Legislature, and seeking his removal.

It is under the rule of those who acted in this manner against an officer for performing his duty that the Finance Minister now takes a few figures from an audit report and tries to fling mud at the DMK Government. The diamond lines of the Revolutionary Poet come to mind:

> “Will the fragrance of the lotus vanish because of mud on the bank?  
> Can the sun itself be hidden by pitch darkness?”

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**21 May 2012**

முதலமைச்சர் ஜெயலலிதா தனது 20-11-2011 தேதிய அறிக்கையில், அரசு பொதுத் துறை நிறுவனங்கள் எல்லாம் நஷ்டத்தில் இயங்கக் காரணம், கடந்த கால தி.மு.க.

ஆட்சிதான் என்று சொல்லியிருக்கிறார். அதை அப்படியே “காப்பி” அடித்துத்தான், தற்போது நிதித் துறை அமைச்சரும் கூறியிருக்கிறார். சில உதாரணங்களைக் கூறுகிறேன்.

அரசு பொதுத் துறை நிறுவனங்களில் ஒன்று “சிப்காட்”.
2001-2002ஆம் ஆண்டு ஜெயலலிதா ஆட்சிக் காலத்தில் 11.76 கோடி ரூபாய் நஷ்டம். 2002-2003ஆம் ஆண்டு ஜெயலலிதா ஆட்சிக் காலத்தில் 29.15 கோடி ரூபாய் நஷ்டம்.

2006-2007ஆம் ஆண்டு தி.மு. கழக ஆட்சிக் காலத்தில் 56.69 கோடி ரூபாய் இலாபம்.
