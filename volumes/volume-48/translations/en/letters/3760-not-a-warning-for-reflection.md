# 3760. Not a Warning; Just Something to Think About!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 22 May 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

Replying in the Assembly on 13 May 2013 to a special calling-attention motion, Chief Minister Jayalalithaa concluded her long speech by saying:

> “A political party must work in every way for the progress of a country, act with the welfare of the public foremost, and serve as an example to other organisations. If opposition has to be expressed on any issue, it must be expressed only democratically and through peaceful, principled means. No one can accept the expression of opposition through violence. It is also the responsibility and duty of party leaders to keep their cadres disciplined, guide them along the path of social progress, and ensure that they do not engage in unlawful acts. ... If any party engages in violent acts that disturb public peace, damage public property or destroy law and order, this government will not hesitate to ban that party; it will certainly take action.”

When journalists met me and asked about the Chief Minister's statement in the Assembly that the PMK might be banned, I replied on 16 May 2013:

> “How can we take what she said as referring specifically to the PMK? She said generally that violent parties would be banned. A few years ago in Dharmapuri, three women were burned, buses were smashed, and three women students of the Coimbatore Agricultural College were burned alive inside a bus, their ashes sent back to their parents. If one reflects on what explanation they offered for that violence, the belief that any party can be suppressed merely by invoking the word ‘violence’ will remain only a dream.”

Because the killing of the agricultural-college students took place many years ago, I think it necessary to recall the details somewhat fully.

A case had been filed by the Directorate of Vigilance and Anti-Corruption against the present Chief Minister and others over granting unlawful permission to the seven-storey **Pleasant Stay** hotel in Kodaikanal in violation of development-control rules. On 2 February 2000, the Second Special Judge, Radhakrishnan, who heard the case, sentenced Jayalalithaa to one year's imprisonment. To enable an appeal, he also stayed operation of the judgment until 3 March 2000. But what happened that day?

As soon as the judgment was delivered, AIADMK supporters gathered outside the court complex resorted to violence there itself. They smashed buses passing that way. They pelted shops with stones and looted them, causing panic among people in the area. And not there alone—AIADMK supporters engaged in violence in many parts of Tamil Nadu, setting buses on fire and looting shops.

At that time, male and female students from the Coimbatore Agricultural College were returning to Coimbatore in two buses after conducting a study at the Paiyur Agricultural Research Station near Krishnagiri. Those were the buses AIADMK supporters attempted to block and attack. Despite the women students pleading to be spared, petrol bombs were thrown at the buses and the students were burned inside. Three women students—Kokilavani of Namakkal, Gayathri of Virudhachalam and Hemalatha of Chennai—were burned alive; seventeen more women students suffered burn injuries. The news caused students across Tamil Nadu to erupt in anger.

But in a statement issued then, Jayalalithaa said:

> “In Dharmapuri district, where extremists are raising their heads, grave atrocities have been committed and an attempt is being made to blame AIADMK members. DMK people, with the help of rowdies, mischief-makers and goondas, committed these acts of lawlessness and are blaming AIADMK members.”

She also demanded at the time that the DMK government be **“dismissed”** over the incident.

Although the case relating to the burning alive of the three students proceeded in due course, after the AIADMK returned to power in 2001 the case slowed down.

Trial began in the Dharmapuri Sessions Court on 5 November 2001. Twenty-two people were listed as witnesses. Since it was AIADMK rule, the source says twenty witnesses turned hostile. Kokilavani's father, Veerasamy, challenged this before the Madras High Court. In a judgment delivered on 20 September 2003, Justice Kanagaraj said, in the passage reproduced in the letter:

> “Because those accused in the case belong to the ruling party, police officers have treated this case like an orphaned child. They have acted in a manner that destroys trust and respect for the police. For this Himalayan error, this Court condemns the Home Secretary and the Additional S.P., CB-CID. The I.G. of CB-CID must initiate disciplinary action against the Additional S.P., CB-CID, for allowing prosecution witnesses to be broken down. To uphold the strength and majesty of law, and to ensure a fair and independent trial, the case is transferred from the Dharmapuri District Sessions Court to the Additional District Sessions Court at Salem.
>
> “Cases must be registered and action taken against the Village Administrative Officer and Village Assistant of Viruppatchipuram for offences such as giving false evidence, signing a false report, causing evidence connected with the case to be destroyed and enabling the accused to escape legal proceedings. Their conduct, which undermined public trust in government officials, was base and shameful.”

Were these charges made by Justice Kanagaraj ordinary ones? Yet even after that, trial did not begin in the Salem court.

On 3 December 2004, the Salem judge asked the police officer why, although fourteen months had passed since the bus-burning case was transferred from Krishnagiri to Salem, investigating officers failed to appear at each hearing and the case was being delayed. The judge asked what difficulty there was in obtaining the order appointing a public prosecutor. The police officer replied that he had written to higher officials asking that notification be issued for appointment of a public prosecutor, and that his superior too had written repeatedly to the government.

Kokilavani's father therefore again approached the Madras High Court. His petition said that the government had not issued a proper order appointing a public prosecutor and that the trial was not proceeding quickly in accordance with the High Court's earlier direction.

When the petition came up on 17 February 2005, Government Advocate Duraisamy said that documents relating to the Dharmapuri bus-burning case had gone missing, that court employees were searching for them, and that time was needed to reconstruct the missing records. Justice Kanagaraj then warned the State police:

> “Three students have been brutally burned to death. The Tamil Nadu Government is acting irresponsibly in this matter. The files must be found within three days and the trial expedited. If not, I will order a CBI investigation.”

When the matter came up again on 18 February 2005, Justice Kanagaraj's criticism, as reproduced in the source, was:

> “We had ordered that the case concerning the burning of the students be tried quickly in the Salem court. But it was not done. The case has been kept pending for the past fifteen months. The Tamil Nadu Government must explain why the trial was not conducted. I do not wish to place excessive blame on the government lawyer. From the date of the High Court's order until now, the trial has not been conducted. Every Home Secretary who held responsibility in Tamil Nadu from the date of the court's order until now must file an affidavit by the 27th next week explaining the reason for the delay. They must explain the reason for every day's delay in this case.”

That is the extent to which the case was dragged out under AIADMK rule.

Forgetting all this, and apparently assuming that the people of Tamil Nadu too will have forgotten it, the present rulers continue taking action against opposition parties. Police refuse permission even for public meetings. Reports say permission was denied even for a meeting proposed by the Viduthalai Chiruthaigal movement, and that the District Collector prohibited its leader Thirumavalavan from entering Villupuram district.

When DMK Treasurer Thalapathi M.K. Stalin participated in a wedding and public meeting in Karur district on the 15th, cases were filed against people who had put up advertising boards for the events. It is not good, once in government, to forget what one did when one was in opposition in the past.

**No government remains permanent forever. Today's rulers should not forget that. This is not a warning; it is for them to think about!**

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**22 May 2013**
