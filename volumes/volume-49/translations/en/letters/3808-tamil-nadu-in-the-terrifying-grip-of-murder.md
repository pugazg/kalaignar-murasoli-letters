# 3808. Tamil Nadu in the Terrifying Grip of Murder!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 21 September 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

When I criticise the police on the basis of events that have actually occurred, it is dismissed as criticism from the opposition. I shall therefore begin by citing reports from *Junior Vikatan*, a weekly generally regarded as supportive of the ruling party.

It reported that, in incidents of violence against women—the assault on a student in Delhi and on a journalist in Mumbai—the police in the northern States moved quickly to arrest the culprits. Yet nearly a month after Tavheed Sultana, an eighth-standard student in Tiruchi, was found on a railway track with her body mutilated, the police had not even established the cause of death. At a protest in Tiruchi, speakers bitterly charged that although the case was successively transferred among the Railway Police, Tiruchi City Police and the CB-CID, the police machinery was in such a coma that it had not even recovered Sultana's right hand and the parts of her body below the waist.

A district secretary of the Tamil Nadu Muslim Munnetra Kazhagam was reported as saying that the Railway Police, who first investigated the case, immediately declared that Sultana had committed suicide and refused the demand that the post-mortem be videographed. The City Police then used two mobile numbers written in her notebook to detain and question five engineering students. They claimed that Sultana had loved one of them, Umar Farooq, and that on the evening of the 13th he took her on his motorcycle to Rettaimalai temple, where he and his friends assaulted and killed her before placing the body on the railway track. The five were taken to the scene for investigation, and every newspaper reported it. Yet those five were later released and the death was again described as suicide. Sultana was found unclothed and her body was mutilated. Would anyone remove their clothing before committing suicide? Near the same stretch of track, a young man named Parthiban from Punganur in Tiruchi was found dead under mysterious circumstances on the 5th. In the murder of Ramajayam on 29 March the previous year, and in the earlier deaths of real-estate businessmen Durairaj and Thangavelu, the CB-CID had still arrested no one.

Under the headline “Rape and cruelty against women increase in Tamil Nadu,” *Dinamalar* reported on 13 September 2013 that cases of rape and cruelty inflicted on women by husbands and family members had increased over the previous year. Rape cases were especially high in Chennai, Villupuram and Tirunelveli districts. Crimes against women, which numbered 3,426 in 2009, rose to 4,306 the previous year. By July of the current year, 4,287 such crimes had already been recorded.

The *Indian Express* stated:

> The State Government has registered as many as 20,633 cases against 12,624 persons involved in hoarding and illegal transporting of essential commodities meant for PDS and 454 persons have been arrested under the Prevention of Black Marketing and Maintenance of Supplies of Essential Commodities Act

In another weekly, a question was asked: “Why have the culprits still not been found in the murders of Vellore Vellaiyappan, Salem auditor Ramesh and Tiruchi Ramajayam?” The reply was that three cases might become thirty and then three hundred. A young woman had been mysteriously killed in Tiruchi a few weeks earlier, and that case too had been transferred from the Tiruchi Police to the CB-CID. If so, what purpose did the ordinary police serve in maintaining law and order? The growing number of unsolved crimes reflected badly both on the Chief Minister, who controlled the Police Department, and on the Tamil Nadu Director-General of Police.

Between 1 May and 31 August, while prohibitory orders under Section 144 were in force, Villupuram district alone recorded twenty-eight murders, three bomb threats against schools, attempted murders and robberies, according to another report.

There is scarcely any need to write separately about illegal sand mining, for hardly a day passes without a newspaper report. The *Express* of the 14th published photographs of lorries standing in a long line while sand was removed from the Thenpennai river. In Sivaganga district, an attempt had been made only two days earlier to run a lorry over a Kallal police sub-inspector who had gone to act against sand mining. In Tirunelveli district, sand smugglers were using poclain machines at night to remove sand from the Thamirabarani in areas including Suthamalli, Vannarpettai, Rajavallipuram, Manappadai Veedu, Keelanatham, Seevalaperi and Palamadai. A member of the Police Department who aided the smuggling had himself been arrested.

The story of gold robberies across the State is equally grim: seven and a half kilograms from Coimbatore jeweller Jainlal; one and a half kilograms from a Coimbatore jewellery workshop; another one and a half kilograms in R.S. Puram; one kilogram after attacking a businessman in Kotagiri; three kilograms from a jewellery shop in Tiruverumbur, Tiruchi; four kilograms from Sriperumbudur jeweller Badhanchand; gold worth ₹20 crore from jewellery shops in Dharmapuri, Vellore, Tirunelveli and Panruti; and two kilograms in Meenakshipuram, Nagercoil.

On 16 September 2013, Mr Ramu wrote in *Theekkathir* under the title “Mercenary gangs that shake Tamil Nadu.” He said that murders had recently increased, that hired gangs were being used to target important leaders, and that even legislators—the first pillar of democracy—had reached the humiliating position of seeking police protection because they feared such gangs. According to the National Crime Records Bureau, the State's crime rate had risen by five per cent in the previous two years because of the activity of hired gangs. In Tamil Nadu, those gangs were said to charge between ₹10,000 and ₹1 lakh for a murder and between ₹25,000 and ₹1.5 lakh for an abduction, depending on the status of the victim. The broker who arranged the gang reportedly received a commission of at least twenty-five per cent.

Reports have also stated that thousands of driver posts in the Tamil Nadu Police and Fire and Rescue Services remain vacant, seriously affecting daily work.

A *Junior Vikatan* article observed that Chief Minister Jayalalithaa had once claimed that, immediately after the change of government, robbers, rowdies and thieves had all fled to Andhra Pradesh. Yet murder, robbery and abduction had risen in Chennai to such an extent that one might wonder whether they had all returned. In a single week there had been the abduction of a schoolboy for money, the abduction of a child to force it to beg, and the kidnapping of a businessman for ransom.

At Thiruvalluvar University in Vellore, unidentified persons entered and set fire to the Vice-Chancellor's room. In Mayiladuthurai, thieves carried away the gold finial from the Mayuranathar temple.

In a single day, twelve or thirteen people were murdered across Tamil Nadu. Four were killed in Chennai alone. Near Tambaram, student Sadasmi and her younger brother Yugesh were killed; Praveen Kumar was hacked to death in Jafferkhanpet; father and daughter Chelladurai and Leema were hacked to death in Nazareth; Venkatesh and Ilayaraja were killed near Sivaganga; elderly Bakkiyathammal was found with her throat cut in Thanjavur; and alleged chain-snatcher Sudhakar and his associate Thangaraj were killed in R.K. Nagar, Chennai. Near Thoothukudi, tea-shop owner Sembulingam was hacked to death. Looking at the longer list, the killers of Hindu Munnani leader Vellaiyappan, BJP functionary auditor Ramesh and Ramajayam, the younger brother of Tiruchi Nehru, had still not been caught. Seven people were hacked to death within twenty-four hours in Thoothukudi, Madurai, Sivaganga and Thanjavur. Ten murders had occurred within three months in the R.K. Nagar police-station area of Tondiarpet, and residents were reported to be terrified by the continuing killings.

The reports listed further incidents: Oil Raja murdered near the Tondiarpet IOC area in a dispute over protection money; a drug gang in the same area hacking B.T.C. mechanic Vasudevan of Medavakkam to pieces for money; newly married Kamalraj having his throat cut in Korukkupet; rowdies Double Suresh and Gopi killed in a gang clash; and rowdy Dhanasekaran murdered in broad daylight on the 15th in a revenge attack.

Near Aravakurichi in Karur district, a woman was found brutally murdered on the 1st, with a stone dropped on her head. Police investigation identified her as Thilagavathi of Periyakulam in Theni district and as a relative of an AIADMK legislator from Tiruchi East who was also the Government Chief Whip. Kathiresan of Periyakulam surrendered before a Madurai court on the 6th in connection with the case. Aravakurichi Police took him into custody for questioning, and he died during police custody the day before yesterday. Policemen drove away journalists who came to photograph the body. How Kathiresan died remains unknown. Meanwhile, the Aravakurichi inspector has been temporarily suspended. This is today's news.

Day after day, newspapers continue to carry reports about the police, murders and robberies. Yet those responsible for government appear unconcerned. They are preoccupied with preventing opposition parties from speaking, filing defamation cases when they do speak, imposing Section 144 orders in districts, banning leaders from entering districts, and obstructing political-party events. Because the police are deployed for these anti-democratic purposes, much of their time is consumed by them. Their basic duties—maintaining law and order and investigating murders and robberies—are neglected, and the people of Tamil Nadu suffer every day in the cruel grip of fear.

The present Chief Minister issued statements every day during the DMK Government claiming that the Police Department had collapsed. This letter has been written so that the truth about the present condition of the police under her own responsibility may be understood from the details compiled by Tamil Nadu's newspapers themselves.

**With affection,**
**M.K.**
21 September 2013

கடந்த 6ஆம் தேதி பெரியகுளம் பகுதியைச் சேர்ந்த கதிரேசன்
என்பவர் மதுரை நீதிமன்றத்தில் இந்த வழக்கு காரணமாகச்
சரணடைந்தார். அரவக்குறிச்சி போலீசார், கதிரேசனை போலீஸ்
காவலில் எடுத்து விசாரித்தனர். போலீஸ் காவலின் போது அந்தக்
கதிரேசன் நேற்று முன்தினம் இறந்திருக்கிறார். புகைப்படம் எடுக்க
வந்த பத்திரிகைக்காரர்களை அனுமதிக்காமல் காவலர் விரட்டி
விட்டனர். கதிரேசன் எப்படி இறந்தார் என்று தெரியவில்லை.
இதற்கிடையே அரவக்குறிச்சி இன்ஸ்பெக்டர் தற்காலிகப் பணி
நீக்கம் செய்யப்பட்டிருக்கிறார். இது இன்றைய செய்தி.

இவ்வாறு தொடர்ந்து அன்றாடம் காவல் துறையைப் பற்றியும்,
கொலை, கொள்ளைகளைப் பற்றியும் ஏடுகளில் செய்திகள் வந்து
கொண்டே இருக்கின்றன. இதைப்பற்றி ஆட்சிப் பொறுப்பிலே
இருப்பவர்கள் யாரும் கவலைப்படுவதாகவும் தெரியவில்லை.
எதிர்க்கட்சிகளை பேசவிடாமல் செய்வது, பேசினாலும் அவதூறு
வழக்குத் தொடுப்பது, மாவட்டங்களில் 144 தடை உத்தரவு
பிறப்பிப்பது, மாவட்டங்களுக்குள் வரவே கூடாது என்று

ஆணை பிறப்பிப்பது, அரசியல் கட்சிகளின் நிகழ்ச்சிகளுக்குத்
தடை விதிப்பது என்று ஆட்சியினர் ஜனநாயகத்திற்கு
விரோதமான முறையில் செயல்பட்டு வருகிறார்கள்; அத்தகைய
ஜனநாயகத்துக்கு விரோதமான காரியங்களில் காவல்துறையினர்
ஈடுபடுத்தப்படுவதால், அவர்களுடைய பெரும்பாலான நேரம்
அதிலேயே கழிந்து, அடிப்படைப் பணிகளான சட்டம் - ஒழுங்கு,
கொலை, கொள்ளைச் சம்பவங்களில் புலனாய்வு போன்றவை
புறக்கணிக்கப்பட்டு, தமிழக மக்கள் அச்சத்தின் கோரப்பிடியில்
அனுதினமும் தவிக்கிறார்கள்!
கழக ஆட்சி நடைபெற்ற போது காவல் துறை சீரழிந்து
விட்டது என்று அன்றாடம் அறிக்கை கொடுத்த இன்றைய
முதலமைச்சரின் பொறுப்பிலே உள்ள காவல் துறை எந்த
நிலையிலே உள்ளது என்பதை தமிழ்நாட்டுப் பத்திரிகைகள்
தொகுத்துத் தந்துள்ள இந்த விவரங்களிலிருந்து உண்மையைப்
புரிந்து கொள்வதற்காகவே இந்தக் கடிதம்!
