# 3733. Let Us Raise Our Voice!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 14 April 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

What *Dinamalar* and *Dina Thanthi* wrote a few months ago about how the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly had functioned in its previous session has now come back to my mind.

*Dinamalar* wrote:

> “DMDK MLAs staged walkouts several times. DMK MLAs were expelled twice. An Assembly is one in which the opposition too is included. Even if the ruling party has brute majority strength, legislative democracy must be followed. The opposition parties must be enabled to remain in the Assembly. The AIADMK government is failing to do this. It is improper—and even a violation of the rules—for the Chief Minister to make every announcement under Rule 110. It is unacceptable to make announcements under Rule 110 merely so that members may speak only in praise, with no debate or criticism. However intense the hostility, not even a single member has so far been expelled from Parliament. The Assembly too is founded on such democracy. But here, murders of democracy are being enacted. This is not good for the country. A system in which no issue can be debated and only announcements are made is not democratic. Only in Tamil Nadu does this happen in this way. Under the AIADMK government led by Jayalalithaa, it has even become routine. What justice is there in insisting that everything must be accepted without debate? As a result, what happens in the Assembly does not even become known outside. Such practices are good neither for democracy nor for the country!”

An editorial in *Dina Thanthi* said:

> “During the winter session of the Assembly that ended three days ago, 23 Bills were passed in a single day. They too were passed without debate. The parties represented in the Legislative Assembly should have sought discussion on the Bills, and they should have been passed only after debate. When a superfast express train passes stations on its route without stopping, it is called a ‘run-through’. In the same way, without debate, 23 Bills were passed as a ‘run-through’ in a single hour.”

These were criticisms published even in newspapers generally supportive of the ruling party! Refusing even to listen to the opposition's views—and, to go further, deciding not to permit an opposition to exist at all and making every effort necessary to eliminate it—is not becoming of a good democratic government.

After the 2011 Assembly election in Tamil Nadu, the DMDK became the principal opposition party. In that election, the DMDK had been an ally of the AIADMK. What is that party now being made to suffer under this government? There is no alliance between the DMDK and the DMK. Even so, it is painful to see the actions the AIADMK government is taking against the DMDK as the opposition party. The ruling party and the opposition are two sides of the same coin. In a democracy, each must accord the other due respect.

Suddenly, five DMDK MLAs meet the Chief Minister. They say they met her only to secure benefits needed by the people of their constituencies. If opposition MLAs were going to meet the Chief Minister for their constituents, should they not have obtained permission from their party leadership? It was because that party too worked with the AIADMK in the last election that those now in government were able to come to power and exercise authority. Is this how gratitude is shown for that?

Did *Dinamalar* not report that a woman MLA from the Dalit community who had been elected on the DMDK ticket cried out that she was “living in an environment without security”? Did the paper not report her as saying: “When I go to attend a government function, ruling-party people issue death threats against me. There is no security here for life or property. The situation is so unsafe that women cannot go out”?

At the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly sitting on 8 February 2013, an MLA who had been elected on the DMDK ticket and was said to have broken away from the party—Tamilazhagan—said in the House that because he had left the party, the government was doing good things for his constituency, and openly invited the other DMDK members to take the same decision so that they too could benefit. Was it proper to issue such an invitation in the Assembly itself, to the point of making a mockery of legislative democracy? DMDK MLAs objected to that speech in the House and a dispute arose between the two sides. This was an issue among members who had all been elected on the DMDK ticket. Yet, on the allegation that one member who had been elected for the DMDK but was acting outside its party discipline had been attacked, action was initiated against six members who remained firmly with the DMDK, and they were suspended for six months.

Even before all this, on 3 February 2012, Vijayakanth, who was then Leader of the Opposition, was suspended from the Assembly session for ten days on the ground that he had behaved in a manner that obstructed the proceedings. For those days he was also placed in a position where he could receive none of the benefits, salary, concessions or entitlements available to him either as Leader of the Opposition or as an MLA.

It did not stop with action inside the Assembly. He was the Leader of the Opposition—how many defamation cases were filed against him? And not against him alone: how many cases have been filed under this government against newspapers, television channels and the media? Even yesterday there was a defamation case against CNN-IBN! Do you know what their offence was? In a cartoon they published, a journalist was shown asking the Chief Minister how she ran the government, and she was shown replying, “Like a boss.” A defamation case for that. *Ananda Vikatan* published an article about Tamil Nadu ministers. I wrote that such an article had appeared. Immediately, every minister mentioned in that article separately filed a defamation case against me. What is happening in Tamil Nadu? Is this an Emergency? In the interests of the people of Tamil Nadu, is no one permitted to criticise the government or the Chief Minister?

Last year, on the basis of some complaint against the DMDK MLA from the Tiruttani constituency, he was arrested. He came out on bail the very next day. Immediately an appeal was filed in the High Court, the bail was cancelled, and now, for the past three days, four special teams are said to be searching to arrest him. What cruelty is this? Is this military rule? Is this dictatorship?

Even permission sought by the Viduthalai Chiruthaigal Katchi to hold a procession and public meeting for Annal Ambedkar's birthday was denied under this government. The party approached the High Court; its leader Thirumavalavan himself went to court and assured it that no untoward incident would occur, after which the High Court granted permission to hold the Ambedkar birthday procession. But do you know what is painful? It is said that the Tamil Nadu government is appealing against that order. This anti-democratic approach is an injustice inflicted by the AIADMK government upon both the Constitution of India and Annal Ambedkar, who framed it.

Similarly, this government denied permission for TESO to hold a conference for Sri Lankan Tamils; later, by the grace of the court, that conference was held successfully. Are these the practices of good governance, or the cruelties of jungle rule?

When the DMK returned to office in 1989 after a gap of thirteen years, I had just begun reading the Budget for the first time when AIADMK members led by Jayalalithaa tried to snatch it from my hands, tear it up and attack me. They were suspended from Assembly proceedings for a few days. When the Congress party requested that even that suspension be revoked, I rose and said: “Neither I nor this government certainly has the narrow-minded attitude that the proceedings of this House should be conducted without the opposition. Let what happened remain what happened; let what happens hereafter be good. It is on that basis that I approach this issue. I request that the resolution passed that day as an echo of the emotion and agitation then prevailing be withdrawn, and that the Leader of the Opposition and the others be allowed to work in the House.”

Only a few days ago, I cited several examples in the Assembly of the generosity with which the DMK had acted. On 7 February 2011, when the Legislative Assembly met in the new Assembly building constructed by the DMK government at the Omandurar complex, O. Panneerselvam, then Deputy Leader of the Opposition, moved a resolution under Assembly Rule 20(1): “As doctors have advised Leader of the Opposition J. Jayalalithaa to take complete rest because of ill health, permission should be granted for her absence from this session of the Legislative Assembly.” The resolution was seconded by AIADMK Assembly Whip K.A. Sengottaiyan and passed by voice vote. Although the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam, then the ruling party, and its allies had an overwhelming majority in the Assembly, they accepted that opposition resolution on humanitarian grounds without raising any objection. That is “political civility”!

But now affairs are conducted with the idea that there should be no opposition at all—or, even if one exists, that it should not be allowed to discharge its duty. Some may attribute ulterior motives and criticise me merely because I say that politics must have, at its foundation, dignity, culture and civility. In a democracy, politics should be met politically; it is not right to think that one can confront it and succeed through such uncivilised practices. I lovingly call upon everyone who believes in democratic values: come, let us unite and raise our voice!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**14 April 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. **DMDK** is used for `தே.மு.தி.க.`; party names and allegations remain attributed exactly to the source context.
2. **Rule 110** and **Rule 20(1)** are retained as Assembly procedural references.
3. The source's train metaphor `ரன்-த்ரூ` is retained as **run-through** in the translated newspaper quotation.
4. `காட்டாட்சி` is rendered **jungle rule** to preserve the source's rhetorical contrast with `நல்லாட்சி` (“good governance”).

குரல் வாக்கெடுப்பின் மூலம் இத்தீர்மானம் நிறைவேற்றப்பட்டது.
சட்டப்பேரவையில் ஆளுங்கட்சியாக இருந்த திராவிட
முன்னேற்றக் கழகமும், அதன் தோழமைக் கட்சிகளும் அதிகப்
பெரும்பான்மையாக இருந்தாலும், எதிர்க்கட்சி கொண்டு
வந்த அந்தத் தீர்மானத்திற்கு மனிதாபிமான அடிப்படையில்,
எந்தவிதமான எதிர்ப்பும் தெரிவிக்காமல் ஏற்றுக்கொண்டன.
அதெல்லாம் “அரசியல் நாகரீகம்”!
ஆனால் தற்போது எதிர்க்கட்சி என்ற ஒன்றே இருக்கக்
கூடாது; இருந்தாலும் அது தன் கடமையை ஆற்ற அனுமதிக்கக்
கூடாது என்ற எண்ணத்தோடு, காரியங்கள் நடத்தப்படுகின்றன.
அரசியலிலே, அடிப்படையில் ஒரு கண்ணியமும், பண்பாடும்
நாகரிகமும் இருந்திட வேண்டுமென்பதற்காக இந்தக்
கருத்துக்களை நான் தெரிவிப்பதற்கே உள் நோக்கம் கற்பித்து;
ஒரு சிலர் விமர்சிக்கக்கூடும். ஜனநாயகத்தில் அரசியலை
அரசியலாகச் சந்திக்க வேண்டுமே தவிர, இதுபோன்ற நாகரிகமற்ற
செயல்பாடுகளின் மூலமாகச் சந்தித்துச் சாதிக்கலாம் என்று
நினைப்பது சரியல்ல என்பதே என் கருத்து. ஜனநாயக மாண்புகளில்
நம்பிக்கை கொண்டுள்ள அனைவரும் ஒன்றிணைந்து ஓங்கிக்
குரல் கொடுத்திட “வாரீர்” என்று அன்புடன் அழைக்கிறேன்.
