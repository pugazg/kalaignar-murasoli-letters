# 3796. Women’s Advancement — Then and Now!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

A building for an Alternative Dispute Resolution Centre was constructed on the Madras High Court campus by the Tamil Nadu State Legal Services Authority at a cost of ₹3.20 crore. When it was inaugurated on 20 August 2013, Chief Minister Jayalalithaa attended and recited the various schemes that the AIADMK Government was implementing for women. She said those schemes were based on the principle that women should not be subjected to social or economic hardship, and claimed that her Government was setting an example for every other State. As I read her words, what appeared before my eyes were the three students—Kokilavani, Hemalatha and Gayathri—who screamed as they were burned alive when AIADMK members stopped and set fire to a Coimbatore Agricultural University bus in Dharmapuri on 2 February 2000.

In that case, the Dharmapuri Sessions Judge spoke in severe terms:

> “Because those connected with the case belonged to the ruling party, police officers treated the case like an orphaned child. They acted in a manner that damaged public confidence and respect for the police. For this Himalayan blunder, this Court censures the Home Secretary and the Additional Superintendent of Police, CB-CID. The Inspector-General, CB-CID, must take disciplinary action against the Additional Superintendent of Police for permitting the prosecution evidence to be undermined.”

That judgment too came back to mind. Students were burned to death—and now lofty claims are made about honouring women.

Nor is that all. On 21 December 2012, *The Times of India* published a long article on rape cases in Tamil Nadu. Do you know its headline? It said that more than 500 cases had been registered during the year up to September and that rape in Tamil Nadu had risen by nine per cent. According to the Tamil Nadu Police website, 484 rape cases were recorded between January and September 2011, while 528 were recorded during the same period in 2012. Of those 528 cases, the largest number—75—was registered in Chennai alone. It is under this AIADMK Government, which claims to honour women, that such crimes have been increasing.

There was another atrocity near Srivaikuntam. Punitha, a twelve-year-old Class VII student who had gone to school, was raped by an unidentified man and strangled to death. The story appeared prominently in every newspaper—under this “glorious” Government.

On 12 April, another atrocity occurred in the industrial city of Tiruppur. An eight-year-old Malayali girl, alone at home after returning from school, was gang-raped despite her screams.

It was not only in Tiruppur. Near Andipatti in Theni district, Nadia, an SSLC student from Palakkombai village, was raped and murdered.

When a sexual crime occurs in Delhi, the entire capital is convulsed; there are marches and siege protests. The moment news came that a Kerala girl had been sexually assaulted, the Kerala Chief Minister and Malayali associations expressed concern and extended comfort. Yet in the Tamil State ruled by a woman, sexual crimes occur widely with no one to demand accountability. Only yesterday the Chief Minister said the Government had instructed the police to treat offences involving sexual violence as grave crimes and take action. But such brutal offences are spreading in direct contradiction of what she says.

In Tiruchi, Dr Rani filed a case against AIADMK legislator Paranjothi. Although the court directed that action be taken, none was taken for many days. The affected woman has still received no remedy under the AIADMK Government.

Likewise, Vasanthi alleged that police officers brutally raped her inside the Kadamalaikundu police station. When the police failed to act on her complaint, she approached the Madurai Bench of the High Court. The judge observed:

> “Rape is itself a crime against society. When the allegation is that police personnel committed it inside a police station, the Superintendent of Police, as the senior police authority, should have taken severe action. Instead, he even gave an interview declaring Vasanthi’s complaint false. Despite the Court’s order, no inquiry has been conducted for all these days.”

That too happened under this “glorious” Government. The AIADMK administration watches while the fence itself devours the crop.

A list of women and girls who were raped, or raped and murdered, would keep growing:

- Gayathri, a nine-year-old girl from Andiyappan village near Tirupattur in Vellore district, was sexually assaulted and murdered on 29 December 2012.
- Nandini of Sivagiri was raped and murdered.
- The body of a woman named Vijaya was found in Saidapet, according to *Dina Thanthi* of 11 January 2013.
- In Nagapattinam district, two men raped an eleven-year-old Dalit girl, according to *Dinamani* of 23 December 2012.
- A young woman named Suganthi was raped on the banks of the Manimuthar in Virudhachalam, according to *Dinamani* of 27 December 2012.
- Near Chidambaram, Sandhya, a young woman from Manjakuzhi village, was raped and thrown from a third-floor building into the street, according to *Theekkathir* of 27 December 2012.
- In Thatharavalasai village of Harur taluk, Dharmapuri, a married young woman named Menaka was raped and murdered, according to *Dinamani* of 28 December 2012.
- Mariammal, a young woman living with her father and three children after separating from her husband, was raped when assailants entered her house in the early morning at Matha Nagar Second Street, Thoothukudi, according to *Dinamani* of 29 December 2012.
- Kalki, a schoolgirl from Rukmanipuram in Vanur, Villupuram, was raped and her body was left hanging in a casuarina grove, according to *Dina Thanthi* of 5 January 2013.
- A twelve-year-old girl was sexually assaulted in Udumalpet and, unable to bear the humiliation, set herself on fire.
- An eighteen-year-old young woman was raped by a gang of four near the District Collector’s office in Namakkal, according to *Dina Thanthi* of 6 January 2013.
- Abinaya, a seventeen-year-old girl from Timiri near Arcot in Vellore district, set herself on fire and died because of threats and intimidation directed at her chastity, according to *Dina Thanthi* of 6 January 2013.
- An intruder entered a house in Mogappair, Ambattur, and attempted to rape a Plus Two student, according to *Dina Thanthi* of 6 January 2013.
- Premalatha of Pozhichalur, Chennai, was raped and murdered, and her body was thrown away, according to *Dina Thanthi* of 6 January 2013.
- Meena, a married young woman from near Thottiyam in Tiruchi district, was abducted in a car and raped by a gang of four when she had gone to a relative’s wedding.
- Kannamma, a domestic worker in Koyambedu, Chennai, was raped by the owner of the house, according to *Dina Thanthi* of 7 January 2013.
- An eleven-year-old Class VI student grazing goats at Pudur in Orathanadu was raped, according to *Dina Thanthi* of 4 January 2013.
- Near Ranipet, the burned body of a murdered girl was found in a cremation ground, according to *Dina Thanthi* of 13 January 2013.
- A woman working at Selvi Xerox in Adambakkam, Chennai, was raped, according to *Dina Thanthi* of 4 January 2013.

The newspaper list of dreadful atrocities committed against women under the AIADMK Government could go on and on.

Enough has been said about the countless injustices being inflicted upon women under AIADMK rule. You may ask: can I, like Chief Minister Jayalalithaa, list what the DMK Government achieved for women? I shall answer that too.

Based on the resolution adopted at the Self-Respect Conference held in Chengalpattu in 1929, it was the DMK Government under my leadership that passed a special law in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly on 6 May 1989 granting women equal rights in parental property.

Yesterday the Chief Minister spoke of the marriage-assistance schemes being implemented under AIADMK rule. The Moovalur Ramamirtham Ammaiyar Marriage Assistance Scheme was first introduced under the DMK Government in 1989. The Anjugam Ammaiyar Memorial Inter-Caste Marriage Assistance Scheme was also established by the DMK Government in 1989 for those entering inter-caste marriages. But because Anjugam Ammaiyar happened to be the woman who gave birth to me, persons of “goodwill” (?) have now removed her name from the scheme.

The Dr Dharmambal Ammaiyar Memorial Widow Remarriage Scheme was introduced by the DMK Government in 1975.

When the DMK Government assumed office in 1989–90, it ordered free education up to the undergraduate level for women students from families whose annual income was below ₹12,000. It was named the E.V.R. Nagammaiyar Memorial Free Undergraduate Education Scheme for Women. When the DMK returned to office in 1996, the income ceiling was raised from ₹12,000 to ₹24,000. There was likewise the E.V.R. Maniammaiyar Memorial Marriage Assistance Scheme for the Daughters of Poor Widows.

The Dr Muthulakshmi Memorial Maternity Financial Assistance Scheme was introduced by the DMK Government in 1989. The DMK administration also implemented financial assistance for women entrepreneurs, priority for women in the allotment of industrial plots, assistance to fisherwomen, fair-price shops managed by women and small-business credit schemes for women.

It was under the DMK Government that, for the first time in India, thirty-three per cent reservation was provided for women in local-body elections. As a result, the Government created the remarkable situation in which 44,143 women assumed office in local bodies.

The Chief Minister said that all-women police stations were established under AIADMK rule in 1992. But as early as 1973, the DMK Government had begun the programme of appointing women to the police force.

On 1 June 1975, the DMK Government introduced financial assistance for destitute widows and women deserted by their husbands.

In 1990, the DMK Government became the first in India to implement thirty per cent reservation for women in government employment, opening the way for many thousands of women to obtain public-service jobs.

As a programme supporting impoverished women, the DMK Government introduced in July 2008 a monthly assistance of ₹400 for 9,100 poor women above the age of fifty who remained unmarried and were unable to earn a livelihood.

Through the women’s programme begun by the DMK Government in Dharmapuri in May 1989, numerous women’s self-help groups were established in rural areas throughout Tamil Nadu. By joining together and undertaking enterprises through those groups, rural women have earned incomes and improved their standard of living.

Thus, under DMK rule, standing in the path of Periyar and Anna, major measures were undertaken across many fields so that women could advance socially, educationally and economically, and stand as equals of men. Those concerned with women’s advancement cannot have forgotten them. Nor can today’s rulers conceal them.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**

22 August 2013

உதவிகள், மகளிர் நடத்தும் நியாய விலைக்கடைகள், மகளிர்
சிறுவணிகக் கடன் திட்டம் போன்ற திட்டங்களும் கழக ஆட்சியில்
நிறைவேற்றப்பட்டன.

இந்தியாவிலேயே முதலாவதாக உள்ளாட்சித் தேர்தல்களில்
பெண்களுக்கு 33 விழுக்காடு இட ஒதுக்கீடு வழங்கப்பட்டதும்
தி.மு. கழக ஆட்சியிலேதான். அதன் காரணமாக அப்போது 44,143
பெண்கள் உள்ளாட்சி அமைப்புகளில் பதவியேற்கும் உன்னத
நிலையைக் கழக அரசு உருவாக்கிக் காட்டியது.

1992இல் அ.தி.மு.க. ஆட்சியில்தான் அனைத்து மகளிர்
காவல் நிலையம் ஏற்படுத்தப்பட்டது என்று முதலமைச்சர்
சொல்லியிருக்கிறார். ஆனால் 1973ஆம் ஆண்டிலேயே, தி.மு.
கழக ஆட்சியில் “காவல் துறையில் பெண்களை நியமனம் செய்யும்
திட்டம்” தொடங்கப்பட்டுவிட்டது.

1-6-1975இல் ஆதரவற்ற விதவைப் பெண்கள் மற்றும்
கணவனால் கைவிடப்பட்டோருக்கு உதவித்தொகை வழங்கும்
திட்டம் கழக ஆட்சியில் தொடங்கப்பட்டது.

1990இல் தி.மு. கழக ஆட்சியில் இந்தியாவிலேயே முதல்
முறையாக அரசுப்பணிகளில் பெண்களுக்கு 30 விழுக்காடு
இட ஒதுக்கீடு வழங்கும் திட்டம் நடைமுறைப்படுத்தப்பட்டு,
பல்லாயிரக்கணக்கான பெண்கள் அரசு வேலை வாய்ப்புகள் பெற
வழிவகுக்கப்பட்டது.

2008 முதல் புதிய திட்டமாகக் கழக ஆட்சியில் தொடங்கப்பட்டு,
