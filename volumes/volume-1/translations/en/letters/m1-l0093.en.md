# 93. The Birthday Celebration and a Few Forgotten Details!

## Translator's Note

Kalaignar begins by warmly acknowledging the scale and success of Kamaraj's seventy-second birthday celebration. His argument is not against honouring a Tamil leader. It is against the double standard by which Congress can organise arches, posters and a vast procession, while DMK celebrations draw ridicule, and against *Navasakthi* continuing to caricature Anna five years after his death.

The letter combines political memory with a precise contemporary example. It recalls Kamaraj's harsh words about the DMK, N. S. Krishnan, Periyar and Anna, then turns to a *Dinamani* description of the birthday procession. Kalaignar accepts a float glorifying Kamaraj, but questions the propriety of another float showing a policeman drinking with a man and woman. He deliberately closes by praising Congress workers' organisational success before promising a later political analysis.

`Udanpirappē` is retained because no single English substitute carries its literal sense of one born alongside him together with the equality, affection, shared identity, and movement solidarity Kalaignar gives it.

## English Translation

Udanpirappē,

Kamaraj's seventy-second birthday was celebrated in magnificent fashion in the city of Chennai. Decorative arches at seventy-two places; many kinds of posters; on every poster, Kamaraj in a different sacred likeness; garlands of praise in posters and speeches calling him “Gandhi's heir,” “the man who came to save the world,” and “the one visible god today embodying discipline, honesty and freedom from corruption.” Musical concerts and words of praise! Festive festoons and a great procession! Beautiful pictures and multicoloured vehicles! Sights that grow sweeter the more one thinks of them! And countless other marvels!

When one hears that a Tamil has risen to eminence, it is natural for another Tamil with feeling to rejoice. Rejoice he must.

When Kamaraj travelled to Russia, Arignar Anna expressed precisely such a view and praised him.

When a Congress meeting was held in Teynampet to honour Kamaraj upon his return from Russia, Kamaraj showered forth these golden words: “I doubt the very birth of the DMK men!”

Even after that, the country knows that neither members of the movement nor its leader Anna used platforms or newspapers to fling back heated words in reply.

At that time, however, we did explain to the people what manner of man Kamaraj was by pointing to his speeches.

When even Prime Minister Indira Gandhi is no exception to his harsh Tamil words and turns of phrase, what account are people like us?

We have not forgotten Kamaraj thundering at a beach meeting about Kalaivanar N. S. Krishnan:

“Some clown has gone to Russia, returned, and is babbling something!”

Nor are these political events that can be forgotten: Kamaraj roaring, “I shall cut off Periyar's big toe,” and Kuthoosi Gurusamy raining the sharpest arrows of condemnation in response.

The country remembers Kamaraj referring to Anna on platforms as “this fellow” and “that fellow,” and Anna displaying political magnanimity by replying, “Even devotees address God himself as ‘he’ and ‘him.’”

Even if we try to forget all this, today's official Congress newspaper, *Navasakthi*, and Congress speakers keep warning us not to forget any of it.

Though five years have passed since Anna died and became immortal, that newspaper has still not ceased writing obscenely about him or publishing a mocking cartoon of him in connection with Kamaraj's birthday.

Everyone has old stories! The point is that those stories ought not to be stirred up, not that they cannot be stirred up.

Our view is that political arenas must not be turned into stages for the performances of street-corner balladeers. Even when our own people cross the limit a little, we condemn and restrain them.

But the mockery of Anna by *Navasakthi*, which functions as the official Congress newspaper, cannot be tolerated. That is why I have now reminded you of just one or two examples of Kamaraj's political culture.

Someone may argue that *Navasakthi* is not the Congress's official newspaper. Yet whether the Congress in the North is the official Congress, or the Congress in Tamil Nadu under Kamaraj is official, remains a riddle no one can explain!

Opposing the Katchatheevu agreement, every opposition party in Parliament except the Communists, including the Old Congress, staged a walkout. But here *Navasakthi* reduced that news to a small item and printed Foreign Minister Swaran Singh's reply, “The agreement is right,” in large bold letters.

This is the confusion of policy within the Old Congress.

Setting all this aside, we too rejoice that Kamaraj's birthday celebration was conducted splendidly.

Two or three years ago, I myself attended a Kamaraj birthday celebration conducted by the Student Congress and spoke in greeting.

Udanpirappē, did you notice one thing? They may celebrate a birthday; paste posters of many kinds; conduct arches, festoons and processions. But when our people celebrate a birthday, how their anger overflows!

Mockery.

Taunting.

Abuse.

Derision.

Sarcasm.

How many explosions of hatred! How many editorials! How many mocking cartoons!

The country will not fail to compare us, who came in Anna's path after he said he rejoiced at the honour accorded to a distinguished Tamil, with the generosity of Congress members who drew an obscene caricature of that same Anna at Kamaraj's celebration.

The Kamaraj birthday procession was indeed magnificent. Yes, it was one of the largest processions held in Chennai. Let us admit it!

See how *Dinamani* describes the procession:

“A decorated float portraying Kamaraj as ‘Karikalan’ and ‘Senguttuvan’ travelled in the procession.”

This is part of *Dinamani*'s description. The workers of his party have the right and affection to compare Kamaraj with whomever they wish. No one wants to interfere in that.

Now see what *Dinamani* says about another float in the procession:

“One decorated float contained a scene in which a policeman drank liquor while, beside him, a man and woman also drank and travelled along making merry.”

This was one of the special features of the Kamaraj birthday procession! Kamaraj saw that scene, waved his hand and rejoiced!

Some police officers may commit wrongs. But we must consider whether it is “proper procedure” to include, in a former Chief Minister's birthday procession, a display that blames the whole department, and for *Dinamani* to publish it with delight.

I cannot understand how this decorated vehicle escaped the eyes of our friend Kaviyarasu Kannadasan, who enthusiastically supervised the procession arrangements and birthday events.

I praise the enthusiasm of the Congress workers who celebrated Kamaraj's birthday more splendidly than Gandhi Jayanti or Nehru's birthday, and I rejoice at the success they achieved in that effort.

I have spoken about the celebration. Next week I shall explain many more details, politically.

With affection,

M. K.

(28-07-1974)

எத்தனை எத்தனை வெறுப்பு வெடிகள்! எத்தனை தலையங்கங்கள்! எத்தனை கேலிச் சித்திரங்கள்!

ஒரு தமிழ்ப் பெருமகனுக்கு நடைபெறுகின்ற பாராட்டைக் கண்டு, மகிழ்வதாகச் சொன்ன அண்ணாவின் வழி வந்த நம்மையும், அந்த அண்ணாவுக்குக் காமராஜர் விழாவில் ஆபாசக் கேலிச் சித்திரம் வரைந்து காட்டும் காங்கிரசார் மனவளத்தையும், நாடு ஒப்பிட்டுப் பார்க்காமல் போய்விடாது!

காமராஜர் பிறந்த நாள் விழா ஊர்வலம், மகத்தானதுதான்; ஆம் - சென்னையில் நடைபெற்ற பெரிய ஊர்வலங்களில் ஒன்று - ஒப்புக் கொள்வோம்!

அந்த ஊர்வலச் சிறப்புப் பற்றி, ‘தினமணி’ வர்ணிக்கிறது பார் -

“காமராஜரைக் ‘கரிகாலன்’ என்றும், ‘செங்குட்டுவன்’ என்றும் சித்தரிக்கும் அலங்கார வண்டி ஒன்று ஊர்வலத்தில் சென்றது.”

“தினமணி” வர்ணனையில் இது ஒரு பகுதி; காமராஜரை யாருடனும் ஒப்பிடும் உரிமையும் - பாசமும் அவர்களது கட்சித் தொண்டர்களுக்கு உண்டு; அதில் யாரும் குறுக்கிட விரும்பவில்லை.

ஊர்வலத்தில் சென்ற இன்னொரு வண்டியைப் பற்றி வர்ணிக்கிறது பார், ‘தினமணி’ -

“ஓர் அலங்கார வண்டியில் போலீஸ்காரர் மது அருந்த - அவருக்குப் பக்கத்தில் ஓர் ஆணும் பெண்ணும் மதுவருந்திக் கும்மாளம் போட்டுச் செல்லும் காட்சி இடம் பெற்றிருக்கிறது.”

இது, காமராஜர் பிறந்த நாள் விழா ஊர்வலச் சிறப்புக்களில் ஒன்று! அந்தக் காட்சி கண்டு காமராஜர் கை அசைத்து மகிழ்ந்திருக்கிறார்!

போலீசாரில் சிலர் தவறு செய்யக்கூடும்; அதற்காக அந்த இலாகாவையே பழி கூறுகின்ற ஒரு காட்சியை - முன்னாள் முதலவர் ஒருவரின் பிறந்த நாளில் இடம்பெறச் செய்வதும், அதனை மகிழ்ச்சியுடன் ‘தினமணியார்’ வெளியிடுவதும் ‘ஒழுங்குமுறை’தானா என்று சிந்திக்க வேண்டும்.

ஊர்வல ஏற்பாடுகள், பிறந்த நாள் நிகழ்ச்சிகள் இவைகளை உற்சாகத்துடன் கவனித்த நண்பர் கவியரசு கண்ணதாசன் அவர்களின் கண்களிலே இந்த அலங்கார வண்டி எப்படித் தென்படாமல் போயிற்று என்றே எனக்குப் புரியவில்லை.

காந்தி ஜெயந்தியைவிட - நேரு பிறந்த நாளைவிட - மிக விமரிசையாகக் காமராஜர் பிறந்த நாள் விழாவைக் கொண்டாடிய காங்கிரசுத் தொண்டர்களின் ஆர்வத்தை நான் பாராட்டுகிறேன்; அவர்கள், அந்த முயற்சியில் பெற்ற வெற்றிக்காகவும் மகிழ்கிறேன்.

விழா பற்றிச் சொன்னேன்; அடுத்த கிழமை, மேலும் பல விவரங்களை விளக்குகிறேன் - அரசியல் ரீதியாக!

அன்புள்ள,

மு. க.

(28-07-1974)
