# 3797. The Day of Bidding Them Farewell Is Not Far Away!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

It is said that the world has 6,800 languages. Yet only about 2,000 of the languages now in existence possess global importance. Among them, only seven—Greek, Latin, Arabic, Persian, Chinese, Hebrew and Sanskrit—had been accorded the status of a “Classical Language.”

Tamil stands above the other classical languages. There is abundant evidence for this.

Among the classical languages, Latin and Hebrew are no longer in common use. Although Greek declined for a period, it is only now again growing richer. Sanskrit is not a spoken language today. Since Chinese uses an ideographic writing system, linguists say it cannot clearly express the feelings of the heart. Arabic is much later in age, while Persian is written in the Arabic script.

When all these classical languages are considered, it becomes clear that Tamil possesses a distinctive excellence above them all. In literary richness, Tamil has an uninterrupted and continuous body of literature stretching across 2,500 years.

Tamil does not depend upon any other language; it is a language of its own distinct character. Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam, Kannada, Tulu and several others are described as Dravidian languages. Tamil stands as the source language of this entire Dravidian family.

It was Dr Caldwell who explained this truth to us and to the world. His *Comparative Grammar of the Dravidian Languages*, based on his study of twelve Dravidian languages including Tamil, Telugu and Malayalam, established it.

Tamil has now received the distinction of being a Classical Language and brought joy and pride to all of us. Was not the DMK responsible for achieving it?

For more than a century, Parithimar Kalaignar and other Tamil scholars dreamed that Tamil should be accorded classical-language status. That dream was realised because the United Progressive Alliance, in which the DMK participated, was in office at the Union level and because the demand was firmly pressed through Mrs Sonia Gandhi. Later, when the institution for Classical Tamil research was located in Karnataka, we repeatedly demanded that it be moved to Tamil Nadu. That too was secured during DMK rule. The DMK Government allotted the necessary land for the institution and began measures to construct an impressive permanent campus there.

In the meantime, the Classical Tamil research institution functioned temporarily from the State-owned Palar Illam. Its library contained many rare research books and ancient palm-leaf manuscripts. When it needed more space, the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly had already moved from Fort St George to the newly constructed complex at Omandurar Government Estate. The DMK Government therefore established the institute’s library in the vacated space at the Fort and named it after Pavendhar.

But the moment the AIADMK came to power, it could not tolerate that Tamil had received classical status during Karunanidhi’s administration. Without allotting any alternative premises to the library of the Classical Tamil institute, it emptied the place overnight.

Newspapers reported that all the books had been piled into three rooms; that the Tamil Nadu Government had not replied to the institute’s many letters about the lack of space; and that scholars and research students were demanding that both the library they had used and the institute itself be made fully functional again.

Perhaps because the very name Parithimar Kalaignar—the first scholar to demand classical status for Tamil—contains the word “Kalaignar,” or because the institute was brought to Chennai under a DMK Government, the AIADMK administration has climbed to the summit of political spite and subjected the institution to every possible hardship.

In a letter to me dated 8 November 2005, Mrs Sonia Gandhi, Chairperson of the United Progressive Alliance, wrote:

> “I am glad that all the formalities for declaring Tamil as a Classical Language have now been completed. This is an achievement for all the constituents of the UPA Govt., but particular credit goes to you and your party.”

In Tamil, she conveyed that she was happy all formalities for declaring Tamil a Classical Language had been completed; that it was an achievement for every constituent of the UPA Government; but that the particular credit belonged to me and my party.

After honouring Tamil by declaring it a Classical Language, the Union Government entrusted Classical Tamil research, development and dissemination to the Central Institute of Indian Languages in Mysuru.

On 28 November 2006, I wrote to the Union Minister for Human Resource Development:

> “The objectives of the Central Institute of Indian Languages at Mysuru were not framed from the perspective of a classical language. That institution works to promote science, technology and a sense of unity through all Indian languages. Therefore, with the advancement of Tamil’s distinctive qualities as its sole objective, a Central Institute of Classical Tamil must be established in Chennai.”

I addressed that letter to Union Minister Arjun Singh.

The very next day, 29 November 2006, the Honourable Minister wrote back, concluding:

> “I am asking the Ministry to include a suitable proposal for an Institute for Classical Tamil in the Ministry’s XI th plan proposals”.

That is, he had instructed his Ministry to include an appropriate proposal for establishing an Institute of Classical Tamil in the Eleventh Plan.

A site was selected for the Central Institute of Classical Tamil. The DMK Government of Tamil Nadu allotted seventeen acres in Perumbakkam village near Sholinganallur in Kanchipuram district, along the Old Mahabalipuram Road—today’s Rajiv Gandhi Salai.

At the Union Cabinet meeting chaired by Prime Minister Dr Manmohan Singh on 30 January 2008, it was decided that the Central Institute of Classical Tamil would be established in Tamil Nadu in a building to be constructed on those seventeen acres at a cost of ₹76.32 crore, and that the Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu would serve as the institute’s Chairperson. I was asked to consent to assuming that responsibility, and I gave my consent. The Union Government approved the permanent buildings, allocated funds and work commenced.

But what is the condition of Classical Tamil under the present AIADMK Government? Last week, a university professor of Tamil told *Kalki* magazine:

> “The Central Institute of Classical Tamil, begun with great publicity, celebration and hope, is now lost in a forest without direction. If the State and Union Governments continue to neglect its functioning, a stage may come when the institute itself has to be closed. The awards presented by the institute have been suspended for two years. Union funding keeps declining, and even the money allotted is returned without being spent. The Governing Council has not been reconstituted. Without it, the Academic Council and Finance Committee have not been formed. The institute has no full-time Director. With neither leadership nor a Governing Council, its work has slowed and stagnated. No action has been taken to construct its building on the land allotted at suburban Perumbakkam. Many research projects have consequently been affected. An institution that once had 120 employees now has only 72. Even they face salary problems and have no enthusiasm in their work. Classical Tamil is suffering, trapped in political mire.”

For the Classical Tamil institute, I donated ₹1 crore from my personal funds and asked that the interest be used to present an annual award worth ₹10 lakh. At the World Classical Tamil Conference held in Coimbatore under the DMK Government, that award was presented to Mr Asko Parpola. Two years have passed since then, but the award has not been presented again. Neither the AIADMK Government nor Chief Minister Jayalalithaa has taken any initiative. Such is the affection the lady has for Classical Tamil and for Tamil itself!

Because the institute enjoyed the Government’s full support during DMK rule, work began on critical editions of forty-one classical works dating from before the sixth century CE. Apart from critical editions of *Iraiyanar Kalaviyal*, *Ainkurunuru* and the Marutham poems, the catalogue of digitised classical manuscripts was completed. The *Tirukkural* was translated into Manipuri and Punjabi. Several works of Sangam literature were translated into English. Iravatham Mahadevan’s *The Indus Civilisation and Sangam Literature* was translated into Tamil. My own book, *Some Copper Plates in the History of Classical Tamil*, was published. Ancient Tamil literature, grammar, inscriptions and coinage were taken to modern information platforms through compact discs and visual presentations. More than ten thousand words absent from dictionaries were documented. Financial assistance was provided to over a hundred researchers. Twenty-three Tamil scholars were selected for Classical Tamil awards, which were presented by the President of India. More than three hundred research meetings and seminars were conducted. A library containing forty thousand books, palm-leaf manuscripts and compact discs was created.

But all this happened only while the DMK was in office. Once the AIADMK came to power in 2011, everything stopped. Since the Chief Minister is Chairperson of the Governing Council, every effort to approach her on the institute’s affairs failed.

Having struggled so hard to secure classical status for Tamil, to establish the research institute in Tamil Nadu’s capital and to ensure that it carried out so many services for the language, I cannot now think of its fate without tears.

In the heart of Chennai, the DMK Government recovered public land that had been occupied by private interests and created Semmozhi Poonga in the name of Classical Tamil. Please visit it once and see its present condition.

The people of Tamil Nadu have watched all this in silence. They removed from office the very person who helped secure classical status for Tamil; bring the Sethusamudram project into implementation; develop essential infrastructure such as the Maduravoyal–Port elevated road; and construct the beautiful Omandurar complex for the Secretariat and Legislative Assembly. But the day when those same people bid farewell to the present rulers, who have conducted closing ceremonies for all these projects, is not far away.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**

23 August 2013

இருந்தபோதுதான்! 2011இல் அ.தி.மு.க. ஆட்சிக்கு வந்தவுடன்
அனைத்தும் நின்றுபோயின. ஆட்சிமன்றக் குழுவுக்கு
முதலமைச்சர்தான் தலைவர் என்பதால், பல விஷயங்களுக்கு
அவரை அணுக முயன்ற முயற்சிகளே தோற்றுப்போயின.
அவ்வளவு அரும்பாடுபட்டு தமிழைச் செம்மொழியாக்கவும்,
செம்மொழி ஆய்வு நிறுவனம் தமிழகத்தின் தலைநகரத்திலே
அமையவும், அதன் சார்பில் பல்வேறு பணிகள் தமிழ் மொழிக்காக
நடைபெறவும் பாடுபட்டவன் என்ற முறையில், இன்று அதன்
கதியை நினைத்துக் கண்ணீர் சிந்தாமல் இருக்க முடியவில்லை.
சென்னை நகரின் இதயம் போன்ற இடத்திலே தனியார்
அனுபவித்து வந்த அரசு நிலத்தைக் கைப்பற்றி, செம்மொழியின்
பெயரால் கழக ஆட்சியில் “செம்மொழிப் பூங்கா” ஒன்றினை
ஏற்படுத்தினோம். அதன் நிலை என்ன என்பதை தயவுசெய்து
அங்கே ஒருமுறை சென்று பாருங்கள்!
தமிழ்நாட்டு மக்கள் இவ்வளவையும் பார்த்துக் கொண்டு
அமைதியாகத்தான் இருக்கிறார்கள். தமிழைச் செம்மொழியாக
அறிவிக்கவும், சேது சமுத்திரம் திட்டத்தை நடைமுறைக்குக்
கொண்டு வரவும், மதுரவாயல், துறைமுகம் பறக்கும் சாலைத்
திட்டம் போன்ற அடிப்படைக் கட்டமைப்பு வசதிகளை
மேம்படுத்தும் பல திட்டங்களைச் செயற்படுத்தவும், தலைமைச்
செயலகத்திற்காகவும் - சட்டப்பேரவை நடைபெறவும் ஓமந்தூரார்
வளாகத்தில் அழகான கட்டிடத்தைக் கட்டக் காரணமாக இருந்த
என்னையே ஆட்சிப் பொறுப்பிலிருந்து அகற்றிய தமிழ்நாட்டு
