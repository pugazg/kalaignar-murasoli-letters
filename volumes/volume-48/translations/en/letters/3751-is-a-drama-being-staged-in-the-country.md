# 3751. Isn’t a Drama Being Staged in the Country!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

The Budget for 2013–2014 was presented in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly on 21 March 2013. The general debate on it concluded, and the Finance Minister gave a lengthy reply. Thereafter, demands for grants for the various departments of the government were moved by the respective ministers; members expressed their views, and the ministers replied and made announcements.

In particular, the Minister for Food and Consumer Protection on 8 April 2013, the Minister for Animal Husbandry on 7 April 2013 and the Minister for Agriculture on 5 April 2013 sought the funds required for their respective departmental demands for grants, and the Assembly approved those amounts. Yet on 6 May 2013 the Chief Minister read three statements under Rule 110 containing a large number of announcements relating to those same three departments; as usual, leaders of the parties then spoke in praise of those announcements.

As far as the Agriculture Department is concerned, in that one day alone the Chief Minister announced new schemes estimated at ₹984 crore 70 lakh. But the total allocation made for this department in the Budget is printed in the source as only **₹5.19 crore and 15 lakh**. In other words, the Chief Minister herself has announced, under Rule 110, an amount said to be about one-fifth—18.97 per cent—of the allocation. Do these newly announced schemes come under the funds already allocated in the Budget? Or will fresh funds be allotted separately for them? Or do they fall merely under the category of “announcements”? Those who praised the Chief Minister in the Assembly for these announcements must be the ones who know.

In her statement under Rule 110, the Chief Minister found fault with the smaller allocations made to various departments during DMK rule and claimed that those amounts had now increased. As revenue rises year after year, allocations too will rise. When C. Subramaniam, as Finance Minister, presented the Budget in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly, our Budget was only around ₹100 crore. Today it has crossed ₹1 lakh crore. So there is little point in the Chief Minister boasting merely that the present allocation is higher.

For example, do you know the total allocation to agriculture during the five years of AIADMK rule from 2001 to 2006? ₹3,393 crore 62 lakh. But during the DMK government's period from 2006 to 2011, the allocation to agriculture was ₹7,414 crore—more than twice as much.

Next, while speaking of food production, Chief Minister Jayalalithaa praised herself by saying that because of the steps she took in 2011 to increase agricultural production, foodgrain output crossed the record level of 100 lakh tonnes. But the DMK government was in office until May 2011. If agricultural production rose in that year, the DMK government too has a share in that credit. What is the situation now? This entire year has been under AIADMK rule, has it not? Have they increased food production?

To go further: under AIADMK rule in 2005–2006, foodgrain production in Tamil Nadu was only 61.46 lakh tonnes. If it rose during DMK rule to 82.63 lakh tonnes in 2006–2007, 91.11 lakh tonnes in 2008–2009 and 95.60 lakh tonnes in 2009–2010, is that not an achievement of the DMK government?

The Chief Minister has announced in the Assembly that a sugarcane-production scheme will be implemented over 50,000 acres at a cost of ₹275 crore 20 lakh in order to increase sugarcane productivity. But what do Tamil Nadu's sugarcane farmers expect from this government? They are asking for a proper price for sugarcane.

Chief Minister Jayalalithaa announced, **“For the 2012–2013 sugarcane season, I have ordered the price to be fixed at ₹2,350 per tonne of sugarcane.”** At that very time, the Executive President of the South Indian Sugarcane Producers Association said that sugarcane growers were continuing cultivation amid severe crises—power shortage, rising production costs and water shortage—and had been demanding and agitating for a procurement price of ₹3,000 per tonne after setting out their production costs with evidence. He said it was painful that the Tamil Nadu government had raised the existing price by only ₹250 and announced ₹2,350 per tonne, and that sugarcane producers were not satisfied.

On page 3 of the AIADMK election manifesto, under the heading **“Sugarcane Production Revolution Scheme,”** it had said that in order to increase ethanol-fuel production, the procurement price of sugarcane would be raised to ₹2,500 per tonne and government sugar mills would be modernised and their administration restructured. Although ₹2,500 had been promised in the manifesto, last year too only ₹2,100 was announced. Sugarcane farmers expected that at least this year the AIADMK government would announce ₹2,500 per tonne and were disappointed. Now, hearing that only ₹2,350 has been announced this year too, they are distressed.

Whenever the DMK came to power, it convened tripartite meetings of sugar-mill owners, farmers, representatives of farmers' associations, and special officers of cooperative and public-sector sugar mills, and announced a remunerative State-advised price for farmers after consultation.

But the AIADMK government that came to power in 2001 did not convene such tripartite meetings during its five years, nor did it decide after seeking the views of farmers, farmers' associations, mill owners and the special officers of cooperative and public-sector sugar mills; sugarcane farmers know that it paid only the Union Government price, and they certainly have not forgotten it.

So instead of saying anything in the Rule 110 statement about raising the sugarcane price, the Chief Minister has again spoken of a sugarcane-production scheme and of spending ₹275 crore on it.

Referring also to horticulture, the Chief Minister announced under Rule 110 that ₹112 crore 49 lakh would be allotted to that department. I have already stated, with several pieces of evidence, that land worth many crores belonging to the Horticulture Department had been “swallowed” by people of the ruling party. There is no indication that the government has so far taken any step to recover that land. In this setting, the Chief Minister has announced a further allocation of ₹112 crore to the department.

In the statement she read on Cooperation, Food and Consumer Protection, the Chief Minister alleged under Rule 110 that primary agricultural cooperative credit societies were in a weakened condition when her government assumed office. When the DMK came to power in 2006 too, nearly 1,200 primary cooperative banks were in a weakened state. Under AIADMK rule, cooperative societies had lost public confidence because they were unable to return deposits amounting to about ₹171 crore. Of the 22 district central cooperative banks, 12 were ineligible to borrow from national banks. Several cooperative marketing societies were inactive, and cooperative consumer societies were running losses of many crores. Through various measures, the DMK government brought this weakened cooperative movement to a stronger position. It also revived and made functional 199 primary agricultural cooperative societies that had been kept out of operation during AIADMK rule because of administrative irregularities.

We are now told that ₹50 crore will be spent on computerisation to monitor the movement of goods from Tamil Nadu Civil Supplies Corporation godowns to fair-price shops. During DMK rule itself, common software was developed for district central cooperative banks and urban cooperative banks, and 1,021 of 1,033 bank branches were computerised. A common accounting system for all primary agricultural cooperative credit societies was also planned, common software was developed, and arrangements were made to use it in all 4,531 such societies in Tamil Nadu. The National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development had, even then, expressed interest in using this software—developed at a cost of ₹38 lakh—to computerise primary agricultural cooperative credit societies throughout India.

Chief Minister Jayalalithaa has also proudly said that two new veterinary colleges were created under AIADMK rule. But it was under DMK rule in 1990 that, for the first time in Asia, an entire new university was established for veterinary education and research. A new building for that university, costing ₹2 crore 3 lakh, was also built under DMK rule.

In her Rule 110 statement, Chief Minister Jayalalithaa said that an unprecedented ₹274 crore had been allotted to improve various infrastructure facilities in the Animal Husbandry Department. But the Budget presented in the Assembly says on pages 23–24 that in 2013–2014 another 100 veterinary sub-centres would be upgraded as veterinary hospitals; buildings would be constructed in 450 veterinary hospitals at a cost of ₹115 crore; and ₹25 crore had been allotted in the Budget to further improve existing veterinary hospitals. The government must explain which of these is true.

Altogether, on 6 May 2013 alone, announcements were piled up for ₹965 crore in agriculture, ₹771 crore in food and ₹15 crore in animal husbandry. Were all these announcements involving crores upon crores discussed in the Cabinet and decided upon? When announcements involving such enormous sums are being made every day by the Chief Minister, why were all these schemes not included in the Budget? There are more than thirty ministers handling the various departments; why did they not make all these announcements when replying to the debates on their own demands for grants?

The Chief Minister herself is reading, under Rule 110, a statement that ought to be read under the Assembly's rules! Who, now, cares about what? **Isn't a drama being staged in the country!**

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**9 May 2013**
