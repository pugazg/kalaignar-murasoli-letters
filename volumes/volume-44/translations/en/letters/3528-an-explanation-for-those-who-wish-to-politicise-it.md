# 3528. An Explanation for Those Who Wish to Politicise It!

> **Translator’s note**
>

**Udanpirappē,**

The spectrum matter is before the court. The Public Accounts Committee in Parliament, headed by a BJP leader, is investigating it. A committee headed by Justice Shivraj Patil and appointed by the Union Government is also investigating it. The Union Government’s CBI and Enforcement Directorate are separately investigating it under the supervision of the Supreme Court. Meanwhile, many people are expressing views about it.

In this situation I have largely refrained from commenting. Some people nevertheless magnify the matter in speech and writing. Since the Central Bureau of Investigation is examining it and the case is before the Supreme Court, I felt it would not be proper for us to force our own assertions into the matter. That is why I had not been speaking about it. But because opposition parties wish to magnify the issue, make politics out of it, confuse people and turn it into propaganda, I want to explain a few points so that DMK Udanpirappugal who wish to answer them will be clear about the matter.

When the audit report itself spoke of a loss of **₹1 lakh 76 thousand crore**, it used the very word **“presumptive”**, meaning that the figure was based on an assumption. Yet opposition parties immediately began propagating that very amount as bribery and corruption. They cite certain comparisons to say that, if things had been done differently, the government might have earned so much revenue — and call that corruption.

Let me give an example. On **24-1-2011**, I attended the wedding at the home of Sivaganga district DMK secretary and Hindu Religious and Charitable Endowments Minister, brother Periyakaruppan. The wedding was held at Kamarajar Arangam, named after Perunthalaivar Kamaraj. While greeting the couple, our former minister brother C. Madhavan sought to illustrate Periyakaruppan’s influence by saying: “Here, lakhs upon lakhs of people have gathered in this hall.”

I smiled and said to Union Home Minister P. Chidambaram, who was seated beside me: “This is how they speak of corruption of ₹1 lakh 76 thousand crore in spectrum too.”

Our friend Madhavan, an educated former minister known to the world, knows that even if this hall were packed, it could not hold more than ten thousand people. Yet while speaking he says “lakhs upon lakhs.” Perhaps this is how, in spectrum too, they increase the estimate in speech to lakhs, thousands and crores of corruption, I said.

Union Minister Kapil Sibal has stated categorically that there was no loss of that magnitude in the 2G spectrum allocation. According to the letter, he said that when the National Democratic Alliance was in power in **1999**, the telecommunications policy brought in under the BJP government, under which licences were issued without auction, had officially been found to have caused the government a revenue loss of **₹1 lakh 43 thousand crore**. The letter says Planning Commission Deputy Chairman Montek Singh Ahluwalia expressed the same view.

The allegation is made by assuming how much revenue might have come if the spectrum had been auctioned. Explanations have also been given by many as to why it was not auctioned. If the aim in telecommunications had been not to give concessions to the people — if the government had thought that someone should win an auction and collect a larger amount from the public — then this supposed loss from spectrum allocation would not have arisen. On the contrary, there might even have been a profit.

But a welfare government does not function with a commercial aim whose sole objective is profit. Government should act with **social benefit (Social Benefit)** in mind; it is not right to look only at financial loss and surplus.

For example, when the Union Government announced the Sixth Pay Commission, we were the first to implement it in Tamil Nadu. The expenditure on that alone was **₹5,500 crore**.

The government spends **₹4,000 crore** as food subsidy for schemes such as rice at one rupee a kilogram.

It spends **₹2,250 crore** on the Kalaignar Housing Scheme.

It spends **₹1,674 crore** as subsidy for the Electricity Board.

It spends **₹1,419 crore** on old-age assistance.

It spends **₹1,185 crore** on the Nutritious Meal Scheme; **₹765 crore** on the Kalaignar Insurance Scheme; **₹360 crore** to provide pregnant women financial assistance of ₹6,000 each; **₹302 crore** on marriage assistance; **₹240 crore** on crop insurance; and **₹276 crore** on a training scheme to provide employment opportunities to graduate youth — all with the intention of providing benefits to people.

Together, these amount to **₹17,921 crore** spent by the government in a year.

If the government did not implement any of these schemes and simply said, “What does it matter how people live?”, it would not incur this additional annual expenditure of roughly ₹17,000 crore. Can anyone call this ₹17,000 crore a loss to the government?

At the Union level, the letter says, **₹55,578 crore** was allocated as food subsidy in 2010–2011, **₹49,980 crore** for fertiliser subsidy and **₹29,483 crore** for educational development. Are all these losses to the government?

The Comptroller and Auditor General, appearing before Parliament’s Public Accounts Committee for examination, confirmed, according to the letter, that the loss figure had been stated without evidence and on the basis of assumptions.

The audit officer was also reported as saying that calculations had been made from different angles to see whether there might have been a loss, and that under one such approach the possible loss was only **₹57,666 crore**.

Former Telecommunications Minister Arun Shourie, the letter says, put the possible loss at only about **₹30,000 crore**. The source prints the following English passage:

> Arun Shourie, Former Union Minister for Communications
> advised the media not to run after the “hypothetical figure of loss (Rs.
> 1.76 lakh crore) to the exchequer” mentioned by the CAG. The realistic
> figure, he said, could be around Rs. 30,000 crores-The Hindu dated
> 19.12.2010

How, then, are different people calculating the loss in different ways? One argument behind the **₹1 lakh 76 thousand crore** figure is to calculate what might have been obtained if 2G spectrum had been auctioned in the manner in which 3G spectrum was later auctioned. Someone says: “Instead of giving this licence at ₹1,650 crore, if it had been given to me, I would have paid ₹6,000 crore.” On that basis, the loss would be **₹58,000 crore**. Thus the amount described as loss varies according to the method of calculation.

That is why, the letter says, at the end of the audit officer’s report the question of how much the loss was is described as **“(It can be debated)”** — that is, as a matter open to debate.

Those who assume that 2G would have yielded the same revenue as 3G are also treating the two spectrum systems as though they were identical. The source argues that they are not: with 2G, when one speaks on a mobile phone one hears only sound, whereas with a 3G phone one can see the face of the person one is speaking to, as though watching video.

The source further states that 2G spectrum has a speed of **4.2 MHz**, whereas 3G has **6.4 MHz**, and argues that one should neither forget nor conceal that 3G is superior to 2G. These technical statements are retained here exactly as claims made in the audited source.

The BJP and Jayalalithaa say that only a Joint Parliamentary Committee can resolve the issue. The letter points to earlier Joint Parliamentary Committee inquiries concerning the **1992 military-equipment matter**, the **1992 coffin purchase**, the **2001 stock-market scam** and the **2003 allegation concerning pesticide in Coca-Cola**, and says that no result came from those inquiries at the time.

At present the Public Accounts Committee is investigating spectrum. All members of the Public Accounts Committee are Members of Parliament; members of a Joint Parliamentary Committee would also be Members of Parliament. The Public Accounts Committee is chaired by a member of the opposition BJP. If a Joint Parliamentary Committee were constituted, its chair would belong to the ruling party. Yet the BJP and Jayalalithaa say that a Joint Parliamentary Committee alone must investigate and that an inquiry by the Public Accounts Committee headed by a BJP member is unnecessary. The letter also argues that audit reports have normally been examined by the Public Accounts Committee and that the demand for a separate Joint Parliamentary Committee deliberately departs from that practice.

When mobile phones were first introduced, a handset cost more than **₹30,000**. Now, the letter says, ordinary people — “Muniyan, Muthan” — have mobile phones, and phones are available for ₹1,000, ₹2,000 or ₹3,000.

A few years earlier, when brother Raja assumed office, the number of mobile-phone users was **30 crore**; the source says it had since risen to **73 crore**.

In the beginning, for a mobile call, the caller had to pay **₹16** and the person receiving the call **₹8**. Now, the source says, a one-minute call costs **40 paise or 30 paise**, and people say it may fall still further. Is it not the public who benefit?

For example, revenue received by mobile companies from each user **(Revenue per User)** had been **₹340 per month** and had fallen to **₹120**. The letter calculates that this saves each mobile user **₹220 a month**, amounting to **₹1 lakh 92 thousand 720 crore** in savings to the public in a year. It immediately adds: **this too is only an assumption.**

The telecommunications department’s target was to extend the benefit of telecommunications to **600 million people by 2011**. The source says the benefit had already reached **720 million people**.

Why was there no auction? The letter says that **TRAI**, the body authorised to advise the telecommunications department, had said that an auction was unnecessary.

In **1994**, the Government of India formulated a new telecommunications policy under which licences were auctioned. Some bidders offered high amounts, but, the source says, they could not operate the licences successfully and telecommunications facilities did not adequately reach the public. Therefore, under the BJP government in **1998**, a Group of Ministers formulated a new telecommunications policy deciding that there should no longer be such auctions and that telecommunications service should reach the public; licences could instead be issued on a **first-come, first-served** basis.

It is therefore wrong, the letter argues, to say that brother Raja alone arbitrarily took and implemented this decision. From after **2001** until Raja took office in **May 2007**, no minister had followed an auction method.

Another allegation is that Raja issued licences on a first-come, first-served basis. The letter says that this policy itself arose from the National Telecommunications Policy formulated under BJP rule. The minister then was **Pramod Mahajan**, followed by **Arun Shourie**. The same procedure they had followed in issuing licences continued to be followed.

**Udanpirappē,** there are so many issues within this spectrum matter. But concealing all of them, people keep their eyes shut and propagate “spectrum — corruption of ₹1 lakh 76 thousand crore,” seeking to mislead the public. Brother Raja is blamed simply because he was minister of the department. When reporters asked me about the issue as early as **8-12-2010**, I said that if Raja were proved guilty, the party would take action without favour or indulgence. That, the letter says, is how transparent the DMK is.

After that, is it fair to keep issuing daily statements, without evidence and on the basis of conjecture, calling it corruption of ₹1 lakh 76 thousand crore and to publish that figure prominently? Is not their objective simply to politicise this matter? I trust, Udanpirappē, that you will understand that and explain these points to the people.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
26 January 2011
