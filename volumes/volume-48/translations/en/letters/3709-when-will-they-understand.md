# 3709. When Will They Understand?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

In a statement issued to the newspapers in Chief Minister Jayalalithaa's name on 21 February 2013, she said that the twentieth Asian Athletics Championships, which were to be held in Tamil Nadu in July this year, would not be conducted by the Tamil Nadu government because Sri Lanka too would participate; that the main reason was the Sri Lankan government's continuing actions against the Tamils living in Sri Lanka; and that another reason was that no reply had been received from the athletics federation to the Tamil Nadu government's letter saying that Sri Lankan athletes should not be allowed to participate.

But when Chief Minister Jayalalithaa read a statement under Rule 110 in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly on 10 May 2012, she had happily announced:

> “I am pleased to inform the House that the Asian Athletics Championships are to be conducted in Chennai in August 2013 with the cooperation of the Government of Tamil Nadu. Athletes—men and women—from 44 countries are expected to participate in these competitions.”

That announcement appeared in all the newspapers in huge letters as an **“Announcement by Revolutionary Leader Amma.”**

The announcement Jayalalithaa made in the Assembly last May has been cancelled by the same Jayalalithaa this February. When the Chief Minister made that announcement in May, did she not know that Sri Lankan athletes would participate? She herself said athletes from 44 countries would take part. Did she not know then that Sri Lanka was one of those countries? When Amma first made the announcement, was the Sri Lankan government doing good to all the Tamils living in Sri Lanka, and did it begin committing atrocities only now? Can the Chief Minister announce that the athletics championships will be conducted when she feels like it, and cancel them when her mind changes?

The Chief Minister also referred in her statement to the killing of Prabhakaran's son Balachandran by the Sri Lankan military and said, **“That is an unforgivable, enormous war crime.”** But what was the resolution moved by this same Jayalalithaa in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly on 16 April 2002, under the AIADMK government, regarding Balachandran's father, Velupillai Prabhakaran?

- This Legislative Assembly urges the Union Government to take immediate action to ensure that the Sri Lankan government immediately arrests Velupillai Prabhakaran, leader of the Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam, and hands him over to the Government of India.
- This Assembly urges the Union Government not to permit any person belonging to the banned Liberation Tigers organisation to enter the Indian nation.
- The Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam and its leader Prabhakaran, who pose a threat to India's security, unity and sovereignty, will never be accepted or permitted to gain a foothold on Indian soil.
- The Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly asks the Union Government to undertake every possible effort, through legal and diplomatic procedures with the Sri Lankan government, to bring Prabhakaran to India and subject him to proper trial in an Indian court for the offences he committed.
- If the Sri Lankan government is unable to capture and deport LTTE leader Prabhakaran, the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly asks the Union Government, with the permission of the Sri Lankan government, to send the Indian Army to assist the Sri Lankan government and make the necessary effort to capture Prabhakaran.

Who moved this resolution? None other than this very Jayalalithaa! Having passed such a resolution in the Assembly then, with such fury and anger against Prabhakaran, if she sheds tears today over the killing of the same LTTE leader Prabhakaran's son, one is naturally reminded of the saying: **“Ghee is dripping from finger millet!”**

The same person who proposed in that resolution that even the Indian Army should be sent to assist the Sri Lankan government in capturing Prabhakaran said in yesterday's statement that she had written many letters to the Indian Prime Minister insisting that “Sri Lankan soldiers must not be trained anywhere in India, including Tamil Nadu,” and that all those letters had been left unattended, like “a conch blown into a deaf man's ear”. Can we not understand from this what kind of double role Chief Minister Jayalalithaa is playing on the Sri Lankan Tamil issue?

Speaking in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly on 16 April 2002, Jayalalithaa said:

> “Whenever I (Jayalalithaa) was Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu, I urged the Government of India to contact the Sri Lankan government, bring Prabhakaran, the leader of the terrorist organisation the Liberation Tigers, here from Sri Lanka and produce him before the court.”

That remains in the Assembly record to this day. The same Jayalalithaa who then presented herself to the outside world almost as the “sworn enemy” of the Liberation Tigers now speaks and issues statements with the air of false innocence expressed by the saying, **“Would this cat ever drink milk?”** And there are people ready to beat the *jalra* in accompaniment!

That is not all. Do you know what this same Jayalalithaa said when the Sri Lankan military was killing Eelam Tamils in large numbers?

In a statement issued on 17 January 2009, she said:

> “The Sri Lankan Army does not intend to kill Sri Lankan Tamils. When a war—a battle—is taking place, it is only natural for innocent people to be killed. What is happening in Sri Lanka now is that the Liberation Tigers are preventing Sri Lankan Tamils from moving to safer places, holding them there and forcibly using them as a shield in front of the military.”

She was the one who portrayed the Liberation Tigers “as terrorists” and placed the blame upon them. Now the same person is trying to present herself as though she supports those Liberation Tigers, opposes the Rajapaksa government of Sri Lanka and supports the Eelam Tamils.

Further, when the DMK was in government and an all-party meeting in Tamil Nadu unanimously passed a resolution condemning the genocide of Tamils in Sri Lanka and demanding that a ceasefire be declared immediately, Jayalalithaa issued a statement saying:

> “Looking at the resolution passed at the all-party meeting (14 October 2008), a suspicion has arisen in the minds of the people of Tamil Nadu that Karunanidhi may be acting in support of the Liberation Tigers. It is astonishing that Karunanidhi, who has been Chief Minister five times, does not understand that the Government of India has no authority to stop the civil war now taking place in Sri Lanka. If the Government of India intervenes in Sri Lanka's internal matter, it will create an opportunity for neighbouring countries later to intervene in our country's internal affairs, and that would harm India's sovereignty. The nations of the world will not accept intervention in another country's internal affairs.”

That was how she acted as an advocate for the Union Government at the time.

Can we forget that in 1997, when the DMK was in government, Jayalalithaa issued a statement accusing us of supporting the Liberation Tigers and said, **“Karunanidhi is trying to disrupt law and order. The Liberation Tigers have once again raised their heads in Tamil Nadu,”** speaking as though the Liberation Tigers were enemies of India? And now, it is for the death of that LTTE leader Prabhakaran's son that Jayalalithaa is said to be shedding tears!

Yet even though conditions in Sri Lanka have become grave enough for Jayalalithaa herself to shed tears, the Government of India still does not appear to have fully understood the unprecedented and continuing suffering of the Eelam Tamils, who have lost their livelihoods and been deprived of their rights to live. In particular, even in the address delivered in Parliament yesterday by the Hon'ble President of India, Pranab Mukherjee, there seems to have been an attempt merely to gloss over the issue. It said:

> “Relations with Sri Lanka continue to improve; India continues to stress measures such as the resettlement and rehabilitation of displaced people there. India is taking firm steps to ensure a peaceful life with dignity and equal status for the displaced Tamil people.”

These are merely phrases; they are not ideas that echo the pain in Tamil hearts.

Sri Lankan President Rajapaksa presents himself to the Government of India as though he cares for the Eelam Tamils and obtains the assistance he needs from India. At the same time, instead of using that assistance for the Eelam Tamils, it is used for the benefit of the Sinhalese; towns with Tamil names are changed to Sinhala names; temples belonging to Tamils are demolished and levelled; and Sinhala occupation of Tamil land, homes and traditional property—and a frenzied process of Sinhalisation—is encouraged. Such racist actions continue unabated.

There is a story about Meypporul Nayanar in the *Periya Puranam*. Describing the arrival of a minor king named Muthanathan, who came to murder Meypporul Nayanar, Sekkizhar writes aptly:

> “His whole body smeared with sacred ash, his matted locks bound up,
> a bundle of books in his hand concealing a weapon,
> like a lamp wrapped in blackness, darkness held within his mind,
> Muthanathan entered wearing the false guise of an ascetic.”

That is, Muthanathan, a minor king who had plotted to murder King Meypporul Nayanar, smeared sacred ash over his whole body, arranged his hair like an ascetic, donned an ascetic's guise, and entered the palace carrying books in his hand with a knife hidden among them. Sekkizhar says that while he entered wearing an ascetic's guise outwardly, he carried the evil intention to murder in his mind—like a burning lamp with light at the tip of its wick and black soot below it.

Like Muthanathan, who realised he could not defeat his opponent openly in battle and therefore tried to deceive and kill him in the guise of an ascetic, Sinhala President Rajapaksa acts as a very close friend of the Government of India and secures everything he needs, while at the same time relentlessly hunting the Eelam Tamils without mercy. At a time when countries around the world are recognising his tyrannical actions against humanity and human rights, severely condemning him as **“Rajapaksa, an international war criminal”** and moving to punish him, I cannot understand why the Government of India alone has still failed to understand his true forms and has said in the President's Address that **“relations with Sri Lanka are improving.”** When the countries of the world have recognised the authoritarian conspirator, when will the Union Government understand Sri Lanka's “lamp wrapped in blackness”—poison in the heart and the mask of friendship on the face?

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**24 February 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. **“Ghee is dripping from finger millet”** is retained close to the Tamil proverbial image; in context it mocks a claim or display of feeling that the writer considers implausible.
2. `jalra` is retained from the Tamil colloquial political idiom: literally the small cymbal used for accompaniment, here meaning people who provide sycophantic applause.
3. The *Periya Puranam* verse is translated for sense and image; Kalaignar immediately explains the comparison in prose, which remains the controlling context.
4. Allegations concerning Sri Lanka, the LTTE and political actors remain attributed exactly as Kalaignar presents them in the source.

தவ வேடமணிந்து கையில் புத்தகங்களும், புத்தகங்களுக்கு
இடையே கத்தியையும் வைத்துக்கொண்டு, அரண்மனைக்குள்
நுழைகிறான்; அப்படி நுழையும்போது மனத்தில் கொலை செய்ய
வேண்டுமென்ற தீய எண்ணத்துடன் நுழைந்தான் என்பதைச்
சேக்கிழார், விளக்கு எரியும்போது திரியின் நுனியில் ஒளியும்,
திரியின் அடியில் கரியும் இருப்பதைப் போல வெளியிலே தவ
வேடமும், உள்ளே கொலை செய்யும் கெட்ட நோக்கமும் கொண்டு
நுழைந்தான் என்கிறார்.

போரில் எதிர்த்து நின்று வெல்ல முடியாது என்ற நிலையில்,
தவ முனிவர் வேடமணிந்து ஏமாற்றிக் கொல்ல முற்பட்ட
முத்தநாதனைப் போல, சிங்கள அதிபர் ராஜபக்ஷே அவர்கள்
இந்திய அரசுக்கு மிகவும் நெருங்கிய நண்பராக நடித்து,
தனக்குத் தேவையானதையெல்லாம் சாதித்துக்கொண்டு; அதே
நேரத்தில் ஈழத் தமிழர்களை ஈவிரக்கமின்றி வேட்டையாடிக்
கொண்டிருக்கிறார். மனித நேயத்திற்கு எதிரான - மனித உரிமைகளுக்குப் புறம்பான அவரது கொடுங்கோன்மைச் செயல்பாடுகளை
உலக நாடுகள் எல்லாம் புரிந்துகொண்டு “ராஜபக்ஷே ஒரு
சர்வதேசப் போர்க் குற்றவாளி” என்று கடுமையாகக் கண்டிக்கவும்
தண்டிக்கவும் முற்படுகின்ற வேளையில், இந்திய அரசு மட்டும்
அவரைப் பற்றிய உண்மை விகாரங்களை இன்னமும் புரிந்து
கொள்ளாமல், “இலங்கையுடனான உறவு மேம்பட்டு வருகிறது”
என்று குடியரசுத் தலைவரின் உரையிலே குறிப்பிட்டிருப்பது
ஏன் என்று புரியவில்லை. உலக நாடுகள் எல்லாம் சர்வாதிகாரச்
சதிகாரரை உணர்ந்துள்ள சூழலில், நெஞ்சில் நஞ்சும் முகத்தில்
நட்பு வேடமும் பூண்டுள்ள, இலங்கையின் “மைபொதி விளக்கை”
மத்திய அரசு எப்போதுதான் புரிந்துகொள்ளுமோ?
