# 3460. The National Commission for Scheduled Castes and the DMK Government!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This letter responds point by point to remarks attributed to the Vice-Chairman of the National Commission for Scheduled Castes after a review of welfare measures in Tamil Nadu. It documents correspondence on the three-per-cent Arunthathiyar internal reservation, housing figures, reservation policy and welfare allocations. The source’s chronology is preserved as printed, including its description of the review as having occurred “yesterday” while giving the date `3-2-2010` in a letter dated 20 February 2010.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3460](../../../chapters/3460-thesiya-aathi-thiravidar-aanaiyamum-dmk-arasum.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 260](../../../pages/page-260.md)–[PDF 265](../../../pages/page-265.md)  
**Date:** 20 February 2010

**Udanpirappē,**

In accordance with Ayyan Tiruvalluvar’s declaration, “All lives are equal by birth,” the DMK government under my leadership has followed, not merely in the country but also at home, the conviction that caste and religious differences must be removed in Tamil Nadu and an egalitarian society created. To advance the people of the oppressed communities who have lain at the bottom of society for years and years, it has been implementing various constructive schemes with distinction.

In this situation, on behalf of the National Commission for Scheduled Castes, a review was conducted yesterday (3-2-2010) at the Tamil Nadu Secretariat in Chennai regarding welfare schemes and assistance being provided for Scheduled Castes in Tamil Nadu. Professor N. M. Kamble, Vice-Chairman of the National Commission for Scheduled Castes, and member Mahendra Bodh, who participated in that meeting, conveyed certain matters to reporters after it ended. In particular, they said that the Tamil Nadu Government had not informed them about the internal reservation made for Arunthathiyars. I wish to provide an explanation regarding that.

On the basis that special concessions were necessary for the advancement of the Arunthathiyar community, which is among the most backward within the Scheduled Castes, a one-member committee consisting of Justice M. S. Janarthanam was constituted to examine the granting of an internal reservation for Arunthathiyars within the reservation already provided to Scheduled Castes. Before implementing the committee report by granting a three-per-cent internal reservation to Arunthathiyars, the Chief Secretary, on behalf of the Tamil Nadu Government, wrote on 25-11-2008 to Mr Buta Singh, Chairman of the National Commission for Scheduled Castes.

In the interview he gave yesterday, the Commission’s Vice-Chairman said that the Tamil Nadu Government had never informed them. Not only that: the Commission itself wrote a reply to us on 19-2-2009 in response to the letter our Chief Secretary had written to the National Commission for Scheduled Castes. That letter too is with our government. Therefore, for the Vice-Chairman of that Commission, holding an important position, to have listened to somebody’s words and told reporters yesterday that the Tamil Nadu Government had not informed them is completely incorrect information.

There is also only a rule that the proposal for internal reservation for Arunthathiyars must be brought to the attention of the National Commission for Scheduled Castes; there is no compulsion whatsoever that this internal reservation may be made only after obtaining its approval. That is why, after the Tamil Nadu Government informed the National Commission for Scheduled Castes about this internal reservation, and when approval had not been received from the Commission, we consulted Justice Janarthanam, who had chaired the one-member committee, and — out of concern that there should be no further delay in providing reservation for Arunthathiyars — granted the internal reservation.

Even while I was admitted to hospital for surgery, I had the Deputy Chief Minister introduce this Bill in the Assembly, and I myself sent a letter from the hospital requesting the members of the House to pass the Bill. The Bill was passed unanimously.

For the Vice-Chairman of the National Commission for Scheduled Castes himself to say in an interview that this reservation is contrary to the Constitution and that even a court case could be filed against it not only amounts to obstructing the benefit provided to the Arunthathiyar community; it could also cause harm to that community itself. It is therefore a matter of great regret.

Further, even while participating in the meeting, Mr Kamble, departing from the usual practice, asked where certain other statistical details were, apart from the data taken up for discussion at the meeting. He then told reporters that all those details had not been supplied. All the information responding to the questionnaire sent in advance specifying what matters would be taken up for review had in fact been prepared as a book by the Tamil Nadu Government’s Adi Dravidar Welfare Department and supplied at that meeting.

Further, in his interview he said: “We were told that in Tamil Nadu human beings do not enter sewers, but we have photographs relating to this. That is what shocks us.” Everyone in Tamil Nadu knows that it is in Tamil Nadu that a policy was announced that human beings must not enter sewers in municipal corporations, municipalities and town panchayats; the necessary funds were allocated to implement it; and the required machines were purchased and supplied. Whether it is proper for the Vice-Chairman of a Commission to criticise a State government in this manner merely by holding up a photograph taken at some earlier time is something the Scheduled Caste people of Tamil Nadu themselves must consider.

He further said in his interview: “The conviction rate in cases filed in courts is low in Tamil Nadu. This may be because government advocates lack ability. Otherwise, it may be because these cases are not being given importance in the courts.” This deliberately finds fault with government advocates and also amounts to finding fault with the courts. Even though the Chief Secretary, Finance Secretary, Home Secretary and other officials had given proper explanations to these questions during the meeting itself, he later made these unnecessary criticisms to reporters.

Mr Kamble has also complained that he asked about housing for Scheduled Castes and was told that the scheme was under the control of the Rural Development Department. The housing scheme for Scheduled Castes is one of the Union Government’s schemes, and the State government too has a share in it. In the statistical material placed by the Tamil Nadu Government before this review meeting concerning that scheme, page 39 states that 36,556 houses (60 per cent) were constructed in 2006–2007; 47,256 houses (56.32 per cent) in 2007–2008; and 45,728 houses (53 per cent) in 2008–2009. Even after that, he said in the interview that nothing had been properly stated.

Finally, Mr Kamble’s statement criticising all States — that no State meets their expectations — creates confusion and amounts to accusing the Union Government and the other State governments without any basis. I also wish to give briefly the details showing the extent to which the DMK government, during the periods it has been in office, has been committed to Scheduled Caste welfare and provided benefits.

Until 1969, the Backward Classes Welfare Department was combined with the Adi Dravidar Welfare Department. On 2.5.1969, it was the DMK government that created a separate new Backward Classes Welfare Department, thereby making it possible for the Adi Dravidar and Tribal Welfare Department to function wholly for the welfare of Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes. Likewise, it was the DMK government that created a separate department for Tribal people in the year 2000.

It was the DMK government that, in 1971, raised from 16 per cent to 18 per cent the reservation that existed in education and employment for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes. Later, in 1990, it was also the DMK government that took one percentage point from the general category and provided one per cent reservation for Scheduled Tribes, while making it possible for Scheduled Castes alone to receive the full 18 per cent reservation.

To remove caste and religious conflicts and create conditions in which people of every community could live together harmoniously with a sense of equality, the innovative and revolutionary “Periyar Memorial Samathuvapuram” scheme was created by the DMK government in 1997 on the occasion of the golden jubilee of Indian Independence. In every Samathuvapuram settlement created under this scheme, 100 houses are built: 40 are allotted to Scheduled Castes, 25 to Most Backward Classes, 25 to Backward Classes, and 10 to other sections. So far, 172 Samathuvapurams have been opened in Tamil Nadu, and people of all sections live there with a sense of harmony. It is noteworthy that no other State in India has such a revolutionary scheme.

Under the Special Component Plan, the Tamil Nadu Government has for the past three years been monitoring to ensure that more than 19 per cent of the State Annual Plan funds go to oppressed people.

As a constructive effort to lay a foundation for improving the standard of living of Scheduled Caste people who had lived in mud huts for generations, it was in Tamil Nadu, for the first time in India, that the Adi Dravidar Free Housing Corporation was started on 15.2.1974 to build and provide them free concrete houses. This scheme was later adopted by the Union Government as the “Indira Housing Scheme” and implemented throughout the country.

To eradicate caste differences, the “Anjugam Ammaiyar Memorial Inter-Caste Marriage Assistance Scheme” continues to be implemented under the DMK government. If one of the spouses is a Scheduled Caste person, financial assistance of ₹20,000 is provided as an incentive.

It is the DMK government that stands in the forefront in India in honouring Ambedkar. In Chennai, the first law university in India in the name of Annal Ambedkar was established.

For the advancement of Scheduled Caste and Scheduled Tribe people, a separate allocation is made under the Scheduled Caste Sub-Plan from those State-government development programmes that can be apportioned, and schemes are being implemented. This allocation, which was ₹567 crore in 2005–2006, was increased fourfold to ₹2,615 crore in the 2009–2010 financial year.

Concealing all this, the Vice-Chairman of a Commission has spoken as though the Tamil Nadu Government were a government without concern for Scheduled Caste welfare. I record my regret over that, and I am also bringing the details of this matter to the attention of the Union Government, particularly to the attention of the Prime Minister.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**20-02-2010**

முன்னேற்றத்திற்காக மாநில அரசின் திட்டப் பணிகளில்
பிரித்தளிக்கக் கூடியவற்றில் - ஆதி திராவிடர் துணைக் கூறுத்
திட்டத்திற்கென தனி ஒதுக்கீடு செய்யப்பட்டு, திட்டங்கள்
செயல்படுத்தப்பட்டு வருகின்றன. 2005-2006 ஆம் ஆண்டில்
ரூபாய் 567 கோடியாக இருந்த இந்த ஒதுக்கீடு - 2009-2010 ஆம்
ஆண்டு நிதியாண்டில் நான்கு மடங்காக அதாவது 2,615 கோடியாக
உயர்த்தப்பட்டது என்பதையெல்லாம் மறைத்து விட்டு - தமிழக
அரசு ஆதிதிராவிடர் நலனில் அக்கறையில்லாத அரசு என்பதைப்
போல ஒரு ஆணையத்தின் துணைத் தலைவர் சொல்லியிருப்பதற்
காக எனது வருத்தத்தைத் தெரிவித்துக் கொள்வதோடு - இது
பற்றிய விவரத்தை மத்திய அரசின் கவனத்திற்கு, குறிப்பாக
பிரதமரின் கவனத்திற்குக் கொண்டு செல்வதாகவும் இருக்கிறேன்.
