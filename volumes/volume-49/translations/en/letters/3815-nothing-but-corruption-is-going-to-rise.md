# 3815. Nothing but Corruption Is Going to Rise!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 9 October 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

In the “Questions and Answers” section dated 29 September 2013, I wrote in detail about the procurement price for paddy announced by the AIADMK Government and the extent to which that price had been raised during the DMK administration. Even the daily newspaper of the Communist Party of India, which supports the AIADMK Government, carried an editorial on 7 October 2013 calling upon the Union and State Governments to “Raise the Paddy Procurement Price.”

Under the headline “The Procurement Price That Is Killing the Farmer,” the Tamil daily *The Hindu* wrote:

> There is an old saying that if the man who ploughs calculates his accounts, not even a measure of grain will remain. The pain today is that this saying is turning into a reality that strikes the heart. When production costs and procurement prices are added and subtracted, farmers shed enough tears to fill even the water bodies that have dried up. In the past year alone, more than ten farmers have taken their own lives after suffering losses in agriculture. Ponnambalam of the CREATE organisation gives the shocking information that many people have abandoned farming because paddy no longer receives a remunerative price. Increasing the Government procurement price is now the only way to revive Indian agriculture. The Government machinery pays a pension of one thousand rupees a month even to elderly people who have neither work nor anyone to care for them; yet the income it leaves a farmer after six months of labour in the fields is only five thousand rupees per acre. This is why agricultural lands are being converted into housing plots.

In a statement issued on behalf of the Communist Party of India, its Tiruvarur district secretary, Thiru V. Veerasenan, said:

> Because water has not reached the tail-end areas dependent on irrigation, and because even the minimum necessary rainfall has not been received, single-season samba paddy and SRI paddy crops have withered and suffered damage. In the Vadavaru extension canal areas of Mannargudi and Needamangalam unions, paddy cultivation has been severely affected because no water has arrived at all.

In my own Tiruvarur constituency, and in the neighbouring constituencies of Nannilam, Thiruthuraipoondi and Mannargudi, no river water has reached the irrigated tail-end areas. I strongly urge the Tamil Nadu Government to take immediate steps to remove this suffering of the farmers.

Farmers in Tiruvarur district, desperate to save their withering samba crops somehow, are said to be collecting the small quantity of water that crawls along the bottom of the canals, carrying it in pots with the help of their families, and pouring it into the fields. This is the miserable condition to which they have been reduced. In the delta districts of Tiruvarur, Thanjavur and Nagapattinam, twelve lakh acres that once yielded three crops are now dry, with no possibility of producing even a single crop.

During the AIADMK Government from 2001 to 2006, the total allocation for the Agriculture Department was 3,396 crore rupees. During the DMK administration from 2006 to 2011, the allocation for the same department was 7,414 crore rupees. It was the DMK Government that waived 7,000 crore rupees in cooperative agricultural loans and thereby protected the families of 22,40,739 farmers.

The interest rate on crop loans to farmers stood at nine per cent in 2005–2006 under the AIADMK Government. Under the DMK Government:

- in 2006–2007, it was reduced to seven per cent, and 1,250 crore and 62 lakh rupees was lent to 6,31,283 farmers;
- in 2007–2008, it was reduced to five per cent, and 1,393 crore and 97 lakh rupees was lent to 6,48,397 farmers;
- in 2008–2009, it was reduced to four per cent, and 1,571 crore rupees was lent to 6,91,192 farmers;
- in 2009–2010, interest on crop loans was abolished altogether, and 2,169 crore and 48 lakh rupees was lent to 8,98,450 farmers; and
- in 2010–2011, 2,453 crore rupees was lent to 8,01,960 farmers.

Thus, over five years of DMK rule, crop loans totalling 8,838 crore and 29 lakh rupees were provided to 36,71,372 farmers. Whoever else may forget this, the farmers cannot.

For the past two years cultivation has failed, and farmers already living in anxiety are now suffering severely under the burden of agricultural debt. I urge that this Government cancel their loans completely and also provide new credit to farmers who, because they could not repay earlier loans, are now unable to obtain fresh finance—so that they may continue cultivation.

Likewise, under the social-security programme for farmers and agricultural labourers, 1,74,65,498 farmers were enrolled in the Agricultural Labourers Welfare Board. Under the DMK Government, assistance totalling 684 crore, 2 lakh, 56 thousand and 686 rupees was paid to the families of 8,72,981 members for old-age pension, education, marriage, accidents and other needs.

It was under the DMK Government that head tax, additional head tax and water cess on farmers were abolished; that free electricity was provided to farmers; that a Coconut Farmers Welfare Board was established; that Uzhavar Sandhais were created; and that farmers were organised into self-help groups and provided revolving funds.

Small and marginal farmers must obtain certificates from the appropriate official in order to receive Government concessions. For example, they must hold certificates identifying them as small or marginal farmers to receive subsidised seed and pesticides. Yet farmers say that they are unable to obtain these certificates from Government officials.

On the procurement price for paddy, the Thanjavur regional farmers’ conference held in Tiruvarur on 2 October 2013—under the leadership of DMK Farmers’ Wing Secretary Dr K. P. Ramalingam, MP, and in the presence of Tiruvarur district secretary Poondi K. Kalaivanan—adopted a resolution demanding that the price be fixed at two thousand rupees per quintal for fine varieties and 1,800 rupees per quintal for ordinary varieties.

At the executive committee meeting of the Communist Party of India (Marxist), held in Chennai two days ago, a resolution was adopted demanding that the paddy procurement price be raised to two thousand rupees. It also stated that merely continuing last year’s incentive payment was inadequate in a situation where the prices of all commodities had risen, and demanded an incentive of two hundred rupees per quintal for coarse varieties and 250 rupees for fine varieties.

During the DMK administration, before the sugarcane crushing season began, a tripartite meeting of farmers’ representatives, sugar-mill owners and State Government officials was held, and only after those discussions was the sugarcane price fixed. That was the established practice. For the past two years, however, the AIADMK Government—apparently to assist private sugar mills—has not come forward to conduct tripartite negotiations before fixing the cane price.

The Union Government has now removed the sugar levy system, bringing substantial profits to sugar mills. Yet the Tamil Nadu Government has made no effort to fix a higher price for sugarcane. At least now, it should properly conduct the tripartite meeting. Taking production costs into account, and without including transport charges within the stated price, the Government should pay Tamil Nadu’s sugarcane farmers 3,500 rupees per tonne. All outstanding payments due to cane farmers must also be settled by 15 November.

I wish to remind the Government that the DMK Farmers’ Wing has resolved to conduct demonstrations in front of sugar mills across Tamil Nadu on 3 November 2013 if these demands are not accepted.

Because AIADMK members now hold the administrative posts in Primary Agricultural Cooperative Banks, agricultural loans, seed paddy, fertiliser and other inputs intended for farmers are being supplied only to AIADMK members and their supporters. An anti-democratic practice has been created—as though a Government run by the AIADMK exists only for AIADMK members and not for the general public.

The Coconut Welfare Board established under the DMK Government has been rendered inactive under the present administration. Whether the AIADMK Government will revive it in the interests of coconut farmers remains to be seen.

The AIADMK Government has been closing, out of hostility, many important schemes begun by the DMK administration. Though an agricultural college was established at Keevalur in Nagapattinam district under the DMK Government, the present Government has prevented it from functioning. Let us wait and see whether, setting aside its hostility and considering the common interests of farmers, it will permit that college to function properly and continuously.

Irrigation-water management has been gravely damaged. On 4 October 2013, farmers staged a road blockade at Thiruthuraipoondi demanding water for samba cultivation. Though the Mettur Dam was opened on 2 August, water has still not reached the tail-end areas of the Cauvery delta. Farmers near Thiruthuraipoondi have demanded that, to save samba crops withering for lack of water, water from the Adapparu be released continuously into the North Rajan Canal without rotational restrictions.

Avvai Piratti sang:

> When the bund rises, the water rises;  
> when the water rises, the paddy rises;  
> when the paddy rises, the people rise;  
> when the people rise, the ruler rises.

But under the AIADMK Government, the bund has not risen; the water has not risen; the paddy has not risen; and the people have not risen. Nothing is going to rise except the corruption that keeps piling higher!

**With affection,**  
**M. K.**
9 October 2013

“வரப்புயர நீர் உயரும்;
நீர் உயர நெல் உயரும்;
நெல் உயர குடி உயரும்;
குடி உயர கோன் உயரும்?”
என்று பாடினார் அவ்வைப் பிராட்டி! ஆனால் அ.தி.மு.க.
ஆட்சியில் வரப்பும் உயரவில்லை; நீரும் உயரவில்லை; நெல்லும்
உயரவில்லை; குடிமக்களும் உயரவில்லை; குவியும் ஊழலைத்
தவிர வேறு எதுவும் உயரப் போவதில்லை!
அன்புள்ள,
மு.க.
9-10-2013
