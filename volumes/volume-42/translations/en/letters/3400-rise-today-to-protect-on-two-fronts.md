# 3400. Rise Today — to Protect on Two Fronts!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This letter combines an explicitly partisan 2009 Parliamentary-election appeal with concern for Sri Lankan Tamils. It presents “two fronts” requiring attention: electoral campaigning in Tamil Nadu and efforts, through the Union Government, regarding Sri Lanka. Campaign appeals are preserved only as historical source text. The complete audited Tamil is reproduced below and remains authoritative.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3400](../../../chapters/3400-iru-munai-paathukaappukkaaga-indre-ezhuga.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 252](../../../pages/page-252.md)–[PDF 257](../../../pages/page-257.md)  
**Date:** 26 April 2009

**Udanpirappē,**

The period for filing nominations for the election to India’s Fifteenth Parliament has ended.

In this election, within the Democratic Progressive Alliance led by the DMK:

- the DMK is contesting 21 seats;
- Congress 16;
- Viduthalai Chiruthaigal 2;
- the Indian Union Muslim League 1.

I read every day in the newspapers — and hear from some by telephone — that all candidates are actively engaged in canvassing for votes.

In some constituencies the work is proceeding rapidly.

In a few places, however, the work of canvassing is not moving at the level I expect.

I do not write this as an accusation.

I write with the desire and urgency that the work should become faster and more vigorous.

You will not have forgotten that around the 1967 election, senior leader Bhaktavatsalam, after observing the election work of DMK comrades, praised them by saying:

> “DMK workers are people who can drink a single tea and work for hours.”

From people who have earned such praise, I expect still more of that work.

This election is not an election to the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly.

It is the election that will decide who will sit in the Union Government.

We take pride in the fact that we remain with the same allies with whom we formed an alliance and won in 2004, and with whom we have worked for the past five years.

At no point did we obstruct the Union Government during those five years.

Nor did we fail to raise our rightful voice so that good things could happen.

We acted there, says the source, as an example of how an alliance should function.

You know, and Tamil Nadu knows, that Congress, the principal partner in the Union Government, fulfilled the requests we placed before it.

The source says that even for the single achievement of making our language a Classical Language, we could support that party again and again.

Likewise, for how many years had we demanded the Sethusamudram project?

Was it not the United Progressive Alliance government that came forward to give us that project, valued at ₹2,427 crore?

Was it not the government that, immediately after taking office and after our request, removed the CENVAT tax affecting weavers?

Was it not the government that waived farmers’ loans?

Was it not the government that shifted the Classical Tamil centre from Mysore and established it in Chennai?

Was it not the government that accepted the request to establish a seawater-desalination project near Chennai and came forward with about ₹1,000 crore?

Was it not the same UPA government that enabled thirteen people from Tamil Nadu to become Union Ministers?

I could keep writing, says the author.

Only the page would run out.

You are reading this letter on 26.4.2009.

Only seventeen days remain.

For what?

For you to enter the polling booth and cast your vote —

and for all the voters you have gathered through your efforts in support of our alliance to cast their sincere votes for our alliance candidates.

The DMK, the source says, has the reputation of working even harder for allied-party candidates than for its own candidates.

Therefore, do not wait expecting members of allied parties to come and invite us personally or give us special respect.

What matters is not which party’s candidate is standing.

What matters is not the individual identity of the person contesting on behalf of the Democratic Progressive Alliance.

The goal is the victory of the alliance.

I know you will work with that feeling.

Yet you should not later ask, “Did elder brother write and remind us?”

I must not fail in my duty.

That is why I remind you.

Perhaps you wished the DMK to contest your constituency, but under the seat-sharing arrangement it may have gone to an allied party.

Perhaps you yourself hoped to contest and that opportunity went to someone else.

The time for thinking about all that has ended.

We have entered the field.

From now on, victory alone must be the goal.

I, your elder brother, lovingly ask my dear younger brother to work toward it.

Election campaigning has begun.

General Secretary Professor has already completed two tours on behalf of the Kazhagam.

He told me with satisfaction that wherever he went, you were working well.

Treasurer brother M. K. Stalin has completed his first round of touring the southern districts and returned.

Every day he tells me the condition of each constituency.

Perhaps you can obtain a certificate from me, but obtaining one from him is difficult!

Even so, he has told me that many among you are working well in several places, while a few are not working with enough intensity.

Those Udanpirappugal who have been identified in that way should not wait for a summons from me.

From today itself, engage in election-campaign work.

Important Kazhagam speakers and our friends from the arts are also participating in the campaign.

I know that many comrades feel disappointed that, during this election, I cannot come in person and ask for votes after having undergone a dangerous operation.

Every day I am engaged in a struggle with the doctors and my family.

You know well what my election tours were like in earlier times.

If I entered the van at two or three in the afternoon on the first day, I would travel hundreds of miles and campaign in that same van until dawn the next morning and the sun rose.

I remember tours in Krishnagiri and Thoothukudi that ended only around seven in the morning after sunrise.

I also remember starting a campaign in the morning from Meenambakkam airport with Mother Indira Gandhi and completing it at Kanyakumari around daybreak the following day.

Even if I cannot campaign like that now, I am thinking of coming at least to a few important towns and campaigning in support of the candidates in those areas.

Because I underwent spinal surgery, every day I suffer little by little.

Even so, I endure it with the thought:

there is only one life;

if it must go, let it go for this good cause.

With that thought I undertake these journeys.

Therefore, do not give excessive importance to the fact that I am unable to come personally to every constituency.

Continue the election work.

The headquarters has announced the towns I will visit.

At the same time that we are engaged this intensely in election work, we see and hear through newspapers and television the daily deaths of Eelam Tamils in Sri Lanka, who share an umbilical-cord relationship with us.

We are burning in the fire of anguish.

We continue every effort we can.

From many directions we are pressing our Union Government:

somehow protect the Tamil people;

undertake the necessary efforts.

Members of the film industry and people from other parties who genuinely care for Sri Lankan Tamils have also raised their voices with concern.

At the same time, while we successfully conducted a work stoppage in Tamil Nadu insisting on a permanent ceasefire in Sri Lanka, the author says some people engaged in election campaigning, worked against the stoppage and obstructed it.

Tamil people, he argues, are capable of identifying who genuinely raises a voice for Sri Lankan Tamils and who merely pretends.

The Union Government and Mrs. Sonia Gandhi, who understand both our position and that of Sri Lankan Tamils, are also working with the desire to help them somehow, says the source.

According to the author, this concern was shown not merely through statements and appeals.

Over the past two days Prime Minister Manmohan Singh held emergency consultations.

National Security Adviser M. K. Narayanan and Foreign Secretary Shivshankar Menon went to Sri Lanka, met Rajapaksa and conveyed the Government of India’s position.

The Prime Minister also spoke with me by telephone.

Home Minister and dear friend P. Chidambaram came to Chennai, met me and explained the details.

So we face two important tasks at the same time:

we must protect Sri Lankan Tamils;

and we must face our opponents in the election field and win.

Therefore, without even a moment’s delay, rise at once, Udanpirappē!

**Protection for our people in Sri Lanka!  
Protection here for our Tamil Nadu and our motherland!**

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**26-4-2009**

எனவே ஒரே நேரத்தில் இரண்டு முக்கியமான பணிகளைச் சந்திக்க வேண்டியவர்களாக இருக்கிறோம். இலங்கைத் தமிழர்களைக் காப்பாற்றியாக வேண்டும் - மற்றொன்று தேர்தல் களத்திலே எதிரிகளைச் சந்தித்து வெற்றி பெற்றாக வேண்டும். எனவே உதவாதினி ஒரு தாமதம் என்று உடன் எழுக; உடன்பிறப்பே! இலங்கையில் இனத்துக்குப் பாதுகாப்பு! இங்கே நமது தமிழகத்துக்கும் தாயகத்துக்கும் பாதுகாப்பு!
