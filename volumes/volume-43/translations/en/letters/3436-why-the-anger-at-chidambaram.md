# 3436. Why the Anger at Chidambaram?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation follows the audited canonical Tamil in source order. It preserves the letter’s political accusations, Liberhan Commission framing, quoted 1992 speech, the author’s earlier poem, the Paul Commission comparison, dates and rhetorical questions without externally reconciling the claims.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3436](../../../chapters/3436-chidambaram-meethu-seetram-en.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 87](../../../pages/page-087.md)–[PDF 92](../../../pages/page-092.md)  
**Date:** 28 November 2009

**Udanpirappē,**

In the countryside there is a saying: “It is one’s own thought that ruins one’s livelihood, my grandson.” In much the same way, somehow our Leader of the Opposition Jayalalithaa must issue a statement as soon as day breaks. Otherwise, it is as though her head would burst. If there is even a little damage to some road in any village in Tamil Nadu, that is enough: immediately there is a statement announcing a demonstration there.

In that fashion, she has now left Tamil Nadu aside and, perhaps because of the dream she had before the Parliamentary election of becoming the next Prime Minister, issued a statement about the leak of the Liberhan Commission report that is being discussed in Parliament. Whatever statement she writes, by the time she finishes it she will invariably write that therefore this minister must resign, that minister must resign, this person must be removed, that person must be removed, or even that the government itself must be dismissed. Now, she says Union Home Minister, the Hon’ble P. Chidambaram, must accept full responsibility for the leak of the Liberhan Commission report and either leave office or be removed. Jayalalithaa has issued such a statement.

It is like peeling a fruit and, instead of eating the segment, lamenting, “Oh! How did this segment appear? Was it not because the peel was removed? We must not let this go!” Or it is like a thief, seeing the stolen property recovered, trying to divert attention by asking, “Who first found this?” Leaving aside everything stated in the Liberhan Commission report, she has plunged deeply into the controversy over how it was leaked and who is responsible.

If one begins examining what is contained in the Liberhan Commission report, the lady herself would also be caught in it. For, at the end of his report, Liberhan has said that a special law should be enacted to punish those who misuse caste and religion with the aim of capturing power, and that regional tribunals should be established in all parts of the country to hear cases relating to this.

At the time of the demolition of the Babri Masjid, Jayalalithaa, who was then Chief Minister, supported kar seva. In 1992, a conference of the Vishwa Hindu Parishad was held at the Jhansi Rani Maidan in Delhi with about 5,000 sadhus participating. A resolution concerning the construction of a Ram temple at Ayodhya was adopted. Pointing out that the then Prime Minister P.V. Narasimha Rao was not supporting the effort to remove the Babri Masjid and build the Ram temple, they decided that kar seva for that purpose would begin on 6 December.

Leaders of various political parties issued statements saying that the Union Government must take firm steps to stop the announced kar seva. Speaking on that matter, I said that just as Prime Minister V.P. Singh had acted under the National Front government without concern for his office, Prime Minister Narasimha Rao too should act; otherwise the Union Congress government would have to bear responsibility for the offence of opening the door to an action that would create bloodshed in the country.

The National Integration Council met and passed a resolution authorising the Prime Minister to take whatever appropriate action was necessary. The resolution was moved by Marxist Communist Party leader Surjeet. At that meeting, Jayalalithaa, who was then Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu, said:

> “The necessary circumstances should be created for the kar seva that the Bharatiya Janata Party, Vishwa Hindu Parishad and certain organisations have announced they will begin on December 6.”

That was published in *Dinamani* dated 24-11-1992. Some further views expressed in her speech were as follows:

> “It is not proper for minorities, against the background of political, historical and social structures, to advance their interests in a manner that affects the ordinary rights and special rights available to the majority under the Constitution. Everyone will agree that the majority community in this country is the Hindus. If they seek peacefully to fulfil their religious aspirations in a manner not contrary to the objectives of the Constitution, that should be permitted.
>
> “The majority too should be allowed to enjoy their rights just as minorities do. To state this specifically in relation to the Ayodhya issue, Hindus should be allowed to carry out construction work as they wish on the land acquired by the Uttar Pradesh Government. All necessary steps should be taken to approach the courts to permit kar seva. I state my request very briefly. Conditions necessary for kar seva should be created on the land acquired by the Uttar Pradesh Government. The desire that a temple should be built at Ayodhya represents the wish of the majority of the people of this country. We should not ignore public opinion — the opinion of the majority of the people of this country.”

It was Jayalalithaa who spoke in this manner in support of kar seva at the National Integration Council meeting itself. That is why she is now talking about a “leak” of the Liberhan Commission report, placing the blame on friend P. Chidambaram and issuing statements demanding that he be made to resign.

At the time when Jayalalithaa thus raised her voice in support of kar seva, look also at the poem I wrote in *Murasoli* dated December 5 under the title “Like a Leaping Spear — Like a Sword Scooping Up Fury”:

> “What indeed is kar seva?
>
> Service to God? Or
>
> service that sows the seed of riot? And is there
>
> a maiden here to lend it a hand?
>
> If Rama was born in the Treta Yuga,
>
> then the Dvapara Yuga followed and ended too,
>
> and when what is now said to be underway is the Kali Yuga,
>
> have not twenty lakh years passed!
>
> So many lakh years ago,
>
> who saw the place where Rama was born?
>
> Who declared it so?
>
> Saying now that this alone is that place,
>
> what justice is there in demolishing an Islamic historical monument?
>
> In Ayodhya city, a temple for Rama —
>
> we all permit it to be built without objection;
>
> but if for that purpose one says,
>
> ‘We will remove the Babri Masjid,’
>
> we will not agree!”

But what happened? The entire structure of the Babri Masjid was demolished by five in the evening. Because severe opposition arose throughout India, Jayalalithaa then did a complete somersault and said she had never supported kar seva at all. Like the somersault over Tamil Eelam — but that is another story.

Forgetting all of that, or believing that the people have forgotten it, Jayalalithaa now says Union Home Minister P. Chidambaram should be removed.

It is common practice for a government to appoint a commission after an incident and for that commission to submit its report to the government. There are also occasions when such reports are released by the opposition before the government publishes them.

To give an example: on 26-11-1980, Subramania Pillai, who was Assistant Commissioner of the Hindu Religious and Charitable Endowments Department, was, according to this letter, murdered in a lodge in the Tiruchendur temple complex by certain trustees belonging to the then ruling AIADMK, and an attempt was made to conceal it by calling it a suicide. The DMK raised a battle cry over the incident. Justice C.J.R. Paul was appointed by Chief Minister M.G.R.’s government to inquire into it. Because the report he gave the government was not favourable to them, the government did not release it. While that was the position, a copy of Justice Paul’s report reached me in my capacity as Leader of the Opposition, and on 24-11-1981 I released copies of that report through the press.

When the Paul Commission report was debated in the Legislative Assembly on 13-2-1982, I spoke on behalf of the DMK and asked the government not to spend its time on how Justice Paul’s report had come out or on whom to take revenge, but instead to concentrate on what the report actually said and take action against the real offenders.

When the government said the matter should be considered only after the Crime Branch reinvestigated the case, the DMK walked out of the Assembly. M.G.R., then Chief Minister, rose and informed the House that the Tiruchendur temple trustees had sent in their resignation letters.

Even after that no action was taken. It is history that on 15-2-1982, I and DMK comrades undertook a long march from Madurai to Tiruchendur demanding justice.

Jayalalithaa now thunders that because the Liberhan Commission report was leaked, Union Home Minister P. Chidambaram must accept responsibility and either leave office or be removed. It would have been appropriate if she had also stated whether, when the Justice Paul Commission report came out, any Chief Minister or minister then in office accepted moral responsibility and resigned or was removed.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**28-11-2009**

## Letter-specific notes

1. `கரசேவை` is retained as **kar seva**, reflecting the source’s political-religious terminology.
2. Statements about the Babri Masjid, the Liberhan Commission and the Paul Commission are translated as claims made within the letter; they are not externally adjudicated here.
3. The rural proverb, fruit/thief analogies, “somersault” accusation and rhetorical close are preserved rather than neutralised.
4. The poem is translated sense-first while retaining its question-and-answer structure and argumentative sequence.
