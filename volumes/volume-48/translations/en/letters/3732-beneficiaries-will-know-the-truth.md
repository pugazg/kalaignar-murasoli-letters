# 3732. The Beneficiaries Will Know the Truth!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 12 April 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

On 21 March 2013, the Finance Minister presented the 2013–2014 Budget in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly. The debate on the demand for grants for the Municipal Administration and Water Supply Department was held in the Assembly on 1 April 2013, and the minister of that department had already replied. Yet yesterday (10 April 2013), Chief Minister Jayalalithaa herself read in the Assembly, under Rule 110, a long statement containing announcements that ought to have been made during the grant debate for the Water Supply Department.

Under Rule 110 she announced, along with several other measures, combined drinking-water schemes costing ₹450 crore in Dindigul district, ₹125 crore in Thanjavur, ₹114 crore 25 lakh in Coimbatore, ₹76 crore 44 lakh in Tiruppur, ₹797 crore 69 lakh in Tiruchirappalli and ₹227 crore 26 lakh in Tirunelveli; that Thanjavur and Dindigul municipalities would be made municipal corporations; and that Karaikudi and Sivakasi municipalities would be upgraded to special-grade municipalities. If all these schemes that the Rural Development Minister should announce are not allowed to be announced by that minister during the grant debate, and a few days later the Chief Minister herself reads them under Rule 110, why then is there a separate minister for that department at all? Let the Chief Minister read half the schemes; if at least the remaining half were left for the departmental ministers to read, would they not have the satisfaction of having fulfilled at least half their duty?

Is the reason simply that newspapers must carry the words “Chief Minister's announcement”? Is the intention that neither the Finance Minister nor the departmental ministers should announce these measures? This same Chief Minister Jayalalithaa, when M.G.R. was alive, wrote in her own hand to the then Prime Minister Rajiv Gandhi that, apart from M.G.R., everyone else here was a “zero.” Are all the other ministers still “zeros” now as well?

If it is not merely the important announcements concerning this one minister, but those of every department that the Chief Minister herself reads under Rule 110, why have debates on departmental grants and ministers' reply speeches at all? From now on, instead of presenting a Budget every year, holding a general debate, debating demands for grants and having ministers reply, why not decide that every day the Chief Minister alone will read a Rule 110 statement on each department, after which the leaders of the other parties and the Speaker can take turns praising those announcements?

At the very beginning of her statement, the Chief Minister also said: “To solve Ramanathapuram district's drinking-water problem, the seawater desalination project at Narippaiyur in Ramanathapuram district, with a capacity of 3.80 million litres, was brought in during my [Jayalalithaa's] period of rule. Similarly, the Ramanathapuram Combined Drinking Water Scheme was designed during my earlier period of rule and was subsequently completed.”

The Ramanathapuram Combined Drinking Water Scheme was launched under DMK rule. It was not through a “video conference”; I myself went to Paramakudi and took part in that major function.

After Perarignar Anna's death, when I had assumed the responsibility of Chief Minister and was taking part in various programmes, I went to the Nilgiris on 21 May 1989 to inaugurate a flower show. After seeing that flower show—the masses of flowers blooming there, the waterfalls leaping along—I had a thought, and I expressed it at that very function: “Even as I look at the Nilgiris, at this prosperity and this garden of flowers, and feel happy, I also think with concern of the parched Ramanathapuram district.”

I did not stop with merely saying it. Even if Ramanathapuram could not be transformed exactly into a prosperous Nilgiris, I worked from office with the determination that it should be changed to some extent and that its basic drinking-water problem must be solved. As a result, when the DMK government was formed in 1996, we implemented eleven brackish-water desalination schemes in Ramanathapuram district at a cost of ₹72 crore 20 lakh. Through the eleven desalination units established under those schemes, a total of 237 village habitations received drinking-water facilities.

After the DMK government returned to office in Tamil Nadu in May 2006, the Budget presented in the Assembly on 22 July 2006 announced: “As a permanent solution to the drinking-water problem that has persisted for many years in the villages and towns of Ramanathapuram district, work on a major ₹671-crore scheme to bring and supply Cauvery water will begin this year.” The Ramanathapuram Combined Drinking Water Scheme was the scheme formulated to put that announcement into effect immediately.

It was I who laid the foundation stone and launched the work at a function held in Paramakudi on 30 January 2007. In the first stage of this great scheme, drinking water began to be supplied from 27 June 2009 to people living in the municipal areas of Ramanathapuram, Rameswaram, Paramakudi and Keelakarai and in the town-panchayat areas of Mandapam, Sayalkudi, Mudukulathur, Kamuthi and Abiramam. In the second stage, supply was extended to 32 habitations in the Viralimalai, Alavayal and Ponnamaravathi areas of Pudukkottai district; to 799 habitations in Sivaganga district, including Sivaganga municipality and areas such as Tiruppattur, Kalaiyarkoil and Ilayangudi; and to various rural parts of Ramanathapuram district. The credit for repeatedly inspecting the work, advising and encouraging officials and completing it within two years belongs to the departmental minister and Deputy Chief Minister, Thambi M.K. Stalin.

It was under DMK rule, in 1971, that the Water Supply and Drainage Board was established so that people could receive drinking water without interruption.

In 1995–96, the final year of AIADMK rule, ₹213 crore 82 lakh was spent on drinking-water works. In 2000–2001, the final year of DMK rule, ₹842 crore was allocated for drinking-water works. These figures themselves show how much concern the DMK government showed in dealing with drinking-water problems.

The Narippaiyur seawater desalination project in Ramanathapuram district, described as the largest such project in Asia, was completed at a cost of ₹51 crore and inaugurated on 27 June 1999. It continues to benefit the people of Ramanathapuram district. Although this project was announced during AIADMK rule in 1995, no funds were allocated for it. Funds were allocated and the project was completed only under DMK rule.

Jayalalithaa has nevertheless told the Assembly that the Narippaiyur drinking-water project too was brought in during her period of rule.

In 1998, under DMK rule, a seawater desalination project was launched at Narippaiyur village in Ramanathapuram district, described as the first of its kind in Asia. The ₹46-crore project supplied drinking water to 296 villages in Kadaladi union. In 1998, “BHEL” secured the tender and the scheme operated under its maintenance for five years. Thereafter it functioned under the maintenance of the Doshian company. When the project began, it produced 38 lakh litres of drinking water a day and supplied 296 villages.

To address Chennai's drinking-water problem, after the DMK government came to office in 1996 it maintained cordial relations with the Andhra Pradesh government, completed the long-stalled Krishna Drinking Water Project and made it possible for Chennai to receive water from the Kandaleru reservoir through the Krishna canal.

Likewise, with Japanese international cooperative financial assistance, work on the ₹1,929-crore Hogenakkal Combined Drinking Water Scheme to eliminate the drinking-water problem in Krishnagiri and Dharmapuri districts was carried forward at full speed under DMK rule.

Although the Minjur seawater desalination project had been initiated during AIADMK rule, it was delayed by a court case and no further constructive steps were taken to implement it. After the DMK government was formed in 2006, negotiations were held and important decisions were taken to expedite the project. In that process, the previously fixed price for the drinking water produced at the plant was reduced to some extent. On 25 February 2007, Thambi M.K. Stalin, then Minister for Local Administration, laid the foundation stone and work began. Following a successful trial run, the project was inaugurated on 31 July 2010.

After the Minjur project came the Nemmeli seawater desalination project. Although it was recently inaugurated by the present Chief Minister Jayalalithaa, it was the DMK government that pursued all the necessary efforts, requested and obtained funds from the Union Government and completed a large part of the work.

Yet in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly, Jayalalithaa speaks as though the DMK government brought in no drinking-water projects at all and as though she herself brought in the Narippaiyur project, the Ramanathapuram Combined Drinking Water Scheme, the Minjur project, the Hogenakkal project and the Nemmeli project. That is why I am placing all these details before the people. At this rate, it would not be surprising if she were to announce under Rule 110 that it was not Karikalan who built the Kallanai, but she herself! This is not Rule 110; it is the fate of the people of Tamil Nadu!

However hard Ammaiyar may try to claim every distinction for herself, can Jayalalithaa deceive through these artificial announcements the people of Tamil Nadu who experience the benefits of these schemes every day in their respective areas? Will the beneficiaries somehow fail to know the truth?

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**12 April 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. **Rule 110** is retained as the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly procedural reference used in the source.
2. The source gives both ₹51 crore and ₹46 crore in its two descriptions of the Narippaiyur project. Both figures are preserved without reconciliation.
3. `விதி` supports Kalaignar's closing wordplay between a legislative “rule” and the people's “fate”; the English preserves the contrast as closely as possible.
4. The company form `தோஷியன்` is rendered **Doshian** by close transliteration from the audited Tamil rather than externally normalised.
