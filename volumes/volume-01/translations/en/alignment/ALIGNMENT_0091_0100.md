# Volume 1 — Bilingual Alignment: Letters 0091–0100

Status: **PASS — no English prose/quotation correction required.**

## Scope

This batch covers canonical English letters **0091–0100**, corresponding to source PDF / printed pages **326–358**.

The English records had already completed canonical migration/source checking. This gate compared each English translation directly with the complete canonical Tamil witness embedded in the record. The canonical Tamil layer had already passed the scan-based visual/textual-fidelity gate; this batch does not claim a new independent scan transcription pass.

## Alignment method

For every record, the review checked argument and narrative sequence; substantive coverage and unsupported additions; paragraph, verse and quotation correspondence; names, dates, figures and political references; lists, metaphors, wordplay and rhetorical questions; repetition, irony and political intensity; movement vocabulary and the established `Udanpirappē` convention; and closing/sign-off integrity.

Only demonstrable English/Tamil correspondence errors would be corrected at this gate. Stylistic polishing remains deferred to the later editorial-consistency gate.

## Batch result

**All ten records PASS bilingual alignment. No English prose/quotation correction was required.**

### 0091 — `நமது விழாக்கள்!`

PASS. The dated festival sequence — Navalar on **15 September**, Anna on **16 September**, Periyar and the movement on **17 September**, followed by the silver-jubilee continuation — remains in source order. The argument that celebrations must become political and social work rather than empty display is complete, including the closing image that the results of festivals must become **supporting roots and seeds** for the movement.

### 0092 — `பயணம் தொடரட்டும்!`

PASS. The sibling/betrayal analogies, quoted violent rhetoric and Kalaignar's darkly sarcastic response remain distinct and source-ordered. The 1971 Salem/Rajaji recollection, the contrast between former state-autonomy advocacy and later conduct, former Supreme Court judge Subba Rao's Centre–State formulation, and the concluding insistence that the historical journey continues beyond any individual all correspond to the Tamil witness.

### 0093 — `பிறந்த நாள் விழாவும் மறந்த சில விவரங்களும்!`

PASS. Kalaignar's opening appreciation of Kamaraj's seventy-second-birthday celebration precedes, as in Tamil, the double-standard argument. The N. S. Krishnan, Periyar and Anna recollections, Katchatheevu / *Navasakthi* material, the lineated `கேலி / கிண்டல் / ஏசல் / ஏளனம் / ஏகடியம்` sequence, both *Dinamani* float quotations, the Kannadasan passage and the closing Kamaraj/Anna appeal are all represented.

### 0094 — `நேரமும் நினைப்பும்!`

PASS. The Police Commission / *Udhaya Suriyan* argument, the complete colloquial police-family lullaby including the **₹75 / ₹155** figures, and the national-prohibition argument all align. The flower-patterned-glass and matchbox satire, repeated rabid-dog image and the final `நேரம் / நினைப்பு` turn retain the source's sequence and bite.

### 0095 — `‘மாம்பழ மங்கை!’`

PASS. The playful maiden-simile chain and reversal from maiden-like-mango to mango-like-maiden remain complete. The Ranipet newspaper report, miracle-worker and credulity satire, Karaikkal Ammaiyar reference, Vadakalai–Thenkalai elephant dispute, apsara/Urvashi/Rambha/Menaka passage and the comparison between a handless mango maiden and attractive parties without principles all correspond. The closing rationalist-work argument follows source wording without speculative repair.

### 0096 — `அதே தேதிகள்!`

PASS. The English correctly preserves the source's abrupt opening rather than inventing a salutation or lost line. Kattabomman's resistance, betrayal through Pudukkottai, **24 September 1799**, the parallel claim that betrayal of the movement began on **24 September 1972** at Chengalpattu, and the reconstructed Kattabomman-fort image all remain in the Tamil order.

### 0097 — `இன்றே தயாராகு!`

PASS. The `முப்பெரும் விழா` mobilisation, distinction between a movement born for principle and merely capturing the Fort, scars/prisons/sacrifices, biting-cold/summer/storm/rain/earthquake sequence and repeated demand to prepare immediately all correspond. The final summons from hamlet, village, town and city to gather in Chennai under the movement flag retains its rally cadence.

### 0098 — `உயர்த்திடுவோம் தோள்!`

PASS. Anna's **`இரட்டைக்குழல் துப்பாக்கி` / double-barrelled gun** formulation for the DK and DMK, Miran Sahib Street exchange, joint anti-Hindi action, Tiruchirappalli signboard episode, Legislative Assembly defence of Periyar and the three-pronged-agitation reference all align. The close preserves the movement as a **thousand-year crop** guarded from betrayal-weeds, opportunist-pests and grazing enemies, followed by the spear/sword and shoulder-to-shoulder exhortation.

### 0099 — `வழி மேல் விழி...`

PASS. The tour continues amid the sudden health crisis of Murasoli Selvam without losing the Madurai/Kanchi/Chennai chronology. Printed initials **`ப. உ. ச.`** remain unexpanded, cadres' affection becomes medicine for Kalaignar's wounded mind, and the family summons to the **29 September** Chennai procession ends with the title's idiom of waiting **with eyes fixed on the road**.

### 0100 — `அழைக்கிறேன் - வா! அன்பே வா!`

PASS. The Dharmapuri–Salem–North Arcot travel chronology, delayed meals, roadside **two vadai**, rain-soaked meetings and district organisers remain substantively complete. The `முக்கனி` comparison of DMK/Anna/Periyar, Chennai mobilisation, Jayamkondar's *Kalingattuparani* sequence, Kulothunga comparison and final reversal — from cadres inviting Kalaignar everywhere to Kalaignar inviting them to Chennai — preserve the Tamil argument and repeated **`வா, வா!`** summons.
