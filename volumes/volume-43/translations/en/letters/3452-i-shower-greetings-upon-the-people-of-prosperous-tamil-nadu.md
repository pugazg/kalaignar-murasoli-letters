# 3452. I Shower Greetings upon the People of Prosperous Tamil Nadu!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This letter links the government’s 2008 decision to observe the first day of Thai as Tamil New Year with Pongal celebrations and with a sequence of public projects and welfare announcements. The historical and policy claims, dates, figures and scheme descriptions are retained in the source’s order without external reconciliation.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3452](../../../chapters/3452-vaazhthu-kuvikkinren-valamaar-tamizhaga-makkalukku.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 208](../../../pages/page-208.md)–[PDF 211](../../../pages/page-211.md)  
**Date:** 9 January 2010

**Udanpirappē,**

Tamil New Year is only a few days away! I know well that you will be engaged in the work of celebrating it with splendour and distinction. In 1921, more than five hundred Tamil savants and poets assembled at Pachaiyappa’s College in Chennai under the leadership of Maraimalai Adigal, the great scholar and founder of the Pure Tamil movement. They declared that Tamils needed a “separate year”; decided to follow a continuous year in the name of Ayyan Tiruvalluvar and honour it as the “Tamil Year”; and determined that the year of Tiruvalluvar’s birth was 31 BCE. Accepting that decision, as long ago as thirty-nine years ago I arranged for it to be put into practice in the Tamil Nadu Government’s diaries from 1971 and in the Tamil Nadu Government Gazette from 1972.

Since it is a truth collectively accepted by all Tamil scholars that the Tamil New Year begins on the first day of the month of Thai, when the Tiruvalluvar year begins, the Tamil Nadu Government decided in 2008 to declare the first day of Thai as the beginning of Tamil New Year and to celebrate it every year; this was also mentioned in that year’s Governor’s Address.

Tamils, who have been celebrating Pongal as the festival of the Tamil people, should from now on also accept that day as the birth of Tamil New Year and celebrate it with double joy — as a sweet day, an incomparable day that gives uplift and pride. I ask you to plant the three fruit-bearing trees — banana, jackfruit and mango; draw colourful kolams; give homes new beauty with rows upon rows of shining lamps; wear new clothes; honour self-respect and Tamil dignity; dance and sing; spread the feeling of equality; and celebrate it as a Tamil New Year praised by the world.

Let us regard that day as a sweet festival on which, without any distinction of caste, religion or race, we remember those whose labour has helped the world and convey our gratitude and greetings to the great community of farmers.

With the feeling that ancient Tamil traditions which have lain dimmed must surge forth, flourish everywhere and prosper, I greet you this year too with emotions rising like a new flood, asking that the first day of Thai be celebrated as Tamil New Year and Pongal festival.

It is in the Tamil New Year about to be born that the new Legislative Assembly and Secretariat complex — destined to stand permanently in history — is being constructed with modern facilities and is to be opened within another two months. Likewise, work on the world-class Arignar Anna Centenary Memorial Library being built in Chennai is to be completed and the library opened. Above all, after the historic event of Tamil being declared a classical language, a five-day conference named the World Classical Tamil Conference is to be held in Coimbatore, bringing together Tamils from every corner of the world to celebrate! Committees for that conference have been announced and each has begun its work. In connection with the Coimbatore conference, work has also begun to create, as its memorial, an international-standard botanical garden in Coimbatore under the name “Classical Tamil Park”; just yesterday the Coimbatore District Collector met me and discussed it.

When Ministers, senior pioneers of the DMK and district DMK secretaries meet me even now, they tell me the many ways in which they are going to celebrate New Year’s Day. Just as Tamils decorate their homes, government buildings, commercial complexes and long bridges on highways should be decorated with coloured lights. In every village, all families should come together to cook Samathuva Pongal — Equality Pongal — and proclaim throughout the village the greatness of the New Year.

New Year’s Day is a sweet festival day for the poor and simple people of Tamil Nadu, for people born as Tamils, and for people who hold their heads high and say that the Dravidian community is their people. Many other days may come and go. But the first day of Thai alone is the beginning of our New Year.

At the time when you are preparing to celebrate this year’s Tamil New Year, the Governor’s Address on behalf of our government has been read in the Assembly. That Address contains the policy announcements prepared on behalf of the Tamil Nadu Government. During the past few weeks, working together with the Government Finance Secretary, day and night with the same thought in mind, we considered what schemes could be announced on behalf of the government for the people of Tamil Nadu and how they could be implemented; the announcements thus arrived at are what the Address contains.

To mention only one or two briefly: the great “Kalaignar Housing Scheme,” under which 21 lakh hut houses in rural areas will be converted into concrete houses at State government expense so as to create a situation in which there are no huts in rural areas; where a family has so far had no graduate, the government will bear the tuition fee, without caste discrimination and without taking an income ceiling into account, for one person from that family to pursue professional education; reduction of the minimum period of residence required for grant of a house-site patta to three years; a decision to provide an additional incentive of ₹50 per quintal for paddy; a Chennai Medical College building estimated at ₹70 crore; ninety acres of land for film workers to build houses, meeting their long-standing demand; cancellation of TAHDCO loans of Adi Dravidar people; cancellation of tsunami loans taken by mechanised-boat fishermen; and an environmental-protection and renewable-energy development fund — the list could go on and on. Along with these newly announced schemes, all the constructive schemes already announced by this government will continue to be implemented.

Generally, as far as this government is concerned — and particularly as far as I am concerned — I am devoting my thought and labour to how the government can help the people of Tamil Nadu in every possible way, fulfil benefits for them and provide concessions. It is as a result of this that one scheme after another has been announced in the Governor’s Address. Funds for these schemes will be provided by the government. My great desire is that the funds so provided by the government should reach the people properly and in full. At this good moment when all of you are preparing to join me in celebrating the New Year with distinction, I consider it my duty to ask every section to provide the cooperation needed for that. With the assurance that my sense of duty to labour for the Tamil people in every possible way will always continue, I shower my heartfelt Tamil New Year greetings upon you and upon all the Udanpirappugal.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**9-1-2010**

## Letter-specific notes

1. The source’s historical account of the Tiruvalluvar year and Tamil New Year is translated as the author’s account; no outside calendrical or historical reconciliation is added.
2. The celebration imagery — the three fruits, kolams, lamps, self-respect, Tamil dignity and Samathuva Pongal — is retained rather than compressed into a generic holiday greeting.
3. All policy figures and scheme descriptions, including 21 lakh houses, ₹50 per quintal, ₹70 crore and 90 acres, are retained in source order.
4. No new recurring glossary treatment or canonical Tamil change was required during this source-check.

50 ஊக்கத் தொகை வழங்க முடிவு; 70 கோடி ரூபாய் மதிப்பீட்டில்
சென்னை மருத்துவ கல்லூரிக்கான கட்டிடம்; கலையுலகினரின்
