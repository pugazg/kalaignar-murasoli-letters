**Date:** 3 February 2012

**Udanpirappē,**

“Can anyone show a government like this one that has completed works and created achievements within a single month?” Chief Minister Jayalalithaa challenged in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly on 31 January 2012. Whom did she challenge? She issued the challenge while replying to a complaint by the DMDK. Which DMDK? The very DMDK with which, only six months ago, they formed an alliance, won the Assembly election and captured power! Looking at that party's leader—the Leader of the Opposition in the Assembly—she said that he had behaved in a disgusting manner and that she regretted having formed an alliance with him. Such a magnificent achievement truly cannot be seen under any other government! Only Jayalalithaa could accomplish such a great achievement.

Has the Chief Minister achieved only this one thing? In the seven months since becoming Chief Minister, there is also the Himalayan achievement of having reshuffled the Cabinet six times—something no other government has done. What of the achievement of sending nine ministers home in eight months? Twenty portfolios have changed hands. The Law Department alone has had three ministers; the Food Department too has had three. Where else has there been the achievement of removing Agri Krishnamoorthy after only one week as School Education Minister and S. P. Velumani after only one week as Revenue Minister?

N. R. Sivapathi, who had been Animal Husbandry Minister and was later removed from the Cabinet for some alleged fault, has now again become a minister. Does that mean the earlier fault has now become all right?

Not only ministers—what is the condition of officials under AIADMK rule? Transfers of officials are normal under any government. But what is the present condition? To what extent are Collectors being tossed about? To what extent are police officials being transferred? Why, what is the condition even of the Chief Minister's security officers?

Coimbatore District Collector Karunakaran was transferred within six months. Newspapers reported that he had worked honestly there, that he was not transferred for administrative reasons, but was forced to go on leave because of pressure from a ruling-party functionary. Hariharan was appointed Coimbatore Collector, went there, assumed charge and worked for only a few days. Before long, he was sent back to his old post and Karunakaran—the very officer who had earlier been directed to go on compulsory leave—was reappointed Coimbatore Collector.

Sivasanmugaraja, who had been Kanchipuram District Collector, was directed to go on compulsory leave at the beginning of January after serving for only a few months. Hanish Chhabra, who had been Deputy Commissioner for Revenue and Finance in the Chennai Corporation, was appointed to that post.

Cuddalore Collector Mrs. Amuthavalli, in a district severely affected by Cyclone Thane, was suddenly transferred on 20 January 2012 while relief work was proceeding at speed. Why? Was it not because the government itself considered that relief work had not been carried out properly that the Collector was transferred within a few months?

Mrs. S. Madhumathi was appointed Chennai Collector. The source then prints this English passage:

> The post of Chennai Collector has been lying vacant for more than six months. This could well be the longest period when the seat has remained unoccupied. V. Shobana, the last Chennai Collector, was transferred to Karur on June 6. 2011.

For more than six months no Collector had been appointed for the capital city of Chennai. The District Revenue Officer himself was holding charge as Collector. He handed over land belonging to the Horticulture Department to a private person. After I wrote about it in *Murasoli*, the land was recovered. Then last week Mrs. Madhumathi was announced as the new Collector for Chennai district, and she assumed charge. What happened? Not even two days had passed after Madhumathi assumed charge as Chennai District Collector when an order was issued removing her from that responsibility. Can achievements like these be seen under any other government?

Mrs. D. Sabitha, I.A.S., was said to have worked closely with the Chief Minister during the earlier AIADMK government. Yet during DMK rule we appointed her School Education Secretary. Under the present AIADMK government she was suddenly transferred. How many months later? Within a few months she has again been transferred back as School Education Secretary. Why was she removed in the first place? Why was she then appointed to the very same post?

Likewise, in Tamil Nadu, Vice-Chancellor posts in six universities are currently vacant. Search committees for appointing Vice-Chancellors have still not been formed. The Tamil Nadu State Council for Higher Education too is like an empty tent, without a Vice-Chairman, Member-Secretary or members. At Madurai Kamaraj University, not only the Vice-Chancellor's post but the Registrar's post is vacant. The Registrar's post at the University of Madras is vacant. Posts of Director and Regional Director of Collegiate Education too remain vacant. Can achievements like these be seen under another government? Certainly not.

Immediately after assuming office, in the Governor's Address read on 3 June 2011, the AIADMK government said it would take strict necessary measures to maintain law and order and enable people to live without fear. What is the condition now?

Is there a day in Tamil Nadu without a murder? Is there a day without robbery? How many cases of waylaying and chain-snatching?

Between 13 May 2011 and 14 October 2011, Tamil Nadu witnessed 154 murders, 165 robberies, 102 chain-snatchings and 27 waylaying incidents.

Until last month, some people were exceedingly dear to the Chief Minister. One of them had lived with her in the same house for years. Now all those who were once dear to her are on the list of those driven out of the Garden! A succession of accusations has been made against them. What is the reason for those accusations? If they committed those offences because they were people close to the Chief Minister, is the Chief Minister connected with those accusations or not? Has an achievement like this ever happened under any other government?

One example of the efficiency of the Tamil Nadu Police, which is under the Chief Minister's charge: it is said that in order to find one private individual named Divakaran—once very dear to the Chief Minister, now no longer wanted—nine special teams comprising two Additional Superintendents, four Deputy Superintendents and nine Inspectors have been searching for the past month, and have still not found him. If this very thing had happened under DMK rule, how many statements would Jayalalithaa have issued calling it an “inept minority government”? Panneerselvam must answer!

Can another government be shown that has performed achievements like those made in the eight months since Jayalalithaa came to power? Here are a few achievements:

1. Shifting the Secretariat.
2. Shifting the Pavendar Classical Tamil Research Library.
3. Enacting a law to cancel the Samacheer Kalvi scheme.
4. Changing the first day of Thai as Tamil New Year.
5. Announcing that the Anna Centenary Memorial Library would be converted into a children's welfare hospital.
6. Cancelling the allotment of land to the Muthamizh Peravai.
7. A resolution to abolish the Legislative Council.
8. Cancelling the medical-insurance scheme.
9. Conducting a closing ceremony for the scheme to provide concrete houses to hut-dwellers.
10. Action against Kazhagam leaders—the Goondas Act—and searches in the houses of leading members.
11. Immediately after coming to power, taxes; milk-price increase; bus-fare increase; and a recommendation to increase electricity tariffs.

After many studies and recommendations from many committees, the Samacheer Kalvi curriculum was implemented and textbooks were printed at a cost of more than ₹200 crore. The government not only said, “We will not accept these Samacheer Kalvi textbooks; only the old curriculum will remain,” but hurriedly introduced and passed an amendment in the Legislative Assembly.

When the matter came before the High Court, Justices S. Rajeswaran and Mrs. K. B. K. Vasuki said that the purpose of the Samacheer Kalvi Act was very clear; that a study committee of eminent experts had examined the matter in detail and recommended implementation of the Samacheer Kalvi system; that those recommendations could not simply be ignored; and that when a very large amount had already been spent, it was necessary to ask whether spending another large amount was required. They said the Advocate General should give the government appropriate advice on all these matters.

Even after that, the Tamil Nadu Government went to the Supreme Court against the judgment. Justices B. S. Chauhan and Swatanter Kumar said in their ruling that Samacheer Kalvi already introduced for Classes 1 and 6 must continue; stopping it midway would cause confusion; it must be implemented that very year for Classes 2 to 5 and 7 to 10; and if there were defects in the curriculum, an expert committee should be appointed to examine them and submit its report to the Madras High Court within two weeks.

The Madras High Court continued hearing the case. The Chief Justice and another judge said there was no hesitation in holding that the State Government had exceeded its authority; the amendment brought by the Tamil Nadu Government was invalid and was being struck down; and textbooks under the Samacheer law must immediately be distributed to students. The Tamil Nadu Government appealed against that judgment too. In its interim order, the Supreme Court said that the Madras High Court judgment could not be stayed and that Samacheer textbooks must be distributed by 2 August 2011.

The Tamil Nadu Government did not respect even that order and did not distribute the textbooks. Counsel for the State said it was not feasible to implement Samacheer Kalvi in the current academic year. The final hearing in the Supreme Court took place before a Bench comprising Justices Panchal, Deepak Verma and Chauhan. At the outset the judges asked: after the High Court had ruled in 2010 itself that the DMK government's Samacheer Kalvi Act was valid, why did the Tamil Nadu Government bring an amendment to suspend the scheme? What necessity was there for it? Government counsel P. P. Rao admitted: “The Tamil Nadu Government was not given proper legal advice; we brought an unnecessary amendment and got ourselves caught in the problem.” In their final judgment, the judges directed that Samacheer Kalvi be implemented within ten days; said that no law should be changed merely because a government changes; that every action must take future consequences into account; and that political parties should not alter the education system for their likes and dislikes. They dismissed the Tamil Nadu Government's petition. This judgment was regarded as the first hammer-blow suffered by Jayalalithaa. Has any other government received such a certificate within six months of assuming office? Is this only one certificate?

Jayalalithaa announced that the Perarignar Anna Memorial Library would be shifted to the D.P.I. campus in Egmore. In the case concerning that decision, a First Bench comprising Chief Justice Eqbal and Justice Sivagnanam asked why such an excellent, modern, well-equipped and beautifully created library should be shifted; said that if a hospital was to be built, it could be built on Marina Beach; and observed that the government's decision appeared to have an ulterior motive. The judges ordered notice to the government seeking an explanation for shifting the library.

When the Jayalalithaa government attempted to take back the land allotted under DMK rule to the T. N. Rajarathinam Memorial Muthamizh Peravai, Justice N. Paul Vasanthakumar asked: “Having given the land with one hand, in what way is it fair to snatch it away with the other? Even if governments change, is not the government one and the same? Why did you suddenly take such a decision? I am staying this. If you wish, you may file a counter-affidavit within four weeks.” He granted an interim stay on the notice issued by the Public Works Department. One can continue writing Jayalalithaa's achievements in this manner.

On 8 November 2011, the Tamil Nadu Government ordered the dismissal of more than 12,000 people's welfare workers. In the case concerning this, Justice Suguna granted an interim stay on the dismissal order. When the case came up on 21 November 2011, it was stated that the court order had not been implemented and the people's welfare workers had not been reinstated. Justice Suguna directed that they be immediately permitted to rejoin duty and that a report on compliance with the court order be filed on the 23rd. The Tamil Nadu Government appealed against Justice Suguna's order.

A First Bench comprising Chief Justice Eqbal and Justice Sivagnanam, hearing that appeal, stated in substance as printed in the source: the people's welfare workers had first been appointed in 1990 on consolidated pay of ₹200 a month; when the government changed in 1991 they were removed; in 1997, after the next government came, they were reappointed on ₹500 consolidated pay; when the government changed in 2001 they were again removed; in 2006, after another government came, they were appointed on ₹1,000 consolidated pay. Their appointment and removal had thus taken place according to the government's wishes. Even assuming for argument that they were temporary employees, they had worked for twenty years. If they were to be dismissed with a single signature, principles of justice and law required that they be given notice and their views sought before removal. Justice Suguna had therefore correctly granted the stay, which could not be vacated. The workers' condition resembled a football game. After a government gives a person employment and fixes pay in accordance with a Supreme Court order, removing that person without proper reason is unlawful. The Bench strongly condemned governments issuing opposite orders whenever power changed, calling it condemnable, painful and regrettable. The government appeal was therefore rejected. If a scheme of the previous government was altered, the employees brought under it should be placed in other work; dismissing them instead was wrong. Though the political character of the matter was visible, the judges said they did not wish to enter into that.

The Tamil Nadu Government nevertheless appealed to the Supreme Court. On 29 November 2011, Justices D. K. Jain and Dave, hearing the petition, asked in condemnation: “What is happening in Tamil Nadu? Why are the previous government's schemes cancelled whenever the government changes?” Has an achievement like this happened under any other government? Even after all this, the Tamil Nadu Government says it will appeal rather than reinstate the people's welfare workers! Is that one achievement not enough?

Immediately after Jayalalithaa came to office, even before placing the Budget before the House, about ₹4,000 crore in taxes was loaded upon the people of Tamil Nadu. Beyond that, increases at every level—tax increases, bus-fare increases, milk-price increases—were announced. How many achievements are these! Could any other government perform so many achievements in just eight months?

In every statement she issued in the past, Jayalalithaa repeatedly accused the DMK government and me, as Chief Minister, of having ruined the Tamil Nadu Electricity Board. Now she has recommended electricity-tariff increases on a scale without precedent in history. Can anyone else equal that achievement?

For lack of space, I will list the corruption issues of this government later. A serious allegation has arisen that revenue due to the government-run public-sector company TASMAC is being diverted to ruling-party people. In the six months since coming to power, three I.A.S. officers have already been changed as TASMAC Managing Director. It is said that corruption itself is the reason.

Under the headline **“Favouritism Alleged”**, an English newspaper published the following on 26 November 2011:

> Tasmac incurs loss in Tirupur - The State-run Tasmac, which operates retail liquor shops across the State, has allegedly showed favours to ruling party members in awarding bars, unmind ful of the revenue loss it caused.

**Tamil rendering printed in the source:** TASMAC, the government company that runs retail liquor shops across the State, is said to have shown concessions to members of the ruling party in awarding bars, without concern for the loss caused to itself in Tiruppur.

The source continues in English:

> A reply given to an RTI petition filed by Mr. M. Karthikeyan, President of Tirupur District Bar Owners Association, has revealed that Tasmac has been incurring Rs. 60 lakh monthly loss in Tirupur alone owing to alleged irregularities in awarding bar tenders.

**Tamil rendering printed in the source:** According to details obtained under the Right to Information Act by Mr. M. Karthikeyan, President of the Tiruppur District Bar Owners Association, irregularities in awarding bar tenders in Tiruppur district alone have caused TASMAC a loss of ₹60 lakh every month.

The source then prints:

> Tirupur district generated a profit of Rs. 61.10 lakh every month from the 250 IMFL shops during the previous fiscal, while it has gone down to Rs. 1.66 lakh per month from the 209 shops under operation in the current financial year, the RTI reply reveals.

**Tamil rendering printed in the source:** The RTI information stated that in the previous financial year the auctioning of 250 TASMAC shops in Tiruppur district yielded the government ₹61.10 lakh profit every month, whereas in the current financial year the 209 shops in operation yield only ₹1.66 lakh a month.

The source further prints:

> For instance, shops No. 1906, 1923, and 1927 located on P.N. Road, Kovai Main Road and Mangalam Road, have been auctioned for just Rs. 100 more than the upset price. Most of the shops were auctioned for less than Rs. 1000 more than the upset price, the RTI reply revealed.

**Tamil rendering printed in the source:** For example, TASMAC shop numbers 1906, 1923 and 1927 on P.N. Road, Kovai Main Road and Mangalam Road were auctioned for only ₹100 above the prescribed minimum price. Most shops were auctioned for amounts less than ₹1,000 above the minimum price.

The English report concludes:

> Mr. Karthikeyan attributed the loss to deliberate undervalue of the auction price by local Tasmac officials to favour ruling party members. Of the 41 shops not auctioned this year, most were withheld only to favour nearby shops awarded to members of the ruling AIADMK, he charged, adding that several applications were received after the deadline on the night before opening the tenders. Those who questioned the irregularities were booked under charges of bootlegging. (Deccan Chronicle-26/11/2011).

**Tamil rendering printed in the source:** Mr. Karthikeyan said that the loss arose because local TASMAC officials deliberately undervalued auction amounts in order to favour local AIADMK functionaries. He alleged that most of the 41 shops not auctioned that year were withheld only to benefit nearby bars allotted to AIADMK members; that numerous applications were received overnight after the deadline, on the eve of opening the tenders; and that those who questioned these irregularities were arrested on false charges of distilling and selling illicit liquor.

A ruling-party-supporting newspaper reported on 27 November 2011 about a Highways Department tender in Villupuram. Tenders for road works in Villupuram, Cuddalore and Ariyalur districts had been held on the 24th and 25th at the office of the Highways Department Superintending Engineer in Villupuram. Tender time ended at 3 p.m. Yet at 5 p.m., cars of AIADMK functionaries were lined up outside the office. Seeing the crowd even two hours after the deadline, a press photographer took pictures. Superintending Engineer Sivakumar, office employees and party functionaries allegedly locked the photographer in a room, threatened him and forced him to “delete” the photographs before releasing him.

The report further alleged that over the two days of tendering, some works were decided for particular people on the claim that ministers had recommended them; other applicants were called and rejected with promises of work next time; officials themselves arranged specified payments to settle some applicants; 165 Speed Post covers were received on time but rejected without conducting the tender properly, reducing competition and allowing tenders to be allotted according to preference. Senior contractors said this would cause the government losses of several crores and raised doubts whether quality road work could be done when substantial money had changed hands in the tender. That newspaper continued to publish reports of this kind. These are only a few examples of the government's functioning.

Following the allegation that police at midnight sexually assaulted four women belonging to the Irular community at Mandapam near Tirukkoyilur, advocate Pugazhenthi submitted a complaint petition to the Madras High Court. Chief Justice Eqbal and Justice Sivagnanam said: “We strongly condemn the action of the police. We accept the Government Advocate's statement that arresting women at midnight is wrong. Would you send the affected women for medical examination only after one month instead of immediately? We direct the Villupuram S.P. to file those reports before the Court immediately. The government must file a detailed counter-affidavit within two weeks.”

The AIADMK government unnecessarily prevented and prohibited Mr. John Pandian from attending an event at Paramakudi. In the indiscriminate police firing on innocent members of the public, particularly Adi Dravidars, six people were unjustly shot dead on the spot. Many others were seriously injured and continued to receive treatment in various hospitals. Has an achievement like this occurred under any other government?

Without worrying about any of these matters, the AIADMK government fabricates false cases against Kazhagam members and former ministers, undertakes arrests and files cases. Can any other government perform such an achievement?

Chief Minister Jayalalithaa said party people would not interfere in police matters under AIADMK rule. Yet within one month of the AIADMK taking office, an incident occurred in Chennai. On 12 June 2011, an MLA belonging to the ruling party—and even a minister—went at night to Nungambakkam Police Station itself and took home seven ruling-party men whom the police had detained. A minister himself complained against a Deputy Commissioner of Police. A police officer who had acted properly was transferred to the battalion in a single day. What justice! Is this one achievement not enough?

I could keep writing like this! I had thought I might refrain from writing. But because all the newspapers reported that the lady had issued a challenge in the Assembly about her achievements, I have listed here what immediately came to my mind. This is only one drop from this government's achievements! Poison; isn't one drop enough?

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**3 February 2012**

உடன்பிறப்பே,

“ஒரே மாதத்தில் பணிகளைச் செய்து சாதனை படைத்த இதுபோல் ஒரு அரசைக் காட்ட முடியுமா?” என்று தமிழகச் சட்டசபையில் முதலமைச்சர் ஜெயலலிதா 31-1-2012 அன்று சவால் விட்டிருக்கிறார். யாரிடம் சவால் விட்டிருக்கிறார் என்றால் தே.மு.தி.க. புகாருக்கு மறுப்புக்கூறி சவால் விட்டிருக்கிறார். எந்த தே.மு.தி.க.? கடந்த ஆறு மாதங்களுக்கு முன்பு நடைபெற்ற சட்டசபைத் தேர்தலில் எந்த தே.மு.தி.க.வுடன் தோழமை பூண்டு வெற்றி பெற்று, ஆட்சியைக் கைப்பற்றினார்களோ, அந்தத் தே.மு.தி.க.வின் தலைவரைப் பார்த்து - சட்டசபை எதிர்க்கட்சித் தலைவரைப் பார்த்து - அவர் அருவருக்கத்தக்க வகையில் நடந்து கொண்டதாகவும், அவருடன் கூட்டணி வைத்துக் கொண்டதற்காக வருத்தப்படுவதாகவும் கூறியிருக்கிறார். இந்த மாபெரும் சாதனையை வேறு எந்த ஆட்சியிலும் காண முடியாததுதான்! ஜெயலலிதா ஒருவரால்தான் இத்தகைய மாபெரும் சாதனையைச் செய்து காட்ட முடியும்.

முதலமைச்சர் இந்த ஒரு சாதனையை மட்டும்தான் செய்திருக்கிறாரா? முதலமைச்சராகப் பொறுப்பேற்ற பிறகு இந்த ஏழு மாதங்களில் ஆறு முறை அமைச்சரவையை மாற்றிய இமாலய சாதனையைப் போல வேறு எந்த ஆட்சியிலும் செய்யப்படவில்லையேதான். எட்டு மாத காலத்தில் 9 அமைச்சர்கள் வீட்டிற்கு அனுப்பப்பட்ட சாதனை என்ன? இருபது இலாகாக்கள் கைமாறியிருக்கின்றன. சட்டத் துறைக்கு மட்டும் மூன்று அமைச்சர்கள் - உணவுத் துறைக்கு மூன்று பேர் மாறியிருக்கிறார்கள். ஒரு வாரம் மட்டுமே பள்ளிக் கல்வித் துறை அமைச்சராக இருந்த அக்ரி கிருஷ்ணமூர்த்தி மற்றும் ஒரு வாரம் மட்டுமே வருவாய்த் துறை அமைச்சராக இருந்த எஸ்.பி. வேலுமணி ஆகியோர் நீக்கப்பட்ட சாதனை வேறு எங்கேயாவது உண்டா?
