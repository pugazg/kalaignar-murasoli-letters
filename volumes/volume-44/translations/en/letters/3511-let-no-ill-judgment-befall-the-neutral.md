# 3511. Let No Ill Judgment Befall the Neutral!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation follows the audited canonical Tamil and the locked Volume 44 conventions. The letter’s criticism of *Dinamani*, law-and-order statistics, crime examples, named allegations and comparisons with an earlier government are preserved as source-framed argument, not independently verified factual findings. Printed English phrases are retained where they function as source wording. The complete audited Tamil is reproduced below and remains authoritative.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3511](../../../chapters/3511-nadunilaiyaalarkku-kedumathi-koodaathu.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 199](../../../pages/page-199.md)–[PDF 206](../../../pages/page-206.md)  
**Date:** 4 November 2010

**Udanpirappē,**

With the aim, the letter says, of helping the opposition — particularly Jayalalithaa — in its continuing false propaganda that law and order has deteriorated in Tamil Nadu, *Dinamani*, wearing the cloak of a neutral newspaper as though it had sworn that supporting people like Jayalalithaa whatever they do was the purpose of its birth, published an editorial yesterday. In order to divert readers, the letter says, it deliberately magnified and exaggerated every report — **“Blown out of proportions”**, as the source itself puts it in English — blowing them up again and again and, figuratively, forcing a mountain into a mustard seed.

Unable to find fault with the effective functioning of the many welfare schemes of the Tamil Nadu government, it says, such critics have taken up the final weapon of “breakdown of law and order” and are making sweeping, diversionary comments about law and order and criminal incidents in the manner of the saying, “Was the mouth sour, or was the mango sour?”

We may all take pride, the letter argues, that Tamil Nadu is one of the States in India where law and order is maintained particularly well. Under the government’s continuous monitoring, the police maintain law and order with great vigilance so that caste and religious conflicts do not raise their heads.

The **Pasumpon Thevar Guru Pooja** held in Ramanathapuram district on 30-10-2010 took place very peacefully. The letter presents this as an example of good maintenance of law and order by the police and district administration. Likewise, as in recent years, the Vinayagar Chaturthi processions this year were conducted without disturbance to peace, which it cites as another example.

Even though AIADMK members repeatedly issued statements saying that Opposition Leader Jayalalithaa had received threatening letters, that attempts were being made to prevent her scheduled public meeting in Madurai on 18-10-2010 and that her safety would be endangered, the letter says that because of effective police action she travelled to Madurai that day, conducted the meeting without any threat and returned safely to Chennai. The entire country knows this, it says; therefore the people of Tamil Nadu will understand that saying law and order has collapsed is contrary to the truth.

A false campaign is being carried on, the letter argues, as though murders, robberies and kidnappings occur continuously in unusually high numbers only in Tamil Nadu and as though the police merely watch without taking any action. It says there is no basis for this malicious campaign.

Because economic and social changes and advances in information technology are occurring very rapidly, different kinds of crimes are taking place in new forms. Even so, the letter says, intensive police action results in offenders being identified quickly and follow-up action being taken in the overwhelming majority of cases.

For example, of 1,644 murder cases in 2009, offenders were identified in 1,562 cases. If total crimes are taken into account, the letter states that during the AIADMK government, in 2004, the number reached a high of 8 lakh 2 thousand 817, while under the DMK government in 2009 there were 7 lakh 17 thousand 957 crimes.

Even on a population basis, the letter says, Tamil Nadu has a lower rate of crime than other States. In 2008, Kerala had 322 crimes per lakh population and Delhi 286, while Tamil Nadu had 266 per lakh.

Comparing Chennai with Bengaluru, Mumbai, Kolkata and Delhi, the letter says statistical data show that crime incidents in metropolitan Chennai were lower. In 2008, Chennai recorded 11,829 incidents, while Bengaluru recorded 29,664, Mumbai 32,770, Kolkata 13,005 and Delhi 49,350.

Looking at murder cases in major cities, the letter gives the following 2008 figures: Chennai 100, Delhi 554, Bengaluru 253 and Mumbai 210. Comparing States, it says that in 2009 Tamil Nadu recorded 573 rape cases, while Andhra Pradesh recorded 1,257, Maharashtra 1,558 and West Bengal 2,263.

The letter explicitly says these comparisons and statistics are **not** being cited to justify crime. It says there can be no two opinions that police must take the necessary preventive action so that crimes do not occur.

Turning to recent cases, the letter says offenders were quickly identified and arrested. It lists examples.

In the case concerning the killing on 20-11-2008 of former TAMIN Chairman Dr Saravanan and members of his family, despite the absence of immediate clues, offenders were arrested, property was recovered, the trial was completed, and three life convicts were each given three life sentences. The letter describes this as highly commendable.

In the case in which Vijay, a relative of former Chief Minister M.G.R., was killed on 4-6-2008, the accused were arrested on 8-9-2008. In the case in which Dr Bhaskaran of Dindigul was kidnapped and killed on 30-4-2009, the killers were arrested on 23-8-2009 and action was taken.

Because of prior enmity toward the brother of the Congress MLA from Ariyalur, the letter says, his son Dharmadurai, a student, was kidnapped on 11-6-2010. One of the two accused was arrested on 14-6-2010, and police recovered the kidnapped boy and the vehicle used in the kidnapping. The other accused was later arrested in Chennai in another case.

In the case in which employees of Pothys textile shop were waylaid and attacked in Chennai’s Mambalam on 3-7-2010 and ₹81 lakh 20 thousand was robbed from them, police identified and arrested ten accused on 15-7-2010 and recovered ₹39 lakh 37 thousand 500, the letter says.

In the murder case of the boy Aditya on 17-7-2010, the accused was arrested within four days.

In Madurai city, after Manikandan was killed on 21-8-2010 and 9 kilograms of gold, 1,274 grams of silver and ₹12,000 were robbed, the offenders were arrested on 24-8-2010 — within 42 hours, as the source puts it — and stolen property worth ₹1 crore 60 lakh was recovered.

On 25-10-2010, while about 11 kilograms of gold jewellery was being taken from Villupuram district to Tiruvannamalai, a vehicle was intercepted and all the jewellery was robbed. Because checks were conducted across Tamil Nadu, the letter says, the offenders were arrested in Tindivanam within two hours and 8 kilograms of gold jewellery was immediately recovered. It presents this as an example of swift police action.

To monitor groups engaged in kidnapping, highway robbery, economic offences, `katta panchayat` and other crimes — **(Organized crime syndicates)** in the source English — and to prevent such offences, the police created a separate intelligence wing this year called the **(Organized Crime Intelligence Unit)**. The letter says this has helped prevent many major crimes.

It states that 2,220 hardened offenders involved in illegal activities and crimes were detained under the Goondas preventive-detention law in 2009 and 1,808 had been detained so far in 2010.

The letter then turns to incidents it says occurred when Jayalalithaa was Chief Minister: the murder of Congress leader Guru Gnanasekaran at Mayiladuthurai; the murder of two relatives of former Minister Subbulakshmi Jagadeesan; three people shot dead during a robbery at a Vellore jewellery shop; a college student at Mayiladuthurai raped and murdered; Dalit People’s Front leader Balu murdered in Chennai before his son’s eyes; worker Kathirvelu beaten to death at Cheyyar; cinema stunt actor Sasi alias Sasikumar hacked to death; Dindigul finance-company owner’s wife Poongothai and daughter murdered by having their throats cut; a youth named Prakash hacked to death near Ambattur; two people including a priest murdered after attackers entered a temple at Karapakkam; DMK member Mani, a petrol-bunk owner near Tirupattur, shot dead; Viji Waris, son of a sub-inspector from Thirumullaivoyal, hacked to death in the middle of the road; Ambattur college student Damodaran murdered; daily-wage worker Venkadapathi stabbed to death at Chetpet, Chennai; advocate Sudhakaran murdered at Kodaikanal; and Kovalan of the Viduthalai Chiruthaigal Katchi hacked to death at Kunampet.

The letter says that when this chain of murders occurred in 2003 alone under Jayalalithaa’s government, *Dinamani* treated them merely as news and did not have the resolve or courage to editorialise that law and order had deteriorated and that such a situation did not befit good governance. Why, it asks, does the paper now write at such length? Will neutral observers not think about the reason?

It next recalls the murder on 3-9-2004 of Sankararaman, manager of the Varadaraja Perumal Temple at Kanchipuram, and says that Jayendra, Vijayendra, his brother Raghu, monastery manager Sundaresa Iyer, Appu, Kathiravan, Ravi Subramanian and others — 25 people in all — were arrested, while the Goondas Act was invoked against eleven including Sundaresa Iyer and Appu. The letter asks whether *Dinamani* then did more than watch, and whether it tried to display its claimed rectitude through editorials.

It further recalls an explosion at the RSS office in Chetpet, Chennai, on 8-8-1993 in which eleven people died; an explosion at the Hindu Munnani office in Chennai on 14-4-1995 in which a person called “Bible” Shanmugam died; and an explosion at film director Mani Ratnam’s house on 10-7-1995. It says that during the five years of Jayalalithaa’s government from 1991 to 1996 there were 333 bomb incidents, in which 135 people died and 394 were injured. Did *Dinamani*, it asks, sharpen its pen then to say law and order had deteriorated?

The letter also refers to former MLA M.K. Balan being kidnapped and killed, and to the brother of DMK MP K.P. Ramalingam, a police inspector in Erode district, suddenly going missing. Did *Dinamani*, it asks, try to point to such incidents as proof that Jayalalithaa’s police had failed?

It continues with the stoning attack on Union Minister P. Chidambaram on 16-8-1991; attacks on AIADMK MLAs Ramesh and P. Sokkalingam; the acid-bottle attack on former IAS officer Chandralekha on 19-5-1992; the attack on the hotel in which former Chief Election Commissioner Seshan was staying on 2-12-1994; the country-bomb attack on Puthiya Tamilagam leader Dr Krishnasamy on 26-7-2004; and the deaths of more than forty innocent people in a crowd crush at Chennai M.G.R. Nagar in December 2005 while seeking relief, which the letter attributes to the approach followed by the Jayalalithaa government. Did *Dinamani*, it asks, then open its mouth about law and order?

At least hereafter, the letter concludes, neutral newspapers should think about publishing constructive news useful to the people; political news without attachment to any party and without likes or dislikes; news that does not assist degradation of civilisation or culture; and work that encourages goodwill and good actions.

Should not those who claim neutrality be free from ill judgment?

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
4-11-2010
