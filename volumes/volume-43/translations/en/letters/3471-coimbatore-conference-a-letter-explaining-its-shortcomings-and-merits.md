# 3471. Coimbatore Conference: A Letter Explaining Its Shortcomings and Merits!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This letter reviews preparations for the World Classical Tamil Conference at Coimbatore. It praises the organising work and the planned public sessions, research halls, exhibition and procession, but sharply objects to DMK flags, banners and portraits filling the city. The source insists that the conference is a Tamil-language event conducted by the Tamil Nadu Government and must not appear even in the smallest measure to be a party conference.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3471](../../../chapters/3471-kovai-maanaadu-kuraiyum-niraiyum-vilakkum-kaditham.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 323](../../../pages/page-323.md)–[PDF 325](../../../pages/page-325.md)  
**Date:** 26 May 2010

**Udanpirappē,**

I stayed in Coimbatore city for two days in order to discuss and personally inspect the arrangements for the World Classical Tamil Conference that is to be held there for five days, from 23 to 27 June. Twenty-one committees have been formed under the leadership of our Honourable Ministers so that the many kinds of conference work may be classified, organised and carried out neatly. At the consultative meeting I held with those committees, we discussed in detail everything that has so far been done for the conference and everything that still has to be done.

All the views expressed by the ministers and officials at that meeting showed the speed and quality with which the conference arrangements are progressing. It gave me great encouragement to see the ministers and officials involving themselves fully and working with initiative to make the conference a success.

I also inspected in detail the CODISSIA complex, where arrangements are under way for the public-stage programmes, research sessions and exhibition. The enormous pavilion for the public-stage programme is taking shape beautifully. Twenty-eight research halls, named after ancient poets, are being prepared in a manner that will astonish and win the praise of Tamil scholars coming from many countries.

The exhibition arrangements too are taking shape not merely as a display, but as a presentation of ideas that will remain in memory for all time. Scenes of ancient Tamil culture and civilisation, and displays from Tamil literature, are to be included. It gives me great happiness that these are being prepared so that today’s Tamil youth may learn about the way ancient Tamils lived and gain feeling and inspiration from it.

Likewise, the preparations for the procession — including the chariots that will move in it — are taking form in a way that will attract those who see them.

Amid all this happiness and enthusiasm, I also had to see one sight in Coimbatore that caused distress to my mind. From Coimbatore airport to the V.O.C. Grounds, and in many other parts of the city as well, DMK flags, banners and “banners” bearing the images of office-bearers serving at every level of the party were everywhere in such abundance as to become tiresome. I heard that Deputy Chief Minister Thambi M.K. Stalin, who had reached Coimbatore the previous day, had noticed and condemned the same sight.

The World Classical Tamil Conference that is to be held is a conference conducted by the Tamil Nadu Government for the Tamil language and for Tamils in a non-party manner; it must not give even the appearance, to the extent of the tip of a sesame seed, of being a party conference. I have been keeping a vigilant eye on this. What surprises me is that the DMK Udanpirappugal of Coimbatore district somehow still seem not to have understood it.

Instead of party flags and banners, if — without causing any inconvenience to the public — banners containing scenes from Sangam literature, banners bearing the emblem of the World Classical Tamil Conference, portraits of ancient kings and paintings of poets are arranged beautifully, that would give fulfilment not only to me but also to the many lakhs of people and visitors from abroad who will come to the conference; it would also explain the greatness, history and culture of the Tamil language. I earnestly request everyone to understand this and act accordingly.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**26-5-2010**

## Letter-specific notes

1. `உலகத் தமிழ்ச் செம்மொழி மாநாடு` follows the locked **World Classical Tamil Conference** treatment.
2. The source’s contrast between a government-organised Tamil conference and a party conference is preserved without softening; `எள்ளின் முனையளவும்` is rendered as “to the extent of the tip of a sesame seed.”
3. `28 ஆய்வரங்கங்கள்`, the 21 organising committees, dates 23–27 June and the CODISSIA/V.O.C. locations are retained in source order.
4. No canonical Tamil change was required.
