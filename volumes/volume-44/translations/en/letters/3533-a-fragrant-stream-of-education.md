# 3533. A Fragrant Stream of Education!

**Udanpirappē,**

Ayyan Valluvar said:

> As water springs forth from a sandy well in proportion to the depth one digs, knowledge springs forth in people in proportion to what they learn.

In keeping with that thought, the achievements of the DMK government in the field of education, which gives knowledge, are by no means few. Education had functioned as one department under one minister. It was the DMK government that reorganised it into two departments and appointed two ministers for education. From this alone, one can understand the importance the DMK government has given to this field.

## Separate ministry for Higher Education

Until 2006, education functioned under a single ministry. In **2006**, it was divided into the **School Education Department** and the **Higher Education Department**, and a separate ministry was newly created for Higher Education.

## Abolition of the entrance examination

The common entrance examination for professional courses, which had been an unnecessary and costly burden on students from rural and poor families, was abolished through legislation enacted on **5-3-2007**.

When the entrance examination existed, only **24,670 rural students** entered engineering colleges through the single-window system in **2006**. After abolition, the number of rural students entering engineering colleges rose to:

- **34,069** in 2007;
- **50,589** in 2008;
- **54,073** in 2009; and
- **76,073** in 2010.

In 2006, when the entrance examination existed, only **11,799 students who had studied +2 through Tamil medium** entered engineering colleges through the single-window system. After abolition, their number rose to:

- **19,966** in 2007;
- **34,039** in 2008;
- **35,434** in 2009; and
- **54,460** in 2010.

Thus, students educated through Tamil medium benefited in large numbers.

From the **2008–2009 academic year**, a student attending engineering-admission counselling and one accompanying person are given a **50 per cent concession on two-way travel fare**.

From **2008–2009**, seats reserved in engineering colleges for the children of ex-servicemen were increased from **81 to 150**, and seats for sportspersons from **42 to 100**.

From the **2008–2009 academic year**, on the recommendation of Tamil Nadu Open University, **10,000 students each year** studying in community colleges are given assistance of **₹1,000 each**.

To help students in government arts and science colleges obtain employment, a **Student Placement Centre** has been started in the Directorate of Collegiate Education.

## Abolition of tuition fees

Until 2006, undergraduate students in government and government-aided arts and science colleges had to pay tuition fees ranging from **₹500 to ₹750**. The DMK government abolished this fee from **2007–2008**. From 2007–2008 through 2010–2011, **14 lakh 59 thousand 990 students** benefited.

From the **2010–2011 academic year**, tuition fees were completely abolished for postgraduate students in government arts and science colleges; **12,784 postgraduate students** in government arts colleges benefited.

The benefit of the **E. V. R. Nagammaiyar Memorial Free Degree Scheme for Poor Women** in government and government-aided colleges was extended from the undergraduate level to postgraduate study from **2007–2008**. The parental annual-income ceiling for receiving this concession was raised from **₹24,000 to ₹50,000** from **2006–2007**, and to **₹1 lakh** from the current year.

The **₹2,500 tuition fee** paid by students in government polytechnic colleges until 2007 was abolished from **2008–2009**; by 2010–2011, **63,192 students** had benefited.

Differently abled students studying in government and government-aided arts and science colleges, engineering colleges, polytechnics and universities functioning under the Higher Education Department have been fully exempted from tuition fees.

## Chief Minister’s Public Relief Fund assistance

**627 poor engineering-college students** received **₹25,000 each**, totalling **₹1 crore 56 lakh 75 thousand**, from the Tamil Nadu Chief Minister’s Public Relief Fund.

## Internet facilities in colleges

To improve employment opportunities for government-college students, **Internet with broadband facilities** were introduced in government colleges with assistance from the Union Government’s Ministry of Information Technology. **681 computers** were supplied to **27 government colleges** at a cost of **₹2 crore 98 lakh 66 thousand**.

## A pioneering scheme in India

From the **2007–2008 academic year**, in the evening section of Presidency College, Chennai, degree courses in **B.Com. (General)** and **B.C.A. (Computer Applications)** were introduced for students with hearing impairment, with **Tamil as the medium of instruction**, described by the source as a pioneering scheme in India.

## Self-financing courses converted into regular courses

All self-financing courses in government engineering colleges and government arts and science colleges were converted into regular courses, reducing fees and enabling poor students to continue higher education.

## Reduction in tuition fees

The tuition fee paid by students in government engineering colleges, which had been **₹12,550** until 2006, was reduced to **₹7,550** from **2006–2007**.

## Tuition-fee concession for first-generation graduate students

Described by the source as the first such measure in India, from the **2010–2011 academic year**, tuition fees were abolished for first-generation students from families with no graduates who entered professional degree courses through counselling.

At an additional cost of **₹168 crore** to the government, the beneficiaries were:

- **67,405 first-year engineering students**; and
- **10,750 students entering directly into the second year through `(Lateral Entry)`**.

## Reduction of minimum marks for engineering admission

From **2008–2009**, to enable more students to enter engineering colleges, the DMK government reduced the minimum admission marks for all categories:

- General category: **55 per cent → 50 per cent**;
- Backward Christian, Backward Muslim and other Backward Classes: **50 per cent → 45 per cent**;
- Most Backward Classes and Denotified Communities: **45 per cent → 40 per cent**;
- Scheduled Castes and hill Scheduled Tribes: from the earlier requirement that a pass was sufficient to **35 per cent**, as printed in the source.

The letter says this increased engineering-college enrolment.

## Free textbooks for government polytechnic students

From **2007–2008**, all students in government polytechnic colleges were given free textbooks in mathematics, chemistry and physics. By **2010–2011**, **33,250 students** had benefited.

## Free bus travel pass

Until 2006, students in government arts and science colleges and government polytechnic colleges received bus passes only at concessional rates. From **2007–2008**, the DMK government introduced free bus travel passes for all such students. Up to **2009–2010**, **4,35,356 students** benefited.

## Counselling-based admission in government arts and science colleges

Until 2006, a student seeking admission to a college had to submit a separate application for each course. From **2007–2008**, each college was treated as a single unit: one application could list the courses a student wanted, and the preferred course would be allotted through counselling according to merit. The letter says parents and students have benefited greatly.

From **2007–2008**, a State-level single-window system was also introduced for admission to all colleges of education, with students admitted through counselling.

## Shift System

From **2006–2007**, the `(Shift System)` was introduced in all government arts and science colleges, enabling approximately **more than 11,000 additional students each year** to enrol and benefit.

From **2009–2010**, the shift system was also introduced in government polytechnic colleges, enabling **more than 3,000 additional students each year** to enrol.

## Increase in government-quota seats in self-financing engineering colleges

Before 2006, government-quota seats constituted **30 per cent** in minority engineering colleges and **50 per cent** in non-minority colleges. Following government negotiations with self-financing engineering-college managements, from **2006–2007** the government quota was fixed at **50 per cent in minority colleges** and **65 per cent in non-minority colleges**, benefiting poor and rural students in greater numbers.

The source gives the following enrolment sequence:

- **2005–2006:** 7,112 students in minority engineering colleges (30 per cent) and 16,621 in non-minority colleges (50 per cent);
- **2006–2007:** 10,283 in minority colleges (50 per cent) and 23,287 in non-minority colleges (65 per cent);
- **2007–2008:** 14,010 in minority colleges and 31,989 in non-minority colleges;
- **2008–2009:** 16,526 in minority colleges and 48,149 in non-minority colleges;
- **2009–2010:** 16,851 in minority colleges and 50,219 in non-minority colleges;
- **2010–2011:** 20,203 in minority colleges and 71,629 in non-minority colleges.

## Increase in seats in government engineering colleges

In government engineering colleges and government-funded constituent engineering colleges of universities, student strength increased from **4,917 in 2005–2006** to **12,142 in 2010–2011**, following the engineering colleges started by the DMK government.

## Engineering degree courses through Tamil medium

At the special moment of the World Classical Tamil Conference held in Coimbatore in **June 2010**, the government ordered that students in civil engineering and mechanical engineering in Anna Universities of Technology should be able to study through their mother tongue, Tamil — described by the source as a first among Indian States. Of the **1,400 seats** fixed for these courses in **2010–2011**, **1,378 students** joined Tamil-medium engineering classes.

Until **2009–2010**, university question papers for engineering degrees were supplied only in English and answers had to be written only in English. From **2010–2011**, universities were instructed to provide question papers in English or Tamil and to permit answers to be written in English or Tamil. The letter says this especially benefits students who studied Class 12 through Tamil medium and then entered engineering colleges.

## Tamil made compulsory as a subject in undergraduate study

To end the position in which students could obtain B.A. and B.Sc. degrees without studying Tamil as a subject, an order was implemented in **2008–2009** requiring Tamil to be taken as a compulsory subject in undergraduate courses and passed.

## Classical Tamil syllabus in colleges

At the World Classical Tamil Conference in Coimbatore, it was announced that the topic **“Classical Tamil”** would be included in Tamil Nadu school, college and university syllabi from the following academic year. Accordingly, the Classical Tamil syllabus is to be implemented in colleges under universities:

- from **2010–2011, second semester**, in all courses except B.Com. and B.C.A.; and
- from **2011–2012**, in B.Com. courses as well.

## New engineering colleges / universities

Before Independence, only **2 government engineering colleges** functioned. Up to **2005–2006**, there were only **6**. During the five-year AIADMK government from 2001 to 2006, the source says not even one new government engineering college was started.

After the DMK government assumed office in 2006, **12 new engineering colleges** were started over five years at **Tindivanam, Villupuram, Panruti, Thirukkuvalai, Ramanathapuram, Ariyalur, Arani, Pattukkottai, Dindigul, Thoothukudi, Nagercoil and Kanchipuram** as constituent colleges of Anna Universities. There are now **18 government engineering colleges**.

## Restoration of the earlier status of government colleges

The previous government had converted all government arts and science colleges and colleges of education into constituent colleges of the relevant universities. The expected benefits were not achieved. To correct the resulting confusion, this government decided to restore their earlier status. The relevant university laws were amended so that all those constituent colleges would once again function as full-fledged government colleges.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
6 March 2011
