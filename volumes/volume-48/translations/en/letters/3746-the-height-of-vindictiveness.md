# 3746. The Height of Vindictiveness!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

At the Mupperum Vizha held in Erode on 15 September 2007, I participated and, at the end of my speech, moved a resolution which was adopted at that meeting. The resolution was as follows:

> “The Sethu Samudram project has remained a dream for nearly a hundred years and one of the distinctive major demands of the Dravidian movement. At a time when Prime Minister Manmohan Singh and Mrs Sonia Gandhi have extended great support and come forward to implement the project, a few communal organisations are standing in the way, seeking to wreck it and prevent the economic prosperity of the people of the South by raising the religious threat of ‘Rama’s Bridge’. The Union Government must not be frightened by this or give room to it. From Erode, the soil where Thanthai Periyar—who fought to uproot religious superstition—was born, through this great gathering we raise our voice on behalf of the people of the South.”

That was a resolution adopted at a DMK public meeting in 2007. But there was another resolution opposing it. Do you know what it said?

> “If the Union Government, in the name of the Sethu Samudram project, attempts to damage Rama’s Bridge, the BJP will oppose it strongly. The BJP will not accept any action that damages Rama’s Bridge. Without Rama’s Bridge there is no Ramayana, and India’s identity too will remain incomplete.”

That resolution was adopted at the BJP National Council meeting on 3 March 2013.

Further, at that meeting the resolution stated that “the BJP appreciates the cooperation being extended by the AIADMK Government of Tamil Nadu to have Rama’s Bridge declared a monument of national importance.”

For how many years has the Sethu Samudram project been the dream of the people of Tamil Nadu?

When the United Progressive Alliance government was formed at the Centre and the DMK too became part of it, we made determined efforts to ensure that the Sethu Samudram project was included at least in the Common Minimum Programme. Accordingly, the Common Minimum Programme presented on 27 May 2004 by the United Progressive Alliance stated clearly: “Long Pending Schemes in specific States that have national significance, like the Sethu Samuthiram Project....will be completed expeditiously”.

The project was also included in the UPA Government’s Budget. At the grand inauguration of the Sethu Samudram project held in Madurai, Prime Minister Dr Manmohan Singh, UPA Chairperson Mrs Sonia Gandhi, I, and the leaders of the other allied parties participated. We were not the only ones who had raised our voices for the Sethu Samudram project at that time.

In the AIADMK manifesto for the 2004 parliamentary election it was stated:

> “The country knows well that the DMK, MDMK and PMK, despite holding ministerial responsibility in the Union Government for five years, failed to take the necessary steps to implement the Sethu Samudram project, which will play an important role in Tamil Nadu’s economic growth and in the country’s overall industrial development. The AIADMK will urge the Union Government that is to be formed to allocate sufficient funds immediately and complete this project within a specified time-frame.”

If the Tamil Nadu government led by Jayalalithaa, which declared in its election manifesto that it wanted the Sethu Samudram project, has now filed a petition in the Supreme Court asking for an order to abandon that very project, is this not precisely what is called a double role and a deceitful drama?

Every study conducted from 1955 until today has established, on technical grounds, that this project is feasible. There has not been even the slightest doubt about that.

For fresh studies, Arun Jaitley, a BJP leader who was Minister for Surface Transport under the National Democratic Alliance government, gave approval on 9 March 2001. The study responsibility was entrusted in October 2001 to NEERI.

The Nagpur-based environmental research institute NEERI examined the matter and concluded that the best route—causing no harm and enabling travel at lower cost—was Alignment No. 6 through the “Adam’s Bridge” area.

Finally, on 31 March 2005, after following its prescribed procedures and carrying out detailed studies, the Union Ministry of Environment and Forests granted environmental clearance for the project.

The various studies and investigations carried out so far have made it clear that there is no substantive scientific evidence of any human-made construction in this “Adam’s Bridge” sea area.

It is precisely because there is no firm evidence that no successive government—including the BJP-led National Democratic Alliance government—declared the project area a monument of national importance or took any steps to do so.

Minister Arun Jaitley on 9 March 2001, Minister S. Thirunavukkarasar on 25 October 2002, Minister V.V. Goyal on 29 October 2002, and before them Minister Shatrughan Sinha, all accepted Route No. 6 through “Adam’s Bridge”.

Yet in the petition filed yesterday, 29 April 2013, by the Tamil Nadu government in the Supreme Court, the AIADMK government now opposes the project by asking that “the Sethu Samudram project should be reconsidered; Rama’s Bridge should be declared a national ancient monument; and the Union Government should be directed not to damage or break Rama’s Bridge.” But what did Jayalalithaa say on pages 83–84 of the 2001 election manifesto?

> “There are at present no continuous routes suitable for shipping around the Indian peninsula. If ships travelling by sea from west to east are to proceed, they have to sail around Sri Lanka. The solution to this is the Sethu Samudram project. Under this project, the principal objective is to remove the sandbanks and rocks in the Adam’s Bridge area between Rameswaram and Talaimannar in Sri Lanka that obstruct shipping, deepen the area and form a canal.”

Jayalalithaa, who then described it in the election manifesto as “Adam’s Bridge” and as “sandbanks”, suddenly calls it “Rama’s Bridge” today. How? Why?

Pandit Nehru, in the source’s account of letters written to Indira Gandhi, is quoted as saying that the Ramayana “is not an epic” in the historical sense, but an imaginative story that magnified a conflict between Dravidians and Aryans and incorporated many tales incompatible with reason.

The source also reproduces this statement by Rajaji, described there as a deeply devout Rama worshipper: “The Ramayana is not history or biography. It is a part of Hindu mythology”.

On 10 May 1986, when M.G.R. was Chief Minister, a resolution was adopted in the Legislative Assembly urging the Union Government to implement the Sethu Samudram project.

The Sethu Canal project was inaugurated in July 2005 and work began. After ₹829 crore had already been spent, is it right for Jayalalithaa—who remained silent until so much money had been spent—to suddenly think of blocking the project now?

If the Sethu project is completed, industry and trade in Tamil Nadu will expand; the country’s foreign-exchange earnings will increase; the distance and time travelled by ships will be greatly reduced; cargo-handling capacity at ports in Tamil Nadu and neighbouring States will increase; a new minor port will emerge at Rameswaram or Mandapam; the development of fishing harbours under the Sethu Canal project will expand maritime trade and improve the economy and standard of living of fishermen; the canal will help fishermen travel between the Gulf of Mannar and Palk Bay; and the transshipment of Indian cargo at ports in other countries, including Sri Lanka, will be avoided.

The country’s coastal security will be strengthened. The project will continuously provide direct and indirect employment to more than 50,000 people, and the Sethu Samudram project will generate eight crore person-days of employment every year.

Large numbers of direct and indirect jobs will be created. The development of Rameswaram and Nagapattinam ports will provide direct employment to about 10,000 people. Use of the Sethu Samudram Canal will reduce the shipping distance between India’s eastern and western coasts by about 424 miles.

This is expected to reduce voyage time by about thirty hours. There will be substantial savings in fuel and foreign exchange, and substantial savings in freight charges.

Because voyage time will be reduced, ships will be able to make additional voyages and handle additional cargo. One of the ports on the east coast of South India will be developed as a hub port for container traffic. Thoothukudi port will grow substantially. Naval and Coast Guard vessels will be able to move very quickly from the eastern region to the western region, thereby greatly strengthening India’s defence capability. Infrastructure development will increase in Ramanathapuram, Nagapattinam and Thoothukudi districts.

The Sethu Samudram project, once inaugurated, was progressing rapidly because of the good efforts of Thambi T.R. Baalu, who was then the minister concerned. The truth is that, because of the fear that completion of the project would bring great credit to the DMK, the AIADMK government—out of sheer vindictiveness—has filed a petition in the Supreme Court to conduct a closing ceremony for a project on which ₹829 crore has already been spent!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**1 May 2013**

அக்டோபரில் ‘நீரி’ நிறுவனத்திடம் ஒப்படைக்கப்பட்டது.

நாகபுரியைத் தலைமையிடமாகக் கொண்டு இயங்கும் (NEERI)
‘நீரி’ என்ற சுற்றுச்சூழல் ஆய்வு மையம் அதனை ஆய்வு செய்து,
எந்தக் கேடும் வராத- குறைந்த செலவில் பயணம் செய்ய ஏற்ற
- சிறந்த தடம் ஆறாம் வழித்தடமான, “ஆதாம் பாலம்” பகுதியில்
அமைவதுதான் என்று கூறிவிட்டது.

இறுதியாக 31.3.2005 அன்று மத்திய சுற்றுச்சூழல்
மற்றும் வனங்கள் துறை அமைச்சகம் தனது முறையான
நடைமுறைகளுக்கும், விரிவான ஆய்வுகளுக்கும் பிறகு,
இத்திட்டத்திற்கான சுற்றுச்சூழல் ஒப்புதலை வழங்கியது.

இதுவரை மேற்கொள்ளப்பட்ட பல்வேறு ஆய்வுகள்,
விசாரணைகள் ஆகியவற்றின் மூலம் இந்த “ஆதாம் பாலம்” கடல்
பகுதியில் மனிதனால் உருவாக்கப்பட்ட எந்த ஒரு கட்டுமான
அமைப்பும் இருப்பதற்கான உருப்படியான அறிவியல் பூர்வமான
ஆதாரம் எதுவும் இல்லை என்பது தெளிவாகியிருக்கிறது.
