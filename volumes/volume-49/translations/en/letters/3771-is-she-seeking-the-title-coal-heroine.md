# 3771. Is She Seeking the Title “Coal Heroine”?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

In the statement Chief Minister Jayalalithaa has issued about the sale of shares in the Neyveli Lignite Corporation, she has accused me of forgetting நிலக்“கரி”—coal—once “கனி”மொழி, Kanimozhi, obtained office. She has attempted a play on the words “Kani” and “Kari”. The statement has come from Kodanadu, has it not? Naturally, it would be like that! Is Jayalalithaa's allegation that the DMK has forgotten the Neyveli Lignite Corporation and its workers true?

I wrote detailed Udanpirappu letters in *Murasoli* about the Neyveli Lignite Corporation on 8 May 2012 and 25 May 2012. Again this year, I wrote an Udanpirappu letter specifically about the sale of NLC shares on 26 May 2013, and only last week, on 22 June 2013, I issued a detailed statement. Yet the Tamil Nadu Chief Minister has released a statement accusing me of having forgotten coal. I believe the following events alone will make clear to everyone whether I am someone who forgot Neyveli.

When 13,000 contract workers of the Neyveli Lignite Corporation began a strike on 21 April 2012 in support of ten demands, I wrote in *Murasoli* on 8 May 2012:

> “Even if the AIADMK Government in the State remains unconcerned about this issue, the Union Government and the Minister responsible for this department must immediately contact the Neyveli management, invite the protesting workers for talks and come forward to find an amicable settlement. The Prime Minister must take the necessary steps for this.”

On 3 May 2012, when members belonging to the Communist parties raised the Neyveli workers' issue in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly, the Government replied: “The NLC workers' protest does not fall within the jurisdiction of the Tamil Nadu Government; therefore, we cannot intervene.” Who forgot நிலக்“கரி”—coal? Was it I, or the AIADMK Government?

The problem of contract workers in the Neyveli Lignite Corporation has recurred once every two years. Through the efforts of the Labour Progressive Federation, the issue of the contract workers who had served from the time the corporation was established was resolved to some extent in 1994.

In 2008, while the DMK was in power, a protest took place in Neyveli. The DMK Government did not say that the State Government had no concern in the matter. As Chief Minister, I intervened directly. On 16 June 2008, a tripartite agreement was reached between the trade union and the management before the Chief Labour Commissioner in Delhi. The strike was withdrawn and the workers returned to duty. Had I forgotten coal, would that tripartite agreement have come about?

In 2010, the contract workers in Neyveli again went on strike. I was then Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu, and on 15 October 2010 I wrote to Prime Minister Manmohan Singh. I later called the management for talks, secured a monthly increase of ₹1,040 and appealed to the workers to accept it. Only the leaders of the Labour Progressive Federation accepted it. The other workers continued their strike. I immediately had representatives of all the trade unions brought to the Secretariat on 26 October 2010 and spoke to them. I persuaded the management as well and secured several further concessions.

While the DMK was in office, the Union Government announced on 22 June 2006 its decision to sell ten per cent of the shares in Neyveli. The very next morning, 23 June, the DMK trade union—the Labour Progressive Federation—held a consultative meeting with the CITU, AITUC, INTUC and the Officers' Association. The meeting decided to begin an indefinite strike on 4 July 2006.

On 25 June 2006, I wrote to the Prime Minister about the issue:

> “The Union Government must not disregard the opposition that has arisen in Tamil Nadu to the proposed disinvestment of ten per cent in Neyveli. Whatever reason may be given for departing from the already agreed Common Minimum Programme, it will be regarded as weakening the commitment to abide by the policy originally accepted. I therefore request the Union Government to reconsider this decision.”

The Common Minimum Programme adopted by the United Progressive Alliance in 2004 had agreed that profitable public-sector enterprises would not be privatised and that government companies regarded as “Navaratnas” would remain under government control.

When no immediate reply came to my letter and the strike began in Neyveli on the 4th, journalists questioned me on 6 July 2006. I said:

> “When the Union Government is in a position where it cannot fulfil the workers' demand in the Neyveli issue, we are considering whether the DMK should remain part of that Government and bear responsibility for the decision taken by it. I have asked the DMK ministers in Delhi to convey this to the Prime Minister.”

Accordingly, Thambi Dayanidhi Maran, who was then a Union Minister, immediately met the Prime Minister and conveyed my view. That very night, the Union Government announced that its decision to sell ten per cent of Neyveli's shares had been kept in abeyance. The workers then withdrew their strike.

But in the statement issued today, the Chief Minister has covered up all these facts and distorted the history by saying: “A massive protest was held on behalf of the AIADMK condemning the Union Government's decision. Following that, the Union Government abandoned its decision.”

Last year, when the Union Government again attempted to sell shares in the Neyveli Lignite Corporation, I immediately issued a statement saying:

> “The Union Government must not again become involved in an attempt to sell shares in NLC, which has Navaratna status. It is an unnecessary act that will drag Tamils, particularly the Neyveli workers, into a needless dispute. I wish to warn the Union Government not to invite yet another protest by engaging in such an act.”

Then, in the Udanpirappu letter I wrote on 26 May 2013, I stated that I understood the Union Government was planning to sell another five per cent of its holdings in the Neyveli Lignite Corporation, that such an effort was undesirable, and that alternative measures should be examined instead of selling NLC shares. With generosity, I also cited what Jayalalithaa had written in her letter to the Prime Minister on 23 May 2013. That is my practice.

But to issue a statement accusing me of forgetting நிலக்“கரி”—coal—for the sake of “கனி”மொழி—Kanimozhi—is Madam's generosity, her practice, her masquerade!

As soon as news came that the Union Cabinet meeting held on 21 June 2013 had decided to sell five per cent of NLC's shares to private parties, all the workers in the corporation were deeply shocked and began preparations for protests to express their strong condemnation and opposition. Pointing this out, I issued a statement saying:

> “It appears that the Union Government will receive only ₹466 crore by selling its five per cent holding. This is not a large amount for the Union Government. The Government may say that the decision is based on a requirement imposed by the Securities and Exchange Board of India—SEBI—that at least ten per cent of the shares in public-sector enterprises must be held by the public.
>
> “But the Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu has already pointed out in her letter to the Union Government that the listing rules could be amended to exempt the Neyveli Lignite Corporation from this requirement, or NLC itself could be removed from the list of companies whose shares are to be sold. The Union Government should take that into consideration. It should also remember that it had previously attempted to sell NLC shares but deferred the decision after accepting Tamil Nadu's request.
>
> “On behalf of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam, I strongly urge the Union Government to cancel the present decision taken by the Union Cabinet.”

If I had forgotten coal, why would I have needed to cite Jayalalithaa's own views and argue with the Union Government?

It was on the same day that I issued my statement—22 June 2013—that the Tamil Nadu Chief Minister too wrote to the Prime Minister. Only after that did a journalist suggest that, instead of selling the five per cent shareholding to private parties, it could be sold to the State Government. The Chief Minister adopted that suggestion as the position of the Tamil Nadu Government and communicated it to the Prime Minister on 25 June 2013.

The trade unions belonging to all parties in Neyveli held detailed discussions on the issue and decided to begin a strike on 3 July. They formally informed the management.

But *The Hindu* of 1 July 2013 carried a report under the headline, “CM's offer to be examined as per SEBI rules, says Jaiswal.” It reported:

> “Asked for reaction to Ms Jayalalithaa's proposal, Mr. Jaiswal told *The Hindu* on Saturday evening that the question whether any State PSUs could pick up stake in a Central undertaking should also be examined.”

In other words, when Union Minister Shri Jaiswal was asked about Jayalalithaa's proposal, he replied only that the question of State public-sector enterprises purchasing shares in a Central undertaking would also have to be examined.

Meanwhile, Union Minister of State for Commerce and Industry Sudarsana Natchiappan, speaking to the press in Chennai on 29 June 2013, said:

> “The Tamil Nadu Government has asked that five per cent of NLC's shares be sold to it. We are prepared to give them. We will even ask that an additional five per cent be given. Our property must remain with us. We will be happy if the Tamil Nadu Government buys the NLC shares.”

That report too appeared in the newspapers:

> “At a press meet in Chennai on Saturday, Mr. Jaiswal's colleague and Union Minister of State for Industry and Commerce E. M. Sudarsana Natchiappan said the Centre would happily sell five per cent stake to the State Government.” — *The Hindu*, 1 July 2013.

It is in this situation that Chief Minister Jayalalithaa, in a statement reported as having been issued from Kodanadu, has said that Union Coal Minister Sriprakash Jaiswal has announced that the sale of Neyveli shares is certain, and has declared that the AIADMK will hold a protest on 2 July.

Is Jayalalithaa's announced protest truly for Neyveli coal? Or is it intended to counter the DMK's protest on the 8th demanding the early implementation of the Sethusamudram Project?

When the Union Government is considering and appears ready to accept Tamil Nadu's request to transfer the NLC shares to State public-sector enterprises; when all the workers at Neyveli have jointly announced a strike from 3 July; why has Jayalalithaa announced a separate AIADMK protest here? Is it to deceive the public and divert attention? Or is it so that she may later celebrate a victory, claim that the Union Government agreed to reconsider the sale of NLC shares only because “we held a protest”, and receive the title “Coal Heroine”?

It is only because a Chief Minister herself wrote all this wordplay about “Kani” and “Kari” that I have been compelled to refer to her as the “Coal Heroine”. What else can one do?

Whatever form she assumes becomes the spectacle; whatever she sees becomes the scene!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**2 July 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. The source repeatedly plays on **கனி** (*Kani*, from Kanimozhi) and **கரி** (*kari*, coal). The English retains the Tamil forms where the pun is central and uses “Coal Heroine” for `நிலக்கரி நாயகி`.
2. **NLC** refers to the Neyveli Lignite Corporation, now NLC India Limited.
3. **Navaratna** is a Government of India classification granted to selected high-performing public-sector enterprises.
4. The closing Tamil idiom `கொண்டதே கோலம், கண்டதே காட்சி` conveys wilful theatricality: whatever pose is taken becomes the show, and whatever is seen is proclaimed as the spectacle.
