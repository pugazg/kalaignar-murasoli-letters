# 3740. Prevent Before It Comes—or Suffer After It Arrives?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 22 April 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

At a time when a drinking-water shortage is tormenting Chennai and all of Tamil Nadu, the AIADMK government, instead of taking the measures necessary to confront the shortage, is spending its time announcing drinking-water schemes under Rule 110.

What is this? Since the AIADMK came to office, Tamil Nadu has faced power cuts on a scale never seen before. People in the villages have been unable to live normally. This year farmers did not cultivate; even those who did cultivate did not obtain a harvest. The reason is that this government could not secure Cauvery water for cultivation! And now the drinking-water shortage has arrived. Mettur has dried up to a degree unprecedented in its history. Newspapers have published photographs showing the Cauvery looking like a mere stream. Yet the Chief Minister conceals all this and has announced in the Assembly “a new ₹1,000-crore drinking-water project to desalinate seawater at Nemmeli.”

Do you know the history of the Nemmeli seawater desalination project inaugurated by the Chief Minister on 22 February 2013?

The Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam had continuously urged the Union Government to establish near Chennai a seawater desalination project to address the city's drinking-water problem. The United Progressive Alliance government formed at the Centre in 2004 immediately accepted this DMK demand.

As a result, while presenting the 2004–2005 Budget in Parliament, the Union Finance Minister announced:

> “₹1,000 crore will be allocated for a seawater desalination project with a production capacity of 300 million litres a day for Chennai and the towns along the Coromandel coast.”

In accordance with that announcement, repeated requests were made to the then AIADMK government to obtain Union financial assistance and implement the seawater desalination project in Chennai. Yet the AIADMK government remained in office until the end without implementing it.

After the DMK government was formed in Tamil Nadu in 2006, in fulfilment of its election-manifesto promise, a project report was prepared to implement the Nemmeli seawater desalination project. On 27 January 2008, I personally handed it to the Union Finance Minister and requested immediate approval and financial assistance.

The Union Ministry of Urban Development recommended an allocation of ₹908 crore 28 lakh for the project. In March 2009, the Union Government released ₹300 crore to the Tamil Nadu government as the first instalment of funding.

Work to implement the project was launched on 23 February 2010 by Thambi Stalin, then Deputy Chief Minister. A target was set to complete all the work by December 2011 and the work was accelerated. The work that should have been completed by December 2011 was delayed after the AIADMK government assumed office, and the inauguration has taken place only now, a year later.

In the statement she read, Chief Minister Jayalalithaa also referred to steps taken during her earlier period in office to establish at Minjur a seawater desalination project with a capacity of 100 million litres. Here is a note about that project.

During AIADMK rule, the Chennai Metropolitan Water Supply and Sewerage Board invited tenders to desalinate seawater at Minjur and supply drinking water to Chennai. Only two companies sought the tender. One was owned by the Russian government and was described as one of the world's important companies of international standard: “Messrs Khrunichev State Research and Production Space Center.” The other bidder was “Messrs Marson Company”, a French company. Because the second company did not submit a proper tender, only the Russian company remained as the final bidder.

When a team representing the Russian company came from Russia to Chennai and met the Tamil Nadu minister in charge of the department and officials, the source says they were orally told directly that an exemption would be given from paying the earnest-money deposit for this tender and that they would not be pressed to pay it.

In September 2003, the Russian company learnt that it would not receive the tender because it had not paid the tender earnest-money deposit and because it was the sole bidder. As far as there being only one tender was concerned, there was no prohibition either in the tender documents or in Tamil Nadu's law governing the award of tenders that prevented the tender from being awarded. Citing all this, the company asked the Water Board to reconsider its petition. Receiving no reply, the company wrote directly to the Tamil Nadu Chief Minister on 15 September 2003 and again on 19 September 2003, asking her to intervene and help resolve the matter.

In its letter to the Chief Minister dated 15 September 2003, the company wrote that there appeared to be another real reason for the Water Board's erroneous decision. It alleged that some people claiming to represent the Water Board's Managing Director and senior ministers had repeatedly contacted both the company and Moscow by telephone and demanded ₹50 crore as a bribe; that those people were waiting outside the hall when the tenders were opened and again pressed their demand; and that the company was prepared to provide details if the State Government conducted a full inquiry.

After these letters, without giving the company any reply and without any reason, advertisements suddenly appeared on 22 September 2003 inviting fresh tenders for the same work. The affected Russian company filed a writ petition in the Madras High Court. Accepting the petition, the High Court restrained the authorities from calling for a fresh tender for the work. During the hearing, counsel for the Russian company stated that they possessed tape-recorded evidence of the alleged demand for a ₹50-crore bribe. The hearing continued.

After the DMK government was formed in 2006, it examined the project and held negotiations with the company. Thereafter, the previously fixed price was reduced to some extent and refixed. On 25 February 2007, Local Administration Minister M.K. Stalin laid the foundation stone. Although various technical reasons caused delay in establishing the project, M.K. Stalin, then both minister for the department and Deputy Chief Minister, launched the trial run of the well-constructed project on 2 June 2010.

In the present situation, the Krishna water flowing to Poondi reservoir has stopped. Water levels in the reservoirs that supply Chennai are continually declining. Mettur Dam too has dried up. Without worrying about any of this, the AIADMK government keeps announcing schemes under Rule 110. When will those schemes come into operation? When will the people of Tamil Nadu drink water from them? What does it matter how things go? What if Tamil Nadu's people get drinking water; what if they do not? Can a mere paper announcement quench the Tamil people's thirst for water?

Madurai, Dindigul, Theni, Virudhunagar, Sivaganga, Ramanathapuram, Tirunelveli, Thoothukudi and Kanyakumari districts are suffering under severe drought. Tanks and wells have dried up. With water needs increasing and power cuts continuing, people are facing great hardship. Protests for drinking water have begun in many parts of Tamil Nadu. People can be seen wandering day and night with empty pots, unable to sleep. Tamil Nadu had been looking to States such as Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh and Kerala for water; social activists accuse the Tamil Nadu government of allowing the State's water situation to deteriorate by failing to maintain cordial relations with those States and instead acting with hostility.

Rivers such as the Cauvery, Vaigai and Thamirabarani have turned into stretches of sand. When there was no rain, the government had not shown concern for carefully storing the water received when rain did fall. As river water levels decline, freshwater in coastal districts has become saline and unusable for drinking.

As far as Chennai is concerned, the combined capacity of the Red Hills, Cholavaram, Chembarambakkam and Poondi reservoirs serving the city's drinking-water needs is 11.057 TMC. But about ten feet of mud and silt has accumulated in these reservoirs, preventing them from storing water to their full capacity. Likewise, because the Krishna canal from Kandaleru has not been properly maintained, incoming water is wasted and stagnates in fields and canals in Andhra Pradesh.

The drinking-water department supplies 831 million litres of water a day to Chennai's people. Of this, 766 million litres is supplied for domestic use, 28 million litres to bulk consumers, and 37 million litres to industries. Apart from this, private tanker-lorry owners bring water from wells and borewells in Tiruvallur and Kanchipuram districts and sell it to large hotels in the city. It is said that 2,000 loads a day are presently being sold in the city and that the number will increase as time goes on.

In villages they say, “letting go of the thumbai and then trying to catch the tail.” But this government has let time pass without even trying to catch the tail. The AIADMK government, which should have foreseen that Tamil Nadu would face a drinking-water shortage and protected the people before it arrived, failed to act before the shortage came. Even after the shortage has now arrived, instead of taking immediate steps to protect the people, it publishes photographs of officials being called in for discussions, reads one or two Rule 110 announcements in the Assembly every day, and spends its time on anti-democratic actions and publicity tactics.

This government denied farmers water for irrigation and left them in tears; imposed power cuts of sixteen and eighteen hours a day, shut factories and left workers suffering; and has now become responsible for a drinking-water shortage that sends women wandering with empty pots. What more cruelties are still to come?

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**22 April 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. Allegations concerning the Minjur tender and an alleged ₹50-crore bribe demand remain attributed to the Russian company's letters, its counsel and the author's account; the translation does not independently establish them as fact.
2. The company names are rendered closely from the audited Tamil: **Messrs Khrunichev State Research and Production Space Center** and **Messrs Marson Company**. They are not externally normalised.
3. The village saying `தும்பை விட்டுவிட்டு வாலைப் பிடிப்பது` is translated close to its source image; in context it criticises action taken only after the crucial opportunity to prevent a problem has been lost.
4. `TMC`, `lakh`, `crore` and **Rule 110** follow the Volume 48 style lock.

கிராமங்களிலே “தும்பை விட்டுவிட்டு வாலைப் பிடிப்பது”
என்பார்கள். ஆனால் இந்த ஆட்சியினர் அவ்வாறு வாலைப்
பிடிக்கவும் முயற்சி செய்யாமல் காலத்தைக் கடத்திவிட்டார்கள்.
தமிழகத்தில் குடிநீர்ப் பஞ்சம் ஏற்படும் என்பதைப் பற்றி
முன்கூட்டியே கணித்து, வருமுன் காத்திட வேண்டிய அ.தி.மு.க.
அரசு, தண்ணீர்ப் பஞ்சம் வருவதற்குமுன் காத்திடத் தவறி
விட்டது. தண்ணீர்ப் பஞ்சம் தற்போது வந்ததற்குப் பின்னரும்,
அதிலிருந்து மக்களைக் காத்திடும் உடனடி நடவடிக்கைகளை
மேற்கொள்ளாமல், அதிகாரிகளை அழைத்துப் பேசுவதாகப்
புகைப்படங்களை வெளியிட்டு, அன்றாடம் ஒன்றிரண்டு 110
அறிவிப்புகளைப் பேரவையிலே படித்துவிட்டு, ஜனநாயக விரோத
நடவடிக்கைகளிலும், விளம்பர உத்திகளிலும் காலத்தைக் கடத்திக்
கொண்டிருக்கிறது.
பாசனத்திற்குத் தண்ணீர் பெற்றுத்தராமல் விவ சாயிகளைக்
கண்ணீரில் தவிக்கவிட்ட இந்த ஆட்சியினர், நாள் ஒன்றுக்கு
16 மணி நேரம், 18 மணி நேரம் என்று மின்வெட்டு செய்து
