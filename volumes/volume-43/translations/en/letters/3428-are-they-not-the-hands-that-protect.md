# 3428. Are They Not the Hands That Protect?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This pilot translation carries Kalaignar’s argument into clear English without recasting it as literary or academic prose. The audited canonical Tamil is the immediate source. Political emphasis, rhetoric, figures, lists, historical claims, colloquial satire, and source-supplied English are preserved in source order. `Udanpirappē` is retained as Kalaignar’s characteristic address. The complete audited Tamil is reproduced below and remains authoritative.

**Udanpirappē,**

As far as the Police Department is concerned, my view has never been one thing when I am in the opposition and another when I am in the ruling party. Generally in our country, some politicians — and, to go further, even some political parties — still have an attitude in which the very word “police” evokes resentment and dislike, and they take a particular pleasure in condemning them. But in my case it has been otherwise. Whether we were in government or in opposition, I want, at the very opening of this letter, to place before people like you a concise account of the views I have held about the police.

When I was Deputy Leader of the Opposition in 1962, this is what I said in the Legislative Assembly on 12-7-1962 during the debate on the Police Department grant:

> “In heavy rain and scorching sun, at midnight, amid murderers and thieves, without concern for their own lives and treating death as insignificant, those men of sacrifice keep fighting in the line of duty. The Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam is duty-bound to appreciate them.”

I am confident that even if others forget this view of mine, my friends in the police will not.

When the DMK was in opposition, I wrote a lullaby on behalf of the police for the play *Udhayasuriyan*, concerning the standard of living of police personnel. The song had been banned, and I sang it in the Assembly itself so that it could be entered in the record:

> “Ārārō! Ārirārō! Handsome child, sleep and grow!
> On a salary of five and ten, with what little remains,
> little one, sleep and grow!
>
> To the poor fellow who goes away for police duty,
> poverty itself, born as his son — sleep and grow!
> To the poor man who sets out carrying a gun,
> while the creditor blocks his way asking for old dues,
> sleep without seeing those scenes!
>
> So that ministers who rule the country may live without hardship,
> son of the father who wears himself down to the bone,
> little lamp of the family crawling inside a sparrow’s-nest of a house!
> Like a water-lily, sleep without fear of mosquito bites!
> Wearing a khaki uniform,
> putting on a red cap,
> to the brave man who guards the country —
>
> if four more like you
> should be born......
> he would need a saffron robe......
> and a begging bowl too....
> my dear child, sleep and grow!”

**Udanpirappē,** it was as a continuation of this outlook that, when I became Chief Minister in 1969, I wanted to bring some peace into the lives of police personnel. I therefore constituted a separate Police Commission in Tamil Nadu — something that had not been created from the days of British rule until then — obtained its recommendations and implemented them. Whenever the DMK returned to power after that, two more Police Commissions were also constituted.

It is because of these recommendations that the basic pay of a constable, which in 1967 was ₹70 with a dearness allowance of ₹59, has now risen to a basic pay of ₹7,300 and a total of ₹10,300 including dearness allowance.

The Tamil Nadu Police, which is celebrating its 150th year in public service, continues to do everything within its capacity to ensure that law and order in the State do not suffer any major impairment. There can be no two opinions about that. If we take just the past year — 2009 — festivals of various religions, demonstrations and protests conducted by different parties, and visits of important dignitaries have all concluded peacefully.

Everyone will accept that the Police Department was a major reason why the Parliamentary election was conducted without incidents disturbing law and order. It was the Tamil Nadu Police who foiled the attempt of extremists to establish a training camp in the Kodaikanal forest area. By arresting 22 persons at the right time and detaining 12 of them under the National Security Act, the police prevented violent attempts by separatist forces to disrupt religious harmony.

The police arrested Naxalite extremist Tamilvaanan, founder of the People’s War Group and a person linked to four murders of landlords who had been absconding for the past 37 years, on 12.04.2009. They arrested Sivalingam alias Siva of Jolarpet, a Naxalite extremist who had killed a police inspector, two constables and three Naxalites in 1980, escaped from custody and remained absconding for 38 years, together with his associate Sami alias Saminathan, on 10.05.2009. They also arrested Naxalite extremist N. K. Gopal, who had been sentenced to life imprisonment, gone out on parole and remained absconding for 22 years, on 24.6.2009.

Over the past three years, the police have registered 70 cases against those who tried to smuggle explosives, medicines and essential commodities from the State to Sri Lanka, those who engaged in illegal activities within Indian territorial waters, and those who assisted them. A total of 299 persons were arrested; 45 of them were placed in preventive detention under the National Security Act.

From 07.01.2009 to 09.01.2009, during the conference held in connection with the “Seventh Pravasi Bharatiya Divas,” attended by the President of India, the Prime Minister of India and prominent figures from around the world, the police made extensive security arrangements despite threats from extremist organisations and enabled the conference to conclude peacefully.

Because offenders have been quickly identified and arrested, criminal activity in our State has been brought under control. In 2006, 89 per cent of crime cases were detected and stolen property worth about ₹34.8 crore was recovered. Likewise, in 2007, 87 per cent of cases were detected and 83 per cent of the stolen property, worth about ₹48.7 crore, was recovered. In 2008, 82 per cent of cases were detected and 76 per cent of the property, worth about ₹47.61 crore, was recovered. Up to September this year, 72 per cent of cases have been detected and 66 per cent of the property, worth about ₹39.7 crore, has been recovered.

Some notable cases detected this year are as follows.

In Chennai, in the case in which a priest at the Chandraprabhu Jain Temple on Mint Street was murdered and 22 kilograms of gold jewellery worth about ₹1.2 crore was looted, six robbers were arrested and 9.2 kilograms of gold jewellery have so far been recovered.

In Chennai housebreaking cases, Kuppan of Tondiarpet, who had been absconding for 15 years, along with Mannar of Saidapet and Maniarasu of Villupuram, were arrested. Stolen property worth about ₹21 lakh, including 88 sovereigns of gold jewellery, was recovered from them.

In the case in which Sureshkumar, a jewellery trader of Flower Bazaar, Sowcarpet, Chennai, was murdered and 2.15 kilograms of gold jewellery worth ₹20 lakh was robbed from him, the accused Nemichand was arrested and the stolen property was recovered from him.

In Sowcarpet, Chennai, in the case in which persons on a motorcycle robbed ₹50 lakh from Rathan R. Jaini, six accused were arrested and ₹44.5 lakh was recovered.

In Chennai city, Suhail Ahmed, an accused involved in theft and housebreaking cases at various places over the past four years, was arrested. In 25 cases, 301 sovereigns of gold jewellery, 14 kilograms of silver and diamond jewellery worth about ₹50 lakh were recovered.

Chennai Police arrested the offender known as Malmannan, who had been absconding for the past 10 years, and recovered gold and diamond jewellery weighing about 325 sovereigns and 13 kilograms of silver, together worth about ₹40 lakh, connected with 45 cases.

In Anna Nagar, Chennai, in the robbery of Ashok Malpani and his wife, Mahesh of Bihar was arrested. Gold and diamond jewellery worth ₹60 lakh and ₹10 lakh in cash were recovered.

From Broad Line Computers System in T. Nagar, Chennai, Saravanan, owner of Wescos Properties and Developers, had fraudulently obtained 250 computers and 300 laptops worth ₹1.27 crore. Within 24 hours of the complaint, the accused was arrested and the articles were recovered.

Chennai Suburban Police arrested Srikanth and six offenders involved in 52 housebreaking and theft cases and recovered 300 sovereigns of jewellery worth ₹34 lakh.

Chennai Suburban Police arrested Sridhar and five persons involved in vehicle-theft cases and recovered four cars, four two-wheelers and gold jewellery worth ₹20 lakh.

Chennai Suburban Police arrested Uma Maheswari and three persons and recovered 24 antique panchaloha idols worth about ₹1 crore.

On 20-5-2009, Chennai Suburban Police arrested Suresh alias Market Suresh, who was involved in 200 cases and had already been detained five times under the Goondas Act, along with three associates, and recovered 70 sovereigns of gold jewellery and silver ornaments.

In Cuddalore district, in the case in which a lorry coming from Tiruchi was intercepted near Veppur and ₹3.72 crore in cash was robbed, Irulandi and 12 others were arrested and ₹2.85 crore in cash was recovered from them.

Vellore District Police arrested Moorthy, who was involved in 13 vehicle-theft cases, and recovered eight mini-lorries and five cars worth about ₹1 crore. They also arrested Babu and two accused involved in 25 housebreaking and theft cases and recovered gold jewellery and silver articles worth about ₹30 lakh.

In Thanjavur district, in the case in which the accountant of Rama Vilas Oil Company was abducted and ₹56,60,000 in cash was robbed from him, the accused Viji and 18 others were arrested and about ₹36 lakh was recovered from them.

In Salem city, 27 accused connected with 11 cases involving kidnapping for money were arrested. ₹9.33 lakh in cash and 47 sovereigns of gold jewellery were recovered. Four four-wheelers and three two-wheelers were also seized from them. Eighteen persons involved in these incidents were placed in preventive detention.

In Coimbatore district, Andipatti Muthu, involved in 15 waylaying cases, was arrested and 100 sovereigns of jewellery worth ₹10 lakh were recovered. Yogesh, Saravanan, Guna and Manohar, involved in four housebreaking cases, were arrested and about 75 sovereigns of gold jewellery worth ₹11 lakh and wristwatches worth about ₹4 lakh were recovered.

Coimbatore City Police arrested all four accused in the case in which Mohanraj and four persons connected with Victory Equities and Forex India Limited defrauded 256 investors of ₹1.53 crore. The entire amount was recovered and returned to the investors.

Tiruppur Police arrested Shankar of Salem, an accused involved in the murder of woman constable Jayamani, seven murders for gain, two other murder cases, one robbery case and 16 theft cases, and recovered the stolen articles.

Tiruchi District Police seized lottery tickets worth ₹5.5 crore being transported on the Howrah Express and arrested two accused, including Sagayaraj.

Karur District Police arrested eight offenders who had stolen 32,522 electric detonators and 4,532 ordinary detonators from an explosives shop and recovered the explosives.

In Virudhunagar district, Karuppasamy, Raja and others involved in robbery, waylaying and theft cases were arrested, and property worth about ₹6 lakh, including 60 sovereigns of gold jewellery and cash, was recovered.

Ramanathapuram District Police arrested the well-known offenders Easwaran and seven accused involved in murder for gain, nine housebreaking cases and five waylaying cases, and recovered 193 sovereigns of gold jewellery worth ₹30 lakh, ₹45 thousand in cash and 50 mobile phones.

Railway Police arrested Rajan Ram and Amarnath Jaiswal of Ranchi and recovered more than 300 sovereigns of gold jewellery connected with 34 train-robbery cases.

The State Crime Branch-CID efficiently investigated and arrested the accused in the case in which Dr. Bhaskaran of Dindigul was abducted and murdered, and also in the murder case of Vijayan, a relative of former Chief Minister M. G. R.

Further, in the gold-coin fraud case involving Gold Quest International Pvt. Ltd., that department conducted an effective investigation, arrested 25 accused, froze company properties worth about ₹192 crore and filed a charge sheet against the accused.

The Economic Offences Wing arrested Ramesh of Pattukkottai and Senthil of Valangaiman and recovered the ancient emerald lingam, said to be worth crores, that had been stolen eight months earlier from the Arulmigu Marundeeswarar–Periya Nayagi Amman Temple at Tiruvarur/Thiruthuraipoondi.

Thus, within a three-year period, police personnel have risked their lives, fought anti-social elements and thieves, and achieved these successes. We must appreciate the accomplishments that have resulted from them.

For protecting society, maintaining law and order, and safeguarding human lives and property, the challenges faced by the police are not merely countless; they are beyond calculation. The advanced technologies used in criminal activity make the problems still more complex. Therefore, in general, we ought to appreciate the duty performed by the police. To take isolated incidents occurring here and there and use them to blame and find fault with the entire Police Department — suddenly placing a black mark upon their long life of public service and trying to create hesitation in their continuing to perform good work — is not something to be welcomed. It would itself become harmful to society. Because I consider it my duty to point this out, I have had to write this long letter.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**1-11-2009**
