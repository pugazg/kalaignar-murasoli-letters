# 3413. Who Benefits from the Insurance Scheme?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This letter responds to a 20 August 2009 article in *Theekkathir* about two medical-insurance schemes. It presents figures, eligibility rules, procurement details and policy comparisons as the author’s source claims. English terms printed in the Tamil source — including **supplementary facility**, **Star Health and Allied Insurance Company** and **co insurers** — are retained. The complete audited Tamil is reproduced below and remains authoritative.

**Source pages:** [PDF 318](../../../pages/page-318.md)–[PDF 324](../../../pages/page-324.md)  
**Date:** 22 August 2009

**Udanpirappē,**

On 20-8-2009, *Theekkathir*, the official newspaper of the Communist Party of India (Marxist), published an article under the heading “Kalaignar Insurance Scheme: Who Benefits?”

The article, the author says, contained many incorrect views and pieces of information because it had not properly understood the two medical-insurance schemes being implemented by the government: the Government Employees’ Medical Insurance Scheme and the Kalaignar Insurance Scheme benefiting poor and simple people.

Both schemes, he says, had received a large measure of welcome from the public and from government employees.

He first explains the Government Employees’ Medical Insurance Scheme, which was implemented earlier, and then turns to the recently introduced Kalaignar Insurance Scheme.

## Government Employees’ Insurance Scheme

The author says that, from 2008–2009, Tamil Nadu became the first State in India to implement a medical-insurance scheme covering all government employees.

He says the scheme was introduced after accepting the demand of government employees, that he himself announced it at their conference, and that it was then put into practice.

Under the scheme, all categories of employees — government employees, employees of Boards, public-sector undertakings and local bodies, nutrition-scheme workers and others — benefit.

He says they have welcomed the scheme wholeheartedly and expressed satisfaction.

As of the date of the letter, more than ten lakh employees had joined the scheme and were benefiting from it.

So far, the source says, **47,283 employees** had received various forms of advanced treatment in private hospitals without paying any charge.

The insurance company had spent **₹154.22 crore** for these treatments.

But the insurance premium paid by the government to the company up to that point was only **₹121.7 crore**.

Thus, the author argues, government employees had already received benefits worth **₹32.52 crore more than the premium paid**, and therefore the claim that the scheme was not functioning satisfactorily was contrary to the facts.

Before the scheme was introduced, the letter says, on average only about **3,000 government employees each year** obtained reimbursement from the government for medical expenses, amounting to about **₹11 crore**.

Earlier, if a government employee unexpectedly required an advanced procedure such as heart surgery, the employee had to pay money upfront to a private hospital, undergo treatment and later seek reimbursement from the government.

That situation had changed, the author says. Under the Government Employees’ Medical Insurance Scheme, an employee could now show the scheme membership card and receive immediate advanced treatment at a private hospital at the appropriate time without paying any fee.

## Kalaignar Insurance Scheme for Life-Saving Advanced Treatment

The current DMK government, the source says, has continued to give proper priority to public health and medical services.

During 2005–2006, the previous government spent **₹1,487.11 crore** on the health department.

After the present government assumed office, the source lists:

- **2006–2007:** ₹1,662.17 crore;
- **2007–2008:** ₹2,047.45 crore;
- **2008–2009 revised estimate:** ₹2,762.33 crore;
- **2009–2010 Budget:** ₹3,391.23 crore, described as the highest allocation.

The author says these allocations show the importance the Tamil Nadu government gives to improving and operating government hospitals.

Because all primary health centres in Tamil Nadu were functioning well, he says, the number of deliveries in private hospitals had fallen while deliveries in government primary health centres had increased threefold.

The source states that deliveries in primary health centres rose from **81,190 in 2005–2006** to **2,59,104 in 2008–2009**, and says the Union Government had repeatedly praised this achievement.

All taluk government hospitals and district government hospitals in Tamil Nadu were also being upgraded under the Tamil Nadu Health Systems Project at a cost of **₹607 crore**.

Under that project, new buildings and equipment needed by government hospitals were being provided, and additional doctor and nurse posts had been created.

After the government came to office, orders were also issued to establish new Government Medical College Hospitals at district headquarters in **Villupuram, Dharmapuri, Tiruvarur, Sivaganga and Perambalur**, and work was under way.

Therefore, the author rejects as untrue the *Theekkathir* article’s claim that the government was abandoning its basic duty to ensure public health through public hospitals.

Even while government-hospital facilities were being continuously improved, the government recognised that there were difficulties in immediately providing every patient who needed advanced treatment — such as heart surgery or kidney transplantation — with such treatment in government hospitals.

To change the situation in which poor people needing life-saving advanced treatment had to wait relying only on government hospitals, and to enable ordinary people, like those with greater means, to obtain such treatment immediately in an appropriate private hospital, the Tamil Nadu government announced what the source calls this revolutionary medical-insurance scheme in the Governor’s Address of 2009.

The source notes that a similar scheme was being implemented in neighbouring Andhra Pradesh under the name **Rajiv Aarogyasri Scheme**.

For ordinary people, treatment for many common illnesses would continue to be available in government hospitals, while the medical-insurance scheme created an additional **supplementary facility**.

The author therefore says there had been no change in the priority given to government hospitals.

The company implementing the scheme, **Star Health and Allied Insurance Company**, was selected through an open tender.

Public-sector insurance companies including **United India Insurance Company** and **Oriental Insurance Company** participate with it as **co insurers**.

The source says Star Health was recognised by the Union Government’s insurance regulatory authority and that the same company was implementing the Andhra Pradesh government’s medical-insurance scheme.

Government public-sector insurers that participated in the tender had, according to the letter, quoted an annual premium of about **₹1,000 per family**.

If the government had accepted that rate as quoted, the source says the expenditure would have been **₹1,000 crore a year**, or **₹4,000 crore over four years**.

Instead, officials held several rounds of negotiations with the lowest bidder, resulting in an annual premium of only **₹517 crore**.

The *Theekkathir* writer had stated:

> “The insurance company has published a list of 358 hospitals. Not even one government hospital appears, in the insurance company’s view, to be an eligible hospital.”

The author responds that, as already stated in the Government Order for the scheme, government hospitals capable of providing advanced treatment would also be included.

A list of government hospitals with the necessary infrastructure was to be finalised and announced by **10.9.2009**.

Thus, he says, the scheme made provision for treatment not only in private hospitals but also in government hospitals.

*Theekkathir* also asked:

> “If the Union Government medical-insurance scheme were implemented throughout Tamil Nadu, workers would benefit greatly. What is Kalaignar’s ulterior motive in avoiding it?”

The author says the article itself admitted that treatment under the Union Government scheme was available only up to **₹30,000**.

Under the Kalaignar Insurance Scheme, he says, advanced treatment was available up to **₹1 lakh**.

Therefore, he argues, it was obvious that the Kalaignar scheme provided greater benefit than the Union Government scheme, and no further explanation was necessary.

In addition, the Union Government scheme, the source says, was available only to families below the poverty line, and even the number of beneficiary families in each district was limited.

Under the Kalaignar Insurance Scheme, by contrast, the source says that the families of more than **78 lakh agricultural labourers and small and marginal farmers**, and more than **35 lakh unorganised workers**, could benefit without any income ceiling.

It also provided eligibility to all families with annual income below **₹72,000**.

Within three weeks of the scheme beginning operation, approval had been given for **298 people** to undergo surgery.

The source says Star Health had by then provided **₹1 crore 72 lakh** in insurance payments for those procedures.

Under the Union Government scheme, beneficiaries had to pay **₹30**, the letter says.

Under the Kalaignar Insurance Scheme, beneficiaries did not have to pay any fee, registration fee or identity-card fee.

All benefits of the scheme were available to beneficiaries free of charge.

Taken together, the author concludes, it was clear that the Kalaignar Insurance Scheme provided many times greater benefit, and therefore there was no ulterior motive in not using the Union Government scheme.

Despite all these facts, he says, *Theekkathir* wrote its article with the intention of finding fault with every government scheme.

In reality, he says, government employees and the general public had welcomed the government’s schemes.

When the “Kalaignar Insurance Scheme for Life-Saving Advanced Treatment” was inaugurated on 23.7.2009 by the Union Minister for Health and Family Welfare, the minister, according to the source, said:

> “The scheme has been designed so that it can reach the doorstep of every ordinary person.”

He also praised Tamil Nadu by saying:

> “In providing medical services, Tamil Nadu stands as a model for all the other States.”

Yet, the author says, the CPI(M) newspaper alone published an article asking who benefited from the scheme.

The answer, he concludes, is:

the public benefit; government employees benefit; why, even those who wrote the article benefit.

**That is the truth! The truth!**

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**22-8-2009**

## உயிர்காக்கும் உயர் சிகிச்சைக்கான
## கலைஞர் காப்பீட்டுத் திட்டம்:

<!-- Source PDF page 320 -->

தற்போது நடைபெற்று வரும் கழக அரசு பொது சுகாதாரம் மற்றும் மருத்துவ துறைகளுக்கு தொடர்ந்து உரிய முன்னுரிமை அளித்து வருகிறது.

2005-2006 ஆம் ஆண்டில் மக்கள் நல்வாழ்வுத் துறைக்கு முந்தைய ஆட்சியில் ரூபாய் 1487.11 கோடி செலவிடப்பட்டது.

இதற்கு மாறாக இந்த அரசு பொறுப்பேற்ற பிறகு 2006-2007ஆம் ஆண்டில் ரூபாய் 1662.17 கோடியும், 2007-2008ஆம் ஆண்டில் ரூபாய் 2047.45 கோடியும் செலவிட்டு 2008-2009ஆம் ஆண்டில் திருத்திய மதிப்பீட்டில் ரூபாய் 2762.33 கோடி ஒதுக்கீடு செய்தும் நடப்பு நிதியாண்டான 2009-2010 வரவு செலவுத் திட்டத்தில் மிக உயர்ந்த ஒதுக்கீடாக ரூபாய் 3391.23 கோடியும் ஒதுக்கீடு செய்யப்பட்டுள்ளது.

இந்த ஒதுக்கீடுகள் எந்த அளவிற்கு அரசு மருத்துவ மனைகளின் மேம்பாட்டிற்கும், செயல்பாட்டிற்கும் தமிழக அரசு முக்கியத்துவம் அளித்து வருகிறது என்பதற்கு எடுத்துக்காட்டாக விளங்குகின்றன.

தமிழகத்தில் உள்ள அனைத்து ஆரம்ப சுகாதார நிலையங்களும் சிறப்பாக செயல்பட்டு வருவதன் காரணமாக; தனியார் மருத்துவ மனைகளில் நடைபெறும் மகப்பேறுகள் குறைந்து; அரசு ஆரம்ப சுகாதார நிலையங்களில் நடைபெறும் மகப்பேறுகளின் எண்ணிக்கை மும்மடங்காக உயர்ந்துள்ளது.

பயனாளிகள் அனைவருக்கும் இலவசமாக கிடைக்கப் பெறுகிறது. ஆகமொத்தத்தில் “கலைஞர் காப்பீட்டுத் திட்டம்” பன்மடங்கு பயனளிக்கும் திட்டம் என்பது தெளிவு. எனவே மத்திய அரசின் திட்டத்தை தவிர்ப்பதில் எந்தவித உள்நோக்கமும் இல்லை என்பது சுட்டிக் காட்டப்படுகிறது.

உண்மை விவரங்கள் இவ்வளவிருக்க - தீக்கதிர் நாளேடு - அரசுத் திட்டங்கள் அத்தனையையும் குறை கூற வேண்டுமென்ற எண்ணத்தோடு கட்டுரை தீட்டியுள்ளது. அரசின் திட்டங்களை உண்மையில் அரசு அலுவலர்களும், பொது மக்களும் பெரிதும் வரவேற்கிறார்கள். 23.7.2009 அன்று “உயிர் காக்கும் உயர் சிகிச்சைக்கான கலைஞர் காப்பீட்டுத் திட்டம்”, மத்திய மக்கள் நல்வாழ்வுத் துறை அமைச்சரால் தொடங்கி வைக்கப்பட்டபோது - “ஒவ்வொரு சாமானிய மனிதனின் வீட்டு வாசலுக்கும் சென்றடையத்தக்க வகையில் இத்திட்டம் வடிவமைக்கப்பட்டுள்ளது” என்று கூறியதோடு - “மருத்துவ சேவையை வழங்குவதில் தமிழ்நாடு இதர மாநிலங்களுக்கெல்லாம் முன் மாதிரியாகத் திகழ்கிறது” என்றும் பாராட்டினார். ஆனால் மார்க்சிஸ்ட் கம்யூனிஸ்ட் கட்சியின் நாளேடு மட்டும் இத்திட்டத்தினால் யாருக்கு ஆதாயம் என்று கட்டுரை தீட்டியுள்ளது. இந்தத் திட்டத்தினால் பொது மக்களுக்கு ஆதாயம் - அரசு அலுவலர்களுக்கு ஆதாயம் - ஏன் கட்டுரை தீட்டியவர்களுக்கும் ஆதாயம் என்பது தான் உண்மை! உண்மை!
