# 3568. Is Everything the Victor Says Scripture?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 15 June 2011

**Udanpirappē,**

In the case filed by Jayalalithaa in the Supreme Court seeking the retrieval of **Katchatheevu**, a decision has been taken that the Tamil Nadu Government's Revenue Department should join the case. For that purpose Jayalalithaa brought a resolution in the Legislative Assembly, and it was passed unanimously on **9-6-2011**.

The Tamil Nadu Government's Revenue Department is, after all, a department under the control of the Tamil Nadu Government. Does the State Government itself have to bring a resolution in the Legislative Assembly for that department to join a case? Could the Revenue Department not have joined and argued that case concerning Tamil Nadu's interests even without such a resolution? Would it not have been enough to obtain approval in circulation and issue an order? Lawyers and senior officials familiar with Secretariat procedures are the ones who must answer such questions.

Whatever the procedure, we are happy that a resolution concerning Katchatheevu was brought in the Assembly. But today's Chief Minister Jayalalithaa, who moved the resolution, and opposition members who spoke in support of it unnecessarily began by saying that **“it was handed over to Sri Lanka during Karunanidhi's rule,”** and poured abuse upon me as though I had asked the Union Government to give Katchatheevu to Sri Lanka. Some others even thundered heroically that I alone was the cause of Tamil Nadu's ruin and that I alone was responsible for the Cauvery problem. From all this it is easy to understand that the Katchatheevu resolution itself was brought so that arrows of criticism could be fired at me.

I have explained many times that Katchatheevu was given to Sri Lanka by the Union Government **despite the opposition of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam government**, and that even the rights contained in the **1974 agreement** were taken away in **1976**, when President's Rule was in force in Tamil Nadu.

In the roughly **34 years** from 1976 to this year, the AIADMK has been the ruling party in Tamil Nadu for about **22 years**, while the DMK has ruled for **12 years**. The AIADMK keeps shouting until its throat tears that the DMK “handed over” Katchatheevu, without learning the facts from the beginning. Why, then, did it not retrieve Katchatheevu during the 22 years it ruled?

In her **1991 Independence Day address**, when she was Chief Minister, Jayalalithaa said:

> “Because Katchatheevu, which belongs to our homeland, was handed over to the Sri Lankan government, our fishermen are now unable even to approach the island to dry their nets. Therefore, I wish to state that this government is prepared to argue with the Union Government, and if necessary to struggle, to retrieve Katchatheevu. This was one of the promises stated in our election manifesto.”

She then moved a resolution on the matter in the Assembly on **3-10-1991**, and it was passed unanimously.

But speaking in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly on **20-4-1992**, Jayalalithaa said:

> “We passed a resolution here seeking the retrieval of Katchatheevu and sent it to the Union Government. But retrieving Katchatheevu does not appear to be something that can happen, or be made to happen, quickly.”

Speaking in the Assembly on **9-6-2011**, Jayalalithaa said that when she had been Chief Minister she had undertaken various efforts to retrieve Katchatheevu, but that when the DMK was in office—particularly when I was Chief Minister—no action had been taken to retrieve it.

She herself has described the efforts she made: she says she pressed the Union Government and the Prime Minister, both in person and by letter, but the Union Government took no action. Yet is it true when she says that I took no action?

Not merely the DMK as a party, but the DMK government too repeatedly raised the Katchatheevu issue with the Union Government and never failed to ask that the island be restored. The DMK government also never failed to point out the impairment of fishermen's rights around Katchatheevu and ask that the harm be remedied.

On **18 June 2009**, while the DMK was in power, the AIADMK, Congress, PMK, Marxist Communist Party, Indian Communist Party and Viduthalai Chiruthaigal submitted an adjournment motion seeking a discussion of the Katchatheevu issue. It was permitted by the Speaker, and honourable members **Jayakumar, Gnanasekaran, G.K. Mani, Mahendran, Sivapunniyam and Ravikumar** placed various views before the House. The sum of all those views was that Katchatheevu should once again be returned to India.

In my speech in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly on that occasion, I said that the DMK had neither agreed nor consented to hand over Katchatheevu, and that if all parties gave an assurance that they were ready to support such a move, I was prepared to bring a resolution in the House itself seeking the island's return.

Speaking in the Assembly on **9-6-2011**, Chief Minister Jayalalithaa said that hundreds of fishermen had died because Katchatheevu had been handed over to Sri Lanka. But on an earlier occasion, speaking about the same matter, she had said:

> “Only when fishermen cross the International Boundary Line and fish in the sea near Katchatheevu do they get enough good-quality fish. If they fish within our Indian maritime boundary, they do not get enough fish of the quality they need. Therefore, when they knowingly or unknowingly cross the International Boundary Line on many occasions, the Sri Lankan Navy fires on their boats, seizes all the fish they have caught and burns their boats. Many such incidents have taken place.”

Some speakers have even spoken as though they know everything and claimed that fishermen began going missing only under the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam government. If one looks at the figures: in **1986**, four fishermen who went fishing disappeared; in **1987**, four; in **1988**, eight; in **1993**, five; in **1995**, two; in **2002**, twenty-two; in **2003**, six; in **2004**, four; and in **2007**, twelve.

I do not cite this comparison to say that such incidents occurred only under one particular government and not under another. Rather, we must not forget the dangerous truth revealed by Jayalalithaa's own explanation: some fishermen, believing that better fish are available beyond that boundary line, cross the limit and end up losing their lives.

Some of those who spoke on the issue repeatedly said that the DMK government had handed over Katchatheevu. No such act of handing it over took place by the DMK in **1974**. When the Union Government decided that year to give Katchatheevu to Sri Lanka, the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam government, which was then the State Government of Tamil Nadu, strongly opposed the decision. Nevertheless, the Union Government gave Katchatheevu to Sri Lanka.

Even at the time it was given, the DMK government of Tamil Nadu insisted that the agreement should contain clauses preserving fishermen's rights to fish around Katchatheevu, the right to make pilgrimages there, and the right to dry fishing nets on the island. Because of that insistence, such a clause was included in the agreement.

But later, during the Emergency, when there was no elected government in Tamil Nadu and Governor's Rule was in force, those clauses disappeared from the agreement in a manner whose details were not even known. Ever since, we have continuously argued with the Union Government that at least those clauses should be restored.

The DMK did not consent to Katchatheevu being handed over; nor did it agree to it. I also wish to point out that when this matter came before Parliament, it was the DMK that staged a walkout.

There is one more thing I must say. On **30-9-1994**, Jayalalithaa, then Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu, wrote a letter to the then Prime Minister **Narasimha Rao**. In it she wrote:

> **“The ceding of this tiny island to the Island Nation had been done by the Government of India in the interest of better bilateral relations”**

That is, in the Tamil rendering printed in this source:

> “The Government of India separated and gave this tiny island—Katchatheevu—to the island nation of Sri Lanka so that good relations might prevail between India and Sri Lanka.”

The same explanation continues to be given by Union Ministers even now. Just as Jayalalithaa herself said then, they say that although there are many issues between Sri Lanka and India, Katchatheevu was given in the interest of maintaining good relations between the two countries. Jayalalithaa has forgotten the explanation she herself gave then and now speaks with heroic fury!

Even in her Assembly speech on the 9th, instead of speaking about Katchatheevu itself or about methods for retrieving it, she spent a long time speaking about me and casting blame upon me. Those who understand the issue would have recognised from that alone what details some people are trying to conceal.

Jayalalithaa told the Assembly that I had consented to sacrificing Katchatheevu and then staged an eyewash drama by moving a resolution in the House. The DMK may have lost the election. But the people of Tamil Nadu know well the DMK's devotion to the homeland and my devotion to Tamil. **Not everyone who wins is devoted to Tamil Nadu—and not everyone who loses is without devotion to Tamil Nadu. Nor is everything the victors say “scripture”!**

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**15-6-2011**

விபரங்களையெல்லாம் மறைத்திட முயற்சிக்கிறார்கள் என்ற
உண்மையைப் புரிந்து கொண்டிருப்பார்கள். கச்சத்தீவை காவு
கொடுப்பதற்கு ஒப்புதல் அளித்துவிட்டு, கண்துடைப்பு நாடகமாக
அவையிலே தீர்மானத்தை முன் மொழிந்தவன் நான் என்று
ஜெயலலிதா பேரவையிலே சொல்லியிருக்கிறார். தி.மு. கழகம்
தேர்தலிலே தோற்றிருக்கலாம். ஆனால் தமிழ்நாட்டு மக்கள் தி.மு.
கழகத்தின் தாயகப்பற்றையும் என்னுடைய தமிழ்ப் பற்றையும்
நன்கறிவார்கள். வெற்றி பெற்றவர்கள் எல்லாம் தமிழ்நாட்டின்
மீது பற்றுக் கொண்டவர்களுமல்ல - தோல்வி அடைந்தவர்கள்
எல்லாம் தமிழ்நாட்டுப் பற்றற்றவர்களும் அல்ல. வென்றவர்கள்
சொல்வதெல்லாம் “வேதமும்” அல்ல!
