# 3541. Do Not Forget That I Too Am With You!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 15 March 2011

**Udanpirappē,**

Every day I have been spending my time compiling and writing to you about the achievements of the various departments of the DMK government—and, during the daytime, sitting at Anna Arivalayam from morning until night, and on some days even until midnight, meeting Udanpirappus who come for interviews. Because of this, I have not had time to write to you about many other matters.

When I meet those who come for the interviews, many of them say, “We did not pay money and come here because we want to stand as candidates. We came because this gives us an opportunity to see you from close by.” When I hear that, I can only think: what good fortune have we earned to receive people like these as Udanpirappus!

And this time the crowd for interviews is greater than in any previous election. Earlier, only twenty or twenty-five people would come for an interview from a constituency. This time, when I ask how many people on average have applied from each constituency, the answer is eighty, ninety. If we were to call them one by one and conduct individual interviews, election day itself would arrive before we finished seeing everyone. That is why, on the first day, even though people from seven districts had been scheduled to be seen, we could not finish seeing even those from two districts. From the second day onwards, therefore, we divided the applicants from each constituency into two or three groups and completed the interviews that way.

Each day, the moment I enter Anna Arivalayam to see those who have come for interviews, it is like a festival crowd. How much happiness they feel simply in seeing me! How much eagerness! Whether or not the person seeking an opportunity ultimately gets a chance to contest, how many people have accompanied each applicant!

Some of those who came for the interviews recalled old memories. A few brought photographs taken in those days when I was sitting with Anna, with N.V.N., and with Thalapathi Annan Azhagiri and others. When they showed them to me, all those old memories of sitting with them in those days floated through my mind.

Here is one letter! A letter I signed and sent on 9 August 1968. A friend brought it and showed it to me as though it were a treasure. That letter has been reproduced here exactly as it was—yes, so that you too may see it! It was the letter I wrote when I contested for the General Council in the Thanjavur district DMK election, asking people to vote for me!

We completed the interviews with those who came from all the constituencies, sitting together with the respective district DMK secretaries. In between, our DMK team was also meeting the leaders and delegations of our allied parties to decide which constituencies would be shared by whom, and discussing those matters with me. General Secretary Perasiriyar and I remained in our room and conveyed our suggestions to them.

By now, discussions with almost all our allied parties have been completed and the allocation of constituencies has been virtually finalised. You may see that list in the newspapers today or tomorrow. All the parties have been making mutual accommodations and exchanging constituencies among themselves. Because two or three parties asked for a few particular constituencies, it took several days to complete the discussions. As a result of the continuing negotiations, all those issues—except for one or two constituencies—have now been resolved.

The candidates contesting on behalf of the DMK must next be announced. As I wrote earlier, when an average of eighty people seek to contest from each constituency, only one among them has to be selected to stand on behalf of the DMK. We have to discuss that with the district DMK secretaries. It would therefore be useful for the district secretaries to remain in Chennai today and tomorrow.

The names of the candidates contesting on behalf of the DMK in this election are to be announced by me to journalists at Anna Arivalayam at about 5 p.m. on Wednesday, 16 March 2011. Filing of nomination papers begins on 19 March 2011. On the previous day, 18 March 2011, at around 5 p.m., a consultation with the leaders of our allied parties will also take place at Anna Arivalayam.

I will release the DMK's election manifesto before journalists at Anna Arivalayam on the evening of 19 March 2011. After that, those announced as candidates must file their nomination papers in their respective constituencies. Then the election campaign must begin. There are only a few days in between. Your labour during those days is absolutely essential. Seeing me work to this extent at this age, I know that you will not hesitate to work.

You yourself know that this time you cannot expect me to undertake an election tour on the same scale as I have done on every previous occasion—and that I too cannot do so. Even if my body does not cooperate, I will nevertheless take part in the election campaign as far as I can, with the strength of my resolve. Like me, our General Secretary, Treasurer, leading members of the DMK, the DMK's artists, and the leaders of our allied parties will all take part in the campaign.

Whether the DMK contests in a constituency or one of the parties in our alliance contests there, the work of DMK comrades must be outstanding everywhere. I have been keeping my eyes open even at midnight and writing to you every day in *Murasoli* about the achievements made through the departments of our ministers during these past five years of government. Do not fail to take those achievements to the voters! You know that the DMK government has worked to such an extent that we can courageously go to the people, speak of our achievements and ask for their votes!

Whenever I find time in between, I will write to you. As you move ceaselessly through the election field and work, do not forget that **I too am there with you!**

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**15-03-2011**

தாக்கல் செய்தாக வேண்டும். அதன் பின்னர் தேர்தல் பிரச்சாரம்
தொடங்கப்பட வேண்டும். இடையிலே இருப்பதோ ஒரு சில
நாட்கள்தான். அந்த நாட்களில் உன்னுடைய உழைப்பு மிகவும்
அவசியம். இந்த வயதில் நான் இந்த அளவிற்கு உழைப்பதைப்
பார்க்கும் நீ உழைக்கத் தயங்க மாட்டாய் என்பதை நான் அறிவேன்.

மாட்டாய் - என்னாலும் முடியாது என்பதை நீயே அறிவாய!
எனினும் உடல் ஒத்துழைக்காவிட்டாலும் உள்ள உறுதியோடு
முடிந்த வரை தேர்தல் பிரச்சாரத்தில் ஈடுபடுவேன். என்னைப்
போலவே நமது பொதுச் செயலாளரும், பொருளாளரும், கழக
முன்னோடிகளும், கழகக் கலைஞர்களும், தோழமைக் கட்சிகளின்
தலைவர்களும் தேர்தல் பிரச்சாரத்தில் ஈடுபடுவார்கள்.
தொகுதிகளில் கழகம் போட்டியிட்டாலும், நமது அணியிலே
உள்ள கட்சிகள் போட்டியிட்டாலும் அங்கெல்லாம் கழகத்
தோழர்களின் பணி சிறப்பாக அமைந்திட வேண்டும். கடந்த
ஐந்தாண்டு கால ஆட்சியில் நமது அமைச்சர்களின் துறைகள்
வாயிலாக என்னென்ன சாதனைகளை செய்திருக்கிறோம் என்பதை
நள்ளிரவிலும் கண் விழித்து உனக்காக அன்றாடம் முரசொலியில்
எழுதிக் கொண்டிருக்கின்றேன். அந்தச் சாதனைகளை வாக்காளப்
பெருமக்களிடம் எடுத்துக் கூறத் தவறி விடாதே! நமது
சாதனைகளைச் சொல்லி தைரியமாக மக்களிடம் வாக்கு கேட்கக்
கூடிய அளவிற்கு கழக அரசு பாடுபட்டிருக்கிறது என்பதை நீ
அறிவாய்! இடையிடையே நேரம் கிடைக்கும் போது உனக்கு
எழுதுகிறேன். தேர்தல் களத்தில் நீ சுழன்று பணிபுரியும் போது;
நானும் உன்னுடன்தான் இருப்பேன் என்பதை மறந்து விடாதே!
