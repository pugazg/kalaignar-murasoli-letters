# 3570. This Letter Is but a Drop of Hope!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 19 June 2011

**Udanpirappē,**

As one who has made it a habit to respect and honour the old patriots and martyrs who gave their body, wealth and life itself so that freedom might blossom, the suffering of the people might be removed and their rights might be secured—whether they belonged to the Congress movement or to other parties—and to do everything necessary so that their memory may live forever, I remembered a great man whom most people in Tamil Nadu had forgotten. Even people of opposing parties had praised him as a **“Ramasamy Naicker without a beard.”** Yes, I mean **Omandur Ramasamy Reddiar**, known as **“Omandurar.”**

When the new Legislative Assembly building and the adjoining complex were created, we honoured his memory by naming the area **“Omandurar Complex.”** We planned the Legislative Assembly, Legislative Council and, alongside them, the Secretariat and the offices of various departments there, and also undertook to create a modernised **Kalaivanar Arangam**. In the nearly **86,450-square-metre** building complex known as **Block A**, the Assembly chamber, Speaker's office, Legislative Council chamber, Legislative Library, meeting hall, Governor's office, Chief Minister's office, Chief Minister's Secretariat, Opposition Leader's office, ministers' offices, Cabinet meeting hall, Chief Secretary's office, Legislative Department, Public Department, Finance Department, Home Department, Law Department, and Planning and Development Department—the six principal departments—were beautifully constructed and completed.

At this new Legislative Assembly and Secretariat complex, an iron dome measuring **120 feet in diameter and 100 feet in height** was placed above the **100-foot-high** Assembly building, adding still more grandeur to the massive structure.

The building was roofed with gold-coloured coated aluminium sheets and polished black-granite slabs. A separate reception building was also constructed so that members of the public could enter and leave the complex without obstruction. The sight of the area called the **“Public Plaza”** in English, admired in wonder by those travelling along Walajah Road in Chennai, was beyond description. A long water tank had been created in that Plaza, and the sight of hundreds of colourful fish swimming and playing in it—watched with delight by schoolchildren, men and women visiting the place—gave the building a distinction of its own.

This great building was inaugurated by Prime Minister **Manmohan Singh** in the presence of **Mrs Sonia Gandhi**. The government itself announced that the external construction work had been completed; that, among the interior works, the Assembly chamber had been completed and sessions of the Assembly had already been held there; that finishing work was continuing in other interior areas; and that **all work would be completed by 30 June**.

The Secretariat building in the Omandurar Complex was not built with my personal money. Yet some newspapers write that it was a building constructed at a cost of **₹1,200 crore**. The amount planned for the building that has now been completed was **₹623 crore 99 lakh**. Of that, **₹479 crore 50 lakh** was spent. The estimated cost of the **Block B** building now under construction was **₹279 crore 56 lakh**; the amount spent on it was **₹72 crore 30 lakh**. Therefore, the claim that ₹1,200 crore was spent is wrong! Wrong!

The Secretariat had already been functioning in **Fort St George**. Because the Indian Army is also present there, a condition had existed for a long time in which the public could not easily enter and leave for security reasons. Further, even if some alteration had to be made to the buildings there—why, even if the grass there had to be trimmed and removed—the approval of the Government of India had to be obtained. Since some other government offices functioned in different parts of the city, the government also continuously incurred expenditure of crores of rupees in the name of **rent**. For those reasons, the AIADMK government itself first searched for a new site where all offices could be located together.

It was announced that the Secretariat would be built at **Kotturpuram, Chennai**, and a foundation-laying ceremony was held there with pujas and rituals; Jayalalithaa, who was then Chief Minister, herself participated in it. Later, while Jayalalithaa was in office, a decision was taken to construct a new Secretariat in the **Queen Mary's College** campus on Chennai's Beach Road. The AIADMK government then said that, despite financial constraints, it would build the new Secretariat at the Queen Mary's College campus with a long-term vision. When Jayalalithaa announced it in the Legislative Assembly, it was said: **“The present Secretariat is not suitable. The Queen Mary's College buildings are in a bad condition. They must be demolished. A new Secretariat complex is essential.”**

Further strengthening that argument, Jayalalithaa said at the time that cracks had appeared in many places and leakage had developed in the **Namakkal Kavignar Maligai**, which had been built under the DMK government and inaugurated on **14-8-1975**, and that the Secretariat therefore had to be shifted immediately from the Fort to another place. Keeping all that in mind, the new Secretariat was created under the DMK government. Yet Jayalalithaa has now begun by ignoring that new Secretariat and saying that they will remain only in the old Secretariat and renovate the Namakkal Kavignar Maligai. One must understand that this is contrarianism—the result of hatred and resentment.

Now the new Secretariat has been constructed; the inauguration has taken place; Assembly sessions have been held; **seven Cabinet meetings** have taken place; and the Chief Minister and all the ministers had moved to the new Secretariat. After all that, the new **“Jaya” government**, saying that the Secretariat would continue to function at the old place and that the newly built Secretariat would not be used, has engaged in removing all the nameboards placed there.

It was in the Secretariat in the Omandurar Complex created under the DMK government that the **Governor of Tripura** met me, toured the building, praised it greatly, came even to the Chief Minister's room and spoke highly of it, and presented a souvenir modelled on **India Gate** before leaving.

It was also in the Chief Minister's room in the new Secretariat that a special award from the Tamil Nadu government was presented to **Mahendra Singh Dhoni**, captain of the Indian cricket team, for winning the World Cup. Dhoni and those who came with him, including **Suresh Raina** and **Ashwin**, toured the Secretariat and left after praising it highly.

Not only that: Union Ministers **P. Chidambaram, G.K. Vasan, Narayanasamy** and others; Tamil Nadu Governor **Surjit Singh Barnala**; and leaders belonging to various political parties toured the new Secretariat complex and praised it.

Why, all the Legislative Assembly members belonging to Jayalalithaa's party themselves came for many days and participated in the Assembly chamber in the new Secretariat. But Jayalalithaa, who had not come to that complex even once, ignored the Secretariat and, even after leaders of various political parties in Tamil Nadu urged otherwise, had the **Pavendhar Classical Tamil Research Library** that had been functioning in the old Secretariat moved out overnight, and held the Assembly there again. From this it becomes very clear that the only reason for her hatred could be that the building in the Omandurar Complex was constructed under the DMK government, and that claims about lack of administrative convenience are only meant to conceal the feeling hidden in her mind.

If the reason is merely that it was built under the DMK government, may the **Namakkal Kavignar Maligai**, also built under a DMK government, alone be renovated and used?

Many flyovers in Tamil Nadu were also created during DMK rule. Does not Mrs Jayalalithaa's car travel across those very flyovers?

Did she stop and say, **“Karunanidhi built this flyover; I will not travel on it”**? To go further: when she travelled to Delhi last week, the **Tamil Nadu House** there was hurriedly renovated and she went and stayed there. How did that Tamil Nadu House come into being?

Once, when **Murasoli Maran** and I were talking in Delhi, I pointed out the large **Andhra Bhavan** to Maran and asked whether, when the Andhra Pradesh government had built such an elegant guest house in Delhi, Tamil Nadu too should not have such a place. After that, Murasoli Maran and our Members of Parliament made efforts, selected the present site, and when the building was being constructed I personally visited four or five times and inspected it. The mansion that rose there is the one that exists today. Mrs Jayalalithaa went and stayed there, did she not? Did she stay away because it was a building that I had taken steps to construct during DMK rule? Her anger is against me; what **“sin”** did the new Secretariat building I constructed commit?

Therefore, Jayalalithaa, who has now become Chief Minister for the third time, should at least from now on transform herself into a politically mature leader. To insist, **“I will not accept anything done by the DMK government,”** does not befit the office she holds. It brings no honour. To reject buildings constructed with the people's tax money is not merely an arbitrary attitude; it is an insult to the people themselves. If the Secretariat is shifted to a building that they themselves said had leaks and cracks, and if some untoward incident later occurs there, the new rulers themselves must bear full responsibility.

By shifting the Assembly chamber again, they have unnecessarily moved the library of the Classical Tamil research centre. This is not a benefit done to Tamil. When the new government has innumerable welfare schemes to implement and major tasks to perform for the people, to spend the entire attention of the government machinery on shifting the Secretariat is wasteful work—an act that diverts government administration for the stubbornness of one individual.

The new Secretariat is on land belonging to the Tamil Nadu government. All offices can be located in one place there. The old Secretariat, however, belongs to the Union Government. Administrative needs arising with changing times cannot be fulfilled there. Deliberately leaving a new place owned by the Tamil Nadu government and going back to the old place is not proper administrative conduct. When governments change, some schemes and laws may change; but there is no necessity that the principle of a change of government should be to cast aside all the public-welfare schemes implemented by the previous government.

I shall stop here. I do not think I need to write anything further. I do not believe that the habit and clarity of examining matters rationally have completely disappeared among the Tamil people. **This letter is but a drop of that hope.**

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**19-6-2011**

பார்வையிட்டு, எழும்பிய மாளிகைதானே தற்போது உள்ளது.
அம்மையார் ஜெயலலிதா அங்கேதானே சென்று தங்கினார். தி.மு.
கழக ஆட்சிக்காலத்தில் நான் முயற்சி எடுத்துக் கட்டிய கட்டிடம்
என்பதற்காக அங்கே போகாமலா இருந்துவிட்டார். கோபம்
என் மீதுதானே தவிர; நான் கட்டிய புதிய தலைமைச் செயலகக்
கட்டிடம் என்ன “பாவம்” செய்தது?

எனவே முதலமைச்சர் பொறுப்புக்கு மூன்றாவது முறையாக
வந்துள்ள ஜெயலலிதா அவர்கள் இனியாவது, அரசியலில்
பக்குவப்பட்ட ஒரு தலைவராக தன்னை மாற்றிக் கொள்ள
வேண்டும். தி.மு.கழக ஆட்சி செய்ததையெல்லாம் நான்
ஏற்றுக்கொள்ள மாட்டேன் என்று பிடிவாதம் பிடிப்பது என்பது
அவர் இருக்கும் பதவிக்கு அழகல்ல. எந்தவிதப் பெருமையையும்
சேர்க்காது. மக்களின் வரிப் பணத்தைச் செலவழித்து கட்டப்பட்ட
கட்டிடங்களை வேண்டாமென்று கூறுவது தன்னிச்சையான
போக்கு மட்டுமல்ல; மக்களையே அவமதிப்பதாகும். அவர்களே
கூறிய கசிவு ஏற்பட்டுள்ள, விரிசல் உள்ள கட்டிடத்திற்கு
தலைமைச் செயலகத்தை மாற்றி, அதன் பிறகு அங்கே ஏதாவது
அசம்பாவிதம் நடைபெற்றால், அதற்கு முழு பொறுப்பையும்

புதிய ஆட்சியாளர்கள்தான் ஏற்க வேண்டும். சட்டப்பேரவை
மண்டபத்தை மீண்டும் மாற்றுவதால் செம்மொழி ஆய்வு
மையத்தின் நூலகத்தை தேவையில்லாமல் மாற்றியிருக்கிறார்கள்.
இது தமிழுக்குச் செய்யப்பட்ட நன்மையல்ல. புதிய அரசு
நிறைவேற்றப்பட வேண்டிய பல்வேறு நலத் திட்டங்கள்,
மக்களுக்கு ஆற்றிட வேண்டிய முக்கிய பணிகள் ஏராளமாக
இருக்கும்போது தலைமைச் செயலகத்தை மாற்றுவதில் அரசு
இயந்திரத்தின் முழுக் கவனத்தையும் செலவழிப்பது வீண் வேலை,
தனி மனிதர் ஒருவரின் பிடிவாதத்திற்காக அரசு நிர்வாகத்தைத்
திசை திருப்பும் செயலாகும்.
புதிய தலைமைச் செயலகம் தமிழக அரசுக்குச் சொந்தமான
இடம். அனைத்து அலுவலகங்களையும் ஒரே இடத்தில்
அமைத்திட முடியும். ஆனால் பழைய தலைமைச் செயலகம்
மத்திய அரசுக்குச் சொந்தமானது. காலத்தின் மாற்றத்திற்கேற்ற
நிர்வாகத் தேவைகளை அங்கே நிறைவு செய்திட முடியாது.
தமிழக அரசுக்குச் சொந்தமான புதிய இடத்தை விட்டு விட்டு,
வேண்டுமென்றே பழைய இடத்திற்கு போவது என்பது முறையான
நிர்வாகச் செயல்பாடு அல்ல. ஆட்சிகள் மாறும்போது சில
திட்டங்கள், சட்டங்கள் மாறலாமே தவிர; அடிப்படையில் முந்தைய
ஆட்சி செய்த மக்கள் நலத் திட்டங்களையெல்லாம் புறந்தள்ள
வேண்டும் என்பது ஓர் ஆட்சி மாற்றத்தின் நெறிமுறையாக இருக்க
வேண்டிய அவசியமில்லை. இத்துடன் நிறுத்துகிறேன். இதற்கு
மேல் நான் எதுவும் எழுதத் தேவையில்லை என்று கருதுகிறேன்.
பகுத்தறிந்து பார்க்கும் - பழக்கமும் தெளிவும் தமிழ் மக்களுக்கு
அறவே அற்றுப்போய் விட்டதாக நான் கருதவில்லை. அந்த
நம்பிக்கையின் ஒரு துளிதான் இந்தக் கடிதம்.
