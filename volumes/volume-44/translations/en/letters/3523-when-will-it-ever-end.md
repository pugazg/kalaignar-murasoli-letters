# 3523. When Will It Ever End?

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation follows the audited canonical Tamil and the locked Volume 44 conventions. The letter is a political rebuttal built from attributed newspaper editorials, court observations, arrests, dismissals, legislation and other allegations concerning the 2001–2006 AIADMK government. Those assertions are preserved as the letter presents them and are not independently adjudicated here. Printed English headings such as `Lawless in TamilNadu` and `Time ripe for Regime Change` are retained as source wording. The complete audited Tamil is reproduced below and remains authoritative.

**Source pages:** [PDF 284](../../../pages/page-284.md)–[PDF 290](../../../pages/page-290.md)  
**Date:** 29 December 2010

**Udanpirappē,**

When some people read Jayalalithaa’s statements, they even ask me, “Why do you answer her?” Those who ask are themselves people who know how to write. Perhaps they too think this is wasted work. That is why they ask me. Let that be as it may.

When I read some of Jayalalithaa’s statements, I cannot suppress my laughter. Shall I give you an example?

In Jaya’s statement of the 26th, she wrote: **“Because I ran a clean government in which not even a small mistake occurred during the 2001–2006 period, Karunanidhi could find nothing at all with which to file a false case against me.”** How can one read such words and not laugh? A clean government in which not even a small mistake occurred!

What, the letter asks, would the People’s Welfare Workers and road workers who lost their jobs under AIADMK rule, the dismissed government employees, and the workers subjected to ESMA and TESMA think when they read that claim?

They say she ran a clean government. Perhaps that is why, the letter says sarcastically, the Marxist Communist Party and the Communist Party of India are competing to line up in Jayalalithaa’s camp, each racing to be ahead of the other.

The very manner in which Jaya assumed office as Chief Minister, the letter argues, became an example of an unclean government. On **15-5-2001**, *The Hindu* wrote in its editorial that it was the first time a person firmly disqualified from contesting an Assembly election had become Chief Minister, and the first time a person legally convicted by a court had occupied such a post; in both respects, it said, the act offended the basic spirit of the Constitution and raised painful legal and moral questions.

The then *Ananda Vikatan* weekly, in its editorial, wrote that Jayalalithaa should occupy the Chief Minister’s office only after legally wiping away the stain upon her; that, it said, would befit both the dignity and honour of the office and Jayalalithaa herself.

In the case challenging Jayalalithaa’s appointment as Chief Minister, a five-judge Supreme Court Bench headed by Justice S. P. Bharucha delivered a unanimous judgment on **21-9-2001**, saying, as quoted by the letter: **“Jayalalithaa’s appointment as Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu is invalid. It is contrary to the Constitution. She can no longer continue as Chief Minister.”**

Only after that, the letter says, her lawyer K. K. Venugopal asked the judges for a few days to make alternative arrangements. The Supreme Court judge responded that when a Chief Minister dies, alternative arrangements are made immediately, and that a party with such a large majority could choose a new leader at any time. Was that too not a certificate for a “clean government”? It was only after this, the letter says, that the interim Chief Minister’s post went to Panneer.

Police registered a case against Assembly member Parithi Ilamvazhuthi under five sections including attempted murder, arrested him on **17-5-2001** and lodged him in Vellore prison. The letter calls this an example of “clean administration” on the third day after Jaya assumed office. On **23-5-2001**, while hearing Parithi’s bail petition, a High Court judge said that the complainant himself had stated that Parithi had not threatened to kill him, and wondered why the police had registered an attempted-murder case.

The letter then lists arrests: Arcot Veerasamy’s brother on **26-5-2001**; former MP Parasuraman on **27-5-2001**; then-MLA Purasaivakkam Renganathan on **30-5-2001**; and figures such as then-MLA Thamaraikani. It also recalls my own arrest on **29-6-2001**, when police entered my house at midnight and came as far as the bedroom. Murasoli Maran and T. R. Baalu, the letter says, were assaulted and arrested in connection with that incident. M. K. Stalin was arrested and taken to Madurai prison; in Madurai, P. T. R. Palanivel Rajan, M. K. Alagiri and others were arrested. These, the letter says sarcastically, were all certificates of Jaya’s clean government.

On **30-5-2001**, the *Indian Express* wrote an editorial under a Tamil-rendered headline meaning **“Revenge Drama, Phase Two”**, with a subheading asking, **“When will Jaya realise that Tamil Nadu is bigger than Poes Garden?”** The letter asks whether Jayalalithaa might now buy those old newspapers and read them.

On **11-6-2001**, police arrested Sudhakaran, who had been described as Jayalalithaa’s foster son. The letter says *The Pioneer* carried an article under a Tamil-rendered heading portraying Jayalalithaa as acting with the fury of Bhadrakali to settle scores.

When another case was registered against me that same June, *Thuglak* — which the letter says now strongly supports Jayalalithaa — wrote in its **27-6-2001** issue that there was room to suspect that the matter was being handled in that manner because of a desire somehow to take revenge on Karunanidhi; that the government had not shown the responsible approach required in such a matter; and that this was not good.

As another example, the letter says Jayalalithaa dismissed **ten thousand road workers** overnight in 2002 without cause. In the case filed by the road workers, the High Court was reported as saying that the dismissal was illegal, that the order was invalid and was being quashed, that the government’s action deserved severe condemnation, that the previous government had provided employment under welfare schemes, and that a government ought to be a good employment provider rather than act in this manner.

The English newspaper *The Statesman*, in an editorial headed **`Lawless in TamilNadu`** with the subheading **`Time ripe for Regime Change`**, was quoted in Tamil as criticising the midnight arrest of M. K. Stalin on allegedly fabricated charges and invoking Supreme Court arrest guidelines in the Joginder Kumar and D. K. Basu cases. It also referred to my own midnight arrest two years earlier and to a Union Home Ministry warning to the State government, and concluded that political-revenge actions had characterised Jayalalithaa’s government from the day she returned to office in 2001.

The letter next says journalist Nakkeeran Gopal was arrested under POTA, and that when his father-in-law R. S. Pandian was taken away on **6-7-2003** and brutally threatened, he later died by suicide. “Is this an example of Jaya’s clean government?” it asks.

Tamil Nadu audit officer Teethan, the letter says, issued a report on **31-7-2004** saying that the AIADMK government’s wrong approach had caused a loss of **₹11,000 crore** to the government. For that, it says, Jayalalithaa’s government sought his removal and issued full-page newspaper advertisements against him. Yet now, the letter says, the same Jayalalithaa is carrying the audit officer’s spectrum report on her head and dancing with it.

Former MLA M. K. Balan, who was abducted in Chennai on **30-12-2001**, was never found, the letter states.

In March 2003, the Jayalalithaa government increased electricity charges by about **₹1,400 crore**, and the AIADMK government withdrew the free electricity that had been provided under DMK rule, the letter says.

On **7-5-2002**, a Bill banning the use of “plastic” products was introduced in the Assembly. On **30-1-2003**, that very Bill was withdrawn by the AIADMK government. What happened in between? The letter says that only the “clean government” can shed light on it.

The 2003 government-employees’ and teachers’ agitation in Tamil Nadu, the letter continues, was another certificate for Jaya’s clean administration. Police went to government employees’ homes and arrested them at midnight. About **two lakh government employees**, it says, were dismissed without being given an opportunity to explain their position. Without even waiting to see what judgment would come in the case, the government newly recruited **15,500** people. The matter went to the Supreme Court, and the judge ordered that the dismissed government employees be reinstated.

In the first week of October 2002, Jayalalithaa’s government promulgated an ordinance prohibiting religious conversion. All political-party leaders in Tamil Nadu condemned it, but, the letter says, Jaya did not listen. In the first week of June 2003, even the Pope criticised it. Jayalalithaa responded that the Pope had neither authority nor right to comment on or oppose laws enacted by elected State governments in India. The letter says it is astonishing that some Christians in Kanniyakumari district have forgotten even this.

The film industry, too, the letter says, was not spared. In 2003, when the industry was already in difficulty, location-shooting fees under Jaya’s government were increased **25-fold**.

Jayalalithaa, the letter says, never fails in her statements to accuse the present government of sand looting and price rises. It therefore quotes a portion of a *Kalki* editorial dated **4-5-2003** about sand quarrying under her “clean” government. The editorial, as reproduced by the letter, said political interference in administration was vividly demonstrated by sand-quarry contracts; that river-sand prices rose sharply soon after the AIADMK came to power; that people who asked why were warned not to interfere; that an officer who challenged the practice was struck by a sand lorry and killed; that other officers too had faced attacks; and that corruption and political interference had to be removed or more officials like Shanmugasundaram would be sacrificed.

Is not this one example enough, the letter asks, of Jayalalithaa’s “clean” five-year government?

**Udanpirappē,** I will stop this list here. I have given all these explanations only because Jayalalithaa wrote that she had run a clean administration during 2001–2006 and that this was why no case had been filed against her.

When cases filed over alleged corruption during her 1991–1996 rule are still continuing in special courts such as Bengaluru, if cases were also to be filed over what happened during 2001–2006 — **when will it ever end?**

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
29 December 2010

2003ஆம் ஆண்டு தமிழகத்தில் நடைபெற்ற அரசு அலுவலர், ஆசிரியர் போராட்டம் ஜெயாவின் தூய்மையான ஆட்சி நிர்வாகத்திற்கு மற்றும் ஓர் சான்றாகும். அரசு ஊழியர்களை அவர்களுடைய வீடுகளுக்கே சென்று நள்ளிரவில் போலீசார் கைது செய்த கொடுமையும் அப்போது நடை பெற்றது. சுமார் 2 இலட்சம் அரசு அலுவலர்கள் தங்கள் நிலைக்கு விளக்கமளிக்க வாய்ப்பு அளிக்கப்படாமலே வேலை நீக்கம் செய்யப்பட்டார்கள். அது குறித்த வழக்கிலே என்ன தீர்ப்பு வருமோ என்றுகூடக் காத்திருக் காமல், புதிதாக 15,500 பேரை பணி நியமனம் செய்தார்கள். வழக்கு உச்சநீதிமன்றம் வரை சென்று, பணி நீக்கம் செய்யப்பட்ட அரசு அலுவலர்களை மீண்டும் பணியிலே அமர்த்த நீதிபதி உத்தர விட்டார். 2002ஆம் ஆண்டு அக்டோபர் திங்கள் முதல் வாரத்தில் ஜெயலலிதா அரசு மற்றுமோர் தூய்மையான நிர்வாகத்திற்கு உதாரணமாக, மதம் மாறுவதைத் தடை செய்து அவசரச் சட்டம் ஒன்றைக் கொண்டு வந்தது. அதைக் கண்டித்து தமிழகத்திலே உள்ள அனைத்துக் கட்சித் தலைவர்களும் கண்டனம் தெரிவித்தும் ஜெயா கேட்கவில்லை. 2003ஆம் ஆண்டு ஜூன் திங்கள் முதல் வாரத்தில் போப் ஆண்டவரே அதனைக் கண்டித்தார். அதுபற்றிகூட ஜெயலலிதா விடுத்த அறிக்கையில், “இந்தியாவில் தேர்ந்தெடுக்கப்பட்ட மாநில அரசுகள் நிறைவேற்றும் சட்டங்கள் குறித்து கருத்துத் தெரிவிக்கவோ, எதிர்ப்புத் தெரிவிக்கவோ போப்புக்கு எந்தவிதமான அதிகாரமும் கிடையாது, உரிமையும் கிடையாது” என்றார். இந்தச் செய்தியைக் கூட கன்னியாகுமரி மாவட்ட கிறித்தவர்கள் சிலர் மறந்து விட்டது தான் ஆச்சரியத்திலும் ஆச்சர்யம்! திரைப்படத் துறையிலும் ஜெயலலிதாவின் தூய்மையான நிர்வாகம் குறுக்கிடாமல் இல்லை. 2003ஆம் ஆண்டு திரைப்படத் துறையினர் மிகுந்த இன்னலுக்கு ஆளாகியிருந்த நேரத்தில் ஜெயா ஆட்சியில் படப்பிடிப்புக் கட்டணங்கள் எல்லாம் 25 மடங்கு அதிகமாக உயர்த்தப்பட்டது. ஜெயலலிதா தனது ஒவ்வொரு அறிக்கையிலும் இந்த ஆட்சியிலே மணல் கொள்ளை. விலை உயர்வு என்றெல்லாம் எழுதத் தவறுவதில்லை. அவருடைய தூய்மையான(?)

ஆட்சியிலே மணல் கொள்ளை பற்றி 4-5-2003 தேதிய “கல்கி” தலையங்கத்தில் குறிப்பிட்டிருந்த ஒரு பகுதி இதோ :- “நிர்வாகத்தில் அரசியல் தலையீடு இருந்தே தீரும் என்பதற்கு மணல் குவாரி காண்ட்ராக்ட் நிதர்சன எடுத்துக்காட்டு! அ.தி.மு.க. ஆட்சிக்கு வந்த சில நாட்களுக்குள்ளேயே ஆற்று மணல் விலை கணிசமாக உயர்ந்தது. ஏன் இந்தத் திடீர் விலை உயர்வு என்று விசாரித்த போது, அதெல்லாம் கண்டுக்காதீங்க, தேவையில்லாமல் தலையை நுழைச்சா, மீண்டு வருமா சந்தேகம்தான் என்ற பதிலே கிடைத்தது. அதற்கேற்பத்தான் இன்று, தட்டிக் கேட்கப்போன ஓர் அதிகாரி, மணல் லாரியாலேயே இடித்துத் தள்ளப்பட்டு பரிதாபமாக உயிர் துறந்திருக்கிறார். இவ்வாறு தாக்குதலுக்கு உள்ளான அதிகாரி இவர் மட்டுமல்ல, இவருக்கு முன்னும் பலர் இருந்திருக்கிறார்கள். மணல் குவாரியில் இறங்கிய காண்ட்ராக்டர்களுக்கு இவ்வளவு துணிவு எங்கிருந்து வந்தது? ஒரு லாரி லோடுக்கு இவ்வளவு என்று நிர்ணயித்து வாங்கப்படும் லஞ்சப் பணம்தான் இத்தகைய துணிவைத் தர முடியும். அங்கிங்கெனாதபடி எங்கும் பரவியுள்ள லஞ்ச ஊழலும், அதன் நேரடி விளைவான அரசியல் தலையீடும் நீக்கப்பட்டாலன்றி சண்முகசுந்தரம் போன்ற அரசு அதிகாரிகள் பலரை நாம் பரிதாபமாகப் பலி கொடுக்கத்தான் நேரும்” - ஜெயலலிதாவின் தூய்மையான ஐந்தாண்டு கால ஆட்சிக்கு இந்த ஒரு உதாரணம் போதாதா? உடன்பிறப்பே, இந்தப் பட்டியலை இத்துடன் நிறுத்தி விடுகிறேன். ஜெயலலிதா 2001-2006 ஆம் ஆண்டு தூய்மையான நிர்வாகம் நடத்தியதாகவும், அதனால்தான் அவர் மீது வழக்குப் போடவில்லை என்றும் அறிக்கையில் எழுதியிருந்த காரணத்தால் இத்தனை விளக்கங்களையும் அளித்துள்ளேன். 1991-1996ஆம் ஆண்டுகளில் அவர் ஆட்சியிலே இருந்த போது நடத்திய ஊழல்களுக்காக போடப்பட்ட வழக்குகளே பெங்களூரு போன்ற சிறப்பு நீதிமன்றங்களில் இன்னும் நீடித்துக் கொண்டிருக்கையில் 2001-2006ஆம் ஆண்டு நடைபெற்றவைகளுக்காக வழக்கு என்றால், அது எப்போதுதான் முடியுமோ? அன்புள்ள, மு.க. 29-12-2010
