**Udanpirappē,**

At a function where awards were presented to Tamil scholars, Jayalalithaa seems to have spent the entire time chanting an *archanai* against me, saying that I changed the Tamil New Year for politics and self-publicity.

Jayalalithaa has changed the beginning of the Tamil New Year from the month of Thai to the first day of Chithirai.

What evidence has she offered for this? She said: “The late Madurai Aadheenam has stated that, because there is a tradition of saying, ‘Come, Chithirai! Come leave a good imprint upon our lives,’ the month of Chithirai is the appropriate time for Tamil New Year.” The Madurai Aadheenam has said so, it seems! What sort of evidence is that?

Is this the evidence for saying that Chithirai is the beginning of the Tamil New Year?

Jayalalithaa further cites as evidence that I myself had, in the past, referred to the first day of Chithirai as the beginning of the Tamil New Year.

Until the DMK Government announced in 2008 that the first day of Thai would be the beginning of the Tamil New Year and made it law, the first day of Chithirai had officially been regarded as Tamil New Year. At that time the Chithirai festival was what had been called Tamil New Year. In that situation, how else could it have been called?

Jayalalithaa has said: “Karunanidhi changed the first day of Thai into Tamil New Year through legislation. Who asked for this law? What benefit did the people get from this law?”

The moment Jayalalithaa came to power, she changed the first day of Thai as Tamil New Year and brought in a law declaring that the first day of Chithirai alone was Tamil New Year. Who asked her for such a law? What benefit did the people receive from her changed law? Can we not ask the question back?

The University of Madras Centenary Auditorium is given only for Government functions. It is not given for party events. What took place there was a Government function.

What all did Jayalalithaa say at the function there on the 13th?

Everything spoken at that Government function was an *archanai* performed for me!

If one is Chief Minister—and especially if that Chief Minister is Jayalalithaa—does that mean one may say anything, anywhere?

Jayalalithaa has said that during AIADMK rule World Tamil Conferences were conducted at Madurai and Thanjavur in order to spread the Tamil language throughout the world.

Were the World Tamil Conferences held at Chennai and Coimbatore during DMK rule not meant to spread Tamil throughout the world? Was not even the name of the Coimbatore conference the World Classical Tamil Conference?

Do Tamils not know the quality of the World Tamil Conference that Jayalalithaa conducted at Thanjavur when she was Chief Minister?

To what extent were overseas Tamil scholars who came to participate—including K. Sivathamby—humiliated? They were turned back without being allowed to participate in the conference.

Speaking in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly on 23 August 2011 itself, Jayalalithaa had sharply criticised us, saying: “There is no evidence for beginning Tamil New Year in the month of Thai. But there is much evidence for beginning it in Chithirai. The law brought in by Karunanidhi for his own self-publicity, in a manner that wounded people’s sentiments, has been repealed.”

Speaking at the University Centenary Auditorium, Chief Minister Jayalalithaa, apparently believing that she was speaking with evidence, said: “Among the Tamil months from Chithirai through Panguni, the name of the star occurring on the full-moon day of a month became the name of that month. For example, because the Chithirai star occurs on the full-moon day in the month of Chithirai, that month is named Chithirai. Likewise, because Visakam occurs on the full-moon day in Vaikasi, that month is named Vaikasi. Each month was named on this basis.”

Let us see how true Jayalalithaa’s statement is! In the month of Thai, the full moon occurs under the star Poosam. But is that month called Poosam? In Maasi, the full moon occurs under Magam. Yet the month is not called Magam. In Panguni, the full moon occurs under Uthiram. That month is not called Uthiram either. Among the twenty-seven stars, apart from Chithirai and Karthigai, the names of the other stars are not month names. Let us hope that tomorrow the Chief Minister will read a statement under Rule 110 in the Assembly explaining all this!

Chief Minister Jayalalithaa has said that the Tamil lexicon published by the University of Madras in 1912 states that the first day of Chithirai is Tamil New Year.

But in the *Vaazhviyal Kalanjiyam*, Volume 13, page 43, published by the Tamil University at Thanjavur—which, as she herself says, was started under AIADMK rule, and published while Jayalalithaa was Chief Minister—it says that people who had sown, laboured and gathered food from the Aadi season onward worship the sun with gratitude on the first day of Thai, when Uttarayanam begins, as the source of rain and agricultural abundance; that the *Bhrigu Samhita* prescribes special worship and festivals on Sankranti; and that the Agamas tell us that a “divine day” is a human year, that the daytime of that divine day begins on the first day of Thai, and that this is celebrated as a festival. I point this out from the very Agamic example so dear to Ammaiyar.

In the *Senthamizh Sorpirappiyal Peragaramuthali*, Fourth Volume, Part Three, page 111, published in 2004 while Jayalalithaa was Chief Minister and carrying her own foreword, a meaning is given for “Thai.” Those who prepared Jayalalithaa’s speech could at least have bought that book and shown it to Ammaiyar, or read it themselves. The book gives the meanings “first month of Tamil year” and “first month of Thiruvalluvar year.” It also prints there that this month, “Surava,” is the beginning of the Tamil year.

Likewise, the *Senthamizh Sorpirappiyal Peragaramuthali*, Third Volume, Part Two, was also published with Ammaiyar’s foreword. There too, while explaining Chithirai as “Mezham,” regarded as the first month of the Tamil year, it says that “Suravam” alone is the beginning of the Tamil year and that treating Mezham as such is inappropriate. Suravam means the month of Thai; Mezham means Chithirai.

Jayalalithaa further said at the University Centenary Auditorium that in a meeting held at Pachaiyappa’s College in 1935, Maraimalai Adigal had said: “It can easily be established that Thiruvalluvar’s period was before the second century. It is the conclusion of my research that Thiruvalluvar was born thirty years before Christ,” as stated by Siruvai Nachinarkkiniyan in the souvenir published in connection with the Fifth World Tamil Conference. But Maraimalai Adigal’s view was that Thiruvalluvar was born not thirty but thirty-one years before Christ. That is the view the Government accepted and has followed. Maraimalai Adigal’s son, Marai. Thirunavukkarasu, who compiled the history of Maraimalai Adigal for us, has stated categorically: “According to Maraimalai Adigal, Thiruvalluvar lived thirty-one years before Christ.”

Not only that. I searched my home library for the souvenir of the Fifth World Tamil Conference, conducted at Madurai in 1981 under my dear friend M.G.R., to which Ammaiyar referred, and read in full the article by Siruvai Nachinarkkiniyan beginning on page 407. Though the passage cited by Ammaiyar appears on page 408, do you know what the very same writer says on page 407?

“‘When Thai is born, a way is born’ is an ancient proverb of Tamil Nadu. Based on the movement of the sun, one part of the year was understood as Thai through Aani, and another as Aadi through Margazhi. The period from Aadi through Margazhi was regarded as a period of darkness. The period from Thai through Aani was regarded as a period of daylight. During that half of the year from Aadi through Margazhi—through the winds of Aadi, the rains of Aippasi, and the dew and cold of Margazhi—the hope ‘when Thai is born, a way is born’ resounds like a great sound in the hearts of the people. When this condition of night changes into the condition of day, the birth of Thai, which brings benefit, was celebrated by ancient Tamils as the sacred day and birth-day of the New Year. Even today Tamils celebrate the birth of Thai with a distinctive greatness. Therefore, let us reckon the month of Thai as the first month of the year and put it into practice.”

Did those who wrote Ammaiyar’s speech forget to see what the very writer had written? Or did they see it and conceal it for fear that Ammaiyar would be caught out and left blinking?

The Governor’s Address of 23 January 2008 announced that, because all Tamil scholars had collectively accepted as true that the first day of Thai, when the Thiruvalluvar Year begins, is the beginning of Tamil New Year, the DMK Government had decided to declare and implement the first day of Thai as the beginning of Tamil New Year. The law was passed in the Assembly on 1 February 2008.

If this same course continues, what next—how could Tamil have been given Classical Language status? They may even say that too was Karunanidhi’s self-publicity, an arrangement made for politics. Because Ammaiyar has such affection for the very word “classical”! As though she had bitten an *ettikkai*! On the very first day after coming to power, Jayalalithaa threw out the Classical Tamil Research Library. To this day no place has been given to that library.

At the United Progressive Alliance election campaign public meeting held at Island Grounds, Chennai, on 6 March 2006, Mrs. Sonia Gandhi said:

> “As soon as the United Progressive Alliance Government was formed at the Centre, we declared Tamil a Classical Language. Through that, the Government of India gave the historic Tamil language a great distinction worthy of its heritage and cultural glory. The entire credit and share in that historic achievement of making Tamil a Classical Language belongs to Dr. Kalaignar. I wish to say from this platform that his role in making Tamil a Classical Language was a great one.”

It is out of the heartburn that this man should receive such praise that Jayalalithaa has searched and searched for achievements accomplished by us during DMK rule and set about trying to reverse them. One such act is this change in the Tamil New Year day.

Raising the question whether the first day of Chithirai or the first day of Thai is Tamil New Year, Pavendhar Bharathidasan sang:

> “Tamil, sleeping in slumber!  
> Chithirai is not your New Year;  
> it is what the Aryan crowd that came here to live off us showed you—  
> a sixty-year count that does not accord with reason.  
> For the Tamil who ruled this earth,  
> the first day of Thai alone is Tamil New Year.”

Did Pavendhar Bharathidasan compose this poem for my self-publicity?

If the first day of Thai is not accepted as Tamil New Year, what is the AIADMK Government going to do with the Thiruvalluvar Year? It was under DMK rule that the two-thousand-year Thiruvalluvar Year celebration was held in 1969. It was under DMK rule in 1972 that the Thiruvalluvar Year was accepted and an order issued that the Government should henceforth put it into practice. The Thiruvalluvar Year has been used in the Tamil Nadu Government diary from 1971, in the Tamil Nadu Government Gazette from 1972, and in all Tamil Nadu Government offices from 1981. Why, even today the masthead of the DMK daily *Murasoli* carries “Thiruvalluvar Year 2043.” Therefore, the first day of Thai, when the Thiruvalluvar Year begins, is New Year’s Day for Tamils.

Tamils living in Malaysia celebrate the first day of Thai as their New Year.

Tamil scholar Dr. Mu. Varadarasanar explained:

> “In earlier times the beginning of the year was not on the first day of Chithirai. Elders celebrated the first day of Thai as the beginning of the year. From that day onward, everyone’s life was to become new in many ways: newness in the house, newness in the street, newness throughout the town, newness in the mind. They cooked fresh raw rice as Pongal. They bought and wore new clothes. They laid fresh earth and red soil on the streets and put them in order. Festivals were conducted throughout the town. In this way Pongal was celebrated in towns as the beginning of the New Year.”

Chief Minister Jayalalithaa even ventured into scientific research in support of her speech, saying that the month of Chithirai was based upon astronomy and the cycle of the seasons.

As though answering that claim, scientist Dr. V. Masilamani, Professor in the Laser Department of King Saud University, wrote a lengthy article titled “The First Day of Thai Is Tamil New Year.” He explained that, in the Tamil environment of a thousand years ago, village astronomers had correctly calculated Thai as the month in which the whole world receives fresh vitality; that is why the last day of Margazhi became Bhogi, when the old and sloth are cast away, and the first day of Thai became Pongal, when thanks are offered to the sun. He wrote that the day on which the sun, having reached the southernmost point, turns back toward us is the day on which the living world receives new vitality; that day is January 14; beginning the year on that day is astronomically correct; and that January 14, the first day of Thai, therefore has this special significance. He then appealed to scientifically interested readers and students: go to the Internet and type “perhelion”; you will certainly say that the month of Thai is the proper New Year.

Therefore, Jayalalithaa’s act of changing the first day of Thai as the beginning of Tamil New Year and announcing Chithirai instead is, as usual, nothing but one more of her schemes born of hostility, seeking to injure everything implemented by the DMK Government!

Did I make the first day of Thai Tamil New Year for self-publicity? Or is today’s Chief Minister Jayalalithaa, who has now changed it and announced Chithirai, doing so for self-publicity? Jayalalithaa’s own group must explain this, along with the indecency of newly using the University Centenary Auditorium—unlike ever before—to deride the DMK and a former Chief Minister for political purposes!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**15 April 2012**

2004ஆம் ஆண்டு, ஜெயலலிதா முதலமைச்சராக இருந்து, அவரே அணிந்துரையும் வழங்கியுள்ள “செந்தமிழ்ச் சொற்பிறப்பியல் பேரகரமுதலி” - நான்காம் மடலம் - மூன்றாம் பாகம் - 111வது பக்கத்தில் “தை” என்பதற்கு பொருள் கூறப்பட்டுள்ளது. ஜெயலலிதாவிற்கு பேச்சு தயாரித்துக் கொடுத்தவர்கள் அந்த நூலையாவது வாங்கி அம்மையாரிடம் காட்டியிருக்கலாம் அல்லது அவர்களே படித்துப் பார்த்திருக்கலாம். அந்த நூலில் தை என்பதற்குப் பொருளாக “தமிழாண்டின் தொடக்க மாதம்” (first month of Tamil year); திருவள்ளுவராண்டின் தொடக்க மாதம் (first month of Thiruvalluvar year) என்றுள்ளது. மேலும் அங்கேயே இச் “சுறவ” மாதமே தமிழாண்டின் தொடக்கம் என்றும் அச்சிடப்பட்டுள்ளது.

அதுபோலவே “செந்தமிழ்ச் சொற்பிறப்பியல் பேரகரமுதலி” - மூன்றாம் மடலம் - இரண்டாம் பாகம் நூலும் அம்மையாரின் அணிந்துரையுடன் வெளியிடப்பட்டுள்ளது. அங்கேயும் சித்திரை என்பதற்குப் பொருளாக தமிழாண்டின் முதல் மாதமாகக் கருதப்படும் “மேழம்” என்று கூறிவிட்டு - “சுறவமே” தமிழாண்டின் தொடக்கம்; மேழத்தைக் கொள்வது பொருத்த மன்று என்று எழுதியுள்ளார்கள். சுறவம் என்றால் தை மாதம்; மேழம் என்றால் சித்திரை மாதமாகும்.

எனவே தைத் திங்கள் முதல் நாள்தான் தமிழ்ப்புத்தாண்டின் துவக்கம் என்பதை மாற்றி சித்திரை என்று ஜெயலலிதா அறிவித்துள்ள செயல் வழக்கம் போலவே அவருடைய காழ்ப்புணர்ச்சி, கழக அரசு நடைமுறைப்படுத்திய செயல்களுக்கெல்லாம் ஊறு தேட முற்படுகின்ற குழ்ச்சிகளிலே ஒன்றே தவிர வேறல்ல!

தமிழ்ப் புத்தாண்டு தைத் திங்கள் முதல் நாள் என்று நான் சுய விளம்பரத்திற்காகச் செய்தேனா? அல்லது அதை மாற்றி சித்திரைத் திங்கள் என்று தற்போது அறிவிக்கும் இன்றைய முதலமைச்சர் ஜெயலலிதா சுய விளம்பரத்திற்காகச் செய்கிறாரா என்பதை; பல்கலைக் கழக நூற்றாண்டு விழா மண்டபத்தை என்றுமில்லாமல் இப்போது புதிதாக தி.மு.க.வினரையும் முன்னாள் முதலமைச்சரையும் எசிப் பேசி அரசியலுக்காகப் பயன்படுத்தியுள்ள அநாகரிகச் செயல் குறித்து ஜெயலலிதாவின் கும்பல்தான் விளக்க வேண்டும்!
