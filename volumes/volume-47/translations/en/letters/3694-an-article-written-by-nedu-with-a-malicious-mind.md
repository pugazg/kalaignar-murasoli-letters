## English translation

Udanpirappē,

On 2 January 2013, some journalists asked me: “You had given a detailed reply to what Ramakrishnan, State Secretary of the Marxist Communist Party, said about you. Even after that, in the statement he issued today, he has asked the same thing again, hasn't he?” I replied: “I know that, personally, he has disliked me from the beginning. I have said many times that from my youth I too have considered myself a communist and that I was attracted to those principles. Ramakrishnan is unable to accept genuine communists.”

Neither comrade Ramakrishnan nor anyone belonging to that party said anything about this reply. I did not deny that I had been attracted to communist principles from my youth. But *Dinamani* has published an article in which Pazha. Nedumaran has attacked my views with extreme ferocity.

Nedumaran says in it that I did not answer Ramakrishnan's question. When Ramakrishnan asked why I was silent about the Cauvery Tribunal's final award not being published in the Central Gazette, I had already answered by pointing out that I had issued a statement on that very matter on 17 December 2012. Since, even after that, he again asked the same question, I said that he personally disliked me and that I had been attracted to communist ideology since my youth. What has Nedumaran got to do with that?

Why should the wolf worry that the goat is getting wet? Communist comrades themselves know very well whether or not I was attracted to communist ideology. What does this man know? Who asked him to poke his nose into this issue? Nedumaran writes that the people of Tamil Nadu know who Karunanidhi really is. The people of Tamil Nadu know who I am—and they also know what kind of man Nedumaran is.

I am not saying only now that I was attracted to communist ideology. I have said it at many meetings attended by Communist Party leaders themselves. Did I ever ask Nedumaran for a certificate declaring whether I am a communist or not? Did I ask this over-eager man, posing as an elder brother, for such a certificate?

Nedumaran has searched somewhere for something Mao Zedong said about communists, inserted it into his article, and grandly declared that my conscience must answer whether Mao's words fit me. What my conscience has said about me is precisely what I have openly stated many times. Today's Nedumaran, who has forgotten the months he spent languishing under POTA and has buried his own conscience, would not know any of that.

Nedumaran cites comrades E.M.S. Namboodiripad and K.T.K. Thangamani as examples, saying they gave all their property to the party and accepted prison suffering with a smile.

Even if I do not come forward to call myself a communist on their scale, I have continuously served poor people to the extent I could. I have even deeded away for the people the only property I possess—my house. I have not accumulated estates or grand mansions for myself. Every item that was personally presented to me at party functions I handed over to the party, and anyone can see all of them today adorning the Kalaignar Karuvoolam at Anna Arivalayam. I am someone who was kept in solitary confinement at Palayamkottai. I have never led any kind of luxurious life.

Nedumaran writes: “Karunanidhi completely abandoned the demand for Dravida Nadu. Even today he has not opened his mouth about it.” It is true that the DMK gave up the demand for Dravida Nadu while Anna was still alive. Anna himself gave many explanations and reasons for doing so at that very time.

Nedumaran says it would be more appropriate for Karunanidhi to compare himself with great capitalists such as Ambani, Tata and Birla. From this one understands the extent to which Nedumaran, long accustomed to shouting while spitting venom, has himself become deadly poison. People of Tamil Nadu, you yourselves must say whether Karunanidhi lives like Ambani, Tata or Birla!

I have served as Chief Minister five times. I have won every Assembly election I contested since 1957 without defeat. I have written stories and dialogues for seventy-five films. Yet to this day I have not bought even a grand multi-storeyed residence for myself. I continue to live in the same “street house” I bought before I came to office—the sort of house one would call a “Street House”—where there is not even enough space for my assistants to sit and work comfortably. Yet Nedumaran mercilessly compares me with Tata. When I think of it, I can only feel pain that he seems to have said “ta-ta” to his conscience.

He says mine is the only family to have entered public life and celebrated a “golden” jubilee for it. Is he saying I accumulated gold and diamonds for the sake of such jubilees? Friends who have affection and attachment towards me organise celebrations for me. Should those who lack such affection burn with jealousy on seeing that? He says members of my family have become first-rate rich people. What am I to do about that? If they earn honestly through their professions, I can only be happy for them; does that mean I myself become the owner of all their wealth?

Nedumaran asks whether Karunanidhi, who served as Chief Minister five times, ever behaved like a communist during his administrations. Does Nedumaran not know comrade Manali Kandasamy, the great Communist Party leader? At the function in Tiruthuraipoondi where house sites occupied by agricultural labourers were made their own property, Manali said: “A right that the Communist Party could not obtain even after shedding blood and struggling for so many years, Kalaignar has accomplished with a single drop of ink from his pen; tonight these lakhs of people will sleep with the satisfaction that they are sleeping on their own land.”

If only younger brother K. Subbu, a lifelong communist, were alive today to answer Nedumaran's question whether I am a communist!

Was nationalising buses and creating transport corporations opposed to communism?

Was bringing in the fifteen-acre land-ceiling law opposed to communism?

Was my role as an inspiration and support when Indira Gandhi announced the nationalisation of banks opposed to communism?

Was it opposed to communism that I built a memorial at Nagercoil to perpetuate the memory of veteran communist leader elder brother P. Jeevanandham and personally went there to inaugurate it?

From my youth I was inspired by Russian writer Maxim Gorky's epic *Mother*; I rendered it in Tamil in poetic form and also had it made into a film. Was that opposed to communism?

The Slum Clearance Board; the Water Supply and Drainage Board; the eyesight-restoration scheme; the rehabilitation scheme for beggars; abolition of hand-pulled rickshaws and distribution of free cycle rickshaws; welfare schemes for persons with disabilities; free concrete houses for the Scheduled Castes; Police Commissions for those serving in the police department; paid holiday on May Day; creation of May Day Park in Chennai; the Government Employees' Family Benefit Scheme; free housing for fishermen; total abolition of dry-land tax; free electricity for farmers; property rights for women; marriage assistance for poor women; legislation fixing wages for agricultural labourers; legislation making the house sites they occupied their own property; and legislation using wasteland to allot land to landless farmers—are all these, in Nedumaran's dictionary, schemes that reject communism?

I named one of my sons “Stalin” not today but sixty years ago. If Nedumaran looks at me and asks whether Karunanidhi has ever acted in support of communism, will people not ask to what extent he has pawned his intelligence?

An event held at Anna Arivalayam on 8 January 2013 itself is sufficient proof of the affection genuine members of the Communist Party have for me.

Comrade A.M. Gopu, one of the leading figures of the Communist Party of India, passed away. His family is installing his statue at Attur in Salem district. They wanted me to inaugurate it. Taking my health into account, they brought the statue itself to Anna Arivalayam, had me unveil it with my own hands, and then took it back. Where is their affection—and where is this Nedumaran's deceitful heart?

Comrade P. Ramamurthi was one of the great leaders of the Communist movement. Even when I was not Chief Minister, I participated in the inauguration of his memorial on 30 March 1991 together with West Bengal Chief Minister Jyoti Basu.

When my platinum-jubilee celebration was held in 1998, R. Nallakannu, A.M. Gopu, G. Palanisamy, K. Subbarayan, Na. Periyasamy, Vai. Sivapunniyam and Nanguneri Krishnan attended on behalf of the Communist Party of India; and N. Varadarajan, T.K. Rangarajan and Meenakshi Sundaram attended on behalf of the Marxist Communist Party.

It was the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam under my leadership that helped comrade T.K. Rangarajan of the Marxist Communist Party and D. Raja of the Communist Party of India win election as Rajya Sabha members.

When my golden-jubilee function in the Legislative Assembly was held on 12 May 2007, the person who presided over the function and praised me in many ways was veteran communist leader Somnath Chatterjee, who was then Speaker of the Lok Sabha.

Do you know why Nedumaran has concealed all this and written an article pouring out such bitterness and hatred against me?

During the AIADMK Government, on 18 December 2003 the Madras High Court granted bail to Pazha. Nedumaran and Suba. Veerapandian, who had been arrested under POTA, after seventeen months in prison. The appeal filed by the AIADMK Government in the Supreme Court on 19 December 2003 seeking cancellation of the bail granted to Nedumaran was dismissed.

Even after that, the AIADMK Government did not release Nedumaran. They filed another case against him. He obtained bail in that case too, and only on 8 January 2004 was Pazha. Nedumaran released from Cuddalore prison. At the prison gate he was welcomed by party district secretary younger brother M.R.K. Panneerselvam, Cuddalore MLA Ila. Pugazhendhi and party comrades. The day after his release from prison, Nedumaran and Suba. Veerapandian met me at Gopalapuram and thanked me. That same day I wrote a poem in *Murasoli* about Nedumaran's release. I began that poem this way:

> “After roaming for eighteen months in the ‘grove where the mango cuckoo sings,’ Nedumaran emerged with his chest held high like a warrior; after he embraced me in unity and joy, after I welcomed him with congratulations and handed him the flag—this was the poem I fashioned!”

The then Deputy General Secretary of the party, younger brother M.K. Stalin, went to Nedumaran's home, draped him with a shawl and expressed his happiness.

Should he not express heartfelt gratitude for all this? That is why Nedumaran has written, steeped in “civility,” this critical article in *Dinamani*!

With affection,
M.K.
11-01-2013
