# 3775. Long Live the Fame of Perunthalaivar Kamarajar!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

*(A tribute written by Kalaignar for a programme broadcast by Vasanth Television on the occasion of Perunthalaivar Kamarajar’s 111th birth anniversary.)*

Today is the birth anniversary of Perunthalaivar Kamarajar! Whatever differences of principle or opinion may have existed between us and departed leaders or eminent people, we have never forgotten—and continue unfailingly to honour—their distinction, noble conduct, exceptional public service, sacrifices and ability.

The distinction of erecting the first statue of Kamarajar in Tamil Nadu belongs to the Chennai Corporation when it was under the administration of the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam.

Before taking decisions on matters such as the Cauvery issue and the situation during the Emergency, I did not think of him merely as a leader of the Opposition. I approached him as a senior and experienced political leader, went to his house, learnt his views and acted accordingly.

When Perarignar Anna, as Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu, conducted the Second World Tamil Conference, it was Kamarajar who welcomed everyone who had come and formally inaugurated the conference.

Who can forget that, during the DMK Government, I pressed and argued with Prime Minister Vajpayee and secured permission to build a worthy memorial hall for Kamarajar on the seashore at Kanniyakumari?

During the DMK Government, Chennai’s Beach Road became **Kamarajar Salai**. It was because of the DMK Government that the domestic airport at Meenambakkam came to bear Kamarajar’s name. The DMK Government converted his birth house at Virudhunagar into a memorial. When his body had to be laid to rest in Chennai, it selected a site at Guindy beside the memorial named after Mahatma Gandhi and created a beautiful memorial there. These were modest offerings made under the DMK Government in recognition of the service rendered by Karma Veerar Kamarajar to the country and society.

The DMK Government named the Thoothukudi District Collectorate complex **Annai Sivagami Ammaiyar Valagam**, so that the name of Kamarajar’s mother would remain in public memory. It gave her name to the Government’s Girl Child Protection Scheme and implemented it. It appointed Vairavan, who had served Kamarajar, as a salaried guide at the Kamarajar Memorial in Chennai and allotted him a house in Government quarters. I regarded Kamarajar’s sister, Mrs Nagammal, as my own sister and, whenever I travelled that way, made it a point to meet her.

When my beloved mother, Anjugam Ammaiyar, died at Stanley Hospital in Chennai on 27 January 1963, Perunthalaivar Kamarajar, who was then Chief Minister, came to my home and waited there even before her body and I arrived. That humane act of his, coming personally to console me, is something I can never forget.

I went in person to invite him to M. K. Stalin’s wedding. He expressed regret that his poor health might prevent him from attending. Precisely so that he could come, we changed the wedding venue and made arrangements for his car to drive right up to the stage, allowing him to alight there. He came and blessed the couple. That event remains evergreen in the memories of both Thambi Stalin and me.

On Kamarajar’s birthday, 15 July 1976, I wrote this poem:

> **Perunthalaiva!**
> Today is your birthday—
> a festival everywhere for the mountain of conviction!
> Born in Virudhunagar, you grew into a hero;
> you stood as a leader who filled the pages of history!
> A sacrificer who accepted prison’s cruelty and torture
> with a smiling face.
> A thousand clashes of opinion there may be—yet
> shall we ever forget your imperishable service?
> O sacred presence who came to make Tamil soil fragrant!
> Embodiment of public service, sweet as nectar!
> Though your body bears its ailment,
> while resolve remains in your heart, you will labour on!
> Khadi adorns your body;
> your arms embrace the poor who cry in distress.
> You would hurl stern words at political parties—yet
> if hardship befell another party’s leader,
> you would be the first to grieve!
> Your tears at Periyar’s resting place!
> Your tears when Perarignar and Mutharignar passed away!
> When I lost my mother,
> you came like my own kin and shed
> tears! Great son! Did not our tears for you
> then surge like a mighty river in flood?
>
> Yours was not the life of an individual;
> it was a chapter in the history of self-respect.
> From Kanniyakumari to the Himalayas you made your flag fly
> and inscribed the fame of glorious Tamil Nadu.
> “Man of virtue! Noble scion!”—thus did
> our Anna, bridegroom of refinement, address you.
> The Father of Rationalism called you a “pure Tamilian”
> and lovingly kissed the crown of your head.
> Did not this sea-girt nation fold its hands
> and worship you as the “Black Gandhi”!
> At the edge of life, you showed us affection too!
> You established your whole life
> as a lesson for everyone!
> Today is your birthday—
> forever it shall be a distinguished day!

Once, Mr Rajaram Naidu handed me a letter in the Legislative Council. In it, Perunthalaivar Kamarajar had written: “I am going to Ooty. When I go there, I may need to stay for a week or two at Aranmore Palace. I request you to direct the officials to grant me the necessary permission.” At the end it was written that, as a Member of Parliament, it would be normal to allot him accommodation and that this should be done. I immediately told Rajaram Naidu: “Kamarajar cannot be allotted a place merely in his capacity as a Member of Parliament. It must be given to him in his capacity as a leader. He may stay there for as many days as he needs.” Such was our attachment, affection and respect for him.

After the DMK Government assumed office, Kamarajar undertook an arduous tour. His personal assistants told us: “Perunthalaivar cannot now sleep without air-conditioning; that is his medical condition. Please arrange air-conditioning in the Government guest houses.” I immediately directed the officials: “Provide air-conditioning in every guest house to which he goes. Do not look at the fact that he is no longer in office. He was the one who made way and showed the way for all of us to come to office. None of you should hesitate in this matter.”

When Congress workers at Ambur told Kamarajar that it was getting late for their public meeting and urged him to come, he is said to have replied angrily: “Keep quiet, ayya! You are calling me only to abuse Karunanidhi, aren’t you? He is the one who has arranged air-conditioning from town to town. You want me to enjoy his air-conditioning and then go and abuse him! All right, come, come!” *(Applause.)* Such was the inward affection he had for me, and I for him.

On another occasion, when Congress students said they were going to show black flags at the Law College, Kamarajar reportedly told them: “Have you no other work? Like me, he was born in a backward community. If he too goes, who else is there? I am not Chief Minister now. Are you going to show black flags to a man from a backward community? Roll up all those black flags and go home.” That was Perunthalaivar Kamarajar.

After I completed the funeral arrangements for Kamarajar, a Congress leader praised me in the Legislative Council: “I cannot even imagine whether a younger brother would have performed so many final rites for an elder brother. Chief Minister Karunanidhi carried out so many rites and arrangements for Perunthalaivar Kamarajar.”

So that Kamarajar’s birthday would be commemorated throughout Tamil Nadu, I announced that it must be celebrated in every educational institution as **Education Development Day**. To prevent a succeeding Government from changing it, I enacted it as law.

I wish to say that all these tributes, honours and words of praise became, for me, a form of atonement for the speeches I had made opposing Kamarajar in politics and for the actions in which I had participated.

**Long live the fame of Perunthalaivar Kamarajar!**

***

**16 July 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. **Perunthalaivar** (“great leader”) and **Karma Veerar** (“hero of action”) are retained as established honorifics associated with K. Kamaraj.
2. The source introduces the poem with the visibly printed form `“பெருந்தலைவ;`. The English renders the intended address as **Perunthalaiva!** without altering the archival Tamil reproduced below.
3. **Mutharignar** is an honorific used in the poem for C. Rajagopalachari. **Father of Rationalism** refers to Periyar E. V. Ramasamy.
