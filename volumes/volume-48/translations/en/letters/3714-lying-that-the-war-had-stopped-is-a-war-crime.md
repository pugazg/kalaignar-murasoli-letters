# 3714. The Lie That the War Had Stopped Was Itself a War Crime!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

Some people continue to speak as though, when the war against the Eelam Tamils was taking place in Sri Lanka in 2009, we did not show then the same deep emotional concern that we show for the Eelam Tamils today. Knowingly or unknowingly, they speak as though the DMK, because it was then in government, could have stopped the final war in Sri Lanka and the killing there of thousands of Eelam Tamils, but failed to do so. Did the DMK really do nothing while the war was taking place in Sri Lanka in 2009?

On behalf of the DMK government, an all-party leaders' meeting was convened on 14 October 2008 over the Sri Lankan Tamil issue. It passed a resolution that if the Government of India did not come forward to secure a ceasefire in Sri Lanka within two weeks, all Members of Parliament from Tamil Nadu would have to resign. Following that resolution, every DMK member of the Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha brought their resignation letters and handed them to me. Thambis L. Ganesan and Gingee Ramachandran, then Members of Parliament of the MDMK, and Professor Kader Mohideen of the Indian Union Muslim League also handed their resignation letters to me.

On the very day the resolution was passed, it was sent to the Prime Minister through Thambi T.R. Baalu. After examining it, the Prime Minister assured Baalu that the Union Government would rapidly take every step to find a political solution; this appeared in all the newspapers at the time. In an interview that same day, the Prime Minister said:

> “The ethnic problem that has arisen in Sri Lanka is a matter of great concern. A military solution to this problem will not be appropriate. A political solution must be found through negotiations in a manner that respects Sri Lanka's sovereignty and the human rights of the Tamils living there. The Union Government will make a fully committed effort to find a solution to Sri Lanka's ethnic problem.”

On 18 October 2008 Prime Minister Dr. Manmohan Singh personally spoke by telephone with Sri Lankan President Rajapaksa. On 22 October 2008 Union Minister Pranab Mukherjee stated in Parliament that the Sri Lankan government had been urged to provide proper protection to Sri Lankan Tamils. On 24 October 2008 we held a **massive human chain** in Chennai. On 26 October 2008 Mr. Pranab Mukherjee came to Chennai and spoke with me for more than an hour about the Sri Lankan Tamil issue. Speaking to journalists in Chennai, he said Kalaignar had urged that the ceasefire brought about through Norway's efforts should be ensured and continued, and that the Sri Lankan government had assured that humanitarian assistance would be provided, through the International Red Cross, the United Nations and the Government of India, to more than two lakh Tamils suffering in the war zones.

On 12 November 2008 I moved a resolution in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly for the Sri Lankan Tamils, and it was adopted. On 4 December 2008 I took the leaders of all parties in Tamil Nadu to Delhi, met the Prime Minister and requested that Union Minister Pranab Mukherjee be sent to Sri Lanka over the Sri Lankan Tamil issue. Pranab subsequently went personally to Sri Lanka and held talks with Rajapaksa. At the DMK General Council meeting held on 27 December 2008, we passed a resolution on the Sri Lankan Tamil issue.

On 12 January 2009, after Tamilar Thalaivar K. Veeramani, PMK leader Dr. Ramadoss and Viduthalai Chiruthaigal leader Thol. Thirumavalavan met me, I had T.R. Baalu, then a Union Minister, and Rajya Sabha member Kanimozhi explain the situation to the Prime Minister and Sonia Gandhi.

On 28 March 2009 I wrote to the Prime Minister and Mrs. Sonia Gandhi asking both of them to help secure an immediate ceasefire in Sri Lanka. In a long letter to me dated 31 March 2009, Mrs. Sonia Gandhi wrote:

> “Our Prime Minister, in the letter he wrote to you on behalf of the Congress, has described the various steps taken by the United Progressive Alliance government regarding the recent events in Sri Lanka, and I too wish to assure you on behalf of everyone in the Congress party... The war now taking place in Sri Lanka, and the immense suffering being endured there by innocent Tamils including mothers and children, causes us grave concern. We expect the authorities in Sri Lanka to undertake every necessary effort to protect them and safeguard their welfare. In fact, as a first step, there must be a ceasefire. Our government is in contact with the Sri Lankan government regarding a ceasefire. Only if there is a ceasefire can innocent people move to safe areas.”

When reporters met me on 5 April 2009, they asked about reports that the military had captured all the LTTE's positions, destroyed its military bases, and that Prabhakaran and his son were hiding in an area inhabited by Tamils. I replied:

> “For the past six months we have continuously been calling for a ceasefire. Respecting our voice, governments abroad, the United Nations and foreign diplomats have been conveying this to Sri Lanka. The Government of India has been asking them to stop the war. Yet Rajapaksa has not stopped the war. We strongly condemn this.”

In a telegram I sent on 7 April 2009 to Prime Minister Manmohan Singh, Mrs. Sonia Gandhi, External Affairs Minister Pranab Mukherjee and Home Minister P. Chidambaram, I asked that **the entire Tamil people in Sri Lanka be saved from being destroyed together, that a ceasefire be ensured there, and that immediate steps be taken for peace talks.** During the final war, it was not only I and not only the Government of India who called for the Sri Lankan Tamils to be protected. Statements issued by major countries such as the United States, Britain, Japan and Norway also called for the protection of Sri Lankan Tamils and for Sri Lankan warplanes to stop bombing the declared safe zone. Beyond even that, U.N. Secretary-General Ban Ki-moon telephoned President Rajapaksa and called for the Sri Lankan Tamils to be protected and for humanitarian action to be taken.

On 9 April 2009 a massive rally was held in Chennai under my leadership, raising the slogan **“Sri Lankan government, stop the war!”** Sri Lankan President Rajapaksa announced a two-day ceasefire in Sri Lanka. The Government of India invited Members of Parliament from parties supporting the Liberation Tigers to come to India for talks.

In a statement on 14 April 2009, my friend P. Chidambaram, then Union Home Minister, said India would continue pressing the Sri Lankan government for the ceasefire to continue. In a statement, Tamilar Thalaivar Ilaval K. Veeramani said:

> “Because of pressure from countries around the world, the Rajapaksa government has announced a 48-hour ceasefire. The relentless pressure applied by Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Kalaignar is an important reason for this. The Government of India too has not merely stood by watching.”

On 21 April 2009 I sent a telegram to Prime Minister Dr. Manmohan Singh, United Progressive Alliance Chairperson Mrs. Sonia Gandhi and then External Affairs Minister Pranab Mukherjee saying:

> “The war situation in Sri Lanka is dangerous. The suffering being endured by Tamils has reached an extremely dangerous stage. All countries, including the United Nations, have pressed the Sri Lankan government to announce an immediate ceasefire. I urge you to issue a final warning to the Sri Lankan government to declare an immediate and permanent ceasefire in order to save the lives of lakhs of Tamils in Sri Lanka.”

On 21 April 2009 I also wrote an emotional Udanpirappu letter under the title **“An Appeal to the Union Government to Protect the Helpless Eelam Tamils”**, and I issued a statement asking that a one-day strike be held on 23 April 2009 to demand immediate action by the Union Government for a ceasefire to protect the Sri Lankan Tamils.

I wrote to the leaders of all parties—the Congress, AIADMK, CPI(M), CPI, Dravidar Kazhagam, PMK, BJP, MDMK, DMDK, Viduthalai Chiruthaigal, Indian Union Muslim League, MGR Kazhagam, Puthiya Tamilagam and others—and to the newspapers, asking every party to cooperate with that strike. The strike took place peacefully and successfully.

Following the telegram I had sent, on the night of 22 April 2009 the Prime Minister convened an emergency meeting in Delhi of Union ministers and officials and called upon Sri Lanka to stop immediately the war against innocent Tamils. A high-level group of the Prime Minister's special envoys went to Colombo to press that request directly upon the Sri Lankan government. They met Rajapaksa in Colombo and conveyed India's request that immediate steps be taken to stop the war.

On the night of 24 April 2009 P. Chidambaram, then Union Home Minister, came to Anna Arivalayam, met me and explained all the details. The Prime Minister too spoke to me by telephone that same day and described the efforts undertaken by the Union Government.

In a statement issued by the White House on behalf of the newly elected U.S. President Obama on 25 April 2009, it was said that the Sri Lankan government should immediately stop its military offensive and that the United States supported the steps being taken by the Government of India on the Sri Lankan issue. When reporters asked me on 26 April 2009 about a ceasefire in Sri Lanka, I said we would wait through the night and see.

In a statement issued by the Liberation Tigers on that same 26 April 2009, they said:

> “Accepting the requests of India, the United States, the European countries and the United Nations, we are declaring a ceasefire. This indefinite ceasefire comes into force immediately. The suffering of the Tamil people caused by the war being waged by the Sri Lankan military has reached its peak. The Sri Lankan government too must announce a ceasefire.”

After receiving these reports on the 26th, I did not sleep throughout that night. I kept contacting Delhi to ask whether the Sri Lankan government had announced anything about a ceasefire. I contacted Home Minister P. Chidambaram several times. The Prime Minister too contacted me. Many Tamils abroad kept speaking to me by telephone. Until four in the morning I waited to see whether good news would appear on television. No such news came. The only news was that the Sri Lankan government had not stopped the war.

Only after that, at about five o'clock, I told my family that I was going to Arivalayam and went instead to the Anna memorial. In January of that same year, at this age, I had undergone major surgery on my back and was unable to walk, travelling in a wheelchair. Yet without worrying about my health or anything else, I had decided on my own—telling no one, because they would not have permitted it—to undertake a fast unto death.

Speaking that morning to journalists at the fasting venue, I said:

> “Because the Sinhala government has not come forward to stop the war, I have undertaken this fast on behalf of all Tamils in order to offer myself, to give even my life for our Tamil people, and to become one more among the lives taken by the Sri Lankan government.”

At around 11 a.m. on the same day that I began the fast, Sri Lanka's Security Council met. It was reported that the meeting considered several matters, including the war being conducted in Mullaitivu and my fast arising out of it. At the end of the meeting it was decided, the source says, to bring the war in northern Sri Lanka to an end, and at about noon the Sri Lankan government issued a statement saying:

> “The war that was being conducted in northern Sri Lanka is over. The military has been ordered not to use heavy weapons in the northern region any longer. Aerial bombing is also being stopped. Sri Lankan troops will engage in the task of safely rescuing civilians trapped in the north. From now on priority will be given to protecting innocent civilians.”

On the basis of that statement issued by the Sri Lankan government, then Union External Affairs Minister Mr. Pranab Mukherjee issued a statement on 27 April 2009 saying that the Sri Lankan government had announced that combat operations had ended and that orders had been issued that Sri Lankan security forces should not use heavy guns and weapons of war because they would cause large-scale civilian deaths. He further stated that the Sri Lankan security forces would henceforth concentrate on making every necessary effort to rescue and protect civilians. A copy of that statement was sent to the very place where I was fasting, and both the Prime Minister and Mrs. Sonia Gandhi asked me to end my fast.

On the basis of their request, and because leaders and friends from all parties came personally and urged me to give up the fast, I ended it at about one in the afternoon.

I believed the ceasefire announced by the Sri Lankan government to be true; the Government of India too believed it and informed me accordingly, and therefore I withdrew my fast. It was not only we who believed it. The U.S. government itself did so: U.S. government spokesperson Robert Wood said that the United States welcomed the announcements made by the Sri Lankan government and the Liberation Tigers.

But even after that, the Sri Lankan Sinhala government, driven by what the letter calls Rajapaksa's Sinhala-majoritarian obstinacy, continued killing the fighters who remained on the battlefield. We made so many efforts to stop the fighting; even with the Union Government, the Prime Minister and Union ministers supporting those efforts, the Sinhala military continued its attacks. That cruelty continues even now. **Was not the Sinhala government's lie that the war had stopped itself a war crime?**

It is to seek a way forward on this that, even though the DMK is no longer in government, we have reconstituted TESO. I, Perasiriyar, Tamilar Thalaivar Veeramani, Thol. Thirumavalavan, Professor Suba. Veerapandian and Subbulakshmi Jagadeesan are members, and on its behalf we have been conducting protests, conferences and seminars. In that sequence, tomorrow in Delhi we are holding a TESO seminar in which representatives of several countries and prominent figures from various political parties in India will participate. In addition, we have passed a resolution to hold a general strike throughout Tamil Nadu on the 12th.

When the war was taking place in Sri Lanka in 2009, even at this age I began a fast with the intention of fasting unto death, without asking anyone. Could I have anticipated that the Sri Lankan government would suddenly meet and falsely announce a ceasefire? They announced that the war had stopped; the Government of India believed that announcement, conveyed it to me and asked me to end the fast. That is why I had to end it.

Whether others believe it or not, I have acted on the Sri Lankan Tamil issue according to my conscience. In the Assembly itself I have said that if the Sri Lankan Tamils could obtain a good life, I was prepared even to lose office for it. Why, when I was in government, I lost power twice because of the concern I showed for the Sri Lankan Tamil issue. Along with Perasiriyar, I even resigned my position as a Member of the Legislative Assembly. I therefore consider that there is no need to offer any further explanation in answer to malicious propaganda.

Finally, the responsibility for making the general strike to be held on the 12th successful, as always, belongs to people of every section in Tamil Nadu. As requested in the resolution, I ask people of all sides, without party distinction, to bear the inconvenience, recognise that this strike is being held for the Eelam Tamils with whom we share an umbilical-cord bond, and make it a success.

Understanding these actions, I urge the Union Government, without any further delay, to come forward wholeheartedly to support the resolution being brought by the United States at the U.N. Human Rights Council in Geneva against the Sri Lankan government's war crimes, and to issue an announcement to that effect immediately.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**7 March 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. This letter is Kalaignar's retrospective defence of the actions he and the DMK say they took during the 2008–09 final phase of the Sri Lankan war. Political claims and descriptions of responsibility remain attributed to the source rather than independently adjudicated in translation.
2. **TESO** is retained for the Tamil Eelam Supporters Organisation; **Perasiriyar** and **Tamilar Thalaivar** are retained as established movement honorifics.
3. The source distinguishes between LTTE and Sri Lankan-government ceasefire statements and later argues that the government's announcement was false. The translation preserves that argumentative sequence without reconciling it through outside material.
4. `தொப்புள் கொடி உறவு` is rendered **umbilical-cord bond**, preserving the source's image of intimate Tamil kinship across the Palk Strait.

எனக்குத் தெரிவித்ததால் உண்ணாவிரதத்தை நான் திரும்பப்
பெற்றேன். நாம் நம்பியது மாத்திரமல்ல, அமெரிக்க அரசே அதை
நம்பி, அமெரிக்க அரசின் செய்தித் தொடர்பாளர் ராபர்ட் உட்
இலங்கை அரசின் அறிவிப்பையும், விடுதலைப் புலிகளின்
அறிவிப்பையும் வரவேற்பதாகத் தெரிவித்தார்.

ஆனால் அதற்குப் பின்னரும் இலங்கை சிங்கள அரசு
ராஜபக்ஷேவின் சிங்கள பேரினவாதப் பிடிவாதத்தினால்
களத்தில் நின்ற போராளிகளையெல்லாம் கொன்று குவித்தனர்.
போராட்டத்தை நிறுத்துவதற்கான எவ்வளவோ முயற்சிகளை
நாம் மேற்கொண்டோம், அதற்கு உறுதுணையாக மத்திய அரசும்,
பிரதமரும், மத்திய அமைச்சர் பெருமக்களும் இருந்தும்கூட,
சிங்கள ராணுவம் தன்னுடைய தாக்குதலைத் தொடர்ந்தது.
இன்னமும் அந்தக் கொடுமை தொடர்ந்து கொண்டே இருக்கிறது.
போர் நிறுத்தப்பட்டதாகச் சிங்கள அரசு பொய் சொன்னதே ஒரு
போர்க்குற்றம்தானே?

இதற்கு வழி காணத்தான் தற்போது தி.மு.கழகம் ஆட்சியிலே
இல்லாவிட்டாலும்கூட, “டெசோ” இயக்கத்தை மீண்டும்
ஏற்படுத்தி அதிலே நானும், பேராசிரியரும், தமிழர் தலைவர்
வீரமணி அவர்களும், தொல்.திருமாவளவன் அவர்களும்,
பேராசிரியர் சுப.வீரபாண்டியன் அவர்களும், சுப்புலெட்சுமி
ஜெகதீசன் அவர்களும் உறுப்பினர்களாக இடம்பெற்று
அதன் சார்பாகப் பல போராட்டங்களையும், மாநாடுகளையும்,
கருத்தரங்குகளையும் நடத்தி வருகிறோம். அந்த வரிசையில்தான்
நாளை டெல்லியில் “டெசோ” சார்பில் பல நாட்டுப் பிரதிநிதிகளும்,
இந்திய நாட்டில் உள்ள பல்வேறு அரசியல் கட்சிகளின்

பிரமுகர்களும் கலந்துகொள்ளும் கருத்தரங்கினை நடத்துகிறோம்.
அது மாத்திரமின்றி வரும் 12ஆம் தேதியன்று தமிழகம்
முழுவதும் பொது வேலைநிறுத்தம் செய்வதென்று தீர்மானம்
நிறைவேற்றியிருக்கிறோம்.

2009ஆம் ஆண்டு இலங்கையில் போர் நடைபெற்றபோது,
நான் இந்த வயதிலும் சாகும்வரை உண்ணாவிரதம் இருக்கும்
நோக்கோடு தான் யாரிடமும் கேட்காமல் உண்ணாவிரதத்தைத்
தொடங்கினேன். அப்போது இலங்கை அரசு திடீரென்று கூடி
போர் நிறுத்தம் என்று பொய்யாக அறிவிப்பினைச் செய்யுமென்று
நான் எதிர்பார்த்தேனோ? அவர்கள் அவ்வாறு போர் நிறுத்தம்
என்று அறிவித்து, அதனை இந்திய அரசும் நம்பி எனக்குத்
தெரிவித்து, உண்ணாவிரதத்தை நிறுத்திக் கொள்ளுமாறு
கூறியதால்தான் நான் அதனை நிறுத்த நேரிட்டது.
நம்புகிறார்களோ இல்லையோ இலங்கைத் தமிழர் பிரச்சினையில்
என் மனச்சாட்சிக்கொப்ப நான் நடந்துகொண்டுதான் இருக்கிறேன்.
இலங்கைத் தமிழர்களுக்கு நல்வாழ்வு கிடைக்குமென்றால்,
அதற்காக நான் ஆட்சியை இழக்கவே தயாராக இருக்கிறேன்
என்றே பேரவையில் சொல்லியிருக்கிறேன். ஏன் ஆட்சியிலே
இருந்தபோது, இலங்கைத் தமிழர் பிரச்சினையிலே நான் அக்கறை
காட்டியதற்காகவே இரண்டு முறை ஆட்சியை இழந்திருக்கிறேன்.
சட்டப்பேரவை உறுப்பினர் பதவியையே பேராசிரியர் அவர்
களோடு சேர்ந்து ராஜினாமா செய்திருக்கிறேன். இதற்கு
மேலும் விஷமத்தனமான பிரச்சாரங்களுக்கு விளக்கமளிக்கத்
தேவையில்லை என்றே கருதுகிறேன்.

இறுதியாக 12ஆம் தேதி நடைபெறவுள்ள பொது வேலை
நிறுத்தத்தை எப்போதும் போல வெற்றிகரமாக ஆக்கித் தர
வேண்டியது தமிழகத்திலே உள்ள அனைத்துச் சாராரையும்
சேர்ந்ததாகும். தீர்மானத்தில் கேட்டுக்கொண்டவாறு கட்சி
சார்பற்ற முறையில் அனைத்துத் தரப்பினரும், சங்கடங்களைப்
பொறுத்துக் கொண்டு இந்த வேலை நிறுத்தம் என்பது நம்முடைய
தொப்புள் கொடி உறவுகொண்ட ஈழத் தமிழர்களுக்காக
நடைபெறுகிறது என்பதை உணர்ந்து, அதனை வெற்றிகரமான
ஒன்றாக ஆக்கித்தர வேண்டுமென்று கேட்டுக்கொள்கிறேன்.
இந்த நடவடிக்கைகளைப் புரிந்துகொண்டு, மத்திய அரசு

ஆணையத்திலே இலங்கை அரசின் போர்க் குற்றங்களுக்கு
எதிராக அமெரிக்கா கொண்டுவரும் தீர்மானத்தை முழுமனதாக
ஆதரிக்க முன்வர வேண்டுமென்ற வேண்டுகோளையும் விடுத்து,
அதற்கான அறிவிப்பினை மத்திய அரசு உடனடியாக வெளியிட
வேண்டுமென்றும் வலியுறுத்துகிறேன்.
