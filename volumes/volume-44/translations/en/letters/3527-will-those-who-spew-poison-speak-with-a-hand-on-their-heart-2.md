# 3527. Will Those Who Spew Poison Speak with a Hand on Their Heart? (2)

**Date:** 13 January 2011

**Udanpirappē,**

Yesterday I wrote to you in detail, with several examples, about how some journalists distort reports against the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam and the DMK government. I showed how *India Today*, under the name of a special article, had published material contrary to the truth while alleging irregularities in the scheme to provide up to two acres of land free to poor people. I now wish to give you some more examples in continuation of that letter.

You know *Dinamani*. It has continuously been publishing reports against the DMK government that, the letter says, are contrary to the truth. Whether under the heading “People’s Opinion,” as a cartoon or in an editorial, it writes as much against us as it can.

On the front page of *Dinamani* dated **10-1-2011**, in an eight-column report under the large headline **“Advertisements Worth Crores to Family Channels,”** it said that advertisements relating to the **108 free ambulance service** had been given only to Sun Television and Kalaignar Television, which it described as belonging to my family, to the extent of about **₹1 crore**. It wrote as though self-interest itself had been the reason for starting the scheme.

Poor and ordinary people of Tamil Nadu who benefit from the 108 free ambulance service openly praise the scheme every day. Many newspapers have carried articles about how successfully the scheme is operating and how it helped people survive. It is a scheme functioning with assistance from the Union Government. Some people, the letter says, cannot bear the great credit the DMK government receives because of it, and the *Dinamani* article is an expression of that resentment.

There is a subsidiary story in the *Mahabharata*, the letter recalls, that Gandhari, angered by her wish to have a child before Kunti was frustrated, struck her lower abdomen with a grinding stone. In the same way, *Dinamani*, as though wishing that some defect might somehow befall this scheme, published the headline about “advertisements worth crores to family channels.” But when one reads the report to find out how many crores are involved, it says that **₹1 crore** has so far been paid for advertisements. For how many days of advertising was this amount? Were advertisements given only to these two television companies? The newspaper, the letter says, cleverly omitted such details.

After seeing the report in *Dinamani*, I did not remain silent. I immediately asked for the complete details. I was told that, so that the benefits of the 108 free emergency-medical-vehicle service would reach people on a large scale, particularly poor people in rural areas, advertisements about the scheme were given to various television channels watched by the public and not only to Sun Television and Kalaignar Television.

I asked which other television channels received the advertisements. I was told that, besides Sun Television and Kalaignar Television, they were also given to **Podhigai Television, Doordarshan, Vijay Television, Mega Television and S.S. Music**. Is it fair, then, for *Dinamani* to suppress all those details and publish a front-page report in bold type as though advertisements had been given only to Sun and Kalaignar television channels?

It is because of such television advertising, the letter says, that the number of calls to the 108 service rose from **14,000 a day to 30,000 a day**. When a good scheme is so widely welcomed and used by people, if *Dinamani* puts a hostile twist on it and prints that on the front page in bold type, can one not understand the extent of its hostility towards this government?

Could *Theekkathir* remain still after seeing this *Dinamani* report? It seems to have thought: since *Dinamani* has carried a report, why not turn the same report into an editorial in *Theekkathir*? In great haste, it wrote an editorial attacking the Tamil Nadu government over these advertisements. Is it not the newspaper of a party eager to ally with the principal opposition party? Its editorial said that only after “looking more sharply” did it become clear that the advertisements had been given only to two television companies. Yet the government has said that the same advertisements were given not only to those two channels but also to Vijay Television, Doordarshan and Podhigai. Is that true or false?

*Theekkathir* also asked sarcastically whether Sun and Kalaignar television channels should provide their services free for a public-benefit scheme. Government advertisements about many schemes benefiting people have appeared in *Theekkathir* itself many times. On all those occasions, did *Theekkathir* say that it needed no payment and would carry the advertisements free?

Its editorial further wrote: “Those who raised the slogan ‘Tamil everywhere, Tamil in everything’ have changed into ‘corruption everywhere, corruption in everything.’” What corruption is there here? When the government gives advertisements to six organisations, it gave them to these two also. Is that corruption? If the government advertises on television channels watched by the public, is that a sign of corruption?

Does the AIADMK leader, with whom *Theekkathir*’s party is eager to form an alliance, accumulating assets worth **₹66 crore** and facing a continuing trial not look like corruption to it? Members of that very party came to the Secretariat and submitted a petition alleging that Jayalalithaa had occupied lands given by the government to “Dalits” at Siruthavur, following which the Sivasubramaniam Commission was constituted. Did that look like corruption then and truth now? Even if *Dinamani* and *Theekkathir* write false reports as though they were true, can they conceal the public support enjoyed by this scheme? The poor people whose lives have been saved through it will certainly not fall into their web of deceit.

After *India Today*, *Dinamani* and *Theekkathir*, another report appeared prominently with a photograph on the front page of *Dinamalar* dated **11-1-2011**. Its headline was **“Disabled Couple Curse After Asking for Ration Card.”** It published a photograph of two differently abled persons coming down a staircase with their child and wrote, in a manner meant to move readers, that for three years the Ramanathapuram District Collector’s office had been delaying their ration card and that the couple had wept before the *Dinamalar* reporter.

As soon as I read the report, I was distressed. I summoned my secretary, reprimanded him and ordered immediate action against anyone responsible. The Ramanathapuram District Collector was contacted by telephone. The Collector immediately conducted an inquiry and, by **10.30 a.m.**, sent a reply report by fax to the Chief Minister’s Office.

According to that reply, the couple had married for love against their parents’ wishes and had subsequently converted to Islam. They said they had applied for a ration card on **9-2-2010**, while their parents were unwilling to hand over the old family cards. The Collector’s office said that the couple had in fact submitted a petition there only on **10-1-2011**, during the public grievance day.

When the matter was investigated, there was no record of an application from them dated 9-2-2010. Examination of their parents’ family cards also showed that neither person’s name appeared on those cards. Nevertheless, because they were differently abled, a new family card was issued to them on the morning of **11-1-2011**. The Collector’s report also said that, if they had in fact applied earlier and there had truly been a delay, disciplinary action would be taken against those responsible.

Yet *Dinamalar* placed on its front page a photograph making the differently abled couple appear to be coming down the stairs in distress and wrote as though they had spent three years wandering during this government’s tenure seeking a ration card. Does that not show the depth of prejudice against this government?

Some newspapers also carried in bold type a report saying **“Women’s Commando Force in Tamil Nadu Police Disbanded.”** Since I myself hold the police portfolio, I asked when I had signed any file disbanding the women’s commando force. No order whatsoever had been issued to disband the women’s commando unit. Women commandos who completed commando training continue to serve in the Tamil Nadu Special Police units and city/district reserve forces. The reports published on that matter, the letter says, were therefore contrary to the truth.

There was once a time when, if someone told us something and we asked whether it was true, the reply “It appeared in the newspaper itself” was offered as evidence of its truth. Now, with some newspapers twisting reports to suit themselves — and, when a report can be used to criticise this government, jumping with delight, adding eyes and a nose to it, magnifying it into a monster and printing it on the front page in bold type — we are driven instead to ask: “A newspaper report? Which newspaper? What is the true report?”

Is this fair? Is this journalistic ethics? As one who has carried out measure after measure for journalists, is it not natural that these questions should arise in my mind?

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
13 January 2011

“பத்திரிகையிலேயே வந்திருக்கிறது” என்று அந்தச் செய்தி
எவ்வளவு உண்மை என்பதை விளக்கிட உதாரணமாகக் கூறிய
காலம் போய் - பத்திரிகைச் செய்தி தானே? அதுவும் அந்தப்
பத்திரிகைதானே? உண்மைச் செய்தி என்ன? என்று விசாரிக்கக்
கூடிய அளவிற்கு சில ஏடுகள் தற்போதெல்லாம் செய்திகளைத்
தங்கள் வசதிக்குத் திசை திருப்பக்கூடிய வகையில் வெளியிடுவதும்
- அதுவும் இந்த அரசைக் குறை கூறக் கூடிய செய்திகள் என்றால்,
குதூகலத்தில் துள்ளிக் குதித்து, அதற்கு கண் வைத்து, மூக்கு
வைத்து பூதாகாரமாக அதனை ஆக்கி முதல் பக்கத்திலே கொட்டை
எழுத்துக்களில் வெளியிடுவதும் நியாயம்தானா? பத்திரிகை
தர்மம்தானா? என்ற கேள்விகள் பத்திரிகையாளர்களுக்காக
அடுக்கடுக்கான சாதனைகளை நிறைவேற்றியவன் என்ற
முறையில் என்மனதில் எழுவது நியாயந்தானே?
அன்புள்ள,
மு.க.
