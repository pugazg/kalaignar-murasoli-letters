# 3785. Let the Women’s Reservation Bill Be Passed!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

The demand that women be given thirty-three per cent reservation in Parliament and the State Legislatures has been discussed across India for the past sixteen years. At every session of Parliament there is an expectation that the Women’s Reservation Bill will be passed, only for it to be left unpassed for one reason or another. Two days ago, Union Minister Mrs Jayanthi Natarajan told journalists at the airport that the Union Government remained determined to pass the Bill. She said that, when it had last been introduced in Parliament, Opposition parties had created shouting and disorder and prevented its passage; that the Government was now trying to have it passed in the coming monsoon session; and that the Opposition should cooperate.

As far back as 2010, when the DMK was in office in Tamil Nadu, the Bill was introduced in the Rajya Sabha on 8 March amid the expectation that it would at last be enacted. It was natural for the ruling Congress to issue a whip to its members, but even the principal Opposition party, the Bharatiya Janata Party, issued a whip directing its members to support the measure.

Each time people had said, “It will be passed this time,” and each time the demand had been postponed for some reason. In 2010, however, an expectation arose throughout India that it would certainly be passed.

It was while the young leader Rajiv Gandhi was Prime Minister that the issue of reserving thirty-three per cent of seats for women in Parliament and the Legislatures began to be discussed on a large scale. As an initial step, when the Panchayati Raj law relating to local bodies came into force, thirty-three per cent reservation for women in those bodies was made compulsory.

When the DMK Government under my leadership assumed office for the fourth time in 1996, we announced elections to the local bodies, which had not been held for many years. Elections were conducted to the corporations, municipalities and panchayats, enabling 1,16,747 elected representatives to take up administrative responsibility. For the first time, thirty-three per cent of every category of office in the local bodies was reserved for women. Through that major achievement, the DMK Government made it possible for 44,143 women to assume responsibilities in Tamil Nadu’s local bodies.

It was in September of that same year, 1996, that a Bill reserving seats for women in Parliament and the State Legislatures was first introduced in Parliament. Under the United Front Government headed by Prime Minister Deve Gowda, the then Union Law Minister, Mr Ramakant Khalap, introduced it as the Eighty-first Constitutional Amendment. Because of opposition from the Samajwadi Party and the Rashtriya Janata Dal, it was referred to a Joint Parliamentary Committee headed by Member of Parliament Geeta Mukherjee.

The committee’s report was placed before the Eleventh Lok Sabha on 9 December that year. The Bill was again introduced in the succeeding Twelfth Lok Sabha under the National Democratic Alliance Government, as the Eighty-fourth Constitutional Amendment, on 26 June 1998. Opposition resistance, however, forced the House to be adjourned.

It was introduced once more in the Thirteenth Lok Sabha on 22 November 1999, when Mr Vajpayee was Prime Minister. As Law Minister Ram Jethmalani sought to introduce it, Members of Parliament belonging to the Samajwadi Party and the RJD protested, snatched the copy from the Minister’s hand and tore it up. The Bill again failed.

Thereafter it was introduced once in 2002 and twice in 2003, but could not be passed. In 2008, under the United Progressive Alliance Government, Law Minister Bharadwaj introduced it in the Rajya Sabha; disorder in the House prevented its passage then too. The Women’s Reservation Bill was one of the items in the UPA’s Common Minimum Programme. On 25 August 2005, the Union Government convened consultations with the UPA parties and supporting parties—the Left, the Samajwadi Party and the Bahujan Samaj Party—apart from the parties of the National Democratic Alliance. But opposition from leaders such as Lalu Prasad Yadav and Mulayam Singh Yadav prevented a consensus during that parliamentary session, and no agreement could be reached on the draft law.

Following a resolution adopted at the DMK’s 2005 regional conference in Vellore, I issued the call, “Women of Tamil Nadu, come together.” A women’s procession was held under my leadership in Chennai on 15 September, Anna’s birthday. On 6 January 2007, the DMK’s High-Level Action Committee resolved that processions should be held in every district headquarters on 10 February demanding thirty-three per cent reservation for women; those processions were duly conducted. At the DMK Women’s Wing conference held in Cuddalore on 14 and 15 July that year, one of the resolutions demanded enactment of a law enabling women to contest thirty-three per cent of the constituencies in Parliament and the State Legislatures.

The Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam has not merely taken an interest in this question today or yesterday. For many years it has shown deep concern and has never failed to adopt resolutions at its conferences and General Council meetings. Today, throughout India, one can hear the demand that women must receive thirty-three per cent reservation.

Why is a separate reservation law required for women? Can women not contest these offices even now? Does any law prevent them?

The law does not prevent them. A woman may contest for the Assembly or Parliament just as a man may; the Constitution does not forbid it. But male domination does not permit them to do so. How long can one keep battling this ancient, orthodox convention of patriarchy? If a specified number of seats is reserved and the law provides that only women may contest them, the sacred work of laying the foundation for the rights due to women will begin.

At every level, the Constitution already provides reserved constituencies as a means of lifting up the oppressed communities described as Dalits and enabling them to secure these rights. Without disturbing that existing arrangement, socially conscious movements that wish to uphold the justice of granting rights must come forward to accept women’s reservation and make a beginning.

It is responsible to wait patiently for a consensus. But “waiting” cannot be accepted when it is used merely to postpone the demand. Even if the votes needed to pass the Bill cannot be secured, can we not at least identify before the women of India those who refuse to support it?

When this issue was intensely debated in 2010, I said that the first task was to secure thirty-three per cent reservation for women; internal reservation for the Backward Classes and Most Backward Classes could then be worked out step by step. First let us accept the invitation to the feast and enter the house. Once inside, we can say which of the dishes served agrees with our bodies and which does not, and ask that the food be served accordingly.

In 2010, *The Hindu* published an editorial titled “Don’t Fail the Women of India.” It described the UPA Government’s announcement that it intended to push the undiluted Bill forward in the coming week as the best political news in a long time, and expressed the hope that both Houses would adopt the long-pending measure on or around 8 March, the centenary of International Women’s Day.

The *Indian Express*, opening its editorial, observed that the Government’s renewed effort underlined the political value it attached to the measure more than the likelihood of enactment.

On 8 March 2010, the Bill providing thirty-three per cent reservation for women in Parliament and the State Legislatures was introduced in the Rajya Sabha amid great disorder, and the vote was postponed to the following day. The next day, amid heated controversy, shouting and confusion, the Rajya Sabha passed it. There were 186 votes in favour and only one against. Poet Kanimozhi spoke on behalf of the DMK during the debate.

When I was asked for my reaction on 9 March, I said: “Reservation is like a fruit. Taking one segment of that fruit and eating it is internal reservation. We have now obtained the fruit; let us rejoice. When the need arises, we can decide how many segments each group should receive.” I also sent congratulatory telegrams to the Prime Minister and Mrs Sonia Gandhi.

Later, when Mrs Sonia Gandhi and the Prime Minister attended the inauguration of the new Secretariat building, I addressed the gathering before them. I said that they had come to Tamil Nadu for the first time after accomplishing the historic achievement of passing the Women’s Reservation Bill in the Rajya Sabha, and asked everyone to applaud continuously for one minute as a mark of appreciation. The vast pandal resounded as the entire gathering applauded. Speaking afterwards, Mrs Sonia Gandhi said: “Kalaignar has referred here to the passage of the Women’s Reservation Bill in the Rajya Sabha. Let me say this: it would not have been possible without his support.”

The Bill passed by the Rajya Sabha could not be passed in the Lok Sabha because the required two-thirds support could not be assembled. That position continues even now. Every time Parliament meets, assurances are given that the Women’s Reservation Bill will be brought before that session and will certainly be passed.

We are the movement that enacted equal rights for women in property. Reservation for women is one of our central principles. I therefore place before all parties both my hope and my appeal that, at least in the parliamentary session to be held this coming August, the Women’s Reservation Bill will be passed with everyone’s cooperation and wholehearted support.

**Let the Women’s Reservation Bill be passed!**

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**30 July 2013**

## Letter-specific notes

1. **Internal reservation** refers here to the later distribution, within the overall quota for women, among social categories such as the Backward Classes and Most Backward Classes.
2. The source preserves the Indian parliamentary term **whip** through the Tamil form `கொறடா`.
3. English newspaper passages already printed in English in the source are paraphrased in the translation body; their source-exact wording remains in the authoritative Tamil section below.

என்பது பற்றி தேவைப்படும்போது முடிவு செய்வோம்” என்று
கூறியதோடு, பிரதமருக்கும் சோனியா காந்திக்கும் வாழ்த்துத்
தந்தியும் அனுப்பினேன்.

அதற்குப் பிறகுத் தலைமைச் செயலகத்தின் புதிய கட்டிடத்
திறப்பு விழாவில் திருமதி சோனியாகாந்தியும் பிரதமரும் கலந்து
கொண்டபோது, அவர்களுக்கு முன் நான் உரையாற்றிய நேரத்தில்,
“மகளிர் இட ஒதுக்கீடு மசோதாவை மாநிலங்களவையில்

நிறைவேற்றிவிட்டு முதன் முறையாக நமது மாநிலத்திற்கு பிரதமர்
மன்மோகன்சிங் அவர்களும், ஐக்கிய முற்போக்குக் கூட்டணித்
தலைவர் திருமதி சோனியாகாந்தி அவர்களும் வந்துள்ளனர். அந்த
வரலாற்றுச் சாதனையை நிறைவேற்றிவிட்டு முதன் முதலாக நமது
தமிழகத்திற்கு வந்துள்ள அவர்களைப் பாராட்டி மகிழ்வதற்கு
அடையாளமாக அனைவரும் ஒரு நிமிடம் தொடர்ந்து கைதட்ட
வேண்டும்” என்று கேட்டுக்கொண்டேன். அவ்வாறே விழா
நடைபெற்ற பெரும் பந்தலே அதிரும் வண்ணம் வந்திருந்த
அனைவரும் கைதட்டிப் பாராட்டி மகிழ்ந்தார்கள். அதன் பிறகு
உரையாற்றிய திருமதி சோனியாகாந்தி அவர்கள், “கலைஞர்
அவர்களே, இங்கு மாநிலங்களவையில் மகளிர் இட ஒதுக்கீடு
மசோதா நிறைவேறியது பற்றிக் குறிப்பிட்டதால் நான் ஒன்றைச்
சொல்கிறேன்; அவரது ஆதரவு இல்லாமல் இது சாத்தியமாகி
இருக்காது” என்று குறிப்பிட்டார்.

மாநிலங்களவையில் நிறைவேறிய இந்த மசோதாவை,
மக்களவையிலே நிறைவேற்ற முடியவில்லை. மூன்றில் இரண்டு
பங்கு ஆதரவினைத் திரட்ட முடியவில்லை. இந்த நிலைதான்
இப்போதும் தொடர்ந்து நீடித்து வருகிறது. நாடாளுமன்றம்
கூடுகின்ற நேரத்தில் எல்லாம் அந்தக் கூட்டத் தொடரில்
மகளிர் இட ஒதுக்கீட்டுக்கான மசோதா கொண்டுவரப்படும்
என்றும், நிச்சயம் நிறைவேற்றப்படும் என்றும் உறுதிமொழிகள்
தரப்படுகின்றன.

“பெண்களுக்குச் சொத்தில் சம உரிமை” என்று சட்டம்
நிறைவேற்றிய நம்மைப் பொறுத்தவரையில், நம்முடைய
இயக்கத்தின் முக்கிய கொள்கைகளிலே ஒன்று மகளிர்க்கு
இட ஒதுக்கீடு. அந்த முறையில் வரும் ஆகஸ்ட் திங்களில்
நடைபெறவுள்ள கூட்டத்தொடரிலாவது மகளிர்க்கான
இடஒதுக்கீடு மசோதா அனைவரது ஒத்துழைப்போடும்,
நல்லாதரவோடும் நிறைவேறும் என்ற நம்பிக்கையையும்,
நிறைவேற்றப்பட வேண்டுமென்று வேண்டுகோளையும்
முன்வைக்கிறேன். நிறைவேறட்டும் மகளிர் இடஒதுக்கீடு மசோதா!
