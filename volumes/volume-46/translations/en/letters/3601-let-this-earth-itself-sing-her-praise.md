# 3601. Let This Earth Itself Sing Her Praise!

**Date:** 19 November 2011

**Udanpirappē,**

Jayalalithaa, who somehow came to power because of the gullibility of the people of Tamil Nadu, had scarcely assumed office before, even before placing the Budget before the House, she imposed taxes amounting to about ₹4,000 crore upon the people of Tamil Nadu. Not stopping with that, she has continued her effort to undo one after another the good measures carried out under the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam government; and now, in a course of action like “biting the goat, then the cow, and then the human being,” she has announced increases at every level—tax increases, fare increases, milk-price increases, and many other increases.

Was it not because they believed they were going to “rise” that people gave her and the candidates of her front such an abundance of votes and seated them on the throne? Alas, poor people! Unable to run the administration well, Jayalalithaa is suffering—and making the people suffer too. With tax increases, fare increases and price increases severe enough to make the hearts of the poor and middle classes burst, she is now playing a game of saying “increase, increase,” pushing Tamil Nadu's poor into a pit and watching the spectacle! Even while carrying out this act of taking revenge upon the people, she has made a Bhagiratha-like effort to place the blame for it upon the previous government and the Union government.

When the DMK was in office, did the Union government not increase the prices of petrol and diesel? They did keep increasing them. Even so, did the Tamil Nadu government not bear that burden so that the people of Tamil Nadu would not be affected, and ensure that bus fares were not increased? Did it not ensure that Aavin prices did not rise excessively? Where there is a will, there is a way. Instead, in the manner of saying, “What is it to us? It is only the people who are going to suffer,” Jayalalithaa has shut her eyes and announced these fare increases.

As for bus fares, the fare presently charged for ordinary mofussil buses is 28 paise per kilometre. They have raised it by 50 per cent—that is, by another 14 paise—and fixed the new fare at 42 paise per kilometre. I wish to remind you that when the DMK assumed office in 2006, the fare for ordinary mofussil buses was 28 paise per kilometre, and during the entire five-year term it was not raised even by one paise; but within just six months of Jayalalithaa assuming office, she has raised the bus fare to 42 paise per kilometre.

For express and semi-deluxe mofussil buses, the present fare of 32 paise per kilometre has been raised by Jayalalithaa by 75 per cent—that is, by 24 paise—and she has ordered that 56 paise per kilometre be collected henceforth.

For super-deluxe and luxury mofussil buses, the present fare of 38 paise has been raised to 60 paise.

If this is the condition for mofussil buses, then for city buses outside Chennai, the present minimum fare of ₹2 has been raised by another 50 per cent—that is, by ₹1—and fixed at ₹3.

For city buses, the maximum fare presently collected is ₹7. That has been raised by about 70 per cent and fixed at ₹12.

For Chennai city buses, it has been decided to raise the present minimum fare from ₹2 to ₹3, and the maximum fare from ₹12 to ₹14.

To give examples of how much bus fares have risen: the deluxe-bus fare to Tiruchirappalli, which until now was ₹105, has been raised to ₹180. The fare to Madurai, which was ₹145, has been raised to ₹255; the fare to Tirunelveli, which was ₹200, has been raised to ₹350; and the fare to Coimbatore, which was ₹165, has been raised to ₹290. By Jayalalithaa's gracious gaze—and because the people, with generous hearts, voted for the AIADMK—these increases have come within just six months of her taking office.

In the old days, rail fares were higher and bus fares lower. That was why those with greater means travelled by train and those with fewer means by bus. But look at how much higher bus fares have now become than rail fares. To travel to Tiruchirappalli in second class by train costs only ₹80. But if one wishes to travel now in Jayalalithaa's bus, one must pay ₹180. The rail fare to Madurai is ₹108; Jayalalithaa's bus fare is ₹255. The rail fare to Thanjavur is ₹84; Jayalalithaa's bus fare is ₹210. The rail fare to Tirunelveli is ₹132; but because of Jayalalithaa's administrative prowess, if one wishes to travel by bus to that same Tirunelveli, the fare is ₹350.

It must be understood that during the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam government from 2006 until May 2011, the bus fare was not increased even by one paise; whereas Jayalalithaa, after raising bus fares during her earlier period in office, has again, within only a few months of returning to power, raised bus fares and caused hardship to the public.

Jayalalithaa has also raised the price of milk. Forgetting that this is a government, she has issued a statement like a private trader, saying that Aavin milk is sold at a much lower price than milk from private companies.

In a statement issued on 14 February 2011, Jayalalithaa had said: “The minority DMK government has become a reckless government that creates milk shortages and manufactures scarcity. Instead of calling the milk producers, holding talks and bringing the problem to an end, the DMK government is behaving like highway robbers.” Contrary to all that, she has now, without calling the milk producers and holding talks, unilaterally increased both the procurement price and the selling price.

Jayalalithaa, who acknowledged in her statement that the milk producers' demand for a higher procurement price was entirely justified, has raised the procurement price of cow's milk from the present ₹18 per litre to ₹20. She has raised the procurement price of buffalo milk from the present ₹26 per litre to ₹28. That is, she has raised the procurement price by only ₹2 per litre. At the same time, one must also keep in mind how much she has raised the selling price when that milk is sold to the public.

It has been decided to raise the price of toned milk supplied to cardholders from the present ₹17.75 per litre to ₹24. That is an increase of ₹6.25 per litre by Jayalalithaa. If very poor families at the lowest rung buy one litre of milk in the morning and one litre in the evening every day, then from now on they will have to spend ₹375 more every month from their family budget merely on milk. Poor people—they have been pushed into this position for the single offence of having voted for the AIADMK.

During the DMK government, on 1 September 2009, the procurement price of cow's milk was raised from ₹13.45 to ₹15.64 and that of buffalo milk from ₹18 to ₹23. Then, on 5 January 2011, the procurement price of cow's milk was raised from ₹15.64 to ₹16.64 and that of buffalo milk from ₹23 to ₹25.20. On 16 February 2011, the procurement price of cow's milk was raised from ₹16.64 to ₹18 and that of buffalo milk from ₹25.20 to ₹26. Yet, so that the public would not be affected, the selling price of milk was not raised by even one paise from ₹17.75 per litre. Jayalalithaa, who came to power saying that if she came to office she would reduce prices and even bend the sky into a bow, has now raised the selling price of milk by ₹6.25 per litre in one stroke. That too must be remembered.

Third, the Tamil Nadu government has submitted a petition to the Tamil Nadu Electricity Regulatory Commission seeking an increase in electricity tariffs. If that is accepted and electricity charges are raised, they expect revenue of around ₹11,700 crore. How much are they planning to increase the electricity tariff? Not by 25 paise or 30 paise per unit; newspapers have reported that they plan to increase the charge by one and a half rupees per unit.

When Jayalalithaa assumed office last May, she raised taxes enough to bring the government about ₹4,000 crore in revenue. Now she is planning further increases that could yield about another ₹11,000 crore. Apart from that, she has raised bus fares and milk prices and is preparing to squeeze the public through those as well.

Where is the DMK government that ruled for five years without raising these charges, thinking, “If electricity tariffs are raised, if bus fares are increased, if milk prices are raised, it is our people who will suffer”? And where is the Jayalalithaa government that has raised all these charges and then shifts the blame onto others, saying Karunanidhi is responsible for all of it, and so is the Union government?

Even now, *Dinamalar* cannot bring itself to abandon Jayalalithaa after she has simultaneously loaded three burdens upon the people's heads—bus-fare increase, milk-price increase and electricity-tariff increase. It has praised her by saying that she took this decision “to save the Electricity Board, the Transport Department and Aavin, all three of which are staggering under mountain-like debts and facing bankruptcy”; that “during the six months since assuming office, Jayalalithaa had implemented many welfare schemes without taking any decision that would embitter the people”; and it has described these three increases as “bitter medicine.”

Jayalalithaa, having placed all these burdens upon the people's heads, has sought to protect herself by blaming the previous DMK government, saying that the debt burden had risen during that government and that these measures are only to reduce it. It is true that public-sector institutions carry debt burdens. But it was precisely so that those burdens would not be placed upon the heads of the poor and ordinary people that the DMK government did not increase these charges.

A government exists to do good for the people—not to torment them, raise charges and take pleasure in it. During DMK rule, officials too would tell me that these charges should be increased in order to reduce the debt burdens of institutions. But is it not the public who must bear them? Where will they go? I have always said that the government should borrow, if necessary, and protect the people from harm.

I do not have the mentality of saying, “What is it to me who is affected? It is only the people who are going to face all these increases. Raise the charges! Why not announce electricity tariffs, bus fares and milk prices all at the same time?” Because there is compassion in my heart.

Should not a government think? If these three charges are raised at the same time, should it not consider how ordinary poor people, middle-class families and those who run their households by planning around a monthly salary can manage if their expenses rise by ₹500 every month? Should not at least the other senior ministers advise the Chief Minister about this? Is that not what Cabinet meetings are for? Is it good merely to sit there like dolls in a festive display, concentrating only on offering praise and eulogies?

Among the members of the public who have commented on these increases, a housewife from Tiruvallur said that because of the price rise, instead of giving milk to children, they would now be forced to give black tea without milk, and that they had been pushed back toward the Stone Age. Another housewife from Ranganathapuram said that the price increase would affect the middle class and that those who run their families according to a budget would suffer greatly.

At the end of her statement, Jayalalithaa said that a dam must be built before the flood comes and that this decision was being taken because, unless “oxygen” were supplied now to these public-sector institutions lying on their deathbeds, they would cease to function.

In truth, she is not engaged in an effort to provide “oxygen.” By loading all these burdens onto the people's heads, she has pulled out the “oxygen” tube that had until now been supplying oxygen to them. That is the truth.

Let this very earth sing the praise of Jaya—the one who has earned the distinction of placing, all at once, three burdens unprecedented in history upon the heads of Tamil Nadu's poor and middle classes—electricity-tariff increase, milk-price increase and bus-fare increase—and crushing their heads beneath them!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**19 November 2011**

## Letter-specific notes

1. The title and final sentence are sarcastic. `அவர் புகழ் பாடட்டும்` is therefore rendered as “sing her praise” without neutralising the irony.
2. The proverb-like sequence `ஆட்டைக் கடித்து, மாட்டைக் கடித்து, மனிதனைக் கடிக்கின்ற` is translated close to its source imagery rather than replaced with an unrelated English idiom.
3. `பகீரதப் பிரயத்தனம்` is represented as a “Bhagiratha-like effort,” preserving Kalaignar's cultural reference to an immense effort.
4. The source's monetary and fare figures are reproduced as printed and are not independently recalculated.
5. `கசப்பு மருந்து` is rendered as “bitter medicine,” retaining the phrase attributed to *Dinamalar*.
6. The closing “oxygen” metaphor is preserved as Kalaignar develops it: he reverses the government's claim of supplying oxygen to public-sector institutions into an accusation that the oxygen tube has been removed from the people.

தலையிலே ஒரே நேரத்தில் சுமத்திய ஜெயலலிதாவை “தினமலர்”

வகையில் எவ்வித முடிவுகளையும் எடுக்காமல், பல்வேறு நலத்
திட்டங்களை ஜெயலலிதா செயல்படுத்தி வந்தார்” என்றும்

அதிகப்படுத்த வேண்டுமென்று என்னிடமும் கூறுவார்கள்.
ஆனால் பொதுமக்கள் தானே, அதை ஏற்க வேண்டும்? அவர்கள்
