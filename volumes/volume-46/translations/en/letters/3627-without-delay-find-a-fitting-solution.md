# 3627. Without Delay — Find a Fitting Solution!

>

**Udanpirappē,**

In the list of parties that are part of the United Progressive Alliance Government at the Centre, it is not the DMK alone. The Nationalist Congress Party led by Sharad Pawar, the Trinamool Congress led by Mamata Banerjee, the National Conference led by Farooq Abdullah and some other parties together support the majority Congress Party and enable the Government at the Centre to function. All these allied parties in the Government have now refused to accept the petrol-price increase made by the Union Government. Some allies have even announced separate agitations.

The Trinamool Congress, though Mamata Banerjee is Chief Minister and the party is part of the Union Government, is itself leading a procession against the petrol-price increase today. On behalf of the DMK, we are to hold protest demonstrations in front of Union and State Government offices on the 30th. Some journalists asked me whether the DMK, while being part of the UPA Government, could conduct such a protest. I replied that no Government can function by pushing aside the welfare of the people.

As far as the DMK is concerned, whichever party we ally with at the Centre and whichever Union Cabinet we join, we do so after committing ourselves to govern with the welfare of the people foremost through “minimum programmes” — **Common Minimum Programme**. If we were simply to accept every decision taken by the party that leads the Union Government, there would be no need for an allied party at all; we might as well merge into that party. At the same time, it would not be proper for allies to trouble the ruling party to such an extent that every single decision must be taken only after consulting us; that would create administrative stagnation. But in matters such as the petrol-price increase, it is our duty as an ally to convey to the Union Government what exists in the hearts of the people.

The State secretaries of the Communist parties, comrades G. Ramakrishnan and D. Pandian, seem to think they have found an opportunity to criticise the DMK for being part of the UPA. D. Pandian has even said that the DMK should withdraw support to the Union Government over the petrol-price increase. Yet in the 2011 Tamil Nadu Assembly election both Communist parties joined hands with the AIADMK, campaigned for it and made a great effort to bring it to power in the State.

After coming to office, the AIADMK Government raised the price of milk, raised bus fares, raised electricity charges to an unprecedented degree and contributed to price rise. The two Communist parties, despite being allies of the AIADMK, did not fail to condemn these cruelties. Nor did they announce that because the increases had been made without consulting them they would stop opposing the AIADMK or immediately leave the alliance. Even while competing with one another to praise every announcement the Chief Minister made in the Assembly, they still criticised the Government’s mistakes. Yet the Chief Minister remained adamant: what she had increased would remain increased; like the saying that the hare she had caught had only three legs, she refused to accept even the requests of allied parties.

More than that: though the CPI led by D. Pandian had been one of the AIADMK’s closest allies, in the Pudukkottai Assembly by-election scheduled for 12 June, the AIADMK did not even call the CPI for a courtesy discussion in the name of coalition decorum—even though the deceased member who had earlier won that constituency for the AIADMK alliance belonged to the CPI. Before the election date itself was announced, the AIADMK unilaterally declared that it would contest and even named its candidate. Did the CPI immediately rise in anger and declare that henceforth it had no alliance with the AIADMK? Even after the CPI(M) suggested that a CPI candidate could be fielded as a common candidate and offered its support, D. Pandian did not take up the suggestion and withdrew, saying they would not contest. Is it right for him now to advise the DMK to leave the Union Government? Let him think about it himself.

Likewise the CPI(M) has said that the DMK has no qualification to oppose this price increase and is shedding crocodile tears. But even when that same CPI(M) demanded that the Jayalalithaa Government reinstate the Makkal Nala Paniyalargal and reduce bus fares, the Government did not open its mouth. Did the CPI(M), because it was an ally, therefore remain silent without raising its voice? Let them think about that too.

On the petrol-price issue, it is not only opposition parties or allied parties that disagree. Even within the ruling Congress there is no universal acceptance. Union Minister Narayanasamy himself said that the Government was considering a review of the petrol-price increase; that the minister concerned was abroad; that the Prime Minister had asked him to return to the capital immediately to discuss the issue; and that the decision might therefore change.

Union Petroleum Minister Jaipal Reddy, speaking to journalists, said that the oil companies had taken the decision to increase petrol prices, but the hardship caused to the people also had to be considered; that a Government could not act against public sentiment; that the increase arising from the international situation was neither desirable nor a happy decision; that international crude-oil prices were not permanently high but changed from time to time; and that before the Union Government decided whether to withdraw the increase, it would have to wait patiently for a few days and closely watch the situation. Yet some newspapers reported even this as though Jaipal Reddy had refused to reduce the petrol-price increase.

The public who read those newspapers are deeply confused, remembering that petrol prices were increased four times in 2011 alone and that this time the increase was a single jump of ₹7.50 per litre, unprecedented in scale.

The oil companies say they are losing ₹8 on every litre of petrol and ₹13.64 on every litre of diesel; the value of the rupee has fallen to an unprecedented level; and consequently the number of dollars that Union oil companies must spend to buy crude oil from abroad has increased. Even so, when the petrol price has now been raised by ₹7.50 per litre, look at the prices reported in other countries: ₹59 per litre in Pakistan, ₹37 in China, ₹43.40 in Bangladesh and ₹61.70 in the United States. If petrol in India is sold at ₹78 a litre, should not the Union Government consider whether this is proper?

Those in the Union Government must understand the highly agitated mood across the country. Rather than stubbornly declaring that the decision will stand, they should yield and resolve the people’s problem. That is the duty of a people’s welfare Government. Speaking as one among those who are part of the UPA Government, I request that within a day or two the petrol-price increase now imposed be substantially reduced, even if it cannot be cancelled in full, and that a good and amicable decision be reached.

At the same time, the Tamil Nadu Government too must come forward to assure the people that, as the DMK Government did earlier, it will reduce the sales tax it levies when petrol prices rise.

To press the Union and State Governments to accept and announce these demands, I ask district secretaries and frontline functionaries to make arrangements for the DMK’s protest announced for the 30th, and I ask all Kazhagam comrades to work together to make that protest a success.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**27 May 2012**
