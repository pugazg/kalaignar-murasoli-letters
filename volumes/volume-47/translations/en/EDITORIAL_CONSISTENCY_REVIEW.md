# English Editorial Consistency Review — Volume 47

## Scope

Volume-wide English editorial review for all **59 letter records, 3647–3705**, after completion of the bilingual source-alignment gate.

This pass follows the established Volume 49 editorial model while preserving Volume 47's completed Tamil-source alignment. It is an editorial consistency gate, not a new translation or historical fact-checking pass.

## Review rules

The pass checks cross-letter consistency in:

- YAML metadata and heading structure;
- translator-note function and source-authority wording;
- retention and spelling of `Udanpirappē`;
- personal names and Tamil transliteration where the source supplies them;
- recurring institutional, political and legal terminology;
- dates, numbers, currency and source-page presentation;
- quotation and attribution treatment;
- sensitive-content framing;
- `With affection, / M.K. / date` closing conventions;
- preservation of the explicit source-incomplete treatment of letter 3681.

The governing rule remains: **Tamil is authoritative.** Editorial smoothing must not silently add facts, remove political force, modernise period claims into present-day claims, or repair a source gap from outside knowledge.

## Findings

### 1. Translation architecture

The records consistently use the same archival structure: YAML metadata, English title, translator's note, Tamil-source link, source-page range, date, English translation, and embedded original Tamil. No competing translation architecture needs consolidation.

### 2. Address and closing

`Udanpirappē` is consistently retained as Kalaignar's distinctive form of address rather than flattened into a generic English kinship term. The English closings retain the source's affectionate epistolary character through `With affection,` followed by `M.K.` and the source date where present.

### 3. Translator's notes

The notes vary naturally in wording according to the content of individual letters, but consistently identify the translation as thought-preserving/non-literary, keep Tamil authoritative, and identify source-specific features that must survive translation. This variation is editorially useful rather than a defect; forcing every note into identical prose would erase letter-specific cautions.

### 4. Names, institutions and terminology

No volume-wide terminology conflict requiring a source-aligned translation rewrite was identified. Political parties, governments, courts, commissions, statutory bodies, offices and public institutions remain named in forms that are intelligible in English while preserving the source's period framing.

### 5. Dates, figures and source references

The translation records retain their source-derived dates, figures and page ranges. Editorial consistency does not override the bilingual-alignment gate on factual particulars.

### 6. Rhetoric and political voice

The English remains deliberately direct where the Tamil is direct. Rhetorical questions, repetition, accusation, irony and political contrast are not neutralised merely to make the prose sound smoother in English. This is treated as a fidelity requirement rather than an inconsistency.

### 7. Sensitive material

Letters containing descriptions of suicide, sexual violence, criminal punishment or the death penalty remain source translations rather than present-day editorial endorsements. The editorial pass does not euphemise away source-supported content or introduce new factual detail.

### 8. Letter 3681 — source-incomplete exception

Letter **3681** remains the only source-incomplete record. Printed page **252** is absent from the only source PDF. The English therefore remains intentionally limited to the available Tamil source; the missing continuation, closing and printed date are not reconstructed from outside evidence. The date provenance remains explicitly documented rather than silently presented as scan-verified text.

## Corrections required

**No source-aligned translation rewrite is required by this volume-level consistency pass.**

The review found no cross-letter editorial inconsistency serious enough to justify altering already aligned English prose. Minor differences in translator-note wording and sentence rhythm are retained where they reflect the source-specific character of individual letters.
