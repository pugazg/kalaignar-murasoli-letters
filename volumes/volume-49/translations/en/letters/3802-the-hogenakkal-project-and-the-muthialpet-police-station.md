# 3802. The Hogenakkal Project and the Muthialpet Police Station!

**Udanpirappē,**

On 29 May 2013, the Chief Minister inaugurated the Hogenakkal Drinking Water Project by video conference while seated at Fort St George. In her speech, as usual, she made several allegations against the DMK. I answered them in detail on 31 May 2013.

Chief Minister Jayalalithaa claimed that the Hogenakkal project was accelerated only after the AIADMK assumed office. I explained clearly under whose Government the project was rapidly planned, accelerated and implemented; how earnestly Thalapathi M.K. Stalin, then the Minister responsible for the department in the DMK Government, drove it forward; and how a timetable was prepared under which the work was organised to finish by December 2012, with construction proceeding accordingly during the DMK Government.

I was not alone in saying that the Hogenakkal works were incomplete and that, for some reason, this Government hurried to have the Chief Minister inaugurate the scheme before completion. I cited a photograph published by *Dinamalar* itself as evidence that work was still continuing. Yet the Chief Minister has still not answered my question: why was the project inaugurated in such haste?

Proving that what I said was true, *The Hindu* reported: “People in many rural areas were upset that the water did not reach them.”

A report in *Dinamalar* dated 12 August 2013 said that the Hogenakkal Combined Drinking Water Project, launched to supply safe water to the people of Dharmapuri and Krishnagiri districts, remained incomplete. Rural areas were again being supplied fluoride-contaminated water. Groundwater in the two districts contained fluoride above the prescribed level, causing bone and nerve disorders and staining teeth because there was no way to purify it.

The report recalled that the ₹1,928-crore Hogenakkal project had been introduced in 2008, during the DMK Government, with Japanese financial assistance to supply safe drinking water to both districts. Though the work was incomplete, Chief Minister Jayalalithaa inaugurated it by video conference from Chennai. In parts of Uthangarai and Hosur in Krishnagiri district and Harur and Pappireddipatti taluks in Dharmapuri, work was still only at an initial stage. Fifteen to twenty per cent of the project remained unfinished and had been left pending. Although water was supplied to the municipal areas of Dharmapuri, Hosur and Krishnagiri, even those areas could not receive it fully. The two districts were not receiving Hogenakkal water to the extent originally planned. In rural areas, borewell water and Hogenakkal water were being mixed, leaving people still dependent upon fluoride-contaminated water.

Neither my writing nor the reports of *Dinamalar* and *The Hindu* received any reply from the ruling party. Then, on 24 August 2013, *The Hindu* published another detailed illustrated report on the present condition of the scheme that Jayalalithaa had hurriedly inaugurated.

The substance of that report was this: more than two months after the ₹1,928-crore project was launched to meet the needs of Krishnagiri and Dharmapuri districts, rural residents who were supposed to benefit still could not do so. Officials blamed pending pipeline work. The project had been designed to draw 16 crore litres a day from the Cauvery because the districts’ groundwater contained excessive fluoride. Yet even people living beside the Cauvery continued to buy a pot of water for ₹3 or ₹4. The project was meant to serve nearly 33 lakh people in the municipalities of Krishnagiri, Dharmapuri and Hosur and fifteen town panchayats, but 6,755 rural habitations—roughly half the intended coverage—were still unable to obtain drinking water.

The reporter also interviewed residents. K. Chinnaponnu, a widow running a snack stall beside the river at Nagamarai in Pennagaram taluk, said: “The Cauvery may be overflowing its banks, but the people of our village are suffering without drinking water because pipelines have not been laid properly.” R. Chinnamma and M. Mala of Eriyur said that not even the pipes had yet been laid under the scheme. A. Anbazhagan, who had been selling groundwater brought by vehicle for six months, said that he bought 1,000 litres from a private supplier for ₹50 and resold it.

*The Hindu* carried several more interviews. The DMK Government had worked hard to begin this scheme to provide safe and good drinking water to the people of Dharmapuri and Krishnagiri. But, to create the impression that they themselves had completed it, the AIADMK Government launched it hastily and only partially before the work was finished. I explained this at the very time of its inauguration.

Three months have passed since Chief Minister Jayalalithaa launched the Hogenakkal Combined Drinking Water Project. Yet the AIADMK Government has still been unable to supply drinking water to about 90 per cent of Krishnagiri district. In Krishnagiri and Hosur municipalities in particular, water is supplied only once a week through old reservoirs and old pipelines, and even that is said to be fluoride-contaminated. Water scarcity rages across the districts, forcing mothers to take empty pots onto the roads in protest.

To demand full supply of Hogenakkal water to the people of Krishnagiri and Dharmapuri, the Krishnagiri district DMK planned demonstrations in every union on 29 August 2013. Applications seeking police permission were submitted ten days in advance, and the demonstrations were publicised through newspapers and other media.

Yet on the night before the protest, the police pasted notices in all the unions denying permission. Disregarding the refusal, the DMK conducted the demonstrations. Mothers raised slogans with empty pots at ten locations. Across the district, 10,000 people—including District Secretary T. Senguttuvan—participated and were arrested. These details show that Chief Minister Jayalalithaa hurried to inaugurate the Hogenakkal project before its work was complete.

The Government refuses to accept the true position when opposition parties state it. It pays no attention even when newspapers explain it with evidence. What can anyone do? The AIADMK Government appears to believe that, complete or incomplete, it will inaugurate a scheme and the people are fated to accept it. The Hogenakkal Combined Drinking Water Project is another example of that attitude.

When a project was planned during the DMK Government, this regime seeks to hold its closing ceremony; it obstructs rapidly progressing infrastructure work; and, where a scheme cannot be prevented from opening, it implements it only partially. This has become a continuing story under the AIADMK Government. Hogenakkal is an example of hastily opening an unfinished project. Let me now give an example of completed work waiting merely for the Chief Minister’s date.

Chief Minister Jayalalithaa repeatedly claims that she has done this and that for the Police Department. Yet a report says that the Muthialpet Police Station in Chennai is functioning from an unused Corporation school. It has operated there for three years. A new building has already been completed for the station, but is reportedly waiting for a date from the Chief Minister for its inauguration.

The two-storey school building now housing the police station has been declared dangerous by the Public Works Department. Yet police officers entrusted with protecting people’s lives continue to work there. The new three-storey police-station building, constructed at a cost of ₹80 lakh with thirty rooms, has remained locked for a year after completion.

Similarly, the Sembium Police Station has spent three years in a rented building. About 120 police personnel work there in inadequate conditions. A new station building is ready, but no inauguration date has yet been obtained.

At least after reading this, I hope Chief Minister Jayalalithaa—besides writing daily letters to the Prime Minister—will set aside five minutes to open these police-station buildings, even by video conference. Until then, it would be prudent for police officials not to tell her that these buildings were planned during the DMK Government!

I have given two examples: unfinished work inaugurated in haste, and completed work left unopened while time passes. At least now, when opposition parties point out such shortcomings, the Chief Minister should not immediately unleash the police with defamation cases. Should she understand the truth and try to serve the people, she may yet earn some credit for having been of at least limited use to Tamil Nadu during the remaining two and a half years of her Government.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**

6 September 2013
