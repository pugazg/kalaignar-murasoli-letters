# 3688. The Pitiful Ruin of the School Education Department!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This is a thought-preserving, non-literary translation. It retains the source's argument, figures, quoted allegations, rhetorical questions and political framing. Tamil remains authoritative. `Udanpirappē` is retained as Kalaignar's distinctive form of address.

## English translation

Udanpirappē,

For a country's progress, advancement of education is indispensable. Governments therefore devote large sums to education. In Tamil Nadu, the allocation for school education, which was ₹4,348 crore under the AIADMK Government in 2005–06, rose to ₹10,148 crore under the DMK Government in 2010–11. Though the AIADMK Government raised it further to ₹14,553 crore in last year's Budget, the administration has not functioned so that the benefits reach students and teachers at the proper time.

During the 2001–06 AIADMK regime, 45,987 teachers had been appointed on consolidated pay—roughly one-third of what they should have received. When the DMK assumed office in 2006, it placed them on regular time-scale pay, roughly tripling their salaries. After 2006, 26,541 teachers were appointed in elementary education—12,426 secondary-grade and 14,115 graduate teachers. In school education another 27,739 were appointed: 70 secondary-grade teachers, 17,045 graduate teachers, 4,665 teacher trainers, 3,002 postgraduate teachers, 525 Tamil teachers, 1,131 special teachers, 140 vocational teachers and 1,686 computer teachers. District Institutes of Education and Training received 216 lecturers and 32 senior lecturers. Altogether, 55,053 teachers were newly appointed under the DMK Government.

In addition, 1,140 non-teaching employees, 449 employees on compassionate grounds and 176 agriculture instructors for government higher secondary schools were appointed. Between 2006 and 2010, 1,114 qualified persons with disabilities were appointed as teachers in government schools on priority.

With so many appointments under the DMK Government, was there any complaint? Any allegation of malpractice? Any such confusion? What is the position now under the AIADMK Government?

The front-page headline in Dinamalar dated 26 December 2012 was: “Irregularities in selection of 2,000 postgraduate teachers! Jobs given to the unqualified exposed!” Its report alleged that after irregularities in secondary-grade and graduate-teacher selection, major irregularities had emerged in postgraduate-teacher recruitment; physically fit candidates had been selected under the disabled category; people without the proper educational qualifications had entered the final list; pressure from official circles had turned TET recruitment into a tangled mess; and appointment orders had been issued to 18,000 people without proper certificate verification, forcing authorities to re-check certificates after they had already joined duty.

The allegations continued. On another page the same newspaper carried a large report saying 1,000 vocational-teacher posts were vacant and course groups were being closed in higher secondary schools.

Dinamani on the 27th, however, reported officials as saying that most appointees faced no obstacle in joining, only one or two lacked qualifications, the list had been prepared hurriedly at the last moment and was therefore rechecked, minor problems were natural in any very large recruitment, and only candidates claiming Tamil-medium priority required another verification because false certificates had been submitted.

Our question is simple: why were appointment orders issued in such haste without first verifying the certificates? How much confusion and irregularity did that create?

Full-page advertisements had announced that on the 13th the Chief Minister herself would hand appointment orders to 26,000 teachers, and a huge function was organised. Yet she personally gave orders to only 36 teachers, while others distributed the rest. Dinamalar had already reported on the 14th, under the headline “Women teachers suffered at the Chief Minister's function,” that selected teachers were brought to Chennai in 650 buses from the morning of the 12th, housed in large private schools around Pallavaram, given hardly any rest, told to bathe and get ready, and transported from 2 a.m. to the Nandanam venue. With women forming the majority, toilet facilities were inadequate; mobile toilets lacked water, queues exceeded twenty people, and those seated there since the previous night complained of severe hardship and mosquito bites.

Normally certificates are checked repeatedly before appointment orders are issued. This time, for want of time, orders were prepared and issued before verification was completed. If ineligible candidates are now found during post-appointment verification, their selections will have to be cancelled.

Before verification, the Government should also have sent clear instructions on which subjects counted as employment qualifications, which did not, and how Tamil-medium priority was to be applied. Because it did not, different verification teams reportedly followed different approaches.

Another report last week said 7,000 non-teaching posts were vacant in aided schools. Because those posts remain unfilled, teachers are made to perform that work, affecting their teaching of students.

Then came an order requiring about eleven lakh Class X examination candidates to upload all their details online by 4 January, leaving only five days. With eighteen-hour power cuts and some schools lacking computers and internet access, how could every student complete the process by then? I had asked that the deadline be extended, yet there was no action from the Government.

One could go on. From its handling of the Samacheer Kalvi scheme after taking office last year, the education department has proceeded as though the first crooked step made everything crooked. How many ministers have held the portfolio in these eighteen months? How many senior officials have been transferred? How many posts remain vacant? These themselves show how school education functions under the AIADMK Government. If school education is ruined, it is the students' future that suffers. With irregularities and deficiencies everywhere, does the department not now resemble a pond drying up before our eyes?

With affection,
M.K.
28-12-2012
