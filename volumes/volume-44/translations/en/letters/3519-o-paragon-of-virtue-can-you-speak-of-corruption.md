# 3519. O Paragon of Virtue! Can You Speak of Corruption?

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3519](../../../chapters/3519-uththamigaal-neevir-oozhal-patrip-pesalaamaa.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 257](../../../pages/page-257.md)–[PDF 264](../../../pages/page-264.md)  
**Date:** 20 December 2010

**Udanpirappē,**

When I see Jayalalithaa’s statement on the spectrum issue, stretching her accusation as usual and declaring that most politically powerful people at both the Union and State levels stood behind this corruption and aided it, I can only laugh. Would one not laugh when Jayalalithaa issues a statement accusing others of corruption? She even says in that statement that she welcomes the Supreme Court ruling that the Court will monitor the Central Bureau of Investigation inquiry.

Has the Supreme Court delivered a judgment only in this one case? What was the Supreme Court’s judgment about Jayalalithaa buying Tamil Nadu government-owned TANSI land at a throwaway price while she was Chief Minister? Should she not have welcomed that judgment too?

The letter then reproduces what it describes as important portions of the TANSI judgment. It says that land belonging to the public sector was sold to companies in which Jayalalithaa was a partner while she was Chief Minister; that under TANSI rules government approval had to be obtained before the sale and that, until then, the sale was not complete. In this case, it says, approval for the sale had to come from the very government headed by the first accused, Jayalalithaa, creating a conflict of interest because the government itself had to exercise its authority.

Where such conflicting interests exist, the letter’s rendering says, the law should have avoided a sale to a person who had the opportunity to undermine fair sale procedures.

Whatever conclusion might be reached about the value of the properties, it says, the more significant point was that once the Chief Minister herself sought to purchase them, the bureaucracy displayed excessive interest and smoothly completed the sale at the price desired by Chief Minister Jayalalithaa. In any event, the acts attributed to her were contrary to the spirit of the rules of conduct.

Speaking fairly, the letter asks, can there be one law for minor government officials and another for a Chief Minister? In such circumstances, is a code of conduct merely an ornamental object to be kept in a museum, or is it something that must be observed? These matters, the quoted reasoning says, troubled the Court’s conscience.

It further says that Jayalalithaa, the first accused, went so far in trying to escape the case as to deny the very signature on the TANSI deed that her auditor and government officials had accepted as hers.

The source then prints the following English passage, preserved here verbatim:

> Further, criminal law is meant to deal with criminals ordinarily,
> while code of conduct is observed as gentlemen’s agreement. Good
> ethical behaviour on the part of those who are in power is the hallmark
> of a good administration and people in public life must perform their
> duties in a spirit of public service rather than by assuming power to
> indulge in callous cupidity, regardless of self imposed discipline.
> Irrespective of the fact whether we reach the conclusion that A.1 is
> guilty of the offences with which she is charged or not, she must atone
> for the same by answering her conscience in the light of what we have
> stated not only by returning the property to TANSI unconditionally but
> also ponder over whether she had done the right thing in breaching the
> spirit of the Code of Conduct and giving rise to suspicion that rules
> and procedures were bent to acquire the public property for personal
> benefit, though trite to say that suspicion however strong cannot take
> place of legal proof in a criminal case and take steps to expiate herself.

Did Jayalalithaa welcome this Supreme Court judgment? Was she ashamed? Did she give room to her conscience? Is a person with such a record qualified now to issue statements about corruption?

The letter next refers to the disproportionate-assets case alleging accumulation of ₹66 crore beyond known income and says the case had been proceeding against Jayalalithaa for seven years. It recalls the Supreme Court judgment of 18-11-2003 by Justices S. N. Variava and H. K. Sema transferring the trial to Karnataka in order, as the letter renders it, to preserve the sanctity of the administration of justice and the confidence ordinary people place in it.

Does Jayalalithaa cooperate, the letter asks, so that the Bengaluru trial may be completed quickly in accordance with that judgment? It accuses her instead of trying to delay it as far as possible, including by appealing again to the Supreme Court, which the letter says refused to stay the trial. Is that not also a Supreme Court judgment?

The letter then recalls an income-tax prosecution alleging that Jayalalithaa had not filed her return for 1993–94. It says that on 24-2-2006, while she was Chief Minister, Supreme Court Justices B. N. Agrawal and A. K. Mathur criticised her in the following words:

> “You (Jayalalitha) are making a mockery of the Judicial Process. How long you can drag the proceedings?”

Did Jayalalithaa respect that Supreme Court observation? Welcome it? Issue a statement about it?

The letter next reproduces a list that, it says, had appeared in all newspapers some years earlier concerning articles recovered during a search of Jayalalithaa’s residence and their stated values:

- 86 categories of Jayalalithaa’s jewellery — ₹17 lakh 50 thousand 31;
- 62 categories of Sasikala’s jewellery — ₹9 lakh 38 thousand 460;
- 26 categories of Jayalalithaa’s jewellery — ₹19 lakh 30 thousand 852;
- 41 categories of Jayalalithaa’s jewellery — ₹1 crore 40 lakh 75 thousand 958;
- 394 categories of Jayalalithaa’s jewellery — ₹3 crore 12 lakh 67 thousand 725;
- 34 categories of Sasikala’s jewellery — ₹17 lakh 54 thousand 868;
- 1,116 kilograms of silver vessels — ₹48 lakh 80 thousand;
- investment in Indian Bank, Abhiramapuram branch — ₹1 crore;
- luxury bus TN 09-F 2575 — ₹32 lakh 40 thousand 278;
- 91 wristwatches — ₹6 lakh 87 thousand 350;
- seven expensive wristwatches — ₹9 lakh 3 thousand;
- 914 new silk sarees — ₹61 lakh 13 thousand 700;
- 6,195 other new sarees — ₹27 lakh 8 thousand 720;
- 2,140 old sarees and other clothes — ₹4 lakh 21 thousand 870; and
- 389 pairs of footwear — ₹2 lakh 902.

How did Jayalalithaa obtain all these properties, the letter asks rhetorically? Were they not assets arising from corruption?

According to details stated in the Rajya Sabha, the letter says, Jayalalithaa’s assets were valued at ₹2 crore 1 lakh 83 thousand on 1-7-1991. She was Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu from 1991 until May 1996. On 30-4-1996, her assets were valued at ₹66 crore 44 lakh 73 thousand. How, it asks, did these ₹66 crore of assets arise? Is that not why the case is now being tried in the Bengaluru Special Court under the Supreme Court’s order?

The letter then enumerates seven observations it attributes to the Supreme Court while transferring the disproportionate-assets case to Bengaluru:

1. After Jayalalithaa again became Chief Minister, many witnesses changed from statements they had previously given.
2. If a trial is not conducted independently and fairly, public confidence in the system of justice will be damaged.
3. The manner in which the trial was proceeding created a suspicion that justice itself might be defeated.
4. Jayalalithaa, though accused in the case and present in Chennai, did not appear before the trial court when required by the court.
5. A procedure permitting a questionnaire only where an accused was far away — treated by the Supreme Court as an exception in one case — could not properly be applied here.
6. Of 76 prosecution witnesses recalled after the change of government, 64 had turned hostile; the failure of the public prosecutor to proceed against them was also worthy of censure.
7. To preserve the sanctity of the administration of justice and ordinary people’s confidence in it, the trial was being transferred to a Karnataka court.

The letter says Justices S. N. Variava and H. K. Sema gave this judgment on 18-11-2003 and, in concluding it, directed in substance that because the Court considered there to be sufficient prima facie material in the case pursued by the DMK and because a trial in Tamil Nadu might fail to inspire confidence in justice, the case should be transferred.

It reproduces further directions: the Chief Justice of the Karnataka High Court, in consultation with the Karnataka government, should constitute within six weeks a Special Court in Bengaluru under the Prevention of Corruption Act; a senior advocate experienced in criminal cases should be appointed public prosecutor with a junior advocate to assist; Karnataka should initially meet all expenses, including lawyers’ fees, for later reimbursement by Tamil Nadu; the appointed prosecutor should recall and re-examine prosecution witnesses who had turned hostile and separately initiate criminal proceedings against witnesses who deliberately changed their evidence; Karnataka should give adequate police protection if witnesses were threatened; all accused should appear without fail when questioned; the case should be heard **(Daily Basis)**; and Tamil Nadu should immediately transmit all case records to the Special Court.

Can Jayalalithaa, with all this “fame and distinction,” issue statements about corruption?

Can Jayalalithaa, now waiting to see what judgment will follow after examination of witnesses in the Bengaluru Special Court, issue statements about corruption? Would those who read them not laugh? Can she question others about corruption while regarding herself as so virtuous that she might ask, “What price is corruption?”

And why, the letter finally asks, are the major opposition parties that prevented Parliament from functioning and insisted that a Joint Parliamentary Committee must be formed no longer raising their voices so fiercely about it? Why? Why?

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
20 December 2010

30-4-1996 அன்று ஜெயலலிதாவின் சொத்துக்களின் மதிப்பு 66 கோடியே 44 இலட்சத்து 73 ஆயிரம் ரூபாய். எப்படி வந்தது இந்த 66 கோடி ரூபாய் சொத்துக்கள்? இதற்காகத்தானே இப்போது பெங்களூரில் சிறப்பு நீதி மன்றத்தில் உச்ச நீதிமன்றத் தீர்ப்புப்படி வழக்கு நடைபெறுகிறது!

சொத்துக் குவிப்பு வழக்கினை வேறு மாநிலத்துக்கு - பெங்களூருக்கு மாற்றி தீர்ப்பு வழங்கியபோது உச்ச நீதிமன்றம் தெரிவித்தவை வருமாறு :-

நடத்தினால் நியாயம் கிடைக்காமல் போக வாய்ப்புள்ளது. எனவே இவ்வழக்கு மாற்றப்பட வேண்டிய வழக்கேயாகும். நீதி வழங்கப்படுவது முக்கியமெனினும், நீதி வழங்கப்படுவதாக மக்களிடத்தில் நம்பிக்கையை ஏற்படுத்துவதும் நீதிமன்றங்களின் கடமையாகும்; எனவே இந்த வழக்கு மாற்றல் செய்யப்படுகிறது. கர்நாடக உயர் நீதிமன்றத்தின் தலைமை நீதிபதி, கர்நாடக அரசோடு கலந்து பேசி, இந்த வழக்கிற்காக ஒரு சிறப்பு நீதி மன்றத்தை ஊழல் தடுப்புச் சட்டத்தின்கீழ் ஆறு வார காலத்திற்குள் பெங்களூரில் அமைக்க வேண்டும். குற்றவியல் வழக்குகளில் பயிற்சி பெற்ற முதுநிலை வழக்கறிஞர் ஒருவரை இந்த வழக்கில் அரசு வழக்கறிஞராக நியமிப்பதுடன், அவருக்கு இளநிலை வழக்கறிஞர் ஒருவரும் அமர்த்தப்பட வேண்டும். வழக்கறிஞர் ஊதியம் உட்பட, கட்டணங்கள் அனைத்தையும் கர்நாடக அரசு செலுத்த வேண்டும்; பிறகு தமிழ்நாடு அரசு அதனை கர்நாடக அரசுக்குத் திருப்பிச் செலுத்த வேண்டும். தலைமை நீதிபதியினால் நியமிக்கப்படும் அரசு வழக்கறிஞர் ஏற்கெனவே பிறழ் சாட்சியம் அளித்த - பல்டி அடித்த அரசுத் தரப்பு சாட்சிகளை (Hostile) மீண்டும் அழைத்து மறு விசாரணை செய்வதுடன், வேண்டுமென்றே மாற்றி சாட்சியமளித்த அந்தச் சாட்சிகள் மீது குற்றவியல் நடவடிக்கைகளை தனியே எடுக்கவும் அறிவுறுத்தப்படுகின்றது. சாட்சிகளுக்கு மிரட்டுதல் இருப்பதாக கருதும் பட்சத்தில், அவர்களுக்கு கர்நாடக அரசு போதிய போலீஸ் பாதுகாப்பு வழங்க வேண்டும். குற்றவாளிகள் அனைவரும் கேள்வி எழுப்பப்படும்போது நீதிமன்றத்தில் தவறாது ஆஜராவதுடன், இவ்வழக்கை தினமும் (Daily Basis) எடுத்து விசாரிக்க வேண்டும். வழக்கு தொடர்புடைய அனைத்து ஆவணங்களையும் தமிழ்நாடு அரசு உடனடியாக சிறப்பு நீதி மன்றத்திற்கு அனுப்பி வைக்க வேண்டும்” என்று கூறியிருக்கிறார்கள்.

இவ்வளவு புகழுக்கும், பெருமைக்கும் உரிய ஜெயலலிதா ஊழல் பற்றி அறிக்கை விடலாமா?

பெங்களூர் சிறப்பு நீதி மன்றத்தில் தற்போது நடைபெறும் சாட்சிகளின் விசாரணை முடிந்து என்ன தீர்ப்பு அளிக்கப்படவிருக்கிறதோ என்று காத்திருக்கும் நிலையிலே உள்ள ஜெயலலிதா ஊழலைப் பற்றி அறிக்கை விடலாமா? அதைப் படிப்பவர்கள் சிரிக்க மாட்டார்களா? ஊழல் என்றால் என்ன விலை என்று கேட்கக்கூடிய அளவிற்கு தன்னை உத்தமியாகக் கருதிக் கொண்டு, ஜெயலலிதா ஊழலைப் பற்றி கேள்வி கேட்கலாமா?

நாடாளுமன்றக் கூட்டுக் குழு அமைத்தே தீரவேண்டுமென்று நாடாளுமன்றத்தையே நடத்த விடாமல் செய்த பெரிய எதிர்க்கட்சிகள் எல்லாம் இப்போது அதைப் பற்றி வேகமாகக் குரல் கொடுக்க வில்லையே; அது ஏன்? ஏன்?
