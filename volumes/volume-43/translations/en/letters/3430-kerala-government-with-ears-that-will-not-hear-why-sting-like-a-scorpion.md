# 3430. Kerala Government, With Ears That Will Not Hear; Why Sting Like a Scorpion!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This pilot translation follows the audited canonical Tamil and preserves the letter’s legal chronology, political criticism, dam-level figures, dated correspondence, institutional names, and the source’s printed Supreme Court English passages. The source’s title imagery — ears that will not hear and a scorpion’s sting — is retained rather than neutralised. Legal and technical claims remain source-framed and are not reconciled with outside material.

**Udanpirappē,**

The Mullai Periyar issue is now under hearing in the Supreme Court. Lawyers appearing for the Tamil Nadu government, the Kerala government and the Union Government are arguing there. While all parties in Kerala stand united and put forward their demands on behalf of the Kerala government, the painful position for Tamil Nadu is that, when we place our arguments, we must also answer those here who call themselves Tamils but issue statements with the single concern of somehow finding fault with the Tamil Nadu government. They have no concern for Mullai Periyar; their concern is to find some fault with us. Whatever the case, I wish to set out here the true details concerning the Mullai Periyar issue.

This issue has continued from 1886 — from 29-10-1886, when the Periyar lease agreement was entered into between the Maharaja of Travancore and the Madras Presidency. In the cases filed in the Supreme Court by the Tamil Nadu government concerning the raising of the water level in the Mullai Periyar Dam, the Supreme Court delivered judgment on 27-2-2006.

In that judgment, it was stated that the Mullai Periyar Dam was strong; that Tamil Nadu could, as a first stage, store water up to 142 feet from the existing level of 136 feet; and that, because of continuing obstruction by the Kerala government, Tamil Nadu had still been unable to complete the remaining works recommended by the Central Water Commission. It was further stated that after the Tamil Nadu government completed those works to the satisfaction of the Central Water Commission, an independent expert committee should inspect the dam regarding storage up to the dam’s maximum water level of 152 feet.

But the Kerala government continues to create ways of obstructing implementation of this judgment:

> The Kerala Government having adopted an obstructionist approach cannot now be permitted to take shelter under the plea that these are disputed questions of fact

The judgment also stated that Kerala should be directed not to create any obstruction to Tamil Nadu carrying out strengthening works as recommended by the Central Water Commission, and that there was no reason for the Kerala government to cause such obstruction:

> In this regard, at this stage the only prayer of the State of Tamil Nadu is that State of Kerala be directed not to obstruct it in carrying out the strengthening measures as suggested by Central Water Commission. We see no reason for the State of Kerala to cause any obstruction

It further said that the State of Kerala and its officers should not create any obstruction:

> The State of Kerala and its Officers are restrained from causing any obstruction.

Instead of implementing the Supreme Court judgment dated 27.2.2006, the Kerala government, within 16 days of the judgment, on 18-3-2006, brought amendments to its Irrigation and Water Conservation Act, 2003, and passed them in the Legislative Assembly against that judgment.

Seeking an injunction against this, the Jayalalithaa government filed a case in the Supreme Court only 13 days later, on 31.3.2006. It stopped with filing the case. While Jayalalithaa remained in office, no attention was given to taking the legal procedural steps required to register the Supreme Court case properly, obtain its registration number, or bring it up for hearing. Nor, until the Jayalalithaa government left office, was any proper legal effort made even to obtain an injunction.

After the change of government in Tamil Nadu in May 2006 and the formation of the government under my leadership, all the legal steps in that case were taken. It was the DMK government that obtained the registration number that constitutes the first stage of the case. It was the DMK government that brought the case up for hearing.

When the case came up on 25.9.2006, the Supreme Court advised the two State governments to hold talks in the presence of the Union Government and attempt to resolve the issue amicably.

Accordingly, in Delhi on 29.11.2006, in the presence of the Union Minister for Irrigation, I and Kerala Chief Minister Achuthanandan held talks. It was during those talks that the Kerala Chief Minister first advanced the proposal for a new dam. The Tamil Nadu government did not accept it. Therefore no conclusion was reached in the talks.

The Supreme Court was informed of these talks and, on the request of the Tamil Nadu government that the case be taken up again, the Supreme Court resumed hearing it.

Meanwhile, on the basis of a suggestion made by the Prime Minister of India, Dr. Manmohan Singh, the Tamil Nadu government again held talks with the Kerala government on 19.12.2007 at Tamil Nadu House in Delhi in an effort to find an amicable solution. As no conclusion was reached in these talks either, the Supreme Court constituted a special bench to hear the issue and has been hearing the case from 21.10.2009.

In the Mullai Periyar issue, the Kerala government has continued to create obstacles for Tamil Nadu in several ways. This government has continued to take legal action to implement the Supreme Court judgment. The principal obstructions created by the Kerala government include the following:

- On 19.11.2006, the water-measuring instrument at Mullai Periyar was removed by Kerala’s Revenue Divisional Officer.
- On 23.11.2006, the Kerala government invited naval officers from Kochi to inspect the Periyar Dam. The Union Government was informed that this was illegal; the Prime Minister of India was also informed that it could create a situation in which Tamil Nadu would be unable to participate in the forthcoming talks, and that all developments would immediately be brought to the Supreme Court’s attention. As a result of this action, the naval officers returned without undertaking any work.
- On 31.12.2006, some unidentified persons damaged the dam’s parapet wall. In my letter to the Hon’ble Prime Minister of India on 2.1.2007, I again requested, as Tamil Nadu had already done, that the Central Industrial Security Force be sent to protect the Mullai Periyar Dam. This request was also included in the Supplementary Affidavit filed in the Supreme Court on 11.1.2007.
- On 7.2.2007, some unidentified persons claiming to belong to the Central Water Development and Management entered the dam area without authorisation to take Water Samples.
- On 16.3.2007, Kerala Police allowed some persons whose identity could not be established to enter the dam area to make video recordings without prior permission from the Idukki District Collector.
- On 25.3.2007, I brought to the Prime Minister’s attention the Kerala Police’s failure to cooperate and the frequent visits to the dam by persons claiming to be officials of various Kerala departments without prior notice to Tamil Nadu and without Tamil Nadu’s prior permission. I again stressed that either the Central Reserve Police or the Central Industrial Security Force should be sent to protect the dam.
- On 4.5.2009, when a senior official of the Water Resources Department went to the Mullai Periyar Dam, Kerala Police stopped him and did not allow him to enter the dam.
- On 27.7.2009, the Theni District Collector brought to the government’s attention that Kerala Forest and Irrigation officials had stopped Tamil Nadu Public Works Department assistant engineers and assistants from carrying out annual maintenance work for 2009–2010. When this was raised at a meeting convened by the Union Government on 31.7.2009, it was stated that the two States could resolve the issue. Following this, on 4.8.2009, the Kerala government was requested to issue appropriate instructions to the departments concerned.
- The Tamil Nadu Tourism Development Corporation proposed to conduct an exhibition and to include photographs of various reservoirs, including the Mullai Periyar Dam. For this purpose, on 29.10.2009, when an Executive Engineer of the Water Resources Department went with photographers to the boat landing to proceed to the dam area, permission was refused. Yet Kerala government photographers have been going to the dam and taking photographs without prior information to Tamil Nadu engineers and without prior permission. In these circumstances, stating that it was a matter of deep concern that Kerala Forest Department officials were preventing Tamil Nadu Water Resources Department officials from carrying out their official duties, this government wrote to the Kerala government on 2.11.2009 asking it to issue appropriate instructions to the departments concerned not to obstruct our engineers from properly carrying out their work on various occasions.

To prevent the Kerala government from continuing such obstructions, it would be best for the Central Industrial Security Force to take over protection of the dam in place of the Kerala Police who now guard it.

On behalf of the Tamil Nadu government, we have continuously informed the Supreme Court and the Union Government of the various ways in which the Kerala government has disregarded Supreme Court judgments and continued to obstruct Tamil Nadu’s activities.

The General Secretary of a party that has announced a demonstration in Madurai over the Mullai Periyar issue has said in a statement that, although the Kerala Chief Minister met the Prime Minister over this issue, the Tamil Nadu Chief Minister neither met the Prime Minister nor even wrote a letter merely for form’s sake.

Here, then, is the date-wise list of the many letters I wrote on the Mullai Periyar issue to the Kerala Chief Minister, to the Union Minister who then handled the department, and to the Prime Minister of India:

- 14-9-2006 — letter to the Kerala Chief Minister;
- 4-10-2006 — letter to the Kerala Chief Minister;
- 26-10-2006 — letter to the Kerala Chief Minister;
- 31-10-2006 — letter to the Union Irrigation Minister;
- 15-11-2006 — letter to the Union Irrigation Minister;
- 16-11-2006 — letter to the Union Irrigation Minister;
- 23-11-2006 — letter to the Prime Minister;
- 23-11-2006 — letter to the Union Irrigation Minister;
- 23-11-2006 — letter to the Defence Minister;
- 27-11-2006 — letter to the Union Irrigation Minister;
- 2-1-2007 — letter to the Prime Minister;
- 25-3-2007 — letter to the Prime Minister;
- 19-5-2007 — letter to the Prime Minister;
- 18-12-2007 — I met the Prime Minister in person and spoke about Mullai Periyar. It was then that the Prime Minister asked me and the Kerala Chief Minister to meet once in person and talk, and accordingly we met and spoke;
- 20-12-2007 — letter to the Prime Minister;
- 4-2-2008 — letter to the Kerala Chief Minister;
- 13-7-2009 — letter to the Kerala Chief Minister;
- 8-10-2009 — letter to the Prime Minister.

Most of these letters were also published in the newspapers at the time. If, after concealing all this, they issue a statement saying that I did not write even a letter for form’s sake, I can only worry that they have become puppets of the lady who conducts politics by knowingly uttering falsehoods. What else can I do, Udanpirappē!

It is because of the continuous steps we have taken in the Mullai Periyar issue that, even yesterday, when this case came up for hearing in the Supreme Court, newspapers reported that the Court dismissed the new petition filed by the Kerala government claiming that raising the water level of the Mullai Periyar Dam would endanger the dam, and also strongly condemned the Kerala government for passing a new law against the Supreme Court judgment on this issue.

Therefore, to say that the Tamil Nadu government has paid no attention or shown no concern in this issue is nothing but an attempt to deceive the people. After all this, one can only wonder: why do the “Kerala people,” becoming ears that will not hear, sting like a scorpion?

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**6-11-2009**
