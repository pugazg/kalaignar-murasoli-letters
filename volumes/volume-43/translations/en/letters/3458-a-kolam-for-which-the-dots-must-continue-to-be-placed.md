# 3458. A Kolam for Which the Dots Must Continue to Be Placed!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This letter argues for `மாநில சுயாட்சி` — State autonomy — within India and explicitly distinguishes that demand from secession or the creation of a separate government. Its historical references to S. M. Krishna, Farooq Abdullah, Sadiq Batcha and Jyoti Basu, and its clock, necklace-hook and kolam metaphors, are retained in source order.

**Tamil source:** [Letter 3458](../../../chapters/3458-pulliyai-thodarndhu-poda-vendiya-kolam.md)  
**Source pages:** [PDF 254](../../../pages/page-254.md)–[PDF 256](../../../pages/page-256.md)  
**Date:** 18 February 2010

**Udanpirappē,**

Whether or not we come to the responsibility of government — even if the condition of remaining forever in the opposition ranks continues — this movement has pledged itself to protect democracy in the sacred land of India; to labour so that Tamil Nadu may shine as a garden of peace; and to carry out the task of protecting that garden calmly, without clamour and without giving room to alien forces, extending cooperation in every possible way.

The recent function of the Sangath Tamil Peravai, jointly conducted by our Ministers, younger brothers Duraimurugan and Jagathrakshakan, made visibly clear to me the confidence that our DMK organisation possesses the qualification and ability to lead such a movement.

Although the Union Ministers who participated in the function and showered a rain of ideas — my friend of fifty years S. M. Krishna and the honourable Farooq Abdullah — praised me personally, when they pointed out that the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam possesses qualities capable of showing the way to India, my thoughts truly went back to the State Autonomy Conference I led in Chennai around the time Anna passed away, and to the speech delivered there by S. M. Krishna; then to the Chief Ministers’ conference convened in Kashmir by Farooq Abdullah to press policies for State autonomy, at which our late friend Minister Sadiq Batcha participated;

to the actions taken by our DMK on State autonomy, our firmness on the issue, and all that our friend Sadiq explained at that conference;

and then to the just resolutions adopted in Kolkata, while our late comrade Jyoti Basu was Chief Minister of West Bengal, pressing for greater powers for the States.

These are not merely pages of history that will never leave my heart; they are also a dream that must become reality in the future!

I say a dream that must become reality — not a dream that is to remain merely a dream. It is like the hand of a clock pointing out that the dream we see, echoing throughout this sacred land of India from the depths of the Kanyakumari sea to the summit of the Himalayas, is not an empty dream, nor something only for the future, but something that can happen in our own time.

When the hand of the clock turns, the days on the calendar turn in succession. As they have turned, we know very well how many places and countries have obtained autonomy and independent government. That is why I say with confidence, remembering the old freedom song, “A war is coming without sword, without blood”: in this country that has attained independence, it is not wrong to demand autonomy. The demand for State autonomy that, without forgetting and without changing, we place every year in the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly — whether in the Governor’s Address or the Budget — is not a slogan to break up the country or to secede and establish a separate government; nor is it the echo of such a slogan.

State autonomy is the slogan we raise sincerely in order to preserve the independence we have gained in India and to establish firmly the principle that the Union Government can be strong only when the States are strong.

Whether a necklace is made of gold —

or of emerald gems —

it has strength only if the “hook” linking each gem is strong. The name we have given to that strength is State autonomy.

The explanations given about State autonomy by Arignar Anna, Silambu Selvar M. P. Sivagnanam and younger brother Murasoli Maran —

and the books containing those explanations —

serve as great medicines that clear the delirium of thinking that autonomy means secession.

Therefore, Udanpirappē, if we look deeply at the history of the periods, forms and circumstances in which State autonomy had to be pressed by our leaders and our movements, I believe that the State autonomy we sincerely advocate will gain the support of the States throughout the Indian landmass.

The function conducted by my younger brothers at Valluvar Kottam in Chennai was like placing one dot toward that goal.

Is it enough to stop with the dot?

Should not a kolam take shape by continuing from that dot?

Whether or not we live until that kolam has been fully drawn —

if that kolam is completed, it will be a kolam that lives forever!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**18-02-2010**

## Letter-specific notes

1. `மாநில சுயாட்சி` is rendered **State autonomy** and is kept distinct from secession because the source itself makes that distinction central to the argument.
2. The historical sequence of conferences, participants and West Bengal resolutions remains the author’s account and is not externally reconciled.
3. The clock, necklace-hook and kolam images are retained rather than replaced by abstract federalism terminology.
4. No canonical Tamil change was required during this source-check.

வலியுறுத்தும் முதல் அமைச்சர்கள் மாநாட்டிற்கு மறைந்த நண்பர்
