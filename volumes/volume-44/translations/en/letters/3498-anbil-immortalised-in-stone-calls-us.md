# 3498. Anbil, Immortalised in Stone, Calls Us!

> **Translator’s note**
>

**Date:** 7 September 2010

**Udanpirappē,**

On 8-9-2010, at Tiruchirappalli, the statue of my beloved friend Anbil Dharmalingam is to be unveiled. I keep thinking about it. The audited source at this point contains the opaque phrase `சத்துணவுப் நிலையங்கள் விடுக்கோ என முன் எழுந்தால்`; I do not silently repair or replace it. For me, who has lived for nearly eighty-seven years and has lost many close friends, one of the great names among them is the name of Anbil Dharmalingam.

Under the heading “Anbil Calls,” Anna issued a letter — and an invitation to us — for the DMK State conference held at Tiruchirappalli in 1956. The source then states, in its own unusual form, that at the first State conference, in 1929, on the 10th and 11th, the DMK State conference was held. Shall I remind you of the beautiful words with which Anna called us in that invitation?

> “‘Can it be done? It can!’ I shall show you the number and the kind of those who think so, their determination and enthusiasm, and the great ardour that surges in their hearts and pours through their eyes — come, says Anbil! They are going to gather in lakhs; the cry of the ideal is going to be heard. From the Sinhala island, from the Tirunelveli region, from beautiful Chennai and from many other districts will come lion-like young men; young women who mix valour into the honey of love for them; mothers who gave birth to heroes;
>
> “elders who rejoice thinking of their youthful strength; students who know history; working comrades who are to witness a new history. They are coming rank upon rank, raising cries of joy that surpass the waves of the sea. Families upon families are coming; they are coming to behold delight. They are gathering in such a way that anyone may eagerly say, after seeing their shoulders fit to lift a hill and their cool, moon-like gaze, ‘Today we have seen them; henceforth we have won!’ The printed source calls them `திருஇடத்தவர்கள்` and `திருஇடத்தினர்`; those unusual source forms are retained below rather than silently corrected.
>
> “Come to see! Come to realise your duty! Those with unshaken hearts, come to offer thought and to act! Gather! Come together in such a way that all may know with what uprising those brave and valiant people stand arrayed! The motherland, which had been saying, ‘These people whom I bore and reared were lying there with bowed heads, hearing the enemy abuse me without shame,’ now sees the awakening and valour that have arisen, wipes away its tears and welcomes them with the blessing: ‘My treasures whom I bore! My sorrow is over. The hope has been born in me that my chains will now be broken!’ For the service of the mother, dear children, let everyone come!! — thus does Anbil call!”

That is what Anna wrote then. Today neither Anna nor that Anbil is here to invite you to the function at which Anbil’s statue is to be unveiled. I alone remain to call you. With my whole voice and my whole heart, through this letter, I invite all of you to Tiruchirappalli!

After Anna passed away, at the conference held in 1990, Anbil’s son Poyyamozhi was with us as a Member of the Legislative Assembly and an administrator of the Youth Wing. He too is no longer with us.

Among the close friends I had in Tiruchirappalli, the list beginning with Anbil Dharmalingam goes on: Ponmalai Parangusam, Thu. Pa. Azhagamuthu, Dravida Pannai Muthukrishnan, Kulithalai Muthukrishnan, M. S. Mani, M. K. Thu. Natarasan, Uppiliyapuram Ara. Natarasan, Ilamurugu Porkchelvi, Tiruchi Balakrishnan, Kasthuri Raj, A. V. Krishnamurthy who had been city secretary, Nathan Company Pandurangan, Kamatchi, Rabi Sharif — the list keeps growing!

Anbil is a small village in Tiruchirappalli district. But when the word “Anbil” is uttered, for members of our movement — indeed, for everyone in Tamil Nadu — it is not the village name that comes to mind. It is the name of Anbil Dharmalingam. Everyone would say, “Anbil came,” “Anbil went.” There is a meaning even in my saying “came and went.” He would not remain in one place even for a few hours. He had that much work. It was not merely movement work. He had an extraordinary taste for helping others, for running about and making efforts for their welfare. Whenever he saw someone he would address them in a relationship — “machan,” “maappillai.” Did someone need to win an election? Anbil would be there. He would trust anyone easily. Because of that he would suffer betrayal of trust, and then the sight of him blaming himself would make even us feel pity.

It is the statue of Anbil Dharmalingam, first in that line, that I am coming to unveil on the 8th. I have come to Tiruchirappalli so many times at Anbil’s invitation. But I never imagined that I myself would one day come to unveil that Anbil’s statue. There are many incidents through which I could recall the friendship and connection between Anbil and me.

Around 1955, when a little disunity arose among DMK comrades in Tiruchirappalli, Anna decided that he would no longer come to Tiruchirappalli. When all the Tiruchirappalli comrades led by Anbil Dharmalingam approached me, Anna at first would not agree. He sent me to Tiruchirappalli and asked me first to try to create unity within the DMK there. I went accordingly and engaged in that work. Thereafter Anna was pleased; when the DMK decided to sell handloom cloth and the question arose as to who would sell it in Tiruchirappalli, Anna announced, “In Tiruchirappalli, I myself am going to sell handloom cloth.”

On one occasion I had agreed to attend a wedding in the morning at Tirukkattuppalli in Thanjavur district and to speak that evening at a public meeting in a village near Anbil. For the wedding, Maran, Anbil Dharmalingam, Parangusam, Pannai Muthukrishnan, Kulithalai Muthukrishnan and I were travelling by car. It was a road along a riverbank. We had to travel a mile or two along that bank, cross a bridge and then reach the wedding house on the opposite bank. People of the area who wished to reach the wedding house before me were walking through the river. When our car went around by the bridge and came along the other bank, we saw a crowd standing on the riverbank. We stopped the car, and Anbil Dharmalingam went over to find out what had happened. A young man who had walked into the river had drowned and had been carried out. We took him to hospital. He did not survive. After sending away his lifeless body, we went to the wedding house, conducted the wedding and returned to Kallanai.

After speaking at the public meeting in the evening, everyone said that going by a double-bullock cart would mean going all the way around, whereas we could descend into the Kollidam river and reach Kallanai directly on the other side. I nodded agreement. We had gone some distance across the Kollidam when suddenly there was a thunderstorm. The level of the water in the river kept rising. Anbil Dharmalingam lifted Maran onto his shoulders. A comrade named Rathinam lifted me. As time passed, the flood grew and it became doubtful whether we would reach the bank alive. Somehow, after a long struggle, we reached the shore at about one in the morning.

That too is an unforgettable incident.

In 1992–93, my dearest friend Anbil, one of the DMK’s front-line commanders, suffered some decline in health and reached a stage where he could not speak properly. A man who was always talking could not bear not to speak. In the first week of March 1993 his condition worsened somewhat and he was admitted as an inpatient in a private hospital in Tiruchirappalli. I had fixed dates for a tour programme in Sankarankovil, Thoothukudi and Kanyakumari districts. Instead of going there directly, on March 5 I went to Tiruchirappalli, saw Anbil in hospital, and only then proceeded by car towards the Sankarankovil meeting.

While I was on the way came the news that the flame of Self-Respect, my beloved friend, Anna’s dear younger brother, Periyar’s darling child, the valiant Anbil Dharmalingam who had been with me for forty-five years, had passed away. I yearned to return immediately to Tiruchirappalli. Even so, at the Sankarankovil meeting I spoke for fifteen minutes entirely about Anbil Dharmalingam and then returned to Tiruchirappalli.

Anbil Dharmalingam held many positions during his life. He served as panchayat president, director of a cooperative society, chairman of the Tiruchirappalli District Central Cooperative Bank, director of the Chennai Cooperative Bank, secretary of the Tiruchirappalli district DMK, Member of the Legislative Assembly and Minister for Agriculture. In my Cabinet he was not only Minister for Agriculture; he also handled Local Administration, Information and Public Relations, and Revenue.

Anbil, who should have found a place even in Anna’s Cabinet, was made Minister for Agriculture in my Cabinet on 8-3-1972, when I had become Chief Minister for the second time.

At the Mupperum Vizha held in Chennai in 1986, I myself felt proud to present Anbil Dharmalingam with the “Periyar Award” on behalf of the party headquarters.

When Anbil died, I wrote in *Murasoli*:

> “At the time of the 1956 DMK State conference in Tiruchirappalli, ‘Anbil Calls’ was the title of Anna’s letter to his younger brother. How can we forget that it became one of the meaningful phrases Anna gave us? That Anbil is not here today. He has become a picture. Though this end comes to everyone born into this world, I had believed that he would first become a fruit, become an old man, and even then remain a protective fortress of this movement. From the days when, whether I was at home or not, he would have at least ten idlis served by the hand of our mother Anjugam and eat before leaving, he was one of our family. What influence he had, whether in the district or throughout the State! Whether he was a minister or not, there would always be a crowd around him. Born in an ordinary agricultural family, rising through labour, winning the affection of Periyar, Anna, Rajaji,
>
> “Perunthalaivar Kamaraj and Quaid-e-Millath — my friend, my life-comrade — served as Agriculture Minister in the DMK government and developed that department efficiently. Thinking of that, in 1989 I started an ‘Anbil Dharmalingam Agricultural College’ in Tiruchirappalli district on behalf of the DMK government! Newspapers have now published pictures and written about the friendship between Anbil and our dear friend M.G.R. Yet the moment that government came to power, it threw away the name I had given in honour of M.G.R.’s friend Anbil. But the name of that warrior from an agricultural family — that gold of principle, Anbil Dharmalingam — who has planted his flag at the summit of the sky of fame and lives in the hearts of lakhs upon lakhs of DMK cadres can never be erased by anyone!
>
> “My friend! Anbil! Even now it feels as if you are entering our house and walking through the verandah and hall! At Anna Arivalayam I feel as though you are seated beside me and beside the Professor! You have gone nowhere! You are within us!”

After the cruelty of Anbil’s departure, his eldest son, dear Poyyamozhi, would call me “Appa, Appa” with affection. I made him a Member of the Legislative Assembly. But he followed his father and passed away at a very young age. Anbil’s second son, Poyyamozhi’s younger brother Periyasami, has been made Member of the Legislative Assembly for Tiruchirappalli Second Constituency. Whenever I see him, the memory of the departed warriors unfailingly raises so many old thoughts in my heart.

The friendship that began with Anbil and me continued in the second generation through younger brother M. K. Stalin and Anbil’s son Poyyamozhi. Now Stalin’s son Udhayanidhi and Poyyamozhi’s son Mahendran are friends, and the friendship continues into a third generation. I am coming to Tiruchirappalli to unveil Anbil’s statue, the emblem of that friendship, confident that you too will be there with the brave people of Tiruchirappalli.

Yes; standing before you and me in the form of a statue with a smiling face, Anbil calls us. How many moments of anger and emotion — how many estrangements and reunions between us! With the right born of intimacy, with true friendship blended with feeling, he never separated even for a moment; like the pupil of the eye he guarded me, my friendship and our DMK. Though that guardian is no longer here, here he stands with broad shoulders raised, as the symbol of younger brother Nehru’s tireless labour — that golden son whose name we carry in our hearts. Anna once invited us to the first State conference held at Tiruchirappalli, saying “Anbil Calls!” Here I too am calling you. Come, Udanpirappē! Let us meet in that sacred city of Tiruchirappalli!

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**7-9-2010**
