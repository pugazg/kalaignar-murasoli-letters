# 3414. “Look at the Words That Hinder, Brother!”

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This literary-political letter recalls Anna’s taste, prose style and a stage play, then criticises an unnamed contemporary birthday advertisement for what the author sees as artificial alliterative phrasing. The advertisement, the remembered dialogue, the list of Anna’s titles and the quoted poem are preserved in the source’s rhetorical framing. The complete audited Tamil is reproduced below and remains authoritative.

**Source pages:** [PDF 325](../../../pages/page-325.md)–[PDF 329](../../../pages/page-329.md)  
**Date:** 25 August 2009

**Udanpirappē,**

A hundred years have been completed. Though Perarignar Anna did not live through even one full century, he passed away after leaving an impact upon our hearts many times greater than those who did live so long.

Throughout the good world where Tamil is spoken, celebrations are being held for him, and people are looking back at the wonders he brought about during the days he lived.

Beyond all that, we also remember again and again, with joy, many events from his time that were connected with us.

I will tell you one such event. Listen.

It concerns a stage performance put on by a “Nallathambi” who had climbed onto the theatrical stage with the desire that he should speak like Anna, write like Anna, make stacked alliterative phrases flow and let beautiful Tamil flourish.

One day, when I was conversing with Anna at his home, this Nallathambi pressed as strongly as he possibly could that Anna should preside over a play he had written and was acting in, titled *Maranap Padukkai* — *Deathbed* — and that I, Karunanidhi, should take the place of honour.

Anna, that simple man, that leader of the world of art, the golden ruler given by Kanchi, had a special interest in watching amateur drama and street theatre. He agreed to preside and instructed me through his suggestion: “You too come and take the place of honour.”

So both of us went to the play.

From the beginning, I realised that Nallathambi, who had written and was acting in it, had poured out dialogue after dialogue packed with stacked alliterative words — enough to make both hand and neck ache.

Our leader Anna realised it too.

Even in scenes where one ought to have laughed aloud, he merely smiled gently. That was his nature. Had he not learned the civility of the stage?

How could I not know how many times, on how many stages, he had concealed the urge to laugh heartily and expressed his enjoyment quietly?

Yet the dialogue in that play became so much that even he could not bear it. As he listened and listened, he shook with laughter.

That scene still moves like a shadow in my memory.

Udanpirappē, you may ask why that memory came back to me now.

I will tell you why.

A birthday advertisement appeared in the newspapers, declaring a vow that a Tamil Nadu government should be formed under someone’s leadership so that:

> the scorched Public Works Department may perform its responsibilities;  
> the dried-up Home Department may shine with distinction;  
> the darkness of the darkened Electricity Department may be removed;  
> the fallen Law Department may make history;  
> the indebted Education Department may perform its duty;  
> the drought-stricken Revenue Department may find a place in history;  
> the Agriculture Department may create wonder;  
> the ruined Hindu Religious and Charitable Endowments Department may be rescued from ruin;  
> the shrunken Health Department may be praised all around;  
> and the Tamil Nadu Secretariat may be made to hold its head high.

When I read this, the memory of our rationalist sun — the leader who could show in his writing the stride of a lion, the gait of a graceful breeze, the movement of a surging sea and the march of revolutionary Tamil — Perarignar Anna — began circling in my heart, together with the unmatched discipline of his spoken and written eloquence.

Where is Anna’s political editorial:

> **The line rose; the hill sank!**

And his expressions:

> **If defeated like this, how will one win?**

> **He who has a younger brother fears no army!**

> **To remove the country’s distress, merely placing a government in the country is not enough!**

> **Those without money in their hands — those without stain in their minds!**

> **Some unemployed people by the roadside; strange thoughts in the hearts of the unemployed!**

> **Nature caresses; deprivation strikes!**

> **Painting the parrot green?**

> **A five-legged cow!**

> **Coral there, picture here!**

> **Tears and rosewater!**

> **No sweet music can be found in the sound of thunder!**

> **Let stumbling blocks become steps!**

> **The time to settle accounts!**

> **He who conquers himself conquers the world!**

> **A spear in a festering wound!**

> **A lamp placed upon a hill!**

> **This is a day of joy; the heart sings!**

What expressions those writings of Anna were!

We read that new style of good Tamil, sweet as jaggery. Those words shook us and made us swell and surge. Were they not gem-like flowers that fell merely from the shaking?

Having delighted in those flowers, today we see people who imagine that merely matching one “aa” with another “aa” under the name of stacked diction is enough, and that this itself becomes beautiful Tamil. Yet those innocents set out claiming that they will protect Mother Tamil and conduct politics for that very purpose.

In the play Anna and I watched — yes, that *Deathbed* — the hero looks at the villain and says:

> “In your matter, I will be a serpent.”

The villain replies:

> “I will make even that serpent into sambar and eat it.”

Udanpirappē, when this line was spoken, I saw Anna — who would usually remain restrained at such awkward moments — shake with laughter.

Even now that scene appears before me.

Just when I wondered whether I might ever again see a scene like that, I came across this advertisement today.

What can I do?

Anna is no longer here to laugh.

Nor do I have an eye in my forehead to burn this tortoise-like stacked diction.

I could only recite inwardly, like a mantra, the poem:

> Pillai Pandiyan is not here to strike them;  
> nor is Villi here to dig into the ear to its proper depth  
> and cut as far as he can reach;  
> nor is poet Ottakoothan here  
> to tie up one or two, make the heads hang down,  
> and cut them;  
> yet there are ignorant lords  
> to compose and rapidly sing playful verse;  
> so one may roam the whole country as a poet!

All this is not “stacked diction.” Anna himself once wrote a long article in *Dravida Nadu* titled:

> **“Look at the words that hinder, brother!”**

I remembered that too.

And thinking that some people without fitness had set out to inflict such harm upon Tamil, my eyes filled with tears.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**25-8-2009**

வெல்லமாய் இனிக்கும் அந்த நல்ல தமிழ் புதுநடையைப் படித்து விட்டு - பூரித்துப் பொங்கியெழ நம்மை உலுக்கிய அந்தச் சொற்கள் - குலுக்கிய மாத்திரத்தில் உதிர்ந்திட்ட மணிமலர்கள் அல்லவா? அந்த மலர்களைக் கண்டு மகிழ்ந்த நமக்கு இன்று அடுக்குச் சொல் என்ற பெயரால் “ஆனாவுக்கு ஆனா” போட்டால் போதும் - அந்த அடுக்குச் சொல்லே அழகு தமிழ்ச் சொல் என்று எண்ணுகின்ற அப்பாவிகள் அல்லவா; அன்னைத் தமிழைக் காக்கவும், அதற்காகவே அரசியல் நடத்துகிறோம் என்று ஆர்த்திடவும் புறப்பட்டுள்ளனர்.

அண்ணாவும் நானும் கண்ட நாடகத்தில், ஆம், ‘மரணப் படுக்கை’ எனும் அந்த நாடகத்தில் வில்லனைப் பார்த்து கதாநாயகன் பேசுவான்.

“உன் விஷயத்தில் நான் சர்ப்பமாக இருப்பேன்” என்று!

அதற்கு எதிர் வசனம் வில்லன் பேசுவான்.

“அந்த சர்ப்பத்தையும் நான் சாம்பார் வைத்து சாப்பிடுவேன்” என்று!

உடன்பிறப்பே, இந்த வசனத்தைக் கேட்ட போதுதான் - இதுபோன்ற நெருக்கடியான நேரங்களில் அடக்கமாக இருக்கும் அண்ணா அவர்கள் குலுங்கக் குலுங்கச் சிரித்த காட்சியை நான் கண்டேன். இப்போதும் அந்தக் காட்சி என் முன்னால் தெரியத்தான் செய்கிறது. மீண்டும் அப்படியொரு காட்சியைக் காண நேரிடுமோ என்றிருந்த வேளையில்தான் இன்று இந்த விளம்பரத்தைக் கண்டேன்.

என் செய்வது? சிரிப்பதற்கு அண்ணாவும் இல்லை - இந்தக் கச்சபான அடுக்கு மொழியை எரிப்பதற்கு என் நெற்றியிலே கண்ணும் இல்லை.

குட்டுதற்கோ பிள்ளைப் பாண்டியன் இங்கில்லை;
குறும்பி அளவாக் காதைக் குடைந்து தோண்டி
எட்டின மட்டும் அறுப்பதற்கோ வில்லி யில்லை;
இரண்டொன்றா முடிந்து தலை இறங்கப் போட்டு
வெட்டுதற்கோ கவி ஒட்டக்கூத்தன் இல்லை;

விளையாட்டா கவிதை தனை விரைந்து பாடித்
தெட்டுதற்கோ அறிவில்லா துரைகள் உண்டு;
தேசமெங்கும் புலவரெனத் திரியலாமே!

என்ற பாடலைத்தான் மனதிற்குள் மந்திரம் போல் ஜபித்துக் கொண்டேன்.

இதெல்லாம் அடுக்குச் சொல் அல்ல; “தடுக்குச் சொல் பாராய் தம்பீ” என்று ஒரு முறை அண்ணாவே ஒரு நீண்டக் கட்டுரை “திராவிட நாடு” இதழில் எழுதியிருக்கிறார். அதையும் நினைத்துக் கொண்டேன். தமிழுக்கு இப்படி ஒரு கேடு விளைவிக்க தகவிலார் சிலர் புறப்பட்டிருக்கிறார்களே என்பதையும் எண்ணி என் விழிகளை நனைத்துக் கொண்டேன்.
