# 3582. A Sweet Festival; a Festival of Our People!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

The people of Tamil Nadu know well that ever since the AIADMK won the general election held last May and assumed office, it has continuously engaged in bringing major schemes implemented under the DMK Government to an end.

They said that the building constructed at the Omandurar complex for the Tamil Nadu Government Secretariat and Legislative Assembly Secretariat at a cost of about ₹500 crore was not of good quality, and that they would not continue functioning there. Then they said they would now establish a hospital in that building.

They said they would not accept the Samacheer Kalvi law introduced by the DMK Government, and hurriedly brought an amendment bill into the Assembly and passed it. The issue went all the way to the High Court and Supreme Court. Refusing to listen even though all the Opposition parties explained the matter, they were properly rebuked there. Angered by that, Jayalalithaa took another step: through the Hindu Religious and Charitable Endowments Minister, she introduced a bill in the Assembly to move Tamil New Year Day from the first day of Thai back to the first day of Chithirai, and had it passed by voice vote.

In the announcement made by His Excellency the Governor in his address to the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly on 23-1-2008, he said:

“More than five hundred eminent poets, under the leadership of the great scholar Maraimalai Adigal, founder of the Pure Tamil movement, assembled in 1921 at Pachaiyappa's College in Chennai. Considering that Tamils needed a ‘separate year,’ they decided to follow a continuous era in the name of Ayyan Thiruvalluvar, to regard that as the ‘Tamil Year,’ and to place Thiruvalluvar's birth in 31 BCE. Honourable Chief Minister Kalaignar accepted that view thirty-seven years ago and ordered its implementation in the Tamil Nadu Government diary from 1971 and in the Tamil Nadu Government Gazette from 1972.

“As the first day of the month of Thai, when the Thiruvalluvar year begins, is accepted by Tamil scholars collectively as the beginning of the Tamil New Year, this Government has decided to declare and implement the first day of Thai as the beginning of the Tamil New Year. Therefore, the people of Tamil Nadu, who have long celebrated Pongal as the festival of Tamils, will henceforth combine it with the birth of the Tamil New Year and celebrate the day with doubled joy—planting the three fruit trees, banana, mango and jackfruit; drawing colourful kolams; letting homes glow with rows of lamps and new beauty; wearing new clothes; singing and dancing in praise of Tamil honour and self-respect; spreading the spirit of equality; and pouring out love in bounding happiness, saying, ‘This is Tamil New Year.’”

That announcement was received with loud applause.

At the meeting led by Maraimalai Adigal, Tamil Thendral Thiru.Vi. Kalyanasundaranar, Tamil Kavalar K. Subramania Pillai, Saiva Periyar Sachidananda Pillai, Navalar N.Mu. Venkatasamy, Navalar Somasundara Bharathiar, Muthamizh Kavalar Ki.A.Pe. Viswanatham and other eminent scholars took part. Their three decisions were:

1. to follow a continuous era in the name of Thiruvalluvar;
2. to celebrate that as the Tamil Year; and
3. to adopt the method by which adding 31 BCE to the current year gives the Thiruvalluvar year.

Later, in 1939, the All India Tamil Conference met in Tiruchi under the leadership of Somasundara Bharathiar. Thanthai Periyar, Karanthai Tamil Sangam president Umamaheswaranar, Professor K. Subramaniam, T.P. Meenakshisundaranar, Thiru.Vi.K., Maraimalai Adigal, P.T. Rajan, Arcot Ramasamy Mudaliar, revolutionary poet Bharathidasan, Pattukkottai Azhagiri and many others participated. That conference too resolved that the first day of Thai was Tamil New Year and that Pongal was the festival of Tamils.

The very next day after the Governor's announcement, Thavathiru Kundrakudi Ponnambala Adigalar said in a statement: “From ancient times, in religious life, natural life, the life of land and people, and agricultural life, the first day of the month of Thai has been the festival that brings awakening and joy to Tamil life. Thanks and praise are due to Muthamizh scholar and Chief Minister Kalaignar for implementing the declaration, made by great and distinctive Tamil scholars such as Maraimalai Adigal, that the first day of Thai should be Tamil New Year in the name of Ayyan Thiruvalluvar.”

Kavipperarasu Vairamuthu said in his statement: “Feeling Tamils are celebrating and rejoicing at the announcement in the Governor's Address by Kalaignar's Government that the first day of Thai is the opening day of the Tamil New Year. Every national people has certain indestructible identities. Tamils have a territorial identity; they have an ethnic identity; only their identity in time remained confused. Today that darkness of confusion has broken and dawned into light. The Dravidian movement presented Ayyan Thiruvalluvar as the measure of the world of ideas. Today Kalaignar's Government has made Thiruvalluvar the measure of time as well. This is history that sets history right.”

Dravidar Kazhagam president and Tamilar Thalaivar, my younger brother Veeramani, said: “Our long-standing demand that the first day of the month of Thai be the beginning of the Tamil New Year has succeeded. This is a new chapter of renaissance in the direction of Tamil cultural history. We praise it and rejoice.”

This good measure accomplished under the DMK Government now faces a great danger. In Tamil Nadu's history there has always been a group that is hostile to Tamil itself—one that hates, rejects and ignores anything called Classical Tamil. Even in earlier times there were one or two poets who hated Tamil in this way. Here is a story!

In the time of Nakkiran, a man named Kuyakkondan said in a debate at the Tamil Sangam, “Aryan is good; Tamil is bad.” Nakkiran became angry and is said to have sung a curse: “You who said Tamil is bad and the northern language good—may you die.” Kuyakkondan immediately fell down dead. Some who were there pleaded with Nakkiran to restore him. Hearing them, Nakkiran sang: “By the deed of declaring Aryan good and Tamil bad, he fell into Yama's grasp—by the command of Agathiyar of the noble Potiyil, pure Tamil, restore him—svaha!” Kuyakkondan, it is said, came back to life.

When the Tamil Nadu Government's announcement was made, the distinguished Tamil researcher Iravatham Mahadevan said: “If we do not have the courage to examine today's almanacs scientifically and correct them in accordance with astronomy and the present cycle of the seasons, what is wrong in celebrating the harvest festival of Pongal as New Year? Historically too, why should this not be accepted as one among the many New Years now observed in India?”

Following the announcement in the Governor's Address, on 29-1-2008 I introduced before the Assembly the Tamil Nadu Tamil New Year Bill, 2008. The bill was debated on 1-2-2008. E.S.S. Raman spoke for the Congress, K. Arumugam for the PMK, N. Nanmaran for the Marxist Communist Party, Vai. Sivapunniyam for the Communist Party of India, M. Kannappan for the MDMK, and Ku. Selvam for the Viduthalai Chiruthaigal. They welcomed the bill and it was passed.

Tamils in Malaysia now celebrate the first day of Thai itself as New Year. Dr Mu. Varadarasanar explained: “In earlier times New Year did not begin on the first day of Chithirai. Elders celebrated the first day of Thai as New Year. On that day they bought and wore new clothes. Streets were laid with fresh earth, red soil was spread and things were put in order. Festivals were held throughout the town. In this way, in towns, Pongal is celebrated as the birth of the New Year.”

A decision praised and welcomed in all these ways was enacted into law under the DMK Government and had been in force since 2008. But because it had been enacted under a DMK Government, the AIADMK Government introduced and passed a bill in the Assembly to put an end to it. The BJP hurried to welcome this announcement. From that alone one can understand the purpose behind the decision. Yet when journalists told me that both Communist parties had opposed the bill in the Assembly and asked for my view, I replied that I bowed to their Tamil feeling.

PMK founder Dr Ramadoss said in a statement that the AIADMK Government's decision was of a kind that would cause pain to those with Tamil feeling; that suddenly shifting Tamil New Year back to Chithirai was improper; and that it would become a grave historical error. Tamilar Thalaivar K. Veeramani too strongly condemned the decision when speaking to journalists. I thank them.

Speaking on the bill brought by the AIADMK Government, Jayalalithaa declared that the law passed under the DMK Government had been enacted for self-advertisement, to create the illusion among the public that I alone cared about Tamil and Tamils, and that it had hurt the feelings of the people of Tamil Nadu who had been celebrating the beginning of Chithirai as Tamil New Year. Let the people of Tamil Nadu themselves weigh this statement and understand the truth.

Jayalalithaa also said in the Assembly that the law passed by the DMK Government benefited no one; that after it was enacted, all Karunanidhi's “sycophants” praised it at a felicitation function in Chennai; and that Thai had been declared Tamil New Year only to advertise myself.

Do you know who received the honour of being described by Ammaiyar as “sycophants”? Our Perasiriyar Anbazhagan; V.C. Kulandaisamy, who served with distinction as Vice-Chancellor of Anna University and Indira Gandhi Open University; Dr Tamilannal; Dr V.I. Subramaniam; Dr Agathiyalingam; Dr Avvai Natarajan; Dr R. Nagaswamy; Thavathiru Ooran Adigal; former minister Duraimurugan; Union Minister of State Jagathrakshakan; Professor Kana. Sirchabeson; Dr A. Arivoli; Dr Sudha Seshayyan; E. Jayaraj of Sri Lanka; Dr Saraswathi Ramanathan; advocate T. Ramalingam; Professor T. Rajagopalan; Dr Rama. Soundaravalli; Dr R. Selva Ganapathy; and Pulavar Ko. Sarangapani!

It seems I passed that law only to advertise myself! Yet on 23-8-2011 alone, the official *Namadhu M.G.R.* daily carried **45 full-page advertisements** featuring Jayalalithaa in a variety of photographs—the very Jayalalithaa who supposedly has no love for publicity. In this condition she looks at me and says that I advertised myself.

Jayalalithaa said in the Assembly, “Whatever we do, the DMK's work is to destroy it.” That is wrong. The correct statement is: “Whatever the DMK has done, the AIADMK's work is to destroy it!”

Let them do whatever they wish and go. As far as we are concerned, the first day of the month of Thai is the day Tamil New Year begins. On that day, remembering what is called a festival in one's life, let us celebrate it overflowing with joy as a sweet festival—a festival of the heart, a festival of awakening—for Tamil Nadu's poor and humble people, for those born as Tamils, for those who hold their heads high and call the Dravidian community their people.

**With affection, M.K.**  
25-08-2011
