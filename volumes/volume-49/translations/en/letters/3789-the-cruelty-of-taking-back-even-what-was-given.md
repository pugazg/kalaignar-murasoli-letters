# 3789. The Cruelty of Taking Back Even What Was Given!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Udanpirappē,**

On the morning of 26 July 2013, the newspapers carried in large type the headline: “New Pay Increase for Tamil Nadu Government Employees—Employees’ Associations Welcome It.” I read the report wondering: the AIADMK Government has not appointed any new Pay Commission; no increase in dearness allowance is now pending; what pay increase is this? It is being announced only today, and already the employees’ associations have welcomed it?

It was under the DMK Government, in 2009, that revised pay was granted to Tamil Nadu Government employees in accordance with the recommendations of the Union Government’s Sixth Pay Commission. Even earlier, when the DMK assumed office for the third time in 1989, State employees had been receiving scales below those of Union Government employees. The DMK Government changed that position and, for the first time in Tamil Nadu, granted State employees pay scales equivalent to those at the Centre.

Likewise, after the DMK returned to office in 1996, an officers’ committee appointed by the Government examined the Fifth Central Pay Commission’s recommendations, after which the revised Central scales were extended to State Government employees. Later, under the DMK Government that assumed office in 2006, revised pay scales, pensions and family pensions were granted with retrospective effect from 1 January 2006 in accordance with the Sixth Central Pay Commission. This created an additional recurring expenditure of ₹5,155.79 crore a year for the Tamil Nadu Government. Payment of arrears arising from the revised fixation of pay cost the State another ₹11,093 crore.

When employee associations represented to me that a few anomalies remained in the revised pay, I accepted their request. During the DMK period itself, a one-person committee headed by Rajiv Ranjan, IAS, was appointed to investigate and report on those anomalies. Many were removed on its recommendation, resulting in a further recurring expenditure of ₹223 crore for the State.

After the change of Government in 2011, some associations said that further anomalies remained. The AIADMK Government therefore appointed a committee in 2012 under Krishnan, IAS. Additional Secretary to Finance Padmanabhan and Joint Secretary Umanath were also members. On the basis of that committee’s recommendations, Government Orders were issued granting a limited increase in pay scales to certain employees. This was the announcement reported in all the newspapers.

Before there had been time even to learn which employees would receive what increase, reports accompanying the announcement said that the Tamil Nadu Secretariat Association, the Tamil Nadu Government Officials’ Union, the Tamil Nadu Retired Officials’ Association, the State Central Association of Government Office Assistants and Basic Employees, the Tamil Nadu Cooperative Audit Department Officials’ Association and several other bodies had thanked the Chief Minister for removing Sixth Pay Commission anomalies and announcing new pay increases. I wondered how all these associations had learnt the details and issued statements of thanks even before the announcement came out.

After reading the announcement and the Government Orders in full, some people expressed their views in the newspapers over the following days. The Tamil Nadu Government Employees’ Association said that no orders had been issued on several demands relating to pay anomalies and that many of its representations had not been properly considered.

For example, it had demanded a ten per cent monetary benefit on promotion; an increase in the grade pay of assistants; and regular scales for panchayat secretaries, village employees, nutritious-meal workers, anganwadi workers and others receiving special scales. No Government Order addressed those demands. Nor had an order been issued implementing Jayalalithaa’s 2011 Assembly-election promise to abandon the new pension scheme and restore the old one. The association’s State President, Pandian, therefore announced that the planned hunger strike pressing twenty-five demands would be held near the Government Guest House at Chepauk on 2 August.

The very *Dinamalar* that had carried the large headline “Government Employees’ Associations Welcome It” on 26 July published a half-page report on 31 July under the headline: “Pay of Police Inspectors and Tahsildars Cut—Severe Discontent over Government Action.”

That report said it had now become clear that the new Government Order reduced salaries already being received by many employees, causing deep shock among those affected. It listed agricultural officers and horticultural officers; assistant engineers in agricultural engineering; assistant directors and research assistants in animal husbandry; inspectors and research assistants in fisheries; assistant engineers and electronic engineers in highways; assistant engineers in rural development and industries; assistant inspectors of factories; assistant engineers and workshop superintendents in State health transport; technical assistants and general foremen in motor-vehicle maintenance; administrative officers in sericulture; junior engineers in the Public Works Department; district rehabilitation officers; assistant engineers in town panchayats; junior electrical inspectors; assistant engineers in the Chennai Corporation; forest guards; and others.

Before the Sixth Pay revision, these employees had received a basic salary of ₹6,500. When the revision was implemented under the DMK Government, it rose to ₹9,300. On the recommendation of the first one-person committee, again under the DMK Government, it rose to ₹15,600. The present Government now calls that an error and has reduced the basic salary back to ₹9,300, causing a loss of up to ₹10,000 a month.

Similarly, before the Sixth Pay Commission, the basic pay of tahsildars and police inspectors was ₹7,500. It rose to ₹9,300 with the pay revision and to ₹15,600 after the one-person committee’s recommendation, both under the DMK Government. It has now again been cut to ₹9,300, producing severe shock among the employees concerned.

Deputy Secretaries in the Secretariat are also said to be dissatisfied because their request to remove the large disparity between their pay and that of Joint Secretaries was not accepted.

These, then, are the details of the “pay increase” (?) granted to Government employees under the AIADMK regime. Yet the moment the Government announced it as an increase, several associations issued statements thanking the Chief Minister—thanks, thanks!

The *Deccan Chronicle* of 31 July 2013 reported in English that “a volcano” appeared to be simmering within the Tamil Nadu bureaucracy across almost every department; that thousands of employees, including police inspectors, faced steep salary cuts; and that several unions were preparing petitions asking the Chief Minister for a merciful review, according to PWD Engineers’ Association President K. Mohanraj. The same newspaper carried the headline: “Government staff are facing prospect of deep salary cuts following latest order—Asst. Engineers to be hit by Government Order.”

I have set out here the categories affected by the AIADMK Government’s decisions. Keeping in mind the unions’ statement, also reported by the *Deccan Chronicle*, that the “Law of Natural Justice” requires that emoluments and benefits already granted to an employee should not be reduced, the DMK urges the Government to act magnanimously in this pay dispute.

Pichaiyappa, former State President of the Tamil Nadu Government Employees’ Association, explained the matter through an analogy. Suppose the Government gives one person two meals a day and another only one. The person receiving one meal asks to be given two like the other. If the Government responds, “You are asking only because he gets two; therefore I shall reduce his entitlement to one,” that is exactly what this salary-cut affair resembles.

Tamilselvi, State President of the Government Employees’ Association, said that, so far as they could see, none of the anomalies had been corrected. Basic pay had been cut; grade pay had been cut; grade pay needed for promotion had not been increased; and transport allowance, education allowance and house-rent allowance had not been addressed. Overall, she said, the anomalies remained unresolved.

Members of the Tamil Nadu Teachers’ Federation, disappointed by the Government’s Pay Commission order, have announced a three-stage agitation.

It is not good for some employees’ associations and newspapers to close their eyes, rush to endorse an AIADMK announcement without calmly and deeply examining what it contains, and only later, after learning the truth, become shocked and angry.

Let me recall specifically that Chief Minister Jayalalithaa declared in the Assembly that, because it had become impossible to make officials work, she was getting work out of them with the crack of a whip like a “ringmaster.” She also asked what would remain for development work if ninety-four per cent of the Government’s total revenue went to Government employees. Under the AIADMK regime, lakhs of employees were arrested in their homes by the police overnight under ESMA, and one lakh and seventy thousand Government employees were dismissed in a single day.

When all that is compared with the benefits showered upon Government employees under the DMK Government, the employees themselves know very well that the DMK period was the golden age of Government employees and teachers.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**5 August 2013**
