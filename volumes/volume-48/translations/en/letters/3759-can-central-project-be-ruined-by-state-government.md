# 3759. Can a Union Government Project Be Ruined by a State Government?

**Udanpirappē,**

Today's *The Hindu* carries, with a photograph, a report under the headline **“Expressway : Contractor demands Rs. 103.95 Crores”**. The report reads:

> “Soma Constructions Ltd., the Contractor of the Chennai Port -
> Maduravoyal Elevated Expressway Project, has sought Rs. 103.95 crore
> from the National Highways Authority of India. The latest claim from
> the company, for the four-month period from January 1, 2013 is towards
> idling and loss of turnover profit. The Rs. 1815 crore project that will
> allow residents of Poonamallee and Vanagaram to commute to the City
> quicker, has been on hold for over 13 months after the Water Resources
> Department issued stop notice in March 2012 citing a deviation from
> the original alignment. The issue is in Court. Around Rs. 500 crore has
> already been spent on the project and 20 percent of the work completed.
> In February, it had sought Rs. 668.8 crore as compensation for the delay
> in executing the project. In a recent letter to the NHAI, Soma has also
> sought an interest of Rs. 14 crore for March and April for non-payment
> of compensation within 30 days”

In other words, Soma Constructions, which has undertaken the Chennai Port–Maduravoyal Elevated Expressway project, has asked the National Highways Authority of India for ₹103.95 crore. It has sought this amount for four months from 1 January 2013 during which no work took place, and for the resulting loss of profit. The Tamil Nadu Government's Water Resources Department issued a stop notice in March 2012, citing deviation from the original alignment, and the work has consequently remained halted for the past thirteen months. The total estimated cost of the project is ₹1,815 crore. If completed, it would allow those travelling from the Poonamallee and Vanagaram areas to reach Chennai more quickly. The dispute concerning the State Government's notice has gone to court.

Soma Constructions had already sought ₹668.8 crore in February as compensation for delay in implementing the project. In a recent letter to NHAI it has also sought ₹14 crore as interest for March and April because the compensation due to it was not paid within thirty days.

This is what *The Hindu* reported on 20 May 2013.

In the election manifesto issued by the DMK for the 2006 Tamil Nadu Assembly election, we had said:

> “We will implement the **Elevated Highway** scheme in areas of Chennai city that face severe traffic congestion.”

After the DMK assumed office, I wrote to Prime Minister Manmohan Singh on 5 June 2006 requesting approval for a high-level elevated road to Chennai Port.

Through persistent efforts by people including Thambi T.R. Baalu, the Union Government approved the elevated-road project connecting Chennai Port and the Maduravoyal junction. Prime Minister Manmohan Singh himself came on 8 January 2009 and, under my leadership as Chief Minister, laid the foundation stone for the project.

It was announced at the time that this would become the longest elevated road in India.

To facilitate uninterrupted container traffic from Chennai Port, the Tamil Nadu Government gave in-principle approval on 22 June 2007 to the National Highways Authority of India for an elevated expressway along one bank of the Cooum River. The State Government also agreed to bear, on a 50:50 basis with the Chennai Port Trust, the cost of land acquisition required for the project and the cost of rehabilitation and resettlement of those affected. The Tamil Nadu Government's share was ₹130 crore.

In addition, the State Government assured NHAI of full cooperation in matters such as obtaining permission under pollution-control law and shifting drinking-water pipelines, sewer lines and electricity poles in connection with construction of the elevated road.

When very large projects of this kind have been initiated or implemented during DMK rule, how does the present Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu react? Does she not look only for a way to conduct their closing ceremony? Is the Sethu Samudram project not example enough?

That project, Tamil Nadu's 150-year dream, was pursued strenuously during DMK rule. We argued and fought for it with the Union Government, obtained approval, and held the foundation-laying function in Madurai with the Prime Minister, Sonia Gandhi, myself and leaders of major Tamil Nadu parties participating. After more than ₹800 crore of taxpayers' money had been spent, the AIADMK government blocked the project for months by citing some reason. Is Tamil Nadu unaware of that?

And all this despite the fact that when M.G.R. was Chief Minister, a resolution had been passed in the Assembly seeking the Sethu Samudram project. Even AIADMK election manifestos under Jayalalithaa had demanded its implementation. Yet the same party has now filed a petition in the Supreme Court saying the project itself should not proceed. What more evidence is needed of the kind of government now functioning in Tamil Nadu?

*The Hindu*, *Times of India* and *Daily Thanthi* have already published detailed reports about the Chennai Port–Maduravoyal project. According to those reports, for the past year there has not even been a sign that work will restart.

Because the AIADMK government prevented this project from continuing, Soma—the company carrying out the work—had already sought ₹668 crore from NHAI as compensation for its losses. *The Hindu* now reports that it has sought another ₹103.95 crore as compensation for the latest four-month delay.

If the delay continues, there is a danger that the compensation claim will keep increasing. The National Highways Authority of India has been deeply concerned by the Tamil Nadu Government's approach. While major infrastructure projects of this kind are being implemented rapidly in other States without obstruction, with wholehearted cooperation and assistance from State Governments, only in Tamil Nadu has the State Government's negative approach delayed the project to the point where contractors are demanding compensation.

For the past week the Chief Minister has at least had some rest from reading statements under Rule 110. I therefore urge her, at least now, to set vindictiveness aside. Instead of earning a bad name by making arrangements to shelve schemes merely because they were drawn up under DMK rule, she should take immediate steps to restart all these stalled projects.

**With affection,**  
**M.K.**  
**21 May 2013**

இந்தச் சென்னைத் துறைமுகம் - மதுரவாயல் திட்டத்திற்கான

நாளேடுகள் ஏற்கனவே விரிவாகச் செய்திகள் வெளியிட்டுள்ளன.
அவற்றில் வந்துள்ள தகவல்படி, கடந்த ஒராண்டாக மீண்டும்
தொடங்குவதற்கான அறிகுறிகூடத் தெரியவில்லை.
இந்தத் திட்டத்தைத் தொடரவிடாமல் அ.தி.மு.க.
