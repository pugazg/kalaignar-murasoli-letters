# 3805. Only a Referendum Can Give Eelam Tamils a New Life!

> **Translator’s note**
>
> This translation is intended to carry Kalaignar's voice into clear, contemporary English rather than recast the letter as literary or academic prose. It preserves the source's argument, political directness, rhetorical questions, repetition, irony, factual detail, and paragraph order. Names, dates, figures, quotations, and intentional English expressions are retained. Where Tamil idiom cannot be reproduced literally without sounding unnatural, the English follows its sense and rhetorical force without adding claims absent from the source. The original Tamil is reproduced in full below the translation and remains the authoritative text. `Udanpirappē` is retained in Tamil transliteration rather than flattened into “brother,” “sister,” or “comrade.” Literally evoking “one born alongside me,” Kalaignar uses it as a distinctive address of shared identity, equality, affection, and solidarity within the movement.

**Date:** 13 September 2013

**Udanpirappē,**

Following the Sri Lankan Government's statement that the post-war situation could be directly observed, and in order to examine the human-rights violations committed during the final phase of the war in Sri Lanka, United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights Mrs Navanethem Pillay travelled to Sri Lanka on 25 August. During a week-long visit she inspected several areas, met people affected by the war, listened sympathetically to their grievances and anguish, and held discussions with Sri Lankan President Rajapaksa, political parties, human-rights activists and journalists before returning to Geneva. It is significant that Navanethem Pillay was the first senior United Nations official to visit Sri Lanka after the war.

The international community has continued to press Sri Lanka to identify and punish those responsible for war crimes and to accelerate and complete measures for reconciliation. Against this background, Navanethem Pillay's visit, the explanatory address she is to deliver at the twenty-fourth session of the UN Human Rights Council that began on 9 September 2013, and the report she is expected to submit at the March 2014 session have created great expectations among Tamils throughout the world.

In Sri Lanka, Navanethem Pillay travelled from Jaffna to Kilinochchi and then to places including Puthumathalan, Mullivaikkal and the Keppapilavu model village in Mullaitivu District, as well as Trincomalee. She examined the reconstruction measures undertaken in those war-ravaged areas and met Tamil people to learn the details of their situation.

When she went to Mullivaikkal, Tamil women who had been resettled there told her that basic facilities, including housing and livelihoods, had still not been provided. They said their agricultural lands had been seized by the Sinhala military and asked her to help recover them. Women who had lost many members of their families in the war fell at Navanethem Pillay's feet and wept in anguish; seeing them, she too shed tears.

31 August 2013 was the International Day of Forced Disappearances. That day, Navanethem Pillay met journalists in Colombo and said that the situation in Sri Lanka caused her deep concern. Even four years after the war, people continued to face human-rights problems; women remained unsafe; and the report of the Sri Lankan Government's commission on the disappeared was disappointing. She said the number of troops in the Northern and Eastern Provinces must be reduced, Sri Lanka must enact a Right to Information law, and harassment of human-rights activists by the security forces was increasing. The growing signs that Sri Lanka was moving along an authoritarian path were deeply troubling. It was unacceptable, she said, that human-rights activists who had spoken to her during the visit were later harassed by the police and military. People were increasingly being watched and intimidated, critics of the Government were attacked, and the affected communities needed both reconciliation and the means to live with dignity. Her explanation has only intensified international concern over conditions in Sri Lanka.

Navanethem Pillay stated openly:

> I have received reports that people I met during my visit were later questioned. I strongly object to this surveillance and harassment. Srilanka is showing signs of heading in an increasingly authoritarian direction. The war may have ended, but in the meantime democracy has been undermined and the rule of law eroded. Freedom of expression is under a sustained assault. I have called for the right to information act to be adopted, like many of its neighbours in SAARC.

She also told journalists that she had received information that the military and police visited villages and residential areas in Mullaitivu both before and after her own visits.

Sri Lankan poet Theepachelvan told *India Today*: “We hope Navanethem Pillay's report will fulfil our expectations. We firmly believe that the next report of the UN Human Rights Council will not be as mild as the previous reports.”

Sri Lankan Tamil Member of Parliament Yogeswaran said that Navanethem Pillay's call for Sri Lanka to repeal the Prevention of Terrorism Act and investigate those who disappeared during the war had caused a major setback to the Sri Lankan Government.

When Tamil people in Mullivaikkal wept before her, Navanethem Pillay assured them: “I have come from a distant land precisely to examine your problems. I already know about the issue of the disappeared. In the same way, I have a duty to examine the other problems here and seek solutions. I shall use all the authority available to me to find a solution to your problems.”

Nevertheless:

1. The Sri Lankan Government has still not fulfilled its duty to create a peaceful and secure rehabilitation for the Tamil people.
2. It continues to refuse to act in accordance with the Thirteenth Amendment.
3. It has not completely withdrawn the military from Tamil areas.
4. It continues to encourage Sinhala settlement in Tamil areas.
5. There has been no change in the problem of Tamil homes and lands occupied by the military.
6. Equal constitutional status for the Tamil language has not been recognised.
7. No effort has been made to prepare a complete list of the disappeared and give honest information to the Tamil people.
8. As Sinhala military settlements are being established throughout Jaffna, within another five years there may not even be space for Tamil people to live there.
9. Places of worship belonging to religious minorities continue to be attacked and destroyed.
10. Nothing has been announced about the condition of Tamil political prisoners.
11. Before the Northern Provincial election scheduled for 21 September, even basic democratic procedures have not been followed.
12. No effort has been made to find a permanent solution to the Tamil question.

Countless problems of this kind continue to grow more serious without resolution.

The Sri Lankan Government has neither acted in accordance with the April 2011 report of the United Nations Panel of Experts on the genocide and human-rights violations committed in Sri Lanka, nor with the resolution adopted by the UN Human Rights Council in March 2012, nor with the second resolution adopted by that Council in 2013. It has not even considered implementing what those resolutions required.

The Sri Lankan Government has ignored, without the slightest attention, the reports issued after detailed investigations by fact-finding groups established by various human-rights organisations.

Nor has it made any effort to implement fully the recommendations of its own Lessons Learnt and Reconciliation Commission, the LLRC.

In these circumstances, there is growing hope that the report Navanethem Pillay prepares for the UN Human Rights Council, based on what she directly saw, heard and investigated in Sri Lanka, will bring comfort to the Eelam Tamils and carry their question to the next constructive stage.

We have continuously demanded that the attacks carried out against Sri Lankan Tamils by the Sri Lankan Government and those administering it be declared war crimes and genocide; and that a credible, independent international commission be established to investigate, within a fixed period, the war crimes, violations of human rights and humanitarian law, and acts of genocide committed in Sri Lanka. We must wait and see how far Navanethem Pillay's report will support these demands.

Yet only one course can bring the dawn of a new life: a referendum, under United Nations supervision, among the Tamils living in Sri Lanka and Eelam Tamils across the world, granting them the right to determine the political solution they need. Tamils throughout the world eagerly expect that, after Navanethem Pillay submits the report based on her direct investigation, the United Nations will come forward without hesitation to advance such a permanent solution.

**With affection,**
**M.K.**
13 September 2013

அறிக்கைகளைப் போல மென்மையாக இருக்காது என்றும்
நிச்சயமாக நம்புகிறோம்” என்று இலங்கைக் கவிஞர் தீபச்செல்வன்

வேண்டும்; போரில் காணாமல் போனவர்கள் குறித்து ஆய்வு
நடத்த வேண்டும் என்று நவநீதம் பிள்ளை சொல்லியிருப்பது

இலங்கை அரசுக்குப் பெரும் பின்னடைவை ஏற்படுத்தி உள்ளது”
என்று இலங்கைத் தமிழ் எம்.பி., யோகேஸ்வரன் தெரிவித்துள்ளார்.

முள்ளிவாய்க்காலில் தமிழ் மக்கள் கலங்கிக் கண்ணீர்
வடித்தபோது, “நான் தூர தேசத்திலிருந்து உங்களுடைய
பிரச்சினைகளை ஆராய்வதற்காகவே வந்துள்ளேன். காணாமல்
போனவர்கள் பற்றிய பிரச்சினைகளை நான் முன்னரே அறிவேன்.
அதைப்போல இங்குள்ள மற்ற பிரச்சினைகளையும் ஆராய்ந்து
தீர்வைத் தேடித் தரவேண்டிய கடமை எனக்கு இருக்கிறது.
உங்களுடைய பிரச்சினைகளுக்காக, எனக்குள்ள முழு
அதிகாரத்தையும் பயன்படுத்தித் தீர்வைத் தேடித்தருவேன்”
என்று நவநீதம் பிள்ளை உறுதி அளித்துள்ளார்.

எனினும்,

1) தமிழ் மக்களுக்கு நிம்மதியான மறுவாழ்வை
உருவாக்கித் தரவேண்டும் என்ற கடமையை இலங்கை
அரசு இன்னும் நிறைவேற்றவில்லை.

2) பதிமூன்றாவது சட்டத் திருத்தத்தின்படி நடந்துகொள்ள
மறுத்து வருகிறது.

3) தமிழர் பகுதிகளில் இருந்து ராணுவத்தை முற்றிலுமாக
விலக்கிக் கொள்ளவில்லை.

4) தமிழர் பகுதிகளில் சிங்களவர் குடியேற்றத்தை
ஊக்குவித்து வருகிறது.

5) ராணுவத்தால் ஆக்கிரமிப்பு செய்யப்பட்ட தமிழர்களின்
வீடுகள் மற்றும் நிலங்கள் குறித்த பிரச்சினையில்
மாற்றம் இல்லை.

6) தமிழ் மொழிக்கான சமஉரிமை அரசியல் சட்டரீதியாக
அங்கீகாரம் செய்யப்படவில்லை.

7) காணாமல் போனவர்கள் பற்றிய முழுமையான
பட்டியலைத் தயாரித்து நேர்மையான விவரங்களைத்
தமிழ் மக்களிடம் எடுத்துச்சொல்ல எந்த முயற்சியும்
இல்லை.

8) யாழ்ப்பாணத்தில் எல்லா இடங்களிலும் சிங்கள ராணுவக்
குடியிருப்புகள் அமைக்கப்பட்டு வருவதால் இன்னும்

வாழ்வதற்குக்கூட இடமில்லாமல் போகலாம்.

9) மதவழிச் சிறுபான்மையோரின் வழிபாட்டுத்தலங்கள்
தொடர்ந்து தாக்கப்பட்டும் அழிக்கப்பட்டும் வருகின்றன.

10) தமிழ் அரசியல் கைதிகளின் நிலை என்ன என்பது பற்றி
எதுவும் அறிவிக்கப்பட வில்லை.

11) வடக்கு மாகாணத்தில் செப்டம்பர் 21 அன்று நடைபெற

வழிமுறைகள் எதுவும் பின்பற்றப்படவில்லை.

12) தமிழர்கள் பிரச்சினையில் நிரந்தரமாகத் தீர்வு காண எந்த

ஐக்கிய நாடுகள் அவையின் வல்லுனர் குழு இலங்கையில்

இரண்டாவது முறையாக ஐ.நா. மனித உரிமை ஆணையத்தால்
நிறைவேற்றப்பட்ட தீர்மானத்தின்படியோ, இலங்கை அரசு நடந்து

நிறைவேற்றுவது பற்றிச் சிந்தித்துப் பார்க்கவும் இல்லை.

கண்டறியும் குழுக்களை அமைத்து, தீர விசாரித்து வெளியிட்ட
அறிக்கைகள் எதையும் இலங்கை அரசு சிறிதும் காதில் போட்டுக்

அறிக்கையில் உள்ள பரிந்துரைகளை முழுமையாக நிறைவேற்றிட
இலங்கை அரசு எந்தவித முயற்சியும் எடுக்கவில்லை.

இந்த நிலையில், நவநீதம் பிள்ளை இலங்கை சென்று,
நேரடியாகக் கண்டும் கேட்டும், ஆய்ந்தறிந்தும் தெரிவிக்கும்

அவர்களுடைய பிரச்சினையை அடுத்த ஆக்கப்பூர்வமான

வளர்ந்திருக்கிறது.
இலங்கை அரசாலும், இலங்கை அரசாங்கத்தின் நிர்வாகத்தில்
உள்ளவர்களாலும், இலங்கைத் தமிழர்கள்மீது நடத்தப்பட்ட
தாக்குதல்களைப் போர்க் குற்றங்கள் என்றும், இனப் படுகொலை
